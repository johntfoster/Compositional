\begin{abstract}
	A thermodynamically consistent theory is developed for multicomponent,
	multiphase porous media with finite deformation, electrostatics, chemical
	reactions, and phase transformations.  Component mass conservation is written
	for phase-attached material regions and then volume averaged, identifying
	molecular-diffusion and mechanical-dispersion fluxes within the component
	material increments.  Mobile fluids and load-bearing multicomponent solids are
	described with common phase/component variables, so solid composition can
	influence stress-free deformation, elasticity, plastic flow, and nonlinear
	Biot coupling in one notation.  A fixed-pressure Legendre transform gives
	reaction- and composition-modified Biot coefficients, including component and
	phase mixture rules for multiphase solid skeletons.

	The central derivation couples a constrained Hamilton--d'Alembert variation
	for reversible processes with an Onsager stationarity condition for
	dissipative reaction and relative-transport rates.  Varying the component
	material measures and phase Jacobians together produces conversion-insertion
	momentum terms tied to component material insertion and leads to a generalized
	Darcy-like relative-flux law.  The same stationarity system gives
	composition stationarity, normalization equations, compatibility conditions for
	the transfer potential, and equilibrium phase-transfer conditions obtained by
	setting the relevant affinities to zero.  Electrical effects enter by treating
	charge as a component-weighted projection of the mass measures, so the
	potentials driving affinity and relative transport become electrochemical
	when electric field energy is retained.  The entropy inequality identifies
	admissible stress, pressure, reaction-affinity, drag, diffusion, dispersion,
	and plastic-flow relations, while standard compositional flow, black-oil flow,
	nonlinear poromechanics, and single-solid, single-fluid Biot theory emerge as
	special limits of the full solid-reference formulation.
\end{abstract}
