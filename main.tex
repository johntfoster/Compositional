\begin{abstract}
	A thermodynamically consistent theory is developed for multicomponent,
	multiphase porous media undergoing finite deformation, chemical reaction, and
	phase transformation. Component mass conservation is first written for
	phase-attached material regions and then volume averaged. This procedure
	identifies molecular-diffusion and mechanical-dispersion fluxes and their
	accumulated contributions to the component material measures. Mobile fluids
	and load-bearing multicomponent solids are treated with the same
	phase/component variables, while solid composition consistently influences
	stress-free deformation, elasticity, plastic flow, and the nonlinear Biot
	coupling. The distinctive contribution is to vary the phase motion, component
	composition, conversion progress, relative component fluxes, and accumulated
	component mass within one constrained virtual-power statement. Because the
	component material measures and phase Jacobians are varied together, conversion
	and relative-transport accumulation generate momentum terms that are absent
	from conventional compositional balances. These terms produce a generalized
	Darcy-like relative-flux law in which component mass-addition rates modify the
	effective resistance and introduce chemical-potential-gradient and
	momentum-production contributions in addition to the usual pressure, gravity,
	and inertia terms. The same variational statement gives composition stationarity and
	normalization equations, chemical-potential evolution equations, and
	interphase equilibrium as equality of component chemical potentials. It
	therefore provides a coupled route for determining phase compositions from a
	Helmholtz free energy without imposing a separate flash calculation as an
	external closure. The entropy inequality identifies admissible stress,
	pressure, reaction-affinity, drag, diffusion, dispersion, and plastic-flow
	relations. Standard compositional flow, black-oil flow, nonlinear
	poromechanics, and the single-solid, single-fluid Biot theory follow as special
	limits, and the full system is pulled back to the solid reference configuration
	for finite-deformation reservoir simulation.
\end{abstract}
