\begin{abstract}
	A thermodynamically consistent theory is developed for multicomponent,
	multiphase porous media undergoing finite deformation, chemical reaction, and
	phase transformation.  Component mass conservation is first written for
	phase-attached material regions and then volume averaged.  This construction
	identifies molecular-diffusion and mechanical-dispersion fluxes together with
	their accumulated contributions to component material measures.  Mobile fluids
	and load-bearing multicomponent solids are described with the same
	phase/component variables, so solid composition can influence stress-free
	deformation, elasticity, plastic flow, and nonlinear Biot coupling within one
	notation.  The central derivation couples a constrained
	Hamilton--d'Alembert variation for reversible coordinates with an Onsager
	stationarity condition for dissipative reaction and relative-transport rates.
	Because the component material measures and phase Jacobians are varied
	together, conversion insertion generates momentum terms that do not appear in
	conventional compositional balances.  These terms lead to a generalized
	Darcy-like relative-flux law in which conversion mass-addition rates modify the
	effective resistance and introduce transfer-potential and momentum-production
	contributions alongside the usual pressure, gravity, and inertia terms.  The
	coupled stationarity conditions give composition stationarity, normalization
	equations, transfer-potential evolution equations, and interphase equilibrium
	as equality of component chemical potentials.  It
	therefore gives a coupled route for determining phase compositions from a
	Helmholtz free energy, with flash calculations appearing as special closures
	rather than external assumptions.  The entropy inequality identifies
	admissible stress, pressure, reaction-affinity, drag, diffusion, dispersion,
	and plastic-flow relations.  Electrical effects are incorporated by treating
	charge as a component-weighted projection of the same mass measures; when
	electric field energy is retained, the component potential driving affinity
	and relative transport is electrochemical.  Standard compositional flow,
	black-oil flow, nonlinear poromechanics, and the single-solid,
	single-fluid Biot theory follow as special limits, and the full system is
	pulled back to the solid reference configuration for finite-deformation
	reservoir simulation.
\end{abstract}
