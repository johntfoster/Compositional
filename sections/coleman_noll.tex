\subsection{Coleman--Noll procedure for the compositional thermo-reactive mixture}
\label{sec:coleman_noll_compositional}

This subsection records the thermodynamic argument in a form that mirrors the
kinematic split just introduced.  Mobile phases are treated with phase-level
Helmholtz potentials suitable for equation-of-state closures, while the solid
phase is allowed to use component Helmholtz energies subject to density,
composition, and dilation constraints.

The Coleman--Noll calculation is applied on the reduced constitutive state space.
It is not a second derivation of the constrained equations of motion.  Constraints
that define the admissible motions and fields, such as the volume-fraction,
storage, and stoichiometric source constraints, are enforced in the variational
formulation and do not introduce additional Coleman--Noll coefficients unless a
constrained variable is an argument of a constitutive free energy.  Following
the standard Liu multiplier exploitation of constrained entropy
inequalities~\cite{liu1972method}, identically vanishing constraint-rate terms
may be added before collecting coefficients.  Composition enters here because
the free energies depend explicitly on $\eta_\xi^\alpha$; its rate is therefore
restricted to the tangent space of the mass-fraction constraint,
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha=1,
	\qquad
	\sum_\alpha\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha=0 .
	\label{eq:CN_mass_fraction_tangent_constraint}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

The mobile and solid phases use different Helmholtz structures.  Mobile phases
are modeled by phase-level equation-of-state potentials, while the solid skeleton
is allowed to use a component-additive Helmholtz decomposition.  For a mobile
phase \(l\), let
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	e_l=\psi_l+\theta s_l,
	\qquad
	\psi_l=\psi_l(\bar\rho_l,\eta_l^\beta,\theta).
	\label{eq:CN_fluid_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
For each solid component let
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	e_s^\alpha=\psi_s^\alpha+\theta s_s^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_component_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The solid phase free energy is then written as a mass-specific component
mixture potential,
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\psi_s=\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha\psi_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\rho_s\psi_s=\sum_\alpha\rho_s^\alpha\psi_s^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_free_energy_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The component volume fraction within the solid phase, $\phi_s^\alpha$, is a
derived volume weight rather than an independent Coleman--Noll state variable.
The detailed solid component mass partition is introduced below only when the
stress, pressure, and distension mixture rules are read from the reversible
coefficients.
For the solid, the primitive component Helmholtz free energies use the physical
intrinsic density of each solid component.  The mixture density therefore enters
through the component-density parametrization
$\bar\rho_s^\alpha=\bar\rho_s^\alpha(\bar\rho_s,\eta_s^\beta)$.  The reduced
solid state is
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{S} = \{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e, \mbf A_s^e, \bar\rho_s^\beta,
	\theta,\eta_s^\beta \}
	\label{eq:CN_solid_state_set}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%


% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\psi_s^\alpha
	=\psi_s^\alpha(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e, \mbf A_s^e, \bar\rho_s^\alpha,
	\theta,\eta_s^\beta).
	\label{eq:CN_solid_component_free_energy_state}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Here $\bar{\mbf F}_s^e$ is the dependent elastic factor determined by
\eqref{eq:true_elastic_plastic_thermal_split};
$\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta=\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta(\theta,\phi_s^\beta)$ is prescribed,
and $\bar{\mbf F}_s^p$ is closed by a constitutive flow rule.  Neither
$\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta$ nor $\bar{\mbf F}_s^p$ is an elastic free-energy
argument.  Similarly, the distension split has already been introduced in
\eqref{eq:solid_tensor_distension_split}.  The reversible tensor
$\mbf A_s^\theta=\mbf A_s^\theta(\theta,\phi_s^\beta)$ is the stress-free
thermal--compositional pore-architecture, or pore-volume, part of the
distension.  It is distinct from the intrinsic mineral thermal dilation carried
by $\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta$ and depends on composition through the solid
component volume fractions $\phi_s^\beta$, not directly through the mass
fractions $\eta_s^\beta$.  The tensor $\mbf A_s^p$ is a plastic internal
variable whose rate is closed by a constitutive flow rule.  The total distension
tensor $\mbf A_s$ remains the kinematic field in the balance laws and stress
power; the elastic free-energy argument is
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf A_s^e
	=\mbf A_s(\mbf A_s^\theta)^{-1}(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}.
	\label{eq:CN_elastic_distension_argument}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
In the Coleman--Noll collection below the independent mechanical process rates are
$\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e$, $\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p$,
$\dot{\mbf A}_s^e$, and $\dot{\mbf A}_s^p$.  The total rates
$\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s$ and $\dot{\mbf A}_s$ are reconstructed from these rates
and from the stress-free thermal/compositional rates; they are not independent
Coleman--Noll rates in the solid calculation.  Since the balance
laws still use the mixture variables $(\bar\rho_s,\eta_s^\beta)$, density
derivatives of the solid energy are chain-ruled through
$\bar\rho_s^\alpha(\bar\rho_s,\eta_s^\beta)$.  The solid intrinsic density rate
is also not independent.  Following Drumheller's density identity for an
isochoric plastic true deformation \cite[eq.~(87)]{drumheller2000}, but retaining
the thermal/compositional true-dilation factor omitted in Drumheller's model, we
use
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_s}{\bar\rho_s}
	=-\operatorname{tr}
	\left[
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
		+\left(
		\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}\dot\theta
		+\sum_\beta
		\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
		\dot\eta_s^\beta
		\right)(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}
		\right],
	\qquad
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]=0 .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_density_rate_drumheller}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The second term in the trace is the rate of the prescribed stress-free true
dilation.  If $\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta=\mbf I$, this reduces to Drumheller's
elastic identity.  In the solid specialization used below, a mixture-density
change at fixed composition scales the component intrinsic densities at fixed
component volume fraction:
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\dot{\bar\rho}_s^\alpha
	=\frac{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s}\dot{\bar\rho}_s
	+\sum_\beta
	\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
	\dot\eta_s^\beta .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_density_rate_chain_rule}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Derivatives of $\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta$ and $\mbf A_s^\theta$ with respect to
composition are total derivatives through the derived component volume
fractions.  The state sets used in the held-fixed derivative notation are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mc X\in\{\mc S,\mc F,\mc T_s\},
	\qquad
	\mc T_s=\{\theta,\phi_s^\beta\}.
	\label{eq:CN_state_set_collection}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
For the mobile fluid phases, the Coleman--Noll state variables are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mc F
	=\left\{\bar\rho_\ell,\eta_\ell^\beta,\theta\right\}.
	\label{eq:CN_fluid_state_variables}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
When a derivative is written for a generic phase, let
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mc X_\xi =
	\begin{cases}
		\mc S, & \xi=s,    \\
		\mc F, & \xi=\ell,
	\end{cases}
	\label{eq:CN_generic_state_set}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The held-fixed notation is used as follows:
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\p{\Gamma_\xi}{z}\heldfixed{\mc X}
	\label{eq:CN_held_fixed_notation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
This denotes differentiation with respect to $z$ while holding fixed all other
members of the state set $\mc X$.
The stress-free thermal dilation derivative with respect to composition is
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	=\sum_\gamma
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\phi_s^\gamma}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}
	\left.\p{\phi_s^\gamma}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S} .
	\label{eq:CN_thermal_dilation_composition_chain_rule}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
and the corresponding distension derivative is
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\p{\mbf A_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	=\sum_\gamma
	\left.\p{\mbf A_s^\theta}{\phi_s^\gamma}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}
	\left.\p{\phi_s^\gamma}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S} .
	\label{eq:CN_distension_tensor_composition_chain_rule}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The derivatives of the stress-free factors are retained because $\theta$ and
$\eta_s^\beta$ are primitive variables and the component volume fractions are
derived from the mass fractions.
Thus the fluid-phase free-energy rates entering the entropy inequality are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\sum_\ell\rho_\ell\dot\psi_\ell
	 & =\sum_\ell\rho_\ell\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_\ell}{\bar\rho_\ell}\heldfixed{\mc F}\dot{\bar\rho}_\ell
	+\sum_\beta\left.\p{\psi_\ell}{\eta_\ell^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc F}\dot\eta_\ell^\beta
	+\left.\p{\psi_\ell}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc F}\dot\theta
	\right).
	\label{eq:CN_fluid_free_energy_rate}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The dependent quantities
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\rho_\xi^\alpha
	 & = \phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha,
	\notag                                    \\
	\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha
	 & = 1,
	\notag                                    \\
	\bar J_s
	 & = \det\bar{\mbf F}_s
	\notag                                    \\
	 & = \frac{J_s}{a_s}
	\label{eq:CN_kinematic_dependencies}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

For the solid, component additivity gives
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\sum_\alpha\rho_s^\alpha\dot{\psi}_s^\alpha
	 & =\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha\dot\psi_s^\alpha \notag \\
	 & =\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha \left(
	\vphantom{
		\left[
			\begin{aligned}
				 & -\bar\rho_s^\alpha\operatorname{tr}
				\left[
					\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
					\right]
				\\
				 & +\sum_\beta
				\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
				\dot\eta_s^\beta
			\end{aligned}
			\right]
	}
	\right.
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf{F}}^e_s}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	:\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e
	+\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\mbf A_s^e}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	:\dot{\mbf A}_s^e
	\notag                                                     \\
	 & \qquad
	+\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\left[
		\vphantom{
			\begin{aligned}
				 & -\bar\rho_s^\alpha\operatorname{tr}
				\left[
					\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
					\right]
				\\
				 & +\sum_\beta
				\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
				\dot\eta_s^\beta
			\end{aligned}
		}
		\right.
		-\bar\rho_s^\alpha\operatorname{tr}
		\left[
			\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
			+\left(
			\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}\dot\theta
			+\sum_\gamma
			\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\gamma}\heldfixed{\mc S}
			\dot\eta_s^\gamma
			\right)(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}
	\right] \notag                                             \\
	 & \qquad
		+\sum_\beta
		\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
		\dot\eta_s^\beta
		\left. \vphantom{
			\begin{aligned}
				 & -\bar\rho_s^\alpha\operatorname{tr}
				\left[
					\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
					\right]
				\\
				 & +\sum_\beta
				\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
				\dot\eta_s^\beta
			\end{aligned}
		} \right]
	+\sum_\beta\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}\dot\eta_s^\beta
	+\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc S}\dot\theta
	\left. \vphantom{
		\left[
			\begin{aligned}
				 & -\bar\rho_s^\alpha\operatorname{tr}
				\left[
					\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
					\right]
				\\
				 & +\sum_\beta
				\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
				\dot\eta_s^\beta
			\end{aligned}
			\right]
	} \right).
	\label{eq:CN_total_helmholtz_density}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Following Drumheller~\cite{drumheller2000}, the plastic true deformation is
isochoric:
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation*}
	\det\bar{\mbf F}_s^p=1,
	\qquad
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]=0 .
\end{equation*}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The mobile-phase Helmholtz energies are phase EOS potentials using the
fluid-state variables in \eqref{eq:CN_fluid_state_variables}.  They are not
assumed to decompose into mass-fraction averages of component Helmholtz
energies.  No elastic stress power is assigned to the mobile phases, so
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\ell'=\mbf 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_mobile_stress_free}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The reversible interfacial energy used in the variational formulation must also
be included in the entropy bookkeeping.  The argument
$\left\{S_l\right\}_{l\in\mc M}$ denotes the constrained mobile saturation state
defined in \eqref{eq:mobile_saturation_definitions}; equivalently, one may
choose any independent set of mobile saturations and recover the remaining
saturation from the constraint.  For thermal reservoir simulation, the
interfacial energy is allowed to depend on temperature through the interfacial
tension, as in the standard capillary-pressure scaling used in reservoir
engineering \cite{leverett1941capillary}.  For the mobile phases,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\psi_\gamma^{\rm vol}
	=\phi\gamma\left(\left\{S_l\right\}_{l\in\mc M},\theta\right),
	\qquad
	\dot\psi_\gamma^{\rm vol}
	=\sum_l\gamma_l\dot\phi_l
	+\phi
	\left.\p{\gamma}{\theta}\right\vert_{\left\{S_l\right\}_{l\in\mc M}}\dot\theta,
	\label{eq:CN_interfacial_helmholtz_rate}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where $\gamma_l$ are the interfacial variation coefficients collected in
\eqref{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives}, with the constrained mobile saturation
state held fixed for the temperature derivative.  The associated interfacial
entropy per current volume is
$-\phi\,\left.\p{\gamma}{\theta}\right\vert_{\left\{S_l\right\}_{l\in\mc M}}$.
The volume fractions are constrained by $\sum_\xi\phi_\xi=1$, so their rates
are not independent.  When the capillary Helmholtz rate is retained explicitly,
we add the identically vanishing volume-fraction-constraint rate
$\lambda\sum_\xi\dot\phi_\xi$ and retain the corresponding
$\dot\phi_\xi/\phi_\xi$ contribution from the component-storage multiplier.
Define the source-carried specific potentials and entropies by
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\widehat\psi_\xi^\alpha =
	\begin{cases}
		\psi_l,        & \xi=l\hbox{ mobile}, \\
		\psi_s^\alpha, & \xi=s,
	\end{cases}
	\qquad
	\widehat s_\xi^\alpha =
	\begin{cases}
		s_l,        & \xi=l\hbox{ mobile}, \\
		s_s^\alpha, & \xi=s.
	\end{cases}
	\label{eq:CN_source_carried_potentials}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Equivalently, the source-carried internal energy is
$\widehat e_\xi^\alpha=\widehat\psi_\xi^\alpha+\theta\widehat s_\xi^\alpha$.
This notation keeps the open-system source terms phase/component unified:
mobile source mass carries the mobile phase thermodynamic state, while solid
source mass carries the corresponding solid component state.  The energy
balance is written in phase form.  The phase internal-energy rate is left as
$\rho_\xi\dot e_\xi$ here; the mobile and solid Helmholtz substitutions are made
below when collecting the entropy inequality:
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\rho_\xi\dot e_\xi
	+\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha\widehat e_\xi^\alpha
	= & \; \bs\sigma_\xi':\mbf L_\xi
	+\phi_\xi\bar p_\xi\frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}
	+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}\dot\phi_\xi
	+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha
	-\sum_\alpha L_\xi^\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf q_\xi
	+\rho_\xi r_\xi+\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}.
	\label{eq:CN_energy_balance}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The storage-multiplier terms are the volume-fraction and compositional analogs
of the pressure power.  The multipliers $\tau_\xi^\alpha$ enforce component
material storage in \eqref{eq:C2_def}; when the admissible rates
$\dot\phi_\xi$ and $\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha$ are retained, they do work against the
normalized storage multipliers $\tau_\xi^\alpha/\phi_\xi$ and
$\tau_\xi^\alpha/\eta_\xi^\alpha$.  The coefficient $L_\xi^\alpha$ is the same
component conversion-work potential introduced in \eqref{eq:W_def}; the sign
convention here is chosen so that a source $\dot c_\xi^\alpha$ carries the
free-energy cost $\widehat\psi_\xi^\alpha+L_\xi^\alpha$.
For the solid phase, the mechanical power in \eqref{eq:CN_energy_balance} must
be decomposed with the same kinematic split used in the constitutive law.  With
$\mbf F_s=\mbf A_s\bar{\mbf F}_s$,
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf L_s
	=\dot{\mbf A}_s\mbf A_s^{-1}
	+\mbf A_s\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^{-1}\mbf A_s^{-1}.
	\label{eq:CN_F_split_for_power}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Using
$\bar{\mbf F}_s=\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\bar{\mbf F}_s^p\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta$ and
$\mbf A_s=\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p\mbf A_s^\theta$, the stress power is
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\bs\sigma_s':\mbf L_s
	={} &
	\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
		(\bs\sigma_s')^T\dot{\mbf A}_s^e(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}\right]
	+\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T\mbf A_s^e
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^p(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left.\p{\mbf A_s^\theta}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}
		(\mbf A_s^\theta)^{-1}\right]\dot\theta
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_\beta\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left.\p{\mbf A_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
		(\mbf A_s^\theta)^{-1}\right]\dot\eta_s^\beta
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
	\mbf A_s^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T\mbf A_s
	\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}\right]
	+\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}\mbf A_s^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}\right]\dot\theta
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_\beta\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}\right]\dot\eta_s^\beta .
	\label{eq:CN_stress_power_split}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
For later use, define
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf H_s
	=
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\bar{\mbf F}_s^p .
	\label{eq:CN_H_def}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Similarly, define
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\widehat{\mbf H}_s
	=
	(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p .
	\label{eq:CN_Hhat_def}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The entropy inequality is
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	0\le
	 & \sum_l\left[
		\rho_l\dot s_l+\sum_\alpha\dot c_l^\alpha s_l
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\left(\frac{\mbf q_l}{\theta}\right)
	-\frac{\rho_l r_l}{\theta}\right] \notag          \\

\subsubsection{Solid stress terms and pressure identifications}
The elastic true-deformation coefficient gives
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}(\bs\sigma_s')^T\mbf A_s
	 & =
	\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\right)^T
	+\left(
	\phi_s\bar p_s
	-\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\bar\rho_s^\alpha
	\right)\mbf I .
	\label{eq:CN_stress_definition}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The tensor on the left is the stress power conjugate to
$\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}$.  The isotropic term is the
density-power contribution that appears because the solid intrinsic density rate
is tied to the true-deformation rate by
\eqref{eq:CN_solid_density_rate_drumheller}.  In the isotropic
specialization $\mbf A_s=a_s^{1/3}\mbf I$, the mapped stress reduces to the
single-primed Cauchy stress.
The pressure identification comes from the component mass identity
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s^\alpha
	=\phi_s\bar\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha
	=\phi_s\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\eta_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s
	=\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_component_mass_identity}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using this identity in the density-power coefficient gives the reversible solid
pressure identification
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi_s\bar p_s
	=\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\bar\rho_s^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_pressure_identification}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using $\rho_s=\phi_s\bar\rho_s$ and
$\eta_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s=\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_s
	=\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha
	\left(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\right)^2
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}.
	\label{eq:CN_solid_pressure_component_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Thus, defining each component intrinsic pressure by
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_s^\alpha
	=\left(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\right)^2
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S},
	\label{eq:CN_component_intrinsic_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
the solid pressure is
$\bar p_s=\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_s^\alpha$.  The corresponding mobile
phase pressure is
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_l
	=\left(\bar\rho_l\right)^2
	\left.\p{\psi_l}{\bar\rho_l}\heldfixed{\mc F} .
	\label{eq:CN_phase_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Substituting \eqref{eq:CN_solid_pressure_identification} into
\eqref{eq:CN_stress_definition} removes the isotropic density-power term and
solves for the single-primed Cauchy stress as
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\mbf A_s^{-T}

	\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
	\right)
	\mbf A_s^T .
	\label{eq:CN_pressure_identified_stress}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The component partial Cauchy stress uses the current component partial density.
The intrinsic component stress is the corresponding normalized stress within the
solid phase:

\begin{align}

	\notag \\

\end{align}

% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user

	\notag \\

For the solid phase, the composition coefficient contains the component-density

\subsubsection{Solid and mobile phase composition restrictions}

pressure identification \eqref{eq:CN_solid_pressure_identification} has removed

reversible pore-architecture tensor

		\right],
	\notag                                \\
	          & \qquad \beta=1,\ldots,N .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_composition_multiplier_restriction}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

\eqref{eq:CN_solid_composition_multiplier_restriction} is summed over solid

	\notag          \\

\end{empheq}

		\right]\dot r_{(m)} .
	\label{eq:CN_helmholtz_source_projection}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

	\label{eq:CN_L_mu_psi_closure}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \dot c_\xi^\alpha\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	-\sum_m\left(
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\mu_\xi^\alpha

	\label{eq:CN_mu_source_projection}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

	= -\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_composition_affinity_projection}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Thus the source projection \eqref{eq:CN_mu_source_projection} is exactly
the mechanism force power.

\subsubsection{Plastic flow rules and residual dissipation}
The plastic-deformation power in the residual inequality is driven by the
single-primed stress mapped through the distension and true-deformation
gradients to the plastic-flow configuration.  Because the true-plastic flow is
isochoric, only the deviatoric part of that mapped stress contributes.  Define
the deviatoric true-plastic driving stress by
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf S_s'
	\equiv
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	\left(
	(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s')\mbf I
	\right)
	\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e .
	\label{eq:CN_plastic_driving_stress}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The trace-free property follows from the similarity transformation of the
deviatoric stress:
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\operatorname{tr}\mbf S_s'
	=0 .
	\label{eq:CN_plastic_driving_stress_trace}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
This is the direct analogue of Drumheller's
\cite[eqs.~(108)--(112)]{drumheller2000} true-plastic driving stress: his
deviatoric true stress is replaced here by the single-primed stress first mapped
through the total distension tensor.  The additional thermal and compositional
factors only supply the separate $\dot\theta$ and $\dot\eta_s^\beta$ terms in
\eqref{eq:CN_stress_power_split}; they do not alter the plastic driving
coefficient once the $\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}$ term has
been isolated.  Substituting this definition rewrites the plastic
true-deformation power as the trace term below.
After the reversible coefficients have been set to zero, the remaining
inequality can therefore be written as
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		0\le{}\; &
		\sum_m\mc A_{(m)}\dot r_{(m)}
		+\bs\sigma_s':\mbf A_s^e\dot{\mbf A}_s^p
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}  \\
		         & +\operatorname{tr}\left[
			\mbf S_s'\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p
			(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}\right]
		+\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		-\sum_\xi\frac{\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta} .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:CN_residual_dissipation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Admissible reaction kinetics must make $\sum_m\mc A_{(m)}\dot r_{(m)}\ge 0$, for example
through a closure $\dot r_{(m)}=\mc K_{(m)}(\mc A_{(m)},\text{state})$ with
$\mc A_{(m)}\dot r_{(m)}\ge 0$ for each independent mechanism.
Following the same sufficient construction used by Drumheller~\cite{drumheller2000},
one may choose generic uncoupled flow rules
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:CN_generic_plastic_flow_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =\Lambda_F(\mc S)(\mbf S_s')^T,
		\label{eq:CN_generic_plastic_deformation_flow_rule}            \\
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^p(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =\Lambda_A(\mc S)
		(\mbf A_s^e)^T\bs\sigma_s'(\mbf A_s^e)^{-T},
		\label{eq:CN_generic_plastic_distension_flow_rule}             \\
		\Lambda_F(\mc S)
		 & \ge0,
		\label{eq:CN_generic_plastic_deformation_mobility_nonnegative} \\
		\Lambda_A(\mc S)
		 & \ge0 .
		\label{eq:CN_generic_plastic_distension_mobility_nonnegative}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
with $\Lambda_F(\mc S)$ and $\Lambda_A(\mc S)$ vanishing in a neighborhood of
elastic equilibrium.  Then the isochoric constraint is preserved by
$\operatorname{tr}\mbf S_s'=0$, and the two plastic powers in
\eqref{eq:CN_residual_dissipation} are nonnegative term by term.  More general
coupled plastic deformation--distension laws are admissible if their combined
plastic power is nonnegative.

The interphase exchange term can be closed by a pairwise quadratic construction
for reacting porous mixtures~\cite{foster2026reactingporous}.  Following
Bedford and Drumheller~\cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical}, conservation of
energy for the entire mixture requires the internal interaction-energy supplies
and the interphase momentum supplies to satisfy
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\left(
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	+\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)=0 .
	\label{eq:CN_interaction_energy_conservation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Here $\mbf b_\xi$ is the net interphase momentum supply appearing in the phase
momentum balance.  Decompose it into pairwise interaction forces,
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_\xi
	=\sum_{\zeta\ne\xi}\mbf b_{\zeta\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}=-\mbf b_{\xi\zeta},
	\label{eq:CN_pairwise_interaction_force}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
where $\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}$ is the force exerted on phase $\xi$ by phase $\zeta$.
For each unordered pair, let $\mbf v_{\xi\zeta}$ denote the pairwise relative
velocity,
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf v_{\xi\zeta}=\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_\zeta .
	\label{eq:CN_relative_pair_velocity}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
This velocity difference is distinct from mass-weighted Darcy or diffusion
fluxes, which can be introduced later by multiplying a relative velocity by the
appropriate partial density or mobility.  The energy pairing remains simplest in
terms of $\mbf v_{\xi\zeta}$.
Then \eqref{eq:CN_interaction_energy_conservation} gives
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	=-\sum_{\xi<\zeta}\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}\cdot\mbf v_{\xi\zeta}.
	\label{eq:CN_interaction_power_pairing}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
A linear dissipative interaction law may be written as
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}
	=-\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}^{-1}\mbf v_{\xi\zeta},
	\qquad
	\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}=\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}^{T},
	\qquad
	\mbf y\cdot\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}\mbf y>0
	\quad \forall\,\mbf y\ne\mbf 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_interphase_exchange_law}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Substitution into \eqref{eq:CN_interaction_power_pairing} gives
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	=\sum_{\xi<\zeta}
	\mbf v_{\xi\zeta}\cdot
	\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}^{-1}\mbf v_{\xi\zeta}
	\ge 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_interphase_exchange_positive}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Thus interphase momentum exchange contributes nonnegative entropy production
whenever the mobility tensors are positive definite.

The heat-conduction term is made admissible by a Fourier-type closure
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\mbf q_\xi=-\mbf K_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta,
	\qquad
	\mbf K_\xi=\mbf K_\xi^T,
	\qquad
	\mbf y\cdot\mbf K_\xi\mbf y\ge 0 ,
	\label{eq:CN_heat_flux_closure}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
with $\theta>0$.  Then
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_\xi\frac{\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta}
	=
	\sum_\xi
	\frac{\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta\cdot
		\mbf K_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta}
	\ge 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_heat_flux_positive}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Equations \eqref{eq:CN_interphase_exchange_positive} and
\eqref{eq:CN_heat_flux_positive}, together with plastic flow rules that make the
plastic part of \eqref{eq:CN_residual_dissipation} nonnegative, are sufficient
to make \eqref{eq:CN_residual_dissipation} thermodynamically admissible.
