\subsection{Coleman--Noll procedure for the compositional thermo-reactive mixture}
\label{sec:coleman_noll_compositional}

This subsection records the thermodynamic argument in a form that mirrors the
kinematic split just introduced.  The point is not to postulate the solid mixture
rules, but to show how they appear after the component Helmholtz energies are
differentiated subject to the density, composition, and dilation constraints.

The Coleman--Noll calculation is applied on the reduced constitutive state space.
It is not a second derivation of the constrained equations of motion.  Constraints
that define the admissible motions and fields, such as saturation, storage, and
stoichiometric source constraints, are enforced in the variational formulation
and do not introduce additional Coleman--Noll coefficients unless the constrained
variable is an argument of a constitutive free energy.  Following the standard
Liu multiplier exploitation of constrained entropy inequalities~\cite{liu1972method},
we may add identically vanishing constraint-rate terms before collecting
coefficients.  Composition enters here because the free energies depend explicitly
on $\eta_\xi^\alpha$; its rate is therefore restricted to the tangent space of
the mass-fraction constraint,
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha=1,
	\qquad
	\sum_\alpha\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha=0 .
	\label{eq:CN_mass_fraction_tangent_constraint}
\end{equation}

For each phase/component pair let
\begin{equation}
	e_\xi^\alpha=\psi_\xi^\alpha+\theta s_\xi^\alpha
	\label{eq:CN_component_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
be the Legendre relation.  We write each phase free energy as a mass-specific
phase potential
\begin{equation}
	\psi_\xi=\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha\psi_\xi^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\rho_\xi\psi_\xi=\sum_\alpha\rho_\xi^\alpha\psi_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_phase_free_energy_sum}
\end{equation}
For the solid phase we also introduce the component volume fraction within the
solid phase, denoted $\phi_s^\alpha$.  It is not an independent thermodynamic
state variable in the Coleman--Noll derivatives; it is a derived volume-weight
used later to express skeleton mixture rules:
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s^\alpha
	=\phi_s\bar\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha
	=\phi_s\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha=1,
	\qquad
	\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha=1 .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_component_volume_fraction_identity}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\bar\rho_s
		 & =\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha, \\
		\eta_s^\alpha
		 & =\frac{\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s}, \\
		\phi_s^\alpha
		 & =\frac{\eta_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:CN_solid_intrinsic_density_identities}
\end{equation}
For the solid, the primitive component Helmholtz free energies use the physical
intrinsic density of each solid component, rather than an explicit dependence on
the mixture intrinsic density $\bar\rho_s$.  Thus the mixture density enters the
Coleman--Noll calculation through the component-density parametrization
$\bar\rho_s^\alpha=\bar\rho_s^\alpha(\bar\rho_s,\eta_s^\beta)$:
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{S} = \{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M, a_s, \bar\rho_s^\beta,
	\theta,\eta_s^\beta \}
	\label{eq:CN_solid_state_set}
\end{equation}
%


\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
	\psi_s^\alpha
	 & =\psi_s^\alpha(\bar{\mbf F}_s^M, a_s, \bar\rho_s^\alpha,
	\theta,\eta_s^\beta),
	\qquad
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^M=\bar{\mbf F}_s(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:CN_solid_component_free_energy_state}
\end{equation}
where
$\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta=\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta(\theta,\phi_s^\beta)$ is a
prescribed stress-free thermal/compositional dilation, not an independent
thermodynamic state.  Since the balance laws still use the mixture variables
$(\bar\rho_s,\eta_s^\beta)$, density derivatives of the solid energy are
chain-ruled through $\bar\rho_s^\alpha(\bar\rho_s,\eta_s^\beta)$.  In the solid
specialization used below, a mixture-density change at fixed composition scales
the component intrinsic densities at fixed component volume fraction:
\begin{equation}
	\dot{\bar\rho}_s^\alpha
	=\frac{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s}\dot{\bar\rho}_s
	+\sum_\beta
	\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
	\dot\eta_s^\beta .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_density_rate_chain_rule}
\end{equation}
Derivatives of $\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta$ with respect to composition are also total
derivatives through the derived component volume fractions.  Define the state
sets
\begin{equation}
	\mc X\in\{\mc S,\mc F,\mc T_s\},
	\qquad
	\mc T_s=\{\theta,\phi_s^\beta\}.
	\label{eq:CN_state_set_collection}
\end{equation}
For the mobile fluid phases, we postulate the Coleman--Noll state variables
\begin{equation}
	\mc F
	=\left\{\bar\rho_\ell,\eta_\ell^\beta,\theta\right\},
	\qquad
	\psi_\ell^\alpha=\psi_\ell^\alpha(\mc F) .
	\label{eq:CN_fluid_state_variables}
\end{equation}
When a derivative is written for a generic phase, let
\begin{equation}
	\mc X_\xi =
	\begin{cases}
		\mc S, & \xi=s,    \\
		\mc F, & \xi=\ell,
	\end{cases}
	\label{eq:CN_generic_state_set}
\end{equation}
We use the convention that
\begin{equation}
	\left.\p{\Gamma_\xi}{z}\heldfixed{\mc X}
	\label{eq:CN_held_fixed_notation}
\end{equation}
denotes differentiation with respect to $z$ while holding fixed all other
members of the state set $\mc X$.
We write
\begin{equation}
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	=\sum_\gamma
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\phi_s^\gamma}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}
	\left.\p{\phi_s^\gamma}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S} .
	\label{eq:CN_thermal_dilation_composition_chain_rule}
\end{equation}
Thus the fluid-phase free-energy rates entering the entropy inequality are
\begin{align}
	\sum_\ell\sum_\alpha\rho_\ell^\alpha\dot\psi_\ell^\alpha
	 & =\sum_\ell\rho_\ell\sum_\alpha\eta_\ell^\alpha\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_\ell^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\ell}\heldfixed{\mc F}\dot{\bar\rho}_\ell
	+\sum_\beta\left.\p{\psi_\ell^\alpha}{\eta_\ell^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc F}\dot\eta_\ell^\beta
	+\left.\p{\psi_\ell^\alpha}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc F}\dot\theta
	\right).
	\label{eq:CN_fluid_free_energy_rate}
\end{align}
The dependent quantities
\begin{equation}
	\rho_\xi^\alpha=\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha=1,
	\qquad
	\bar J_s=\det\bar{\mbf F}_s=\frac{J_s}{a_s}
	\label{eq:CN_kinematic_dependencies}
\end{equation}

For the solid, component additivity gives
\begin{align}
	\sum_\alpha\rho_s^\alpha\dot{\psi}_s^\alpha
	 & =\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha\dot\psi_s^\alpha \notag \\
	 & =\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha \bigg(
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf{F}}^M_s}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	:\dot{\bar{\mbf{F}}}^M_s
	+\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{a_s}\heldfixed{\mc S}\dot{a}_s
	\notag                                                     \\
	 & \qquad
	+\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\left[
		\frac{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s}\dot{\bar\rho}_s
		+\sum_\beta
		\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
		\dot\eta_s^\beta
		\right]
	+\sum_\beta\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}\dot\eta_s^\beta
	\notag                                                     \\
	 & \qquad
	+\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc S}\dot\theta
	\bigg).
	\label{eq:CN_total_helmholtz_density}
\end{align}
The Coleman--Noll argument treats $\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s$ as the independent
solid deformation rate.  The mechanical deformation gradient is a dependent
constitutive argument, so substituting the prescribed thermal/compositional
stress-free dilation rate gives
\begin{align}
	\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^M
	= & \; \dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}
	-\bar{\mbf F}_s^M
	\left.\frac{\partial\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\partial\theta}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}\dot\theta \notag           \\
	  & \;-
	\sum_\beta
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^M
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-1}\dot\eta_s^\beta .
	\label{eq:CN_FM_rate_chain_rule}
\end{align}
It is useful to define the tensor conjugate to the stress-free dilation,
\begin{equation}
	\mbf H_s^\alpha
	= (\bar{\mbf F}_s^M)^T
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-T} .
	\label{eq:CN_H_def}
\end{equation}
The mobile-phase Helmholtz energies are phase EOS potentials using the
fluid-state variables in \eqref{eq:CN_fluid_state_variables}.  We assign no
elastic stress power to the mobile phases, so
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\ell'=\mbf 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_mobile_stress_free}
\end{equation}
The reversible interfacial energy used in the variational formulation must also
be included in the entropy bookkeeping.  For the mobile phases,
%
\begin{equation}
	\psi_\gamma^{\rm vol}
	=\phi\gamma(S_a,S_g),
	\qquad
	\dot\psi_\gamma^{\rm vol}
	=\sum_l\gamma_l\dot\phi_l,
	\label{eq:CN_interfacial_helmholtz_rate}
\end{equation}
%
where $\gamma_l=\partial(\phi\gamma)/\partial\phi_l$ are the primitive
interfacial potentials introduced in \eqref{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives}.
The volume fractions are constrained by $\sum_\xi\phi_\xi=1$, so their rates
are not independent.  When the capillary Helmholtz rate is retained explicitly,
we add the identically vanishing saturation-constraint rate
$\lambda\sum_\xi\dot\phi_\xi$ and retain the corresponding
$\dot\phi_\xi/\phi_\xi$ contribution from the component-storage multiplier.
This capillary Helmholtz energy is taken to be reversible and independent of
temperature in the present model.  If $\gamma$ were temperature dependent, the
corresponding interfacial entropy term would have to be retained as well.
\begin{align}
	\sum_\alpha\left(\rho_\xi^\alpha\dot e_\xi^\alpha
	+\dot c_\xi^\alpha e_\xi^\alpha\right)
	= & \; \bs\sigma_\xi':\mbf L_\xi
	+\phi_\xi\bar p_\xi\frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}
	+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}\dot\phi_\xi
	+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha
	-\sum_\alpha L_\xi^\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf q_\xi
	+\rho_\xi r_\xi+\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}.
	\label{eq:CN_energy_balance}
\end{align}
The storage-multiplier terms are the volume-fraction and compositional analogs
of the pressure power.  The multipliers $\tau_\xi^\alpha$ enforce component
material storage in \eqref{eq:C2_def}; when the admissible rates
$\dot\phi_\xi$ and $\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha$ are retained, they do work against the
normalized storage multipliers $\tau_\xi^\alpha/\phi_\xi$ and
$\tau_\xi^\alpha/\eta_\xi^\alpha$.  The coefficient $L_\xi^\alpha$ is the same
component conversion-work potential introduced in \eqref{eq:W_def}; the sign
convention here is chosen so that a source $\dot c_\xi^\alpha$ carries the
free-energy cost $\psi_\xi^\alpha+L_\xi^\alpha$ in the entropy inequality.
For the solid phase, the mechanical power in \eqref{eq:CN_energy_balance} must
be decomposed with the same kinematic split used in the constitutive law.  In the
isotropic dilation specialization,
\begin{equation}
	\mbf F_s=a_s^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}_s,
	\label{eq:CN_F_split_for_power}
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
	\mbf L_s
	=\dot{\mbf F}_s\mbf F_s^{-1}
	=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\dot a_s}{a_s}\mbf I
	+\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s\bar{\mbf F}_s^{-1} .
	\label{eq:CN_L_split_for_power}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s':\mbf L_s
	=\left[\bs\sigma_s'\bar{\mbf F}_s^{-T}\right]
	:\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s
	+\frac{1}{3a_s}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s')\dot a_s .
	\label{eq:CN_stress_power_split}
\end{equation}
The entropy inequality is
\begin{equation}
	0\le
	\sum_\xi\left[\sum_\alpha\left(
		\rho_\xi^\alpha\dot s_\xi^\alpha
		+\dot c_\xi^\alpha s_\xi^\alpha\right)
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\left(\frac{\mbf q_\xi}{\theta}\right)
		-\frac{\rho_\xi r_\xi}{\theta}\right].
	\label{eq:CN_entropy_inequality}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:CN_energy_balance} to eliminate $\rho_\xi r_\xi$ from
\eqref{eq:CN_entropy_inequality}, and then using
$e_\xi^\alpha=\psi_\xi^\alpha+\theta s_\xi^\alpha$, gives the reduced entropy
inequality.  At this stage we also add the identically vanishing rate of the
composition-normalization constraint,
$\pi_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha$, and the identically vanishing
saturation-constraint rate $\lambda\sum_\xi\dot\phi_\xi$, so that the
Coleman--Noll coefficient collection is performed on the constrained tangent
spaces.
\begin{align}
	0\le
	\sum_\xi \bigg[ &
		\frac{1}{\theta}\Big(
		\bs\sigma_\xi':\mbf L_\xi
		+\phi_\xi\bar p_\xi\frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}
		+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}\dot\phi_\xi
		\notag                                                \\
		& \qquad
		+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+\pi_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha
		-\sum_\alpha\rho_\xi^\alpha
		\left(\dot\psi_\xi^\alpha+s_\xi^\alpha\dot\theta\right)
		\notag                                                \\
		& \qquad
		-\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha
		\left(\psi_\xi^\alpha+L_\xi^\alpha\right)
		+\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}\Big)
		-\frac{\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta^2}
		\bigg]
		\notag                                                \\
		& +\frac{1}{\theta}\left(
		\lambda\sum_\xi\dot\phi_\xi
		-\sum_l\gamma_l\dot\phi_l\right).
		\label{eq:CN_reduced_entropy_inequality}
\end{align}
Substituting \eqref{eq:CN_total_helmholtz_density},
\eqref{eq:CN_FM_rate_chain_rule}, \eqref{eq:CN_stress_power_split}, and
\eqref{eq:CN_fluid_free_energy_rate} gives the collected solid--fluid
contribution.
\begin{align}
	0\le\; &
	\frac{1}{\theta}\Bigg\{
	\left[
	\bs\sigma_s'\bar{\mbf F}_s^{-T}
	-\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta)^{-T}
	\right]:\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s
	\notag                                               \\
	       & +\left[
			\frac{1}{3a_s}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s')
			-\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
			\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{a_s}\heldfixed{\mc S}
			\right]\dot a_s
		\notag                                               \\
	       & +\left[
			\frac{\phi_s \bar p_s}{\bar\rho_s}
			-\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
			\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
			\frac{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s}
			\right]\dot{\bar\rho}_s
		+\sum_l\left[
			\frac{\phi_l \bar p_l}{\bar\rho_l}
			-\rho_l\sum_\alpha\eta_l^\alpha
			\left.\p{\psi_l^\alpha}{\bar\rho_l}\heldfixed{\mc F}
			\right]\dot{\bar\rho}_l
		\notag                                               \\
	       & +\left[
			-\sum_\xi\rho_\xi\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha
			\left(
			s_\xi^\alpha+\left.\p{\psi_\xi^\alpha}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc X_\xi}
			\right)
			+\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
			\mbf H_s^\alpha:
			\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc T_s}
			\right]\dot\theta
	\notag                                               \\
	       & -\sum_l\rho_l\sum_\beta
		\left.\p{\psi_l}{\eta_l^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc F}
		\dot\eta_l^\beta
		\notag                                               \\
	       & +\rho_s\sum_\beta\left[
			-\left.\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
			-\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
			\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
			\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
			\right.
		\notag                                               \\
	       & \qquad\left.
			+\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha\mbf H_s^\alpha:
			\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
			\right]\dot\eta_s^\beta
			\notag                                               \\
	       & +\sum_\xi\sum_\beta\left(\frac{\tau_\xi^\beta}{\eta_\xi^\beta}
			+\pi_\xi\right)\dot\eta_\xi^\beta
		\notag                                               \\
	       & -\sum_m\left(
			\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi m}^\alpha
			\left(\psi_\xi^\alpha+L_\xi^\alpha\right)
			\right)\dot r_m
		\notag                                               \\
	       & +\sum_\xi\left(
			\lambda+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}
			\right)\dot\phi_\xi
		-\sum_l\gamma_l\dot\phi_l
		\notag                                               \\
	       &
		+\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		-\sum_\xi\frac{\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta}
		\Bigg\} .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_collected_entropy}
\end{align}
The capillary term is not a new dissipative mechanism.  Since the
volume-fraction rates are constrained, the mobile-phase coefficient is the
reversible condition
\begin{align}
	0
	& = \lambda+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_l^\alpha}{\phi_l}-\gamma_l
	\notag \\
	& = \lambda+\bar p_l-\gamma_l .
	\label{eq:CN_capillary_reversible_condition}
\end{align}
Thus the interfacial energy supplies the capillary pressure construction and
equivalent pressure relation, while leaving no residual entropy production for
equilibrium capillarity.  A dynamic or hysteretic capillary law would instead
leave the uncancelled part of the interfacial Helmholtz rate as a capillary
dissipation term.
The phase derivative with respect to composition is the derivative of the phase
Helmholtz potential in \eqref{eq:CN_phase_free_energy_sum}.  For mobile phases no
elastic stress-free dilation term appears, so the composition coefficient gives
the same stationarity condition obtained from Hamilton's principle in
\eqref{eq:el_eta_all}:
\begin{equation}
	0=
	\frac{\tau_\ell^\beta}{\eta_\ell^\beta}
	+\pi_\ell
	-\rho_\ell
	\left.\p{\psi_\ell}{\eta_\ell^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc F},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N .
	\label{eq:CN_composition_multiplier_restriction}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
	\rho_\ell
	\left.\p{\psi_\ell}{\eta_\ell^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc F}
	-\frac{\tau_\ell^\beta}{\eta_\ell^\beta}
	=\pi_\ell .
	\label{eq:CN_composition_componentwise}
\end{equation}
For the solid phase, the same coefficient collection contains both the
component-density chain rule and the explicit stress-free compositional dilation
correction from \eqref{eq:CN_FM_rate_chain_rule}:
\begin{equation}
	0=
	\frac{\tau_s^\beta}{\eta_s^\beta}
	+\pi_s
	-\rho_s
	\left[
		\left.\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
		+\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
		\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
		\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
		\right]
	+\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha\mbf H_s^\alpha:
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_composition_multiplier_restriction}
\end{equation}
Eliminating $\pi_\ell$ gives the mobile-phase projected restriction on the
tangent space,
\begin{equation}
	\rho_\ell\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_\ell}{\eta_\ell^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc F}
	-\left.\p{\psi_\ell}{\eta_\ell^N}\heldfixed{\mc F}
	\right)
	=
	\frac{\tau_\ell^\beta}{\eta_\ell^\beta}
	-\frac{\tau_\ell^N}{\eta_\ell^N},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N-1 .
	\label{eq:CN_composition_projected_restriction}
\end{equation}
For the solid, eliminating $\pi_s$ gives the corresponding projected restriction
with the differences of the density-chain and dilation corrections retained:
\begin{align}
	 & \rho_s\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	-\left.\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^N}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\right)
	+\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\left(
	\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\beta}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
	-\left.\p{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^N}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_s}
	\right)
	\notag                                                     \\
	 & \qquad
	-\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha\mbf H_s^\alpha:
	\left(
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^\beta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	-\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\eta_s^N}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\right)
	\notag                                                     \\
	 & \qquad =
	\frac{\tau_s^\beta}{\eta_s^\beta}
	-\frac{\tau_s^N}{\eta_s^N},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N-1 .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_composition_projected_restriction}
\end{align}
The deformation coefficient gives
\begin{align}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	 & =\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^M)^T
	=\phi_s\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^M)^T .
	\label{eq:CN_stress_definition}
\end{align}
The component partial Cauchy stress uses the current component partial density.
The intrinsic component stress is the corresponding normalized stress within the
solid phase:
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\alpha}
	=\rho_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^M)^T
	=\phi_s\phi_s^\alpha\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\alpha},
	\qquad
	\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\alpha}
	=\bar\rho_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^M)^T .
	\label{eq:CN_intrinsic_component_stress}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=\sum_\alpha\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\alpha}
	=\phi_s\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\alpha}.
	\label{eq:CN_solid_partial_stress_sum}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if $\rho_{s0}^\alpha\psi_s^\alpha$ is the energy per component
reference mixture volume, then
\begin{equation}
	\mbf P_s^{\prime\alpha}
	=\rho_{s0}^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^M}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	=J_s \bs\sigma_s^{\prime\alpha}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^M)^{-T}
	\label{eq:CN_intrinsic_pk_stress}
\end{equation}
is the component first Piola--Kirchhoff stress.  The distension
coefficient gives
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s')
	= \rho_s a_s \sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{a_s}\heldfixed{\mc S},
	\label{eq:CN_distension_force_definition}
\end{equation}
The solid pressure relation follows from the same coefficient collection.  The
component mass identity gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s^\alpha
	=\phi_s\bar\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha
	=\phi_s\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\eta_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s
	=\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_component_mass_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Since $\psi_s^\alpha$ depends on the component intrinsic density rather than
directly on $\bar\rho_s$, the part of the solid Helmholtz rate proportional to
$\dot{\bar\rho}_s$ is
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\frac{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s}\dot{\bar\rho}_s .
	\label{eq:CN_solid_density_rate_pressure_coefficient}
\end{equation}
%
The pressure-power term contributes
$(\phi_s\bar p_s/\bar\rho_s)\dot{\bar\rho}_s$.  Therefore the coefficient of the
independent rate $\dot{\bar\rho}_s$ is
%
\begin{equation}
	0=
	\frac{\phi_s\bar p_s}{\bar\rho_s}
	-\rho_s\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	\frac{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s}.
	\label{eq:CN_solid_density_coefficient_zero}
\end{equation}
%
Using $\rho_s=\phi_s\bar\rho_s$ and
$\eta_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s=\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha$ gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_s
	=\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha
	\left(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\right)^2
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S}.
	\label{eq:CN_solid_pressure_component_sum}
\end{equation}
%
Thus, defining each component intrinsic pressure by
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_s^\alpha
	=\left(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\right)^2
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S},
	\label{eq:CN_component_intrinsic_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
%
the pressure coefficients give
\begin{align}
	\bar p_s
	 & =\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\bar p_s^\alpha
	=\left(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\right)^2
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\heldfixed{\mc S},
	\notag                                                   \\
	\bar p_l
	 & =\left(\bar\rho_l\right)^2\sum_\alpha\eta_l^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_l^\alpha}{\bar\rho_l}\heldfixed{\mc F} .
	\label{eq:CN_phase_pressure_definition}
\end{align}
The entropy coefficient gives
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha s_s^\alpha
	=-\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc S}
	+\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha
	\mbf H_s^\alpha:
	\left.\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^\theta}{\theta}\heldfixed{\mc T_s} .
	\label{eq:CN_entropy_definitions}
\end{equation}
The solid composition restrictions above are the place where component-density
parametrization and stress-free compositional dilation modify the usual
composition coefficient.

Finally, using $\dot c_\xi^\alpha=\sum_m\nu_{\xi m}^\alpha\dot r_m$, the
source term in \eqref{eq:CN_solid_collected_entropy} becomes
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \dot c_\xi^\alpha
	\left(\psi_\xi^\alpha+L_\xi^\alpha\right)
	=
	-\sum_m\left(
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi m}^\alpha
	\left(\psi_\xi^\alpha+L_\xi^\alpha\right)
	\right)\dot r_m .
	\label{eq:CN_helmholtz_source_projection}
\end{equation}
This term is the open-system source contribution to the entropy inequality.  To
make the entropy production use the same component conversion potential that
appears in the variational stoichiometric constraint, we choose
\begin{equation}
	L_\xi^\alpha=\mu_\xi^\alpha-\psi_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_L_mu_psi_closure}
\end{equation}
Then $\psi_\xi^\alpha+L_\xi^\alpha=\mu_\xi^\alpha$, and the source projection is
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \dot c_\xi^\alpha\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	-\sum_m\left(
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi m}^\alpha\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\right)\dot r_m .
	\label{eq:CN_mu_source_projection}
\end{equation}
The mechanism affinity identified by the variational conversion constraint is
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_m
	= -\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi m}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:CN_composition_affinity_projection}
\end{equation}
Thus the source projection \eqref{eq:CN_mu_source_projection} is exactly
the mechanism force power.
After the reversible coefficients have been set to zero, the remaining
inequality can therefore be written as
\begin{align}
	0\le\; &
	\sum_m\mc A_m\dot r_m
	+\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		-\sum_\xi\frac{\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta} .
	\label{eq:CN_residual_dissipation}
\end{align}
Admissible reaction kinetics must make $\sum_m\mc A_m\dot r_m\ge 0$, for example
through a closure $\dot r_m=\mc K_m(\mc A_m,\text{state})$ with
$\mc A_m\dot r_m\ge 0$ for each independent mechanism.

The interphase exchange term can be closed by the same quadratic argument used
for the three-phase reacting mixture.  Following Bedford and
Drumheller~\cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical}, conservation of energy for the
entire mixture requires the internal interaction-energy supplies and the
interphase momentum supplies to satisfy
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\left(
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	+\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)=0 .
	\label{eq:CN_interaction_energy_conservation}
\end{equation}
Here $\mbf b_\xi$ is the net interphase momentum supply appearing in the phase
momentum balance.  Decompose it into pairwise interaction forces,
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_\xi
	=\sum_{\zeta\ne\xi}\mbf b_{\zeta\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}=-\mbf b_{\xi\zeta},
	\label{eq:CN_pairwise_interaction_force}
\end{equation}
where $\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}$ is the force exerted on phase $\xi$ by phase $\zeta$.
For each unordered pair, let $\mbf u_{\xi\zeta}$ denote the pairwise relative
velocity,
\begin{equation}
	\mbf u_{\xi\zeta}=\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_\zeta .
	\label{eq:CN_relative_pair_velocity}
\end{equation}
This velocity difference is distinct from mass-weighted Darcy or diffusion
fluxes, which can be introduced later by multiplying a relative velocity by the
appropriate partial density or mobility.  The energy pairing remains simplest in
terms of $\mbf u_{\xi\zeta}$.
Then \eqref{eq:CN_interaction_energy_conservation} gives
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	=-\sum_{\xi<\zeta}\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}\cdot\mbf u_{\xi\zeta}.
	\label{eq:CN_interaction_power_pairing}
\end{equation}
A linear dissipative interaction law may be written as
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}
	=-\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}^{-1}\mbf u_{\xi\zeta},
	\qquad
	\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}=\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}^{T},
	\qquad
	\mbf y\cdot\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}\mbf y>0
	\quad \forall\,\mbf y\ne\mbf 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_interphase_exchange_law}
\end{equation}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:CN_interaction_power_pairing} gives
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	=\sum_{\xi<\zeta}
	\mbf u_{\xi\zeta}\cdot
	\mbf k_{\xi\zeta}^{-1}\mbf u_{\xi\zeta}
	\ge 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_interphase_exchange_positive}
\end{equation}
Thus interphase momentum exchange contributes nonnegative entropy production
whenever the mobility tensors are positive definite.

The heat-conduction term is made admissible by a Fourier-type closure
\begin{equation}
	\mbf q_\xi=-\mbf K_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta,
	\qquad
	\mbf K_\xi=\mbf K_\xi^T,
	\qquad
	\mbf y\cdot\mbf K_\xi\mbf y\ge 0 ,
	\label{eq:CN_heat_flux_closure}
\end{equation}
with $\theta>0$.  Then
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_\xi\frac{\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta}
	=
	\sum_\xi
	\frac{\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta\cdot
		\mbf K_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta}{\theta}
	\ge 0 .
	\label{eq:CN_heat_flux_positive}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:CN_interphase_exchange_positive} and
\eqref{eq:CN_heat_flux_positive} are sufficient to make
\eqref{eq:CN_residual_dissipation} thermodynamically admissible.
