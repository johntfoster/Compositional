\section{Constitutive models and the entropy inequality}
\label{sec:multicomponent_solid_skeletons}
\label{sec:multicomponent_solid_mixture_rules}
\label{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}

Section~\ref{sec:virtual_power_derivation} supplies the reversible balances.
This section completes the porous-medium constitutive theory for stress,
pressure, reaction, transport, and plastic response.  It first defines the
common solid motion, the fluid and solid thermal subsystems, the constitutive
state variables, and the stored energies.
The entropy inequality then gives the reversible stress and pressure relations
and restricts the dissipative laws.  The electroquasistatic
approximation has already been imposed; electromagnetic-wave dynamics are
outside the model.

Cauchy stresses are tension-positive and enter momentum balance through
\(+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma\).  Thermodynamic phase pressures, capillary
pressures, and the equivalent pore pressure are positive in compression and
therefore enter total stress with a negative spherical contribution.  A single
prime denotes material stress at fixed intrinsic density, a double prime the
fixed-pressure Biot stress, and a superscript \(+\) the electrical stress
contribution.  These conventions will be used when the Coleman--Noll
coefficients are identified below.

Every phase may contain multiple components.  In particular, each
\(s\in\mc S\) is a load-bearing multicomponent solid with mass fractions
\(\eta_s^\alpha\), while all solid phases share the skeleton trajectory
\begin{equation}
	\mbf x=\bs\chi(\mbf X,t),
	\qquad
	\mbf F=\nabla_{\mbf X}\bs\chi,
	\qquad
	J=\det\mbf F .
	\label{eq:common_solid_skeleton_motion}
\end{equation}
Adsorbed species, host minerals, hydrate cages, bound water, and hydrate guest
molecules therefore move with the same skeleton.  Their distinct mechanical
effects enter through the solid composition dependence of the free energy,
stress-free swelling, thermal expansion, elastic moduli, and plastic flow.

The thermal specialization distinguishes an equilibrated mobile-fluid
subsystem from the solid skeleton,
%
\begin{equation}
	\theta_\xi
	=
	\begin{cases}
		\theta_{\mc F}, & \xi=f\in\mc F,  \\
		\theta_{\mc S}, & \xi=s\in\mc S .
	\end{cases}
	\label{eq:MC_two_temperature_specialization}
\end{equation}
%
Thus all mobile phases share \(\theta_{\mc F}\), and all load-bearing solid
phases share \(\theta_{\mc S}\).  Heat exchange between these subsystems is
retained, so either temperature may be larger.  Multiphase geothermal models
use the same fluid/rock temperature grouping \cite{faraz2021boiling}, whereas
constituent mixture theory also permits a separate temperature for every phase
\cite{drumheller2000,hanyga2004thermal}.  The local-thermal-equilibrium limit is
\(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}\).

All constitutive rates are compared in the frame moving with the solid
skeleton.  For a spatial state field \(\Gamma\), define the skeleton velocity
and the ordinary-dot rate by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf v_{\mc S}
	=
	\left.
	\frac{\partial\bs\chi}{\partial t}
	\right\vert_{\mbf X},
	\qquad
	\dot\Gamma
	\equiv
	\frac{D_{\mc S}\Gamma}{Dt}
	=
	\frac{\partial\Gamma}{\partial t}
	+
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\Gamma .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_rate_convention}
\end{equation}
%
This convention applies to
\(\theta_{\mc F}\), \(\theta_{\mc S}\), every \(\phi_\xi\),
\(\bar\rho_\xi\), \(\eta_\xi^\alpha\), and the phase thermodynamic
potentials, and to the solid kinematic fields and internal variables
\(\mbf A_s\) and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s\).  The heavy dot instead marks a rate
following phase \(\xi\), including the component-insertion
rates \(\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha\) and
\(\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha\).  Because all solid phases share the skeleton
trajectory, \(D_s/Dt=D_{\mc S}/Dt\), whereas mobile phases retain their phase
velocities.  For any phase-attributed spatial field
\(\Gamma_\xi\), the phase-following and skeleton-following rates are related
exactly by
%
\begin{equation}
	\phasedot{\Gamma}_\xi
	=
	\dot\Gamma_\xi
	+
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\Gamma_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_skeleton_transport_identity}
\end{equation}
%
The difference \(\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}\) therefore measures bulk phase
motion relative to the skeleton; it is not a molecular-diffusion velocity.
This solid-observer convention is used in thermodynamic formulations of
multicomponent multiphase poroelastic flow
\cite[eq.~(2)]{seguin2019multicomponent} and is consistent with the
solid-observed saturation rate used by Hassanizadeh and Gray
\cite[eq.~(35)]{hassanizadeh1993thermodynamic}.

Summing the component balance
\eqref{eq:averaged_component_spatial_mass} over \(\alpha\), using the
zero-sum relative component flux
\eqref{eq:averaged_relative_flux_sum}, and changing from the Eulerian observer
to the skeleton observer gives the phase mass balance
%
\begin{equation}
	\dot\rho_\xi
	+
	\rho_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left[
		\rho_\xi
		\left(
		\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)
	\right]
	=
	\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_phase_mass_balance}
\end{equation}
%
Consequently, any phase-specific quantity \(\Gamma_\xi\) satisfies the exact
conservative transport identity
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
	\left(
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
	\right)
	+
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	={}&
	\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
	\left(
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
	\right)
	+
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left[
		\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
		\left(
		\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)
	\right]
	\notag \\
	={}&
	\rho_\xi\dot\Gamma_\xi
	+
	\Gamma_\xi
	\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	+
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\Gamma_\xi
	\notag \\
	={}&
	\rho_\xi\phasedot{\Gamma}_\xi
	+
	\Gamma_\xi
	\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity}
\end{align}
%
The divergence in
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity} is transport through a
control surface moving with the skeleton.  It vanishes for a solid phase, but
not for a mobile phase merely because its storage rate is written with an
ordinary dot.

In the same observer frame, the rate of the pointwise volume-fraction
constraint is
%
\begin{equation}
	\dot\phi_\xi
	\equiv
	\frac{D_{\mc S}\phi_\xi}{Dt}
	=
	\frac{\partial\phi_\xi}{\partial t}
	+
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi,
	\qquad
	\sum_\xi\dot\phi_\xi=0 .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_volume_fraction_rates}
\end{equation}
%
Individual volume fractions may change, but their rates must sum to zero.  The
mechanism rate \(\dot r_{(m)}\) is a local process rate shared by every phase
participating in mechanism \((m)\).  The electric potential is one Eulerian
field for the whole mixture.  By contrast, \(D_\xi\tau/Dt\) is phase-following
because the same transfer multiplier \(\tau\) is evaluated along each phase
trajectory in the component-insertion relation.

\subsection{Solid kinematics and state variables}
\label{sec:MC_solid_kinematics}

Extending Drumheller's distension/true-deformation split for reacting mixtures
\cite{drumheller2000} and its porous-media form in Foster et
al.~\cite{foster2026reactingporous}, the solid kinematic split is
phase-specific but uses the common skeleton deformation,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf F=\mbf A_s\bar{\mbf F}_s,
	\qquad
	J=a_s\bar J_s,
	\qquad
	a_s=\det\mbf A_s,
	\qquad
	\bar J_s=\det\bar{\mbf F}_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_decomposition}
\end{equation}
%
The distension tensor \(\mbf A_s\) maps the true deformation of solid phase
\(s\) into the common skeleton deformation.  It represents the pore-space
allocation and packing needed to accommodate swelling and reaction-induced
volume change between phases.  Extending
Drumheller's elastic--plastic treatment of true deformation and distension
\cite{drumheller2000}, the elastic, plastic, and stress-free factors are
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_kinematic_splits}
	\begin{align}
		\mbf A_s
		 & =
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p\mbf A_s^0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_split} \\
		\bar{\mbf F}_s
		 & =
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_true_split}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Here \(\mbf A_s^e\) and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\) are elastic factors,
\(\mbf A_s^p\) and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p\) are plastic internal variables, and
\(\mbf A_s^0\) and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0\) are reversible stress-free maps that can
include temperature and solid-composition effects,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf A_s^0
	=
	\mbf A_s^0(\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\}),
	\qquad
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^0
	=
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^0(\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\}),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_stress_free_maps}
\end{equation}
%
The stress-free maps represent adsorption swelling, desorption shrinkage,
hydrate-cage volume changes, and composition-dependent thermal expansion.
A shared \(\theta_{\mc S}\) remains compatible with phase-specific thermal
expansion: each phase retains its own functions \(\mbf A_s^0\) and
\(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0\), and therefore its own derivative with respect to
\(\theta_{\mc S}\).
Thermo-poroelastic models make the same physical distinction by assigning pore
volume, fluid, and grain thermal expansions separately
\cite{palciauskas1982thermally,ghabezloo2009stress,ghabezloo2011micromechanics}.
As in standard finite-strain multiplicative plasticity
\cite{simo1988framework,simo1998} and Drumheller's mixture treatment
\cite{drumheller2000}, the true-plastic flow is assumed isochoric,
%
\begin{equation}
	\det\bar{\mbf F}_s^p=1,
	\qquad
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]=0,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_isochoric_plastic_flow}
\end{equation}
%
The dependent elastic arguments are
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf A_s^e
	=
	\mbf A_s
	(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
	(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1},
	\qquad
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	=
	\bar{\mbf F}_s
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_elastic_arguments}
\end{equation}
%

The solid component partial densities and mass fractions are
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s^\alpha
	=
	\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha
	=
	\phi_s\bar\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\sum_\alpha\eta_s^\alpha=1,
	\qquad
	\sum_\alpha\dot\eta_s^\alpha=0,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_component_partial_density}
\end{equation}
%
When component intrinsic densities \(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\) and component solid
volume fractions \(\phi_s^\alpha\) are useful, they satisfy
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_density_mixture_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\bar\rho_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_intrinsic_density_rule} \\
		\eta_s^\alpha
		 & =
		\frac{\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_mass_fraction_rule}
	\end{align}
	\begin{equation}
		\phi_s^\alpha
		=
		\frac{\eta_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha},
		\qquad
		\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha=1,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_component_volume_fraction_rule}
	\end{equation}
\end{subequations}
%

The component material balances use the component material increment,
%
\begin{equation}
	J\phi_s\bar\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha
	=
	\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}\eta_{s0}^\alpha
	+\mc C_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_component_material_mass}
\end{equation}
%
Summing over components gives
%
\begin{equation}
	J\phi_s\bar\rho_s
	=
	\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}+\mc C_s,
	\qquad
	\mc C_s=\sum_\alpha\mc C_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_phase_material_mass}
\end{equation}
%
With true local conservation in the solid material,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar J_s\bar\rho_s=\bar\rho_{s0},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_solid_true_mass_conservation}
\end{equation}
%
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_phase_material_mass} becomes
%
\begin{equation}
	a_s\phi_s\bar\rho_{s0}
	=
	\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}+\mc C_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_mass_relation}
\end{equation}
%
The rate form follows from \(J=a_s\bar J_s\),
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\frac{\dot{a}_s}{a_s}
	=
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+\frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_s}{\bar\rho_s},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_evolution}
\end{empheq}
%
The nonelectrical state sets used in the constitutive derivatives are
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc X_s
	=
	\{
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
	\bar\rho_s,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S}
	\},
	\qquad
	\mc X_f
	=
	\{
	\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F}
	\},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_state_sets}
\end{equation}
%
The interfacial state set is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc X_\gamma
	=
	\left\{
	\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}
	\right\},
	\qquad
	\mbf h\in\mathbb R^{N_h}.
	\label{eq:MC_interfacial_state_set}
\end{equation}
%
The phase energies use \(\mc X_f\) and \(\mc X_s\), while \(\mc X_\gamma\)
controls the shared pore-scale interfacial storage.  The capillary-history
variables are therefore internal variables of the fluid subsystem rather than
additional composition coordinates of an individual fluid phase.  The
saturation rates \(\dot S_f\) are process rates and are not members of
\(\mc X_\gamma\).  In the dynamic-capillary branch, the resistance \(\mc T\)
may depend on the current constitutive and pore-structure state, but it is held
fixed while the instantaneous saturation rates are varied by Onsager
stationarity.

The intrinsic density rate needed in the Coleman--Noll analysis follows from
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_true_split}, isochoric plastic flow, and the
composition-dependent stress-free true deformation.  In the derivatives below,
the subscript \(\mc X_s\) indicates that all other members of the solid state
set are held fixed:
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_s}{\bar\rho_s}
	={} &
	-\operatorname{tr}
	\left[
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		\left(
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\dot\theta_{\mc S}
		+
		\sum_\beta
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\dot\eta_s^\beta
		\right)
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
		\right],
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_density_rate}
\end{align}
%

The generic fluid and multicomponent solid Helmholtz energies are
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{alignedat}{2}
		\psi_f
		& =
		\psi_f(
		\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F}),
		\qquad&
		f\in\mc F,
		\\
		\psi_s
		& =
		\psi_s(
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\bar\rho_s,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S}),
		\qquad&
		s\in\mc S .
	\end{alignedat}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_general_free_energy}
\end{equation}
%
The same held-fixed convention applies to derivatives annotated by
\(\mc X_f\).  Because the mass fractions in each phase sum to one, only
\(N-1\) composition rates are independent.  The reversible composition
relations below are therefore written as differences from component \(N\).

\pagebreak[3]
Electrical storage is represented by the phase electric-enthalpy densities
\(\omega^{+}_{\xi}\) introduced in \sref{virtual_power_derivation}.  Because
\(\omega_\xi^+\) is measured per current phase volume, phase \(\xi\) contributes
\(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\) per current mixture volume.  The interfacial Helmholtz
energy has a different normalization: \(\gamma\) is measured per current fluid
volume and contributes \(\phi\gamma\) to the mixture.  All phases experience
the same electric field, but each phase may have a different dielectric
response.  Define the phase and mixture electric displacements by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf d_\xi
	=
	-\left.
	\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\mbf E}
	\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf d
	=
	\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_electric_displacement_definitions}
\end{equation}
%
The electric field \(\mbf E\) is the electrical state variable, and
\(\mbf d_\xi\) is obtained by differentiating the phase electric enthalpy.  The
minus sign is the defining sign of the electric-enthalpy representation
\cite{dorfmann2017nonlinear_electroelasticity}.

For a fluid phase,
\(\omega_f^+=\omega_f^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F})\).
Its density, composition, and temperature dependence permits the scalar
effective permittivity and dielectric pressure to change with fluid state.
Such changes are relevant in porous rocks: the dielectric response of saturated
sandstone depends on porosity and pore-fluid salinity, while measurements on
fluid-saturated artificial shales show dependence on clay content and
pore-fluid salinity and cation composition
\cite{kirichek2018dielectric_sandstone,beloborodov2017dielectric_shales}.
The solid phase has the analogous dependence on the field, intrinsic density,
solid composition, and \(\theta_{\mc S}\).  These dependencies contribute to the
solid dielectric pressure and to the density, composition, and temperature
coefficients collected in the entropy inequality.

As shown in Appendix~\ref{sec:technical_setting}, objectivity of a potential with only
one spatial-vector electrical argument makes \(\omega_\xi^+\) even in
\(\mbf E\) and makes \(\mbf d_\xi\) parallel to \(\mbf E\).  The present
electrical response is therefore isotropic and excludes permanent polarization
at zero field, bedding-directed dielectric anisotropy, and independent
dielectric relaxation.  Bedding-direction effects would require an additional
objective structural argument and corresponding stress and torque terms.
Electrical coupling retained here includes Maxwell stress, phase equilibrium,
electrochemical transport, and electro-osmotic cross-coupling.  This choice is
consistent with laboratory water-driven hydraulic-fracture self-potential
signals in saturated granite for which the response was dominated by
electrokinetic coupling~\cite{moore2007self_potential}.  Plastic evolution
depends on the phase material stress and composition, so electrical loading
affects plastic response through the coupled Maxwell momentum solution.

The phase Maxwell stress \(\bs\sigma_\xi^{+}\), defined in
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}, contributes to the phase momentum
balance through the total reversible stress
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}\).  The density, composition, and temperature
derivatives of \(\omega^{+}_{\xi}\) enter the corresponding coefficients in the
entropy inequality.  Keeping these roles separate prevents Maxwell mechanical
power from being counted twice.

The derivation first allows a general composition-dependent phase Helmholtz
energy \(\psi_\xi\).  The later additive specialization in
\sref{MC_additive_component_specialization} is optional and resolves the solid
energy into component contributions without changing the common solid motion.

\subsection{Coleman--Noll procedure}
\label{sec:MC_coleman_noll_procedure}

The Coleman--Noll procedure tests the constitutive assumptions against the
local entropy inequality.  The balance laws and kinematic constraints restrict
the admissible rates; after those restrictions are imposed, coefficients of the
remaining independent reversible rates must vanish and the residual
force--rate products must be nonnegative
\cite{coleman1974thermodynamics}.

The three variational or thermodynamic steps used in the manuscript have
different purposes.  Hamilton--d'Alembert stationarity supplies the reversible
phase equations and multiplier relations.  Coleman--Noll then determines which
constitutive stresses, pressures, entropies, and source forces are compatible
with the entropy inequality.  Finally, the Onsager construction supplies one
near-equilibrium choice of reaction and relative-transport kinetics.  Relations
obtained independently from the reversible variation and the entropy inequality
must agree because both use the same energies and constraints.

The Coleman--Noll exploitation uses the absolute neutral component potential
defined by the constituent-mass derivative of the phase material and electric
enthalpy storage at fixed phase volume fraction,
%
\begin{equation}
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\equiv
	\left.
	\frac{
		\partial
		\left(
		\rho_\xi\psi_\xi+\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+
		\right)
	}{
		\partial\rho_\xi^\alpha
	}
	\right\vert_{
		\phi_\xi,\theta_{\mc G},\mbf E,
		\{\rho_\xi^\beta\}_{\beta\ne\alpha},
		\text{kinematic and internal fields}
	},
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G .
	\label{eq:MC_absolute_neutral_component_potential}
\end{equation}
%
The corresponding electrochemical potential is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi .
	\label{eq:C45_combined_def}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\varphi\) has units of energy per charge and \(z_\xi^\alpha\) has units
of charge per component mass.  These are constitutive state quantities, not
variational multipliers.  For a solid phase, the kinematic and internal fields
held fixed in
\eqref{eq:MC_absolute_neutral_component_potential} are
\(\mbf A_s\), \(\bar{\mbf F}_s\), \(\mbf A_s^p\), and
\(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p\).  For a fluid phase, the same definition reduces to the
fixed-volume Helmholtz constituent potential, augmented by the composition and
density dependence of \(\omega_f^+\).

Let \(e_\xi\) denote the specific internal energy of phase \(\xi\), and let
\(\mathfrak{s}_\xi\) denote its specific entropy.  For fluid phases,
%
\begin{equation}
	e_f=\psi_f+\theta_{\mc F}\mathfrak{s}_f,
	\qquad
	\psi_f=\psi_f(\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F}),
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
%
For multicomponent solid phases,
%
\begin{equation}
	e_s=\psi_s+\theta_{\mc S}\mathfrak{s}_s,
	\qquad
	\psi_s=\psi_s(
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
	\bar\rho_s,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S}),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
%
The free-energy rates are
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f\dot\psi_f
	={} &
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\rho_f
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\bar\rho_f}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\dot{\bar\rho}_f
	+
	\sum_\beta
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\dot\eta_f^\beta
	+
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\dot\theta_{\mc F}
	\right),
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_free_energy_rate}
	\\
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s\dot\psi_s
	={} &
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\rho_s
	\left[
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		:
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e
		+
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\mbf A_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		:
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^e
		\right.
	\notag       \\
	    & \left.
		+
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\dot{\bar\rho}_s
		+
		\sum_\beta
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\dot\eta_s^\beta
		+
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\dot\theta_{\mc S}
		\right].
	\label{eq:MC_solid_free_energy_rate}
\end{align}
%
The fluid Helmholtz energy has no elastic-deformation argument, so its material
elastic stress is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_f'=\mbf 0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_stress_free}
\end{equation}
%
Pressure is retained separately through the intrinsic-density coefficient.
The remaining reversible stress in the fluid momentum equation is therefore
the Maxwell stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
	=
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{f},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_total_reversible_stress}
\end{equation}
%
The interfacial Helmholtz density \(\gamma\) is measured per current fluid
volume and has the state set \eqref{eq:MC_interfacial_state_set}.  Temperature
dependence permits the capillary pressure to vary with fluid temperature, as in
the nonisothermal porous-media formulation of Hassanizadeh and Gray
\cite{hassanizadeh1993thermodynamic}.  The internal-variable construction makes
the pressure depend on the drainage--imbibition path
\cite{wei2006capillary_hysteresis}.  The mixture stores
\(\phi\gamma\) per current volume.  Differentiating this quantity along the
skeleton trajectory and using the interfacial potentials \(\gamma_f\) from
\eqref{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives} gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
	\left[
		\phi\gamma
		\right]
	=
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\gamma_f\dot\phi_f
	+
	\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\cdot\dot{\mbf h}
	+
	\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\dot\theta_{\mc F} .
	\label{eq:MC_interfacial_helmholtz_rate}
\end{equation}
%
This term is a bulk storage rate in the fluid-subsystem energy balance.  The
history rate records the change in interfacial storage as the pore-scale fluid
arrangement changes; its dissipative restriction is derived below.  The density
\(\phi\gamma\) represents unresolved interfaces; no sharp interface or
interface velocity is introduced.  A
sharp-interface theory would instead require separate surface balances of
energy and entropy
\cite{bothe2022sharp_interface}.

The material-source convention follows Drumheller's constituent balance and
adopts local-equilibrium insertion: mass inserted into or removed from phase
\(\xi\) carries the recipient-phase specific internal energy \(e_\xi\) and
specific entropy \(\mathfrak{s}_\xi\).  Isolating the source-carried parts of the
corresponding conservative storage rates gives
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_closure}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\left[
			\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
			\left(
			\rho_\xi e_\xi
			\right)
			+
			\rho_\xi e_\xi
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
			+
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left[
				\rho_\xi e_\xi
				\left(
				\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
				\right)
			\right]
		\right]
		\right\vert_{\phasedot e_\xi=0}
		 & =
		e_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot c_\xi^\alpha,
		\label{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_energy} \\
		\left.
		\left[
			\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
			\left(
			\rho_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi
			\right)
			+
			\rho_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
			+
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left[
				\rho_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi
				\left(
				\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
				\right)
			\right]
		\right]
		\right\vert_{\phasedot{\mathfrak{s}}_\xi=0}
		 & =
		\mathfrak{s}_\xi
		\sum_\alpha\phasedot c_\xi^\alpha .
		\label{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_entropy}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Thus \(-L_\xi^\alpha\phasedot c_\xi^\alpha\) is distinct generalized transfer
work, not source-carried energy.  A non-equilibrium source state would require
additional source energy and entropy data and a corresponding entropy-production
restriction; it is outside the adopted closure.  Equivalently, setting
\(\Gamma_\xi=e_\xi\) in
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity} produces the
phase-following storage
\(\rho_\xi\phasedot e_\xi+
e_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot c_\xi^\alpha\).  Thus the present convention
transports internal energy, rather than enthalpy, with bulk phase mass; pressure
and other constraint powers remain explicit on the right-hand side.  Let
\(\mbf L_\xi=\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf v_\xi\) be the phase velocity gradient,
\(\mbf q_\xi\) the total nonadvective material-energy flux assigned to phase
\(\xi\), \(\rho_\xi r_\xi\) the external heat supply, and
\(\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}\) the internal-energy supply from the other phases,
all per unit current mixture volume where appropriate.  Thus
\(\mbf q_\xi\) collects the gauge-invariant nonadvective material-energy
transport that remains after bulk phase convection has been separated; it is
not restricted a priori to a purely conductive contribution.  A charged
component flux relative to its phase also receives the electrical work
\(z_\xi^\alpha\mbf j_\xi^\alpha\cdot\mbf E\).  This work is written directly
in terms of the electric field so that both the energy balance and the
nonisothermal transport force are independent of the arbitrary zero of
\(\varphi\).  The uncoupled Fourier specialization adopted below neglects
Dufour and other heat--component-transport cross effects.

One energy balance is assigned to the fluid thermal subsystem and one to the
solid thermal subsystem, following the constituent structure of Drumheller and
Bedford \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical}.  Conversion work follows the
generalized form of Drumheller \cite[eq.~(50)]{drumheller2000}.  The fluid
balance contains the averaged interfacial energy, and the solid balance contains
the solid-phase energies.  Storage rates are evaluated in the skeleton frame
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_rate_convention}, as in the solid-following balances of
Seguin and Walkington \cite[eqs.~(10)--(12)]{seguin2019multicomponent}.
Consistently with those balances, bulk phase motion transports internal energy
through the skeleton control surface and transports entropy in the entropy
inequality below.  These bulk convective fluxes are distinct from the
nonadvective energy flux \(\mbf q_\xi\) and from the component-relative
molecular-diffusion and mechanical-dispersion fluxes.  Internal energy exchanged
between phases cancels by mechanism.  The later closures impose
equal-and-opposite ordinary heat and reaction-associated transfers; the
mechanical contribution cancels against interphase momentum power only after
the subsystem balances are combined with the phase kinetic-energy balances.

Electrical storage is written in the electric-enthalpy representation
\cite{montanaro2015thermo_electroelasticity}.  Phase \(\xi\) contributes
\(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\), while the shared interfacial energy \(\phi\gamma\)
appears only in the fluid-subsystem balance.  Expanding the electric-enthalpy
rate exposes material-state, Maxwell mechanical-power, and common
electric-potential-rate contributions.  In particular,
\(\mbf d_\xi=-\partial\omega_\xi^+/\partial\mbf E\) makes
\(-\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\dot{\mbf E}\) the constitutive electric-field-rate
power; current-volume transport separates its geometric part into
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^+:\mbf L_\xi\) and leaves
	\(\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\dot\varphi\) as the shared
	field-coordinate part.  The relative material flux in the energy balance
	written below therefore carries \(\rho_\xi e_\xi\); the
electrostatic field energy is Eulerian storage whose boundary power is supplied
by the global identity below, not an additional material advective flux.

The last contribution cannot be
assigned independently to the fluid and solid thermal subsystems because
\(\varphi\) is one shared field.  Summing it over all phases and using Gauss'
law gives the global electrostatic power identity
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\dot\varphi
	&=
	\mbf d\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\dot\varphi
	\notag \\
	&=
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\dot\varphi\,\mbf d
	\right)
	-
	\varrho\dot\varphi .
	\label{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}
\end{align}
%
The bulk term \(-\varrho\dot\varphi\) is the rate form of the free-charge
source work in \eqref{eq:W_def}, and the divergence gives the electrical
boundary power.  In particular, its flux is
\(\dot\varphi\bar q\) on \(\Gamma_q\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity} is an entropy-free field-power
	identity, not a third thermal balance.  It is applied globally before either
	thermal-subsystem balance is divided by its temperature.  Here \(\varrho\) is
	the material-carried charge density defined by
\eqref{eq:mixture_charge_density_definition}.

The two thermal-subsystem balances therefore retain the material-state rate of
each phase electric enthalpy at fixed \(\mbf E\) and its Maxwell mechanical
power, while the shared electric-potential-rate term is carried by
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  The resulting balance for
\(\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}\) is
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		 & \sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
			\left[
				\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
				\left(
				\rho_\xi e_\xi
				\right)
				+
				\rho_\xi e_\xi
				\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
				+
				\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
				\left[
					\rho_\xi e_\xi
					\left(
					\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
					\right)
				\right]
				+
				\omega_\xi^+\dot\phi_\xi
			+
			\phi_\xi
			\left.
			\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_\xi}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			\dot{\bar\rho}_\xi
			\right.
		\\
		 & \qquad
			\left.
			+
			\phi_\xi
			\sum_\alpha
			\left.
			\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha
			+
			\phi_\xi
			\left.
			\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			\dot\theta_{\mc G}
			+
			\bs\sigma_\xi^+:\mbf L_\xi
			\right]
		\\
		 & \qquad
		+
		\delta_{\mc G\mc F}
		\left[
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\gamma_f\dot\phi_f
		+
		\phi
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
		\cdot\dot{\mbf h}
		+
		\phi
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
		\dot\theta_{\mc F}
		\right]
		\\
		 & =
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
		\left[
			\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}:\mbf L_\xi
			+
			\phi_\xi\bar p_\xi
			\frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}
			+
			\bar p_\xi\dot\phi_\xi
			+
			\lambda\dot\phi_\xi
			+
			\sum_\alpha
			\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
			\dot\eta_\xi^\alpha
			-
			\sum_\alpha L_\xi^\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
			\right.
		\\
		 & \left.
			\qquad\qquad
			-
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\mbf q_\xi
			+
			\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
			\left(
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
				+
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)\cdot\mbf E
			+
			\rho_\xi r_\xi
			+
			\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
			\right].
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:MC_energy_balance}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\delta_{\mc G\mc F}\) includes the interfacial storage term only in the
fluid balance, and \(e_\xi=\psi_\xi+\theta_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi\).  By
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity}, the first three terms
in each phase contribution are exactly
\(\rho_\xi\phasedot e_\xi+
e_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot c_\xi^\alpha\); no mass-source energy has been
omitted or counted twice.  The material-plus-field first law consists of the
two instances of \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}, the phase kinetic-energy
balances obtained from momentum balance, and the global field-power identity
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  This construction avoids an
arbitrary allocation of the common power
\(\mbf d\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\dot\varphi\) and its source
\(-\varrho\dot\varphi\) between subsystems at different temperatures.
The electrical transport term in \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance} is the work of
the electric field on component charge transported relative to phase
\(\xi\).  It is not a potential-energy flux.  Both
\(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\) and \(\mbf q_\xi\) are unchanged under
\(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\), so each subsystem balance is gauge
invariant term by term.  Phase-advective electrical work belongs to the
Maxwell mechanical and global field-power block; the term displayed here is
only the additional work on charge moving relative to that phase and therefore
does not duplicate the phase mechanical power.
The direct observer-and-gauge test for this term is
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
		(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha)^\star
		+
		(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha)^\star
	\right)\cdot\mbf E^\star
	&=
	\left[
		\mbf Q
		\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
		\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
	\right]\cdot\mbf Q\mbf E
	\notag \\
	&=
	\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot\mbf E .
	\label{eq:MC_relative_current_work_gauge_objectivity}
\end{align}
%
The additive gauge function does not appear because
\(\mbf E^\star=-\nabla_{\mbf x^\star}\varphi^\star=\mbf Q\mbf E\).
The other occurrences of \(\varphi\) in the material-plus-field first law are
only through \(\mbf E\), \(\dot{\mbf E}\), and the globally closed identity
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  Thus the complete energy
equation, not only its final constitutive reduction, passes the gauge test.
When the two subsystem equations are summed, the relative material-energy
transport remains
\(\sum_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot[
\rho_\xi e_\xi(\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S})]\), as it must in a
skeleton-moving control volume.  Adding the phase kinetic-energy balances makes
the mechanical interaction contribution cancel by its later
equal-and-opposite allocation; ordinary heat and reaction-associated transfers
cancel in the same way.  In the nonporous single-phase limit the phase follows the
skeleton, so the relative flux vanishes and the limit derived below is
unchanged.  Thus the observer transformation introduces neither an artificial
source nor a loss of total energy.

The multiplier power
\(\lambda\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\dot\phi_\xi\) transfers reversible work between the
two thermal subsystems.  It vanishes only after the subsystem balances are
summed, because \(\sum_\xi\dot\phi_\xi=0\).  Retaining this power in each
subsystem balance is necessary when
\(\theta_{\mc F}\ne\theta_{\mc S}\), since the two balances are divided by
different temperatures in the entropy inequality.

The mechanical power uses the total reversible stress
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}\).  The electric-enthalpy rate contains the
corresponding Maxwell power explicitly; subtracting it from the total
mechanical power leaves the material stress power used in the constitutive
restrictions.  The electric-potential-gradient, bulk-source, and boundary-power
terms have already been separated through
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}; they are not included in either
local thermal entropy supply.

The connection with a conventional thermo-electroelastic energy balance is most
transparent in the nonporous, single-phase limit.  Let
\(\mc G=\{\xi\}\), set \(\phi_\xi=1\), suppress conversion, composition,
interfacial, and interphase-exchange terms, and let the skeleton observer follow
phase \(\xi\).  Phase mass conservation then gives
\(\dot\rho_\xi/\rho_\xi=-\operatorname{tr}\mbf L_\xi\).  Using
\(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\),
\(\dot{\mbf E}=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\dot\varphi-\mbf L_\xi^T\mbf E\), and
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}, the electrical terms in
\eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance} and
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity} combine into the material-volume
rate of \(\omega_\xi^+\), and the balance reduces to
%
\begin{align}
	\rho_\xi
	\frac{D_\xi}{Dt}
	\left(
	e_\xi+\frac{\omega_\xi^+}{\rho_\xi}
	\right)
	={} &
	\rho_\xi\dot e_\xi
	+
	\dot\omega_\xi^+
	+
	\omega_\xi^+\operatorname{tr}\mbf L_\xi
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\left(
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
	-
	\bar p_\xi\mbf I
		\right):\mbf L_\xi
		-
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf q_\xi
		+
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\dot\varphi\,\mbf d
		\right)
		-
		\varrho\dot\varphi
		+
		\rho_\xi r_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_single_phase_energy_limit}
\end{align}
%
Reference~\cite{montanaro2015thermo_electroelasticity} instead writes its
equation~(12) for the material internal energy \(\varepsilon\), with electrical
power \(\mbf E^{M}\cdot\rho\dot{\bs\pi}\), where
\(\bs\pi=\mbf P/\rho\) is the polarization per unit mass.  Combining that
balance with the electric-work term in its Helmholtz free-energy definition,
equation~(16), gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho
	\frac{D}{Dt}
	\left(
	\varepsilon-\mbf E^{M}\cdot\bs\pi
	\right)
	=
	\bs\tau:\mbf L
	-
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf q
	-
	\mbf P\cdot\dot{\mbf E}^{M}
	+
	\rho r .
	\label{eq:MC_montanaro_electric_enthalpy_limit}
\end{equation}
%
Equations \eqref{eq:MC_single_phase_energy_limit} and
\eqref{eq:MC_montanaro_electric_enthalpy_limit} differ only in where electrical
energy and power are placed.  Reference~\cite{montanaro2015thermo_electroelasticity}
keeps polarization work in the material balance.  The present formulation
combines material and field storage, includes Maxwell stress in the mechanical
power, and displays the free-charge source and electrical boundary flux
explicitly.  Using \eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity} converts between
the material and field-power descriptions.  Polarization is therefore not an
additional state variable here.  If an SI vacuum-field split is desired,
\(\mbf P=\mbf d-\varepsilon_0\mbf E\).  The displacement \(\mbf d\) is retained
because Gauss' law relates it directly to free charge, while the dependence of
\(\omega_\xi^+\) on density, saturation, composition, and temperature already
represents the effective dielectric response of the porous medium.  An
independent polarization evolution law would be required only for relaxation,
hysteresis, ferroelectricity, or other additional dielectric physics.

The solid stress power is decomposed by
\(\mbf F=\mbf A_s\bar{\mbf F}_s\),
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf L_s
	=
	\dot{\mbf A}_s\mbf A_s^{-1}
	+
	\mbf A_s
	\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_F_split_for_power}
\end{equation}
%
Using \eqref{eq:MC_solid_kinematic_splits}, the solid stress power is
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+}:\mbf L_s
	={} &
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^e
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
		\mbf A_s^e
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^p
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
		\right]\dot\theta_{\mc S}
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\sum_\beta
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
		\right]\dot\eta_s^\beta
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
	\mbf A_s
	\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
	\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
	\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
	\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
	\left.

The local entropy inequality is written in the same skeleton frame as the
subsystem energy balances.  Its entropy flux contains entropy convected through
the skeleton control surface, entropy transported by the gauge-invariant
material-energy flux, and the neutral-chemical-potential-weighted contribution

enters through the gauge-invariant volume power in
\eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}, not through a potential-dependent entropy flux.

reduced to \(N-1\) independent differences by subtracting the

\eqref{eq:MC_reduced_entropy_inequality} and the separate term containing the
Helmholtz-energy and temperature gradients are generated together when a
phase-following thermodynamic rate is expressed in the skeleton frame.  Indeed,
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_skeleton_transport_identity} gives the exact identity
%
\begin{align}

	\left(

	\right)
	\notag \\

	\right)\cdot

		+

	\right)
	=
	-\frac{\rho_\xi}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\left(
		\phasedot{\psi}_\xi
		+
		\mathfrak{s}_\xi\phasedot{\theta}_{\mc G}
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity}
\end{align}

Consequently, the second term on the left is not an additional constitutive
force conjugate to bulk phase velocity.  Separating it from the first term is
only an observer-dependent expansion of the single phase-following rate on the
right.  Treating that term as an independent dissipative force would count
the same thermodynamic rate twice: once through the Coleman--Noll coefficients
of the phase state variables and again through a bulk interaction law.

In \eqref{eq:MC_reduced_entropy_inequality}, the interfacial contribution is the
negative of the storage rate
\eqref{eq:MC_interfacial_helmholtz_rate}.  The remaining derivatives of
\(\omega_\xi^+\) measure how electrical storage changes with density,
composition, and temperature.  The explicit relative-velocity term is retained
in the intermediate skeleton-rate form so that the change of observer remains
visible.  Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity} does
not state that this term vanishes.  It states that the term must be recombined
with the skeleton-rate free-energy contribution before the collected inequality
is separated into reversible and residual parts.  The explicit reassembly is
given after the collected inequality; applying the reversible restrictions to
its residual block alone would use the identity incorrectly.
The Maxwell part of the stress power is removed
from the total mechanical power by
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}:\mbf L_\xi
	-
	\bs\sigma_\xi^+:\mbf L_\xi
	=
	\bs\sigma_\xi':\mbf L_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_material_stress_power_identity}
\end{equation}
The material stress \(\bs\sigma_\xi'\) is therefore conjugate to the material
Helmholtz-energy rate, while the momentum equation continues to use the total
stress \(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}\).  This is the same division obtained from an
electromagnetic energy-flux balance: the field contribution supplies Maxwell
power, and the remaining mechanical power acts on the material free energy
\cite{nika2025heterogeneous_dielectrics}.  Both the displacement relation
\(\mbf d_\xi=-\partial\omega_\xi^+/\partial\mbf E\) and the Maxwell stress
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition} are generated from the same phase
electric enthalpy \cite{dorfmann2017nonlinear_electroelasticity}.

\eqref{eq:MC_stress_power_split}.  Next use
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate} to separate component

component-source term and entropy-flux divergence combine by the product rule,
while the direct relative-current work in \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance} supplies
the electric-field part of the transport force.  A charge-rate projection
derived with the source terms below records the equivalent potential-charge

		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		-
		\rho_\xi
		\left(
		\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\left(
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\psi_\xi
		+
		\mathfrak{s}_\xi
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
		\right)
	\right]
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\frac{
		\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	}{
		\theta_{\mc G}^2
		}
		.
		\label{eq:MC_collected_entropy_residual}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%

Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy} is an audit expansion in
skeleton-rate coordinates, not yet a termwise division into reversible and
residual mechanisms.  In particular, the first term on the left of
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity} has been distributed
among the density, composition, temperature, and solid-kinematic coefficients
in \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_elastic_distension}--%
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_temperature}, while its observer correction
appears intact in \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_residual}.  To show the
required operation explicitly, solve
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity} for that correction
and sum over the phases:
%
\begin{align}
	&-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\psi_\xi
	+
	\mathfrak{s}_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	\right)
	\notag \\
	&\quad =
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\phasedot{\psi}_\xi
	+
	\mathfrak{s}_\xi\phasedot{\theta}_{\mc G}
	\right)
	\notag \\
	&\qquad\quad
	+\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\dot\psi_\xi
	+
	\mathfrak{s}_\xi\dot\theta_{\mc G}
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly}
\end{align}
%
The final positive sum in
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly} cancels the negative
skeleton-rate free-energy contribution that was expanded into the preceding
blocks of \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy}.  This is an algebraic
reassembly of the complete collected inequality; it is not a condition that
sets either contribution to zero.  What remains from the pair is the
phase-following rate in the second line.  For a mobile fluid phase its
constitutive chain rule is
%
\begin{align}
	\phasedot{\psi}_f
	={}&
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\bar\rho_f}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\phasedot{\bar\rho}_f
	+
	\sum_\beta
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\phasedot{\eta}_f^\beta
	\notag \\
	&+
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\phasedot{\theta}_{\mc F},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F.
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_phase_following_free_energy_chain_rule}
\end{align}
%
For every solid phase,
\(\mbf v_s=\mbf v_{\mc S}\), so the phase-following and skeleton-following
rates coincide and \eqref{eq:MC_solid_free_energy_rate} already supplies this
chain rule.  Only after
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly} is substituted into the
whole of \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy} are the reversible restrictions
applied.  If the coefficients in the first four blocks were set to zero before
this reassembly, the observer correction in the residual block would
incorrectly survive as an independent force--flux product.

After this ordering is enforced, coefficients of elastic deformation,
intrinsic density, composition, temperature, and volume-fraction rates give
the reversible constitutive restrictions.  Reaction rates, component-relative
fluxes, plastic rates, interphase energy exchange, and heat flux remain as
dissipative force--rate products.  The resulting residual is treated in
the subsequent source-and-closure subsection.

\subsection{Reversible restrictions}
\label{sec:MC_reversible_restrictions}

After the observer correction has been reassembled according to
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly}, the reversible
restrictions are obtained by setting to zero the coefficients of rates that
can vary independently at a fixed constitutive state.  The stress
appearing in these coefficients is the material stress
\(\bs\sigma_\xi'\).  The total stress in the momentum equation is
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}=\bs\sigma_\xi'+\bs\sigma_\xi^+\).

The coefficient of
\(\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}\) gives
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	\mbf A_s
	={} &
	\rho_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\right)^T
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\left(
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\bar\rho_s
	+
	\phi_s
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\bar\rho_s}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
	\bar\rho_s
	-
	\phi_s\bar p_s
	\right)\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_stress_before_pressure}
\end{align}
%
For a fluid, \(\bs\sigma_f'=\mbf0\) because the Helmholtz energy has no elastic
deformation argument.  After the isotropic Maxwell contribution is combined
with the pressure relation, the remaining nonpressure reversible stress enters
the dynamic-capillary fluid momentum balance
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary} through
\(\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f\); the fluid pressure is determined separately by
the intrinsic-density coefficient.

Setting the intrinsic-density coefficients to zero defines the fluid and solid
phase pressures,
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pressure_definitions}
	\begin{align}
		\bar p_f
		 & =
		\bar\rho_f^2
		\left.
		\p{\psi_f}{\bar\rho_f}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
		+
		\bar\rho_f
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\bar\rho_f}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E},
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F,
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_pressure_definition} \\
		\bar p_s
		 & =
		\bar\rho_s^2
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		+
		\bar\rho_s
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\bar\rho_s}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_pressure_definition}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Using the solid pressure relation in the elastic-deformation coefficient removes
the spherical density term and gives the material solid stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\mbf A_s^{-T}
	\left(
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
	\right)
	\mbf A_s^T,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
The electrical part of the solid phase-map variation supplies the solid Maxwell
stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{s}
	=
	\left.
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	\right\vert_{\xi=s},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_solid_electrical_stress_contribution}
\end{equation}
%
The total solid stress is
\(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+}=\bs\sigma_s'+\bs\sigma_s^+\).  This total stress enters
momentum balance, whereas only \(\bs\sigma_s'\) is conjugate to the solid
material free-energy rate.

For the linear dielectric choice
\(\omega^{+}_{\xi}=-\frac{1}{2}\mbf E\cdot\mbf d_\xi\), the sum of the
phase-attributed electrical stresses is the usual Maxwell stress
\(\mbf E\otimes\mbf d-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mbf E\cdot\mbf d\right)\mbf I\), with
\(\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\).

The coefficient of the elastic distension rate gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\mbf A_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	(\mbf A_s^e)^T,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_distension_force_definition}
\end{equation}
%
The solid and fluid volume-fraction rates are constrained by
\(\sum_\xi\dot\phi_\xi=0\), so their reversible coefficients are determined up
to the common multiplier \(\lambda\).  The multiplier power was retained in
both subsystem energy balances; consequently, the same \(\lambda\) appears in
the solid and fluid coefficients even when their temperatures differ.

For the fluid phases, let
\(\dot\phi=D_{\mc S}\phi/Dt\) and use
\(\dot\phi_f=S_f\dot\phi+\phi\dot S_f\), with
\(\sum_f\dot S_f=0\).  The fluid volume-fraction power then separates into a
pore-volume part and a saturation-redistribution part:
%
\begin{align}
	&\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\lambda+\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
	\right)\dot\phi_f
	\notag\\
	&\qquad={}
	\left[
	\lambda
	+\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
	\right)
	\right]\dot\phi
	+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
	\right)\dot S_f .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_rate_decomposition}
\end{align}
%
The solid volume-fraction rates and the total fluid pore-volume rate remain
reversible.  Setting their coefficients to zero gives
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_volume_fraction_restrictions}
	\begin{align}
		0
		      & =
		\lambda
		+
		\bar p_s
		-
		\omega^{+}_{s},
		\quad &
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_volume_fraction_restriction} \\
		0
		      & =
		\lambda
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f
		\left(
		\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
		\right).
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_restriction}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The second relation and
\(\sum_fS_f\gamma_f=\gamma\) preserve the equivalent-pressure identity
\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}.  The final term in
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_rate_decomposition} is retained in the
residual inequality and closed by the dynamic-capillary Onsager relation.  If
\(\mc T=\mbf0\), that closure makes the phase coefficients equal; the common
pore-volume relation then recovers the individual reversible fluid relations.
This split does not require \(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}\).

Only \(N-1\) composition rates are independent in each fluid phase.  Subtracting
the component-\(N\) coefficient from the other coefficients gives
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	&\rho_f
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\right)
	+
	\phi_f
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\eta_f^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E}
	\right)
	\notag \\
	&\qquad =
	\frac{\pi_f^\beta}{\eta_f^\beta}
	-
	\frac{\pi_f^N}{\eta_f^N},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N-1,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_composition_projection}
\end{empheq}
%
The corresponding component differences for a solid phase also contain the
composition dependence of its stress-free maps,
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	& \rho_s
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\right)
	+
	\phi_s
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\eta_s^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\eta_s^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
	\right)
	\notag    \\
	& \quad
	-
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left(
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		-
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\eta_s^N}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\right)
		(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag    \\
	& \quad
	-
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
	\left(
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
	\right)
	\right.
	\notag    \\
	& \left.
	\quad
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\right)
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
	\right]
	\notag    \\
	& \qquad
	=
	\frac{\pi_s^\beta}{\eta_s^\beta}
	-
	\frac{\pi_s^N}{\eta_s^N},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N-1,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_composition_projection}
\end{empheq}
%
Finally, \(\dot\theta_{\mc F}\) and \(\dot\theta_{\mc S}\) are independent, so
their coefficients define separate fluid- and solid-subsystem entropies.  The
interfacial entropy belongs to the fluid subsystem because
\(\gamma=\gamma(\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F})\):
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_entropy_definitions}
	\begin{align}
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f\mathfrak{s}_f
		={} &
		-
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f
		\left.
		\p{\psi_f}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
		-
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E}
		-
		\phi
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h},
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_entropy_definition}
		\\
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s\mathfrak{s}_s
		={} &
		-
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		-
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\phi_s
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
		\notag  \\
		    & +
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\operatorname{tr}\left[
			(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
			(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
			(\bs\sigma_s')^T
			\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
			\left.
			\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\theta_{\mc S}}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
			(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
			\right]
		\notag  \\
		    & +
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
		\mbf A_s^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
		\right].
		\label{eq:MC_solid_entropy_definition}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}

\subsection{Sources, residual dissipation, and closures}
\label{sec:MC_residual_dissipation}

\subsubsection{Component supply and thermodynamic forces}
\label{sec:MC_component_supply_forces}

The material increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) records both conversion and
component transport relative to phase \(\xi\).  Its variational coefficient
\eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} contains the work potential
\(L_\xi^\alpha\), while the entropy inequality contains the combination
\(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\) multiplying the component-supply rate.

Multiplying the complete component-rate balance by
\(\varphi z_\xi^\alpha/\theta_{\mc G}\), applying the product rule to the
relative charge flux, and summing gives the temperature-weighted charge-rate
identity
%
\begin{align}
	0
	={} &
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left\{
		\frac{\varphi z_\xi^\alpha}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\left[
		\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
		-
		\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
		\right]
		\right.
	\notag \\
	& \left.
		\qquad
		+
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\varphi z_\xi^\alpha
			\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)
		\right]
		\right.
	\notag \\
	& \left.
		\qquad
		-
		\frac{z_\xi^\alpha}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right\}.
	\label{eq:MC_charge_rate_entropy_projection}
\end{align}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_charge_rate_entropy_projection} is gauge invariant as a
whole.  Under \(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\), its change is
\(c_\varphi(t)/\theta_{\mc G}\) times the complete charge-weighted component
balance and therefore vanishes.  Its divergence and electric-field terms must
not be separated and inserted individually into the first law.  The adopted
energy representation instead uses the direct, gauge-invariant
relative-current work already present in \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}.
Consequently the charge-rate
identity is an alternative accounting check, not an additional dissipative
term.  The reaction coefficient retained below is the neutral coefficient
\(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\) for every mechanism.  When charge is conserved
separately within each thermal subsystem, its temperature-weighted
stoichiometric sum may instead be written with electrochemical coefficients
because the added electrical sum is zero.  For charge
transfer between subsystems, the neutral form is required by gauge invariance;
its physical reaction-energy transfer may be coupled subject to the
mechanismwise dissipation condition derived with the closures below.

The stress-free maps of a solid phase vary with composition, and
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_elastic_arguments} gives
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_composition_elastic_chain_rule}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^e}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{
			\mbf A_s,\mbf A_s^p,\theta_{\mc S}
		}
		={} &
		-\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_composition_chain_rule}
		\\
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{
			\bar{\mbf F}_s,\bar{\mbf F}_s^p,\theta_{\mc S}
		}
		={} &
		-\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_true_deformation_composition_chain_rule}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The component potential introduces no new primary field: an equation of state
or free-energy material evaluates it algebraically.

Using \(\rho_\xi=\sum_\alpha\rho_\xi^\alpha
=\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\), the constituent-mass chain rule together with the
density relation \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_storage} and the component
stationarity relation \eqref{eq:Pi_componentwise} gives the phase Euler
identity
%
\begin{equation}
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\psi_\xi
	+
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}.
	\label{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Appendix~\ref{app:component_potential_euler_derivation} gives the complete
constituent-mass chain rule leading to
\eqref{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity}.  For solids,
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_composition_elastic_chain_rule} and the material-stress
relations convert the implicit elastic derivatives into the two stress-power
terms in \eqref{eq:MC_solid_composition_projection}, so the same cancellation
applies.

The single transfer potential does not require the absolute identification
\(L_\xi^\alpha=\mu_\xi^\alpha-\psi_\xi\) in every phase.  Subtracting the
reference-component form of \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} from its
component-\(\alpha\) form gives instead
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N
	={} &
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
	-
	\frac{\pi_\xi^N}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^N}
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N,
	\qquad
	\alpha=1,\ldots,N-1 .
	\label{eq:MC_transfer_work_component_difference}
\end{empheq}
%
The second equality follows by subtracting the constituent derivatives
\eqref{eq:MC_absolute_neutral_component_potential}; their common density and
normalization terms cancel, leaving exactly the neutral chemical-potential
difference supplied by the composition restrictions
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_composition_projection} and
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_composition_projection}.  Consequently,
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	-
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^N+z_\xi^N\varphi
	\right)
	=
	\mu_\xi^\alpha-\mu_\xi^N .
	\label{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_potential_difference}
\end{equation}
%
The generalized transfer coefficients therefore retain the usual within-phase
electrochemical-potential differences even though their absolute values need
not equal the electrochemical potentials away from phase equilibrium.  At
nonuniform temperature the transport force uses the equivalent gauge-invariant
split into a neutral-potential thermal gradient and the electric field, derived
below.

Equations \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} and
\eqref{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity} also identify the common
within-phase offset explicitly:
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	-
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_phase_offset}
\end{equation}
%
The right-hand side is independent of the component index, which is the
absolute counterpart of
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_potential_difference}.
Equivalently, every transfer-work coefficient is closed by the explicit
algebraic recovery
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	L_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	-
	\psi_\xi
	+
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_transfer_work_full_recovery}
\end{empheq}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_full_recovery} determines
\(L_\xi^\alpha\) rather than introducing it as an independent constitutive
function.  The phase free-energy model supplies \(\psi_\xi\) and
\(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\), while the transfer-potential equation determines the
component-independent offset
\(D_\xi\tau/Dt-\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi/2\).  Imposing
\(L_\xi^\alpha=\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\psi_\xi\) in every phase would set every
phase-common level to zero and would require
\(D_\xi\tau/Dt=\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi/2\) along every distinct phase
trajectory.  That stronger specialization is admissible at reversible
equilibrium but would overconstrain one shared \(\tau\) for general relative
motion.

To make the retained-\(\tau\) system square, choose one solid phase
\(s_\star\) as the transfer reference.  All solid phases follow the skeleton,
so \(D_{s_\star}/Dt=D_{\mc S}/Dt\).  The normalization
%
\begin{equation}
	\psi_{s_\star}+L_{s_\star}^N
	=
	\hat\mu_{s_\star}^N,
	\qquad
	s_\star\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_transfer_work_reference_normalization}
\end{equation}
%
fixes the common additive transfer-work level.  The reference-component form
of \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} then supplies the single evolution
equation
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{D_{\mc S}\tau}{Dt}
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+
	\hat\mu_{s_\star}^N
	-
	\psi_{s_\star}
	-
	\frac{\pi_{s_\star}^N}
	{\phi_{s_\star}\bar\rho_{s_\star}\eta_{s_\star}^N}
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S},
	\qquad
	s_\star\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_admissible_conversion_component}
\end{align}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_admissible_conversion_component} governs the skeleton
material derivative of \(\tau\); it does not identify \(\tau\) itself with the
skeleton kinetic energy.  For a stationary skeleton,
\(D_{\mc S}\tau/Dt=0\), so \(\tau\) retains its initial value along each
skeleton trajectory.  Appendix~\ref{sec:technical_setting} states the
corresponding additive normalization of its initial value.
The second equality follows from
\eqref{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity}; the expanded first line records
the thermodynamic normalization used by the general phase equations.
Once \(\tau\) is known, the remaining \(P-1\) phase-reference coefficients are
recovered algebraically from
%
\begin{equation}
	L_\xi^N
	=
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	+
	\frac{\pi_\xi^N}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^N},
	\qquad
	\xi\ne s_\star .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_reference_transfer_work_recovery}
\end{equation}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_full_recovery}, or equivalently
\eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_component_difference}, then reconstructs every
\(L_\xi^\alpha\) with \(\alpha\ne N\).  Thus one scalar evolution
determines \(\tau\), \(P-1\) algebraic equations determine the non-reference
phase levels, and the component-difference restrictions introduce no
additional primary fields.

Substituting the component material-rate identity
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate} into the
temperature-weighted supply power separates conversion from transport relative
to the phase,
%
\begin{align}
	 & -
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\notag     \\
	 & \quad =
	-\sum_m
	\left[
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]\dot r_{(m)}
	\notag \\
	 & \qquad +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\frac{
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection}
\end{align}
%
The traditional electrochemical affinity of mechanism \(m\) is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	\equiv
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_affinity_projection}
\end{equation}
%
For a mechanism satisfying \eqref{eq:mechanism_charge_conservation},
the explicit electrical term cancels from the affinity:
%
\begin{align}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	={} &
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\notag \\
	={} &
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	-
	\varphi
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	z_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\notag \\
	={} &
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:electrochemical_affinity_charge_cancellation}
\end{align}
%
Charge conservation for each mechanism cancels the common electric-potential
term from its traditional affinity.  Electrical effects nevertheless remain in
the constitutive state dependence of \(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\), in the field force
on charged transport, and in electrochemical phase equilibrium.

Substituting \eqref{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_phase_offset} into the
generalized stoichiometric coefficient and using
\eqref{eq:MC_affinity_projection} gives
%
\begin{align*}
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	-
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		-
		\frac{1}{2}
		\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\\
	&=
	\mc A_{(m)}
	-
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		-
		\frac{1}{2}
		\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	.
\end{align*}
%
Solving this identity for the traditional affinity gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	+
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		-
		\frac{1}{2}
		\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}
\end{equation}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation} is an identity
between two representations of the conversion driving force, not an
equilibrium condition.  If the transfer-work term vanishes, it reduces to the
usual generalized-coefficient representation of \(\mc A_{(m)}\); it does not
imply \(\mc A_{(m)}=0\).  Only a separate thermodynamic-equilibrium condition
sets \(\mc A_{(m)}=0\).  The transfer-work term is associated with net mass
insertion into a phase.  It vanishes for a chemical reaction that conserves
mass separately within every phase and also at full reversible equilibrium.
It remains for phase-change, dissolution, precipitation, and pore-filling
mechanisms that move net mass between phases.

For a mechanism confined to one thermal subsystem, the conversion contribution
in \eqref{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection} equals
\(\mc A_{(m)}\dot r_{(m)}/\theta_{\mc G}\) when the mechanism conserves mass
separately within every participating phase.  A phase-changing mechanism
retains the transfer-work term in
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}.  A fluid--solid
mechanism additionally contains generalized transfer-potential contributions
evaluated at two different temperatures.  No single reaction temperature is
assigned; its contribution is retained in the temperature-weighted form
%
\begin{equation}
	-
	\dot r_{(m)}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_temperature_weighted_reaction_power}
\end{equation}
%
This neutral reaction contribution is gauge invariant for every mechanism.
When the stoichiometry conserves charge separately within each thermal
subsystem, the corresponding electrochemical sum is identical.  If a
mechanism transfers net charge between the fluid and solid subsystems, inserting
the electric-potential level separately into the two temperature-weighted sums
would make the result gauge dependent.  Its physical reaction-energy transfer
therefore remains a separate contribution and may be coupled to the neutral
reaction force through the admissibility condition below.  This coupling is
local to the bulk representative volume and does not introduce a sharp
interface.

In the local-thermal-equilibrium limit,
\(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}=\theta\), total charge conservation permits the
common electric-potential term to be restored inside the full stoichiometric
sum, and this expression becomes
%
\begin{align}
	-
	\dot r_{(m)}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	-\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{\theta}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\notag \\
	&=
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{\theta}
	\left[
		\mc A_{(m)}
		-
		\sum_\xi
		\left(
			\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
			-
			\frac{1}{2}
			\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
		\right)
		\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\right].
	\label{eq:MC_reaction_power_local_thermal_equilibrium}
\end{align}
%
For phasewise mass-conserving chemistry, or when the transfer-work offsets
vanish at reversible equilibrium, this is the usual affinity power
\(\mc A_{(m)}\dot r_{(m)}/\theta\).
Thus chemical conversion and interphase energy exchange remain distinct
contributions to the two-temperature entropy inequality, as in Drumheller's
constituent formulation \cite[eq.~(88)]{drumheller2000}.  Their closures may be
uncoupled or Onsager-coupled, subject to the combined dissipation inequality
and the interaction-energy conservation condition below.

The remaining part of
\eqref{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection} is the power of component transport
relative to a phase.  Select the same reference component \(N\) used in the
Onsager calculation.  The separate zero-sum convention
\eqref{eq:separate_relative_flux_sum_closure} converts the full component sum
to \(N-1\) independent potential differences.  The first divergence below is
the neutral component-relative term already present in the entropy flux
\eqref{eq:MC_entropy_inequality}.  The second term is the transport part of
\eqref{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection}.  The third is the direct
relative-current work in \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}.  The product rule
cancels the neutral flux divergence pointwise and leaves the electric-field
work unchanged:
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		 & -
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\frac{\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)
		\right]
		\\
		 & \quad +
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
		\\
		 & \quad -
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\left(
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
				+
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\\
		 & \quad =
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\frac{
					\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
				}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right)
			+
			\frac{
				z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right].
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:relative_transport_product_rule}
\end{equation}
%
Thus no boundary divergence is discarded during localization.  The local
entropy production from relative transport is
%
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right].
	\label{eq:MC_relative_transport_power}
\end{equation}
%
After the zero-sum reduction, the first part is the standard neutral
molecular-diffusion force in nonisothermal mixture thermodynamics
\cite{bothe2015continuum,bothe2023diffusion_fluxes}; the second is the electric
force per temperature.  Because it contains \(\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi=-\mbf E\)
rather than \(\varphi\nabla_{\mbf x}(1/\theta_{\mc G})\), the complete force is
invariant under \(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\).  The present theory
adopts the same force for mechanical dispersion as a constitutive analogy to
the molecular-diffusion force.  At uniform local temperature, the two parts
combine into
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}(\mu_\xi^\alpha-\mu_\xi^N)/\theta_{\mc G}\).
The observer and gauge rules collected in
Appendix~\ref{sec:technical_setting} give the direct transformation check
%
\begin{align}
	&
	\nabla_{\mbf x^\star}
	\left(
		\frac{
			(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha)^\star-(\hat\mu_\xi^N)^\star
		}{\theta_{\mc G}^\star}
	\right)
	+
	\frac{
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	}{\theta_{\mc G}^\star}
	\nabla_{\mbf x^\star}\varphi^\star
	\notag \\
	&\qquad =
	\mbf Q
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right].
	\label{eq:MC_transport_force_gauge_objectivity}
\end{align}
%
The additive function \(c_\varphi(t)\) has no spatial gradient, so it does not
enter either side.

\subsubsection{Residual inequality}
\label{sec:MC_residual_inequality}

Plastic flow is driven by the material stress \(\bs\sigma_s'\), not by the
Maxwell stress counted in the field energy.  Mapping \(\bs\sigma_s'\) through
the current distension and elastic true-deformation factors gives the stress
conjugate to the true-plastic rate.  Because true-plastic flow is isochoric, only
the deviatoric part performs plastic work.  Define that driving stress by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf S_s
	\equiv
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	\left(
	(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	-\frac{1}{3}
	\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right)\mbf I
	\right)
	\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S.
	\label{eq:true_plastic_driving_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
The trace of \(\mbf S_s\) vanishes, so it performs no work on a spherical
plastic rate and is conjugate to the isochoric rate
\(\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:true_plastic_driving_stress_definition} extends
Drumheller's deviatoric true-plastic driving stress through the phase distension
map
\cite[eqs.~(108)--(112)]{drumheller2000}.

Applying the reversible pressure, stress, distension, composition, entropy,
and common pore-volume relations from
\sref{MC_reversible_restrictions} removes their rate coefficients from
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy}.  The saturation-redistribution term from
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_rate_decomposition} remains dissipative.
The component-supply terms
are then replaced by the temperature-weighted reaction and relative-transport
powers derived in \eqref{eq:MC_temperature_weighted_reaction_power} and
\eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_power}.  Gauss' law was already used in the
global field-power identity \eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  The
component charge balance constrains the admissible rates and is recorded by
\eqref{eq:MC_charge_rate_entropy_projection}, but its potential-dependent
terms are not inserted separately.  Charge accumulation and the field rate
therefore do not remain as independent dissipative terms.

The bulk-translation term is removed from the residual only through the
ordering displayed in
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly}.  Substituting that
identity into the complete inequality first cancels the skeleton-rate
free-energy contribution recovered from
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_elastic_distension}--%
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_temperature}.  The remaining member of the pair
is the phase-following rate, whose fluid chain rule is
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_phase_following_free_energy_chain_rule}.  The reversible
relations from \sref{MC_reversible_restrictions} are then substituted into this
reassembled form.  No term in
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_residual} is set to zero by itself, and no
phase-momentum multiplier is invoked to delete it.

This change of rate coordinates does not remove a physical phase force.
Pressure, capillary, Maxwell, body-force, inertia, and conversion-insertion
powers remain in the phase momentum equations, while the observer correction
does not enter a second time as an independent constitutive force.  In
particular, the reassembly does not impose
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\psi_\xi+
\mathfrak{s}_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}=\mbf0\).
The remaining bulk-translation constitutive mechanism is interphase mechanical
interaction, whose dissipated power is converted to the internal-energy
supplies below.  What remains is the residual dissipation inequality
%
\begin{align}
		0\le{}\; &
		\sum_m
		\left[
			-
			\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
				\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
				\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
				\left(
				\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
				\right)
			\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
			\right]\dot r_{(m)}
			\notag\\
		         & +
		\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left(
		\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
		\right)\dot S_f
		\notag\\
		         & -
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\frac{
					\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
				}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right)
			+
			\frac{
				z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right]
		\notag\displaybreak[3]\\
		         & -
		\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
		\cdot\dot{\mbf h}
		\notag\\
		         & +
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\bs\sigma_s':
		\mbf A_s^e
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^p
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		\notag\\
		         & +
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\operatorname{tr}\left[
			\mbf S_s
			\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p
			(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
			\right]
			\notag\\
		         & +
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
			-
			\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
		\frac{
			\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
		}{
			\theta_{\mc G}^2
		}.
	\label{eq:MC_residual_dissipation}
\end{align}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} retains the dynamic-capillary and
capillary-history force--rate products together with the other dissipative
mechanisms.  It
contains no standalone
Helmholtz-energy-gradient force on the bulk phase velocity.  The bulk velocity
is determined by phase momentum balance together with the interphase drag law,
so pressure, capillarity, electrostatics, gravity, inertia, and conversion may
all drive a moving phase.  Chemical-potential differences continue to drive
component-relative diffusion and dispersion through
\eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_power}; they are not removed or absorbed into
the bulk drag.  Thermo-osmotic or other cross effects may be added through an
explicit positive-semidefinite coupled closure, but they are additional
constitutive physics rather than the observer-conversion term in
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity}.

\subsubsection{Dynamic-capillary and capillary-history closures}
\label{sec:MC_capillary_history_closure}

The saturation-rate stationarity statement and its general force--rate closure
are \eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_onsager_stationarity} and
\eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_onsager_relation}.  Substitution into the
saturation term in \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} gives
%
\begin{align}
	&\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
	\right)\dot S_f
	\notag\\
	&\qquad={}
	\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\sum_{f\ne f_\star}\sum_{g\ne f_\star}
	\mc T_{fg}\dot S_f\dot S_g
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_dissipation}
\end{align}
%
For two fluid phases, take \(f_\star=w\) and write
\(\mc T_{nw}\equiv\mc T_{nn}\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:pc_def} and
\eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_force_rate_relation} give
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	=
	p_{c,nw}
	+\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+
	+\mc T_{nw}\dot S_n,
	\qquad
	\mc T_{nw}\ge0,
	\qquad
	(w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:MC_two_phase_dynamic_capillary_pressure}
\end{empheq}
%
Because \(\dot S_n=-\dot S_w\), the final term may equivalently be written
\(-\mc T_{nw}\dot S_w\).  Setting \(\mc T=\mbf0\) recovers the algebraic
electrocapillary relation
\eqref{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery}.  This closure adds
rate dependence to the dissipative pressure response without adding
\(\dot S_f\) to the stored interfacial energy.

The reversible coordinate variation holds \(\mbf h\) fixed, so the
capillary-history state has no Euler--Lagrange equation.  Its skeleton-following
rate is an internal dissipative rate.  When it is uncoupled from the other
residual mechanisms, a sufficient pointwise restriction is
%
\begin{equation}
	0
	\le
	-
	\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\cdot\dot{\mbf h} .
	\label{eq:MC_capillary_history_admissibility}
\end{equation}
%
A material model supplies \(N_h\) evolution equations for \(\mbf h\) that
satisfy \eqref{eq:MC_capillary_history_admissibility}, with
\(\dot{\mbf h}=D_{\mc S}\mbf h/Dt\).  Drainage--imbibition rules may use the
saturation-rate direction to select loading or unloading and store reversal and
trapping data in \(\mbf h\).  Between switching events the state is continuous,
so the capillary pressure may follow primary, secondary, and higher-order
scanning paths \cite{wei2006capillary_hysteresis}.  The same history state may
also enter a separately calibrated relative-permeability closure.
Setting \(N_h=0\), or holding \(\mbf h\) fixed, recovers the single-valued
stored capillary law and removes the history-dissipation term.  The displayed
dynamic and history laws are uncoupled.  A joint Onsager block coupling
\(\dot S_f\) and \(\dot{\mbf h}\) is admissible when its symmetric resistance
is positive semidefinite; loading--unloading switches may instead retain the
present nonsmooth history rule.  Coupled capillary, transport, and thermal
kinetics are admissible when their combined force--rate form remains
nonnegative.

\subsubsection{Reaction and relative-transport closures}
\label{sec:MC_reaction_transport_closures}

The source-carried energy in
\eqref{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_energy} accounts for the energy of
mass inserted into each recipient phase.  Any additional reaction-associated
internal-energy supply only reallocates energy among phases and must conserve
mixture energy mechanism by mechanism:
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)}
		 & =0,
		\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_mixture} \\
		\left.
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)}
		 & =
		-
		\left.
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)} .
		\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_subsystems}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
For a mechanism confined to one thermal subsystem, the reaction-associated
allocation may be set to zero because the source-carried terms already enter
that subsystem balance.  For a mechanism spanning the two thermal subsystems,
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_subsystems} is a constitutive allocation
of latent or reaction heat, not a third energy balance.

Inequality \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} is the admissibility condition;
it does not prescribe a unique kinetic law.  The quadratic Onsager potential in
\sref{onsager_reaction_transport} supplies the following uncoupled
near-equilibrium choice for any mechanism:
%
\begin{equation}
	\dot r_{(m)}
	=
	K_{(m)}
	\left[
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right],
	\qquad
	K_{(m)}>0 .
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate}
\end{equation}
%
The force in \eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate} is gauge invariant.  For a
mechanism that conserves charge separately within each thermal subsystem, it
may equivalently be evaluated with electrochemical coefficients.  A mechanism
that transfers charge between the thermal subsystems may couple its reaction
rate to the physical reaction-energy transfer, but the pair must satisfy the
mechanismwise condition
%
\begin{align}
	0\le{}&
	\left[
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\right]\dot r_{(m)}
	\notag \\
	&+
	\left(
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		-
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)
	\left.
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)} .
	\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}
\end{align}
%
The second term follows directly from the equal-and-opposite allocation in
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_subsystems}.  A positive-semidefinite
Onsager matrix acting on these two force--rate pairs is one coupled closure;
block-diagonal choices are also admissible when each term is nonnegative.
Both forces are gauge invariant in the direct-field energy representation.

Stationarity with respect to the dispersion and diffusion fluxes gives
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
	+
	\theta_{\mc G}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
		\frac{
			\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\right)
	+
	\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	 & =\mbf 0,
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_dispersion_stationarity}
	\\
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
	+
	\theta_{\mc G}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
		\frac{
			\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\right)
	+
	\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	 & =\mbf 0,
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N.
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_diffusion_stationarity}
\end{align}
%
The diffusion row is the inverse-mobility form of the reduced
\(N-1\)-component Fick--Onsager closure
\cite{bothe2015continuum,bothe2023diffusion_fluxes}.  For charged species,
Dreyer, Guhlke, and M{\"u}ller write the chemical-potential gradient and
electric force separately; under isothermal electrostatics their force combines
into the electrochemical-potential difference used here
\cite{dreyer2018bulk_surface_electrothermodynamics}.  The nonisothermal closure
keeps those two parts separate: the neutral potential is divided by
\(\theta_{\mc G}\) before differentiation, whereas the electric force is
\((z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N)\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\).  This ordering is required
for electric-gauge invariance.

Solving these relations in the \(N-1\) independent flux coordinates gives
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	 & =-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\beta-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
			z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:MC_dispersion_closure}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	 & =-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\beta-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
			z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:MC_diffusion_closure}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,k}^N
	 & =
	-\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha,
	\qquad
	k\in\{{\rm disp},{\rm diff}\}.
	\label{eq:MC_relative_transport_closures}
\end{align}
%
The final row reconstructs the reference-component flux and enforces the
separate zero-sum specialization
\eqref{eq:separate_relative_flux_sum_closure}.  The reduced mobilities and
their inverses act only on the \(N-1\) independent component coordinates.
Consequently the laws use neutral-potential differences and the electric field
and are invariant under both a common phasewise neutral-potential shift and
\(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\).  At uniform temperature they reduce to
the familiar electrochemical-potential-gradient form.
Substitution of
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate} and
\eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_closures} into the reaction and transport part
of \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} gives
%
\begin{align}
	 & \sum_m
	\left[
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
			\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
			\left(
			\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
			\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]\dot r_{(m)}
	\notag \\
	 & \quad -
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right]
	\notag           \\
	 & \qquad=
	\sum_m
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}^2}{K_{(m)}}
	\notag           \\
	 & \qquad\quad +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
	\notag           \\
	 & \qquad\quad +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
	\ge0.
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_transport_dissipation}
\end{align}
%
Thus the reaction and relative-transport terms are dissipative when each
\(\mbf D_{\xi,k}\) is symmetric positive definite on the \(N-1\) independent
component coordinates.  Diffusion and dispersion may use
different mobilities, but both are driven here by the same
gauge-invariant nonisothermal chemical and electric force; the neutral limit is
obtained by setting \(z_\xi^\alpha=0\).  Dispersion may depend on resolved flow
and pore geometry~\cite{plumb1988dispersion,gray2009tcat_transport}.  Reaction,
diffusion, and dispersion may also be cross-coupled, provided their combined
force--rate form remains nonnegative~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.
When reaction and reaction-energy transfer are cross-coupled, their additional
block must satisfy
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}.

\subsubsection{Plastic-flow closures}
\label{sec:MC_plastic_flow_closures}

The two plastic powers in \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} admit the following
uncoupled associated closures, analogous to Drumheller's sufficient
construction~\cite{drumheller2000}:
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_flow_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}(\mc X_s)
		(\mbf S_s)^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_deformation_flow_rule}            \\
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^p(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}(\mc X_s)
		(\mbf A_s^e)^T
		\bs\sigma_s'
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-T},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_distension_flow_rule}             \\
		\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}(\mc X_s)
		 & \ge0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_deformation_mobility_nonnegative} \\
		\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}(\mc X_s)
		 & \ge0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_distension_mobility_nonnegative}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Because \(\operatorname{tr}\mbf S_s=0\), the first closure preserves
isochoric true-plastic flow.  The nonnegative scalar mobilities make both
plastic powers nonnegative separately.  Their dependence on the state set
\(\mc X_s\) permits composition-dependent mobilities.  Hardening, softening,
and other history dependence require additional internal variables and
evolution equations beyond the state set in \eqref{eq:MC_state_sets}.
Component-resolved mobilities are given in the later additive specialization.
Coupled or non-associated
plastic deformation--distension laws may replace
\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_flow_rules}, but their combined conjugate power in
\eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} must remain nonnegative.

\subsubsection{Interphase mechanical interaction}
\label{sec:MC_interphase_mechanical_interaction}

The component-relative diffusion and dispersion fluxes were closed in
\sref{MC_reaction_transport_closures}.  The interaction considered here is
different: it is the momentum exchange caused by relative motion of the bulk
phases.  Conservation of total mixture energy requires the phase interaction
supplies associated with mechanical momentum exchange to satisfy
\cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
	\left.
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm mech}
	+\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	=0 .
	\label{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\mbf b_\xi\) is the momentum supplied to phase \(\xi\), while
\(\left.\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}\right\vert_{\rm mech}\) is the associated
internal-energy supply.  The full \(e_\xi^{\rm int}\) in the subsystem energy
balance is the sum of this mechanical contribution, ordinary heat exchange, and
any reaction-associated interphase energy transfer.  The reversible solid and
pore-volume constraint powers are absent because their coefficients have
already vanished through \eqref{eq:MC_volume_fraction_restrictions}; the
saturation-redistribution power remains separately in
\eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_dissipation}.
Because the free-energy convection term has been recombined with the
phase-following reversible rate, the entropy contribution of this mechanism is
the temperature-weighted mechanical internal-energy supply.  When it is closed
separately from reaction, plasticity, component-relative transport, and heat
conduction, its sufficient thermodynamic admissibility condition is
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\left.
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm mech}
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility}
\end{equation}
%
The cited mixture theories support different parts of this construction and
should not be read as printing
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} verbatim.  Drumheller and
Bedford's two-temperature inequality couples interaction-energy transfer to
relative mechanical power
\cite[eq.~(4.8)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}; together with their total
energy balance, this is the antecedent for requiring
\eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} and temperature-weighted
interaction heating simultaneously.  Gray's general porous-medium inequalities
contain solid-relative velocities paired with combined interaction and
thermodynamic forces
\cite[eqs.~(33), (37), (44), and (47)]{gray1999thermodynamics}; after the
constitutive reductions, Gray's slow-flow example uses the pressure-gradient
phase momentum equation rather than a separate Helmholtz-gradient restriction
\cite[eq.~(77)]{gray1999thermodynamics}.

Seguin and Walkington provide the closest common-temperature analogue.  Their
unreduced inequality contains a relative-velocity bracket
\cite[eq.~(15)]{seguin2019multicomponent}; the elastic-force and thermodynamic
identities in their equations (33) and (35) remove its reversible part, leaving
only the viscous relative-force product in their equation (32).  Their equations
(37), (40), and (41) then give a pressure-driven force balance, a positive drag
law, and Darcy flow, respectively
\cite[eqs.~(32), (33), (35), (37), (40), and (41)]{seguin2019multicomponent}.
Bothe and Dreyer's equations (88)--(93) make a related combined-force
rearrangement for constituent diffusion in a common-temperature miscible fluid
\cite[eqs.~(88)--(93)]{bothe2015continuum}.  That last comparison concerns
constituent diffusion and Gibbs--Duhem reduction, not porous-phase Darcy flow.

Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} and the
energy-conservation identity \eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} must
hold simultaneously.  A more general Onsager model may couple mechanical
interaction heating to heat transfer or reaction; then nonnegativity is required
only of the coupled block rather than of each mechanism separately.

In a general multiphase closure, the momentum supply may be written as the sum
of antisymmetric pairwise forces,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_\xi
	=
	\sum_{\zeta\ne\xi}
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}
	=
	-\mbf b_{\xi\zeta},
	\label{eq:MC_pairwise_interaction_force}
\end{equation}
%
where \(\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}\) is the force exerted on phase \(\xi\) by phase
\(\zeta\).  The pairwise mechanical power is therefore
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\xi<\zeta}
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}\cdot
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_\zeta
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_interaction_power_pairing}
\end{equation}
%
For a concrete reservoir-oriented closure, retain fluid--skeleton exchange,
use the common solid velocity, and neglect direct fluid--fluid drag.  The
fluid and aggregate solid momentum supplies are prescribed by
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_law}
	\begin{align}
		\mbf b_f
		={}&
		-\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right),
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F,
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_force}
		\\
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\mbf b_s
		={}&
		-\sum_{f\in\mc F}\mbf b_f,
		\label{eq:MC_aggregate_solid_interaction_force}
		\\
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}
		={}&
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^T>0,
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F .
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_drag_tensor}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_force} is a purely dissipative
drag law.  Reversible pressure, capillary, Maxwell, body-force, inertia, and
conversion-insertion terms remain in the phase momentum balance and are not
duplicated inside \(\mbf b_f\).  Here
\(\mbf y\cdot\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}\mbf y>0\) for every
\(\mbf y\ne\mbf 0\).  Because all solid phases have velocity
\(\mbf v_{\mc S}\), \eqref{eq:MC_aggregate_solid_interaction_force} is
sufficient to determine the total solid interaction power.  A constituent
model may partition the aggregate solid supply among the solid phases without
changing the following result.

The associated internal-energy supplies are closed by
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_drag_heating_equal_partition}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		={}&
		\frac{1}{2}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right),
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F,
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_interaction_energy_supply}
		\\
		\left.
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		={}&
		\frac{1}{2}
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right).
		\label{eq:MC_solid_interaction_energy_supply}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Thus the nonnegative drag heating is divided equally between the fluid and solid
thermal subsystems.  This equal partition is a sufficient closure, not a
required microscopic statement about where viscous heating occurs.  Any other
partition is admissible if it satisfies both
\eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} and
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility}.  Summing
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_interaction_energy_supply} and
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_interaction_energy_supply}, and using
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_force} and
\eqref{eq:MC_aggregate_solid_interaction_force}, gives
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\left.
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		+
		\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	={}&
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left[
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)
		+
		\mbf b_f\cdot
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
			\right)
	\right]
	=0 .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_energy_verification}
\end{align}
%
Hence \eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} is satisfied exactly.
Substitution of the same energy supplies into
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} leaves
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		+
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)
	\left(
		\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot
	\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
	\left(
		\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_positive}
\end{equation}
%
This proves the joint admissibility condition for arbitrary skeleton velocity:
only the relative velocities enter.  More general positive-semidefinite block
closures may retain direct fluid--fluid drag or couple this mechanism to heat
transfer, but \eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_law}--
\eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_positive} provide one complete local closure.

\subsubsection{Thermal exchange and heat conduction}
\label{sec:MC_thermal_exchange_conduction}

Ordinary heat exchange transfers internal energy between the fluid and solid
thermal subsystems even when there is no phase-relative motion.  It is distinct
from the drag-generated heating above and from the reaction-associated energy
exchange required by a charged cross-subsystem mechanism.  The latter remains
paired with \(\dot r_{(m)}\) in the coupled closure described after
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate}; it is not included in the heat-transfer
coefficient below.

A sufficient bulk fluid--solid heat-exchange law is
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left.
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm heat}
		 & =
		h_{\mc F\mc S}
		\left(
		\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
		\right),
		\\
		\left.
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm heat}
		 & =
		-h_{\mc F\mc S}
		\left(
		\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
		\right),
		\qquad
		h_{\mc F\mc S}\ge0 .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_law}
\end{equation}
%
Together, the mechanical, ordinary-heat, and reaction-associated allocations
give the explicit mixture cancellation
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		+
		\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	={}&
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\left.
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		+
		\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	\notag \\
	&+
	\left.
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm heat}
	+
	\left.
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm heat}
	\notag \\
	&+
	\sum_m
	\left.
	\sum_\xi
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)}
	\notag \\
	={}&0 .
	\label{eq:MC_total_internal_exchange_cancellation}
\end{align}
%
The first group vanishes by
\eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation}, the second by
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_law}, and each term in the final sum by
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_mixture}.  Adding the global field-power
identity \eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity} then completes the
material-plus-field first-law accounting.  The ordinary heat exchange
contributes
%
\begin{equation}
	h_{\mc F\mc S}
	\left(
	\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
	\right)
	\left(
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	-
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)
	=
	h_{\mc F\mc S}
	\frac{
		\left(
		\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
		\right)^2
	}{
		\theta_{\mc F}\theta_{\mc S}
	}
	\ge0
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_positive}
\end{equation}
%
to the entropy production.

The simplest uncoupled closure represents the gauge-invariant material part of
the nonadvective energy flux within phase \(\xi\in\mc G\) by Fourier's law,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf q_\xi
	=
	-\mbf K_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G},
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\mbf K_\xi=\mbf K_\xi^T\ge0 ,
	\label{eq:MC_heat_flux_law}
\end{equation}
%
where
\(\mbf y\cdot\mbf K_\xi\mbf y\ge0\) for every \(\mbf y\), and
\(\theta_{\mc F},\theta_{\mc S}>0\).  More general closures may couple
\(\mbf q_\xi\) to the component-relative thermodynamic forces, provided the
combined heat--transport Onsager block remains positive semidefinite.  The
uncoupled closure gives the entropy contribution
%
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\frac{
		\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	}{
		\theta_{\mc G}^2
	}
	=
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\frac{
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
		\cdot
		\mbf K_\xi
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	}{
		\theta_{\mc G}^2
	}
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_heat_flux_positive}
\end{equation}
%
The reaction and component-relative transport terms are controlled by
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_transport_dissipation}; the fluid--skeleton
interaction closure reduces the mechanical-interaction condition
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} to the nonnegative form
\eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_positive}; and ordinary heat exchange and
conduction are controlled by
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_positive} and
\eqref{eq:MC_heat_flux_positive}.  These are separate closures even though
their entropy contributions appear in the same residual inequality.

\subsection[Example mechanisms: reactions, phase transformations,
	sorption/desorption, and ion exchange]
{\raggedright Example mechanisms: reactions, phase transformations,
	sorption/desorption, and ion exchange}
\label{sec:MC_example_mechanisms}

The mechanism index \((m)\) covers any local conversion described by the
phase/component stoichiometry \(\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\).  The principal mechanism
types differ only in whether conversion changes the component identity, the
phase label, or both.

A true chemical reaction changes component identity.  It may occur entirely
within one phase or involve components from several phases.  For example,
aqueous carbonic-acid dissociation,
\(\mathrm{H_2CO_3}\rightleftharpoons
\mathrm{H^+}+\mathrm{HCO_3^-}\), changes the aqueous component populations
without transferring a component to another phase.  On a molar progress basis,
the signed molar coefficients are \((-1,+1,+1)\).  If \(M_\alpha\) denotes
component molar mass, the mass coefficients used in the balance and affinity
are
%
\begin{align}
	\left(
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{H^+}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}
	\right)
	&=
	\left(
	-M_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}},
	M_{\mathrm{H^+}},
	M_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}
	\right),
	\notag \\
	\sum_\alpha\nu_{f (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	-M_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}
	+
	M_{\mathrm{H^+}}
	+
	M_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}
	=0,
	\notag \\
	\frac{1}{F_{\rm Far}}
	\sum_\alpha
	z_f^\alpha\nu_{f (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	0+1-1
	=0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_carbonic_acid_mass_charge_checks}
\end{align}
%
where
\(M_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}=
M_{\mathrm{H^+}}+M_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}\) and \(F_{\rm Far}\) is the Faraday
constant.  Thus both mass and charge conservation are explicit before these
coefficients enter the affinity and kinetic relations.

A pure phase transformation preserves component identity and changes only its
phase label.  Vaporization and condensation are examples.  For a component
\(g\) transferred from phase \(\xi\) to phase \(\zeta\), the one-to-one
stoichiometry below uses a mass-progress basis: \(\dot r_{(m)}\) is mass
transferred per current mixture volume per time, and the dimensionless mass
yields are
%
\begin{equation}
	\nu_{\zeta (m)}^g=1,
	\qquad
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^g=-1 .
	\label{eq:MC_one_to_one_transfer_stoichiometry}
\end{equation}
%
All other stoichiometric coefficients vanish.  The corresponding affinity is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	\mu_\xi^g-\mu_\zeta^g .
	\label{eq:MC_one_to_one_transfer_affinity}
\end{equation}
%
The transformation can therefore change phase fractions and phase
compositions even though the total mass of component \(g\) is unchanged.  For
the same charged component in both phases, charge conservation gives
\(z_\xi^g=z_\zeta^g\), so the explicit electric-potential terms cancel.
The conversion power additionally contains the difference of the two
phase-transfer offsets according to
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}; it vanishes at
reversible phase equilibrium but need not vanish during a dynamic
transformation with relative phase motion.

Sorption/desorption is treated by the same one-to-one transfer structure, with
\(\xi=f\) a mobile phase and \(\zeta=s\) a solid phase whose constituents share
the skeleton motion.  It includes surface adsorption and bulk absorption
without requiring those pore-scale processes to be represented separately.
For a neutral component \(g\), away from local thermal equilibrium an
admissible kinetic law must satisfy
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\frac{
	\psi_f+L_f^g
	}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	-
	\frac{
	\psi_s+L_s^g
	}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)\dot r_{(m)}
	\ge0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_sorption_temperature_weighted_dissipation}
\end{equation}
%
Charged sorption that transfers net charge between the thermal subsystems uses
the same gauge-invariant neutral reaction force and must satisfy
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}; its reaction and
reaction-energy rates may be Onsager-coupled.
The sorbed amount \(\eta_s^g\) may also enter \(\psi_s\), \(\mbf A_s^0\),
\(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0\), and the plastic flow law.  Thus adsorption, desorption,
hydrate formation, and hydrate dissociation can change stiffness, swelling,
compaction, and other solid properties through the evolving composition of the
load-bearing phase.

\pagebreak[3]
Ion exchange is a coupled set of transfers between mobile ions and charged
solid-site components.  For example, calcium uptake accompanied by sodium
release can be represented by
\(2\,\mathrm{NaX}_s+\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}_f
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{CaX_2}_s+2\,\mathrm{Na}^{+}_f\), where
\(\mathrm{X^-}\) denotes a monovalent solid exchange site, so
\(\mathrm{NaX}_s\) and \(\mathrm{CaX_2}_s\) are neutral solid components.  The
signed molar coefficients in the order
\((\mathrm{NaX}_s,\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}_f,
\mathrm{CaX_2}_s,\mathrm{Na}^{+}_f)\) are
\((-2,-1,+1,+2)\).  The corresponding mass coefficients and conservation
checks are
%
\begin{align}
	\left(
	\nu_{s (m)}^{\mathrm{NaX}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}},
	\nu_{s (m)}^{\mathrm{CaX_2}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{Na^+}}
	\right)
	&=
	\left(
	-2M_{\mathrm{NaX}},
	-M_{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}},
	M_{\mathrm{CaX_2}},
	2M_{\mathrm{Na^+}}
	\right),
	\notag \\
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	-2M_{\mathrm{NaX}}
	-M_{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}}
	+
	M_{\mathrm{CaX_2}}
	+
	2M_{\mathrm{Na^+}}
	=0,
	\notag \\
	\frac{1}{F_{\rm Far}}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	z_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	0-2+0+2
	=0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_ion_exchange_mass_charge_checks}
\end{align}
%
Here \(M_{\mathrm{NaX}}=M_{\mathrm{Na^+}}+M_{\mathrm X}\) and
\(M_{\mathrm{CaX_2}}=M_{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}}+2M_{\mathrm X}\).  The mechanism
therefore changes fluid composition and solid-site occupancy while satisfying
both conservation restrictions.  Its driving force is the corresponding
electrochemical affinity; equilibrium or kinetic ion-exchange models provide
the remaining closure.

\subsection{Phase reaction-modified Biot coefficients}
\label{sec:MC_phase_biot_legendre_transform}

The entropy inequality defines the single-prime solid stress using intrinsic
density, rather than pressure, as a state variable.  Reservoir and
poromechanics equations are more naturally written at fixed equivalent pore
pressure.  A phase-level Legendre transform exchanges intrinsic specific volume
for \(\bar p_E\) and defines the corresponding double-prime stress
\cite{callen1985thermodynamics}.  This transformation is performed for the
general phase energy before the optional additive component specialization.

Define the phase and intrinsic specific volumes
%
\begin{equation}
	v_s=\frac{1}{\rho_s},
	\qquad
	v_{s0}=\frac{1}{\rho_{s0}},
	\qquad
	\bar v_s=\frac{1}{\bar\rho_s},
	\qquad
	\bar v_{s0}=\frac{1}{\bar\rho_{s0}},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_specific_volumes}
\end{equation}
%
The solid volume-fraction restriction relates \(\bar p_E\) to the solid phase
pressure and electric enthalpy.  In terms of intrinsic specific volume,
%
\begin{align}
	\bar p_E
	 & =
	\bar p_s-\omega_s^+
	\notag \\
	 & =
	-
	\left.
	\p{
		\left(
		\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
		\right)
	}{\bar v_s}
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pressure_specific_volume}
\end{align}
%
Here \(\bar v_s=1/\bar\rho_s\), so
\(\omega_s^+\bar v_s\) is the electric enthalpy per unit intrinsic solid mass.
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_phase_pressure_specific_volume} identifies the partial
derivative with respect to \(\bar v_s\), with the other state variables fixed,
as \(-\bar p_E\).  Consequently, the corresponding term in the total
differential is \(-\bar p_E\,d\bar v_s\).  Adding
\(\bar p_E\bar v_s\) cancels this term:
%
\begin{align}
	\left.
	d\left(
	\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	-\bar p_E\,d\bar v_s,
	\notag \\
	\left.
	d\left(
	\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s+\bar p_E\bar v_s
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	\bar v_s\,d\bar p_E,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_differential_cancellation}
\end{align}
%
Thus \(\bar p_E\), rather than \(\bar p_s\) separately, is conjugate to
\(\bar v_s\), with
\(\bar p_s=\bar p_E+\omega_s^+\).  The transformed phase potential is
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_transform}
	\begin{gather}
		\widetilde\psi_s
		=
		\psi_s
		+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
		+\bar p_E\bar v_s,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_transform_definition} \\
		\bar v_s
		=
		\left.
		\p{\widetilde\psi_s}{\bar p_E}
		\right\vert_{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_transform_pressure_derivative}
	\end{gather}
\end{subequations}
%
Changing the elastic true deformation changes \(\bar v_s\) through the true
Jacobian in \(J=a_s\bar J_s\).  The required chain rule follows from the
material mass balance \eqref{eq:material_mass_2} and true-solid mass
conservation \eqref{eq:MC_solid_true_mass_conservation}.  The derivative is
evaluated at the current distension, plastic, and stress-free state while
holding equivalent pressure, electric field, solid temperature, and composition
fixed,
%
\begin{align}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
	 & =
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
	\frac{\partial J}{\partial\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\notag \\
	 & =
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
	a_s\bar J_s
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-T},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_specific_volume_deformation_chain_rule}
\end{align}
%
The Legendre identity recovers the original single-prime Piola stress by
differentiating the transformed potential at fixed \(\bar p_E\).  The
double-prime Piola stress is instead the derivative of the untransformed
electrothermodynamic phase potential
\(\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s\) along a fixed-\(\bar p_E\) path.  Accordingly,
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pk_stresses}
	\begin{align}
		\mbf P_s'
		 & =
		\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{\widetilde\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\mbf A_s^e,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_phase_single_prime_pk_stress} \\
		\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{\widetilde\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\mbf A_s^e,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
		 & =
		\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar v_s,\mbf A_s^e,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},
			\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_pk_identity}   \\
		\mbf P_s^{\prime\prime}
		 & =
		\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{
			\left(
			\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
			\right)
		}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\mbf A_s^e,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_phase_double_prime_pk_stress}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Differentiating the Legendre transform at fixed \(\bar p_E\) gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf P_s'
	=
	\mbf P_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\bar p_E\rho_{s0}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pk_legendre_split}
\end{equation}
%
Using the specific-volume chain rule, define the phase nonlinear Biot
coefficient by
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	B_s
	\equiv
	1
	-
	\frac{a_s}{v_{s0}}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_biot_coefficient}
\end{empheq}
%
The coefficient \(B_s\) measures how the true solid volume of phase \(s\)
changes with the common skeleton volume at the current reacted state.  The
factor \(a_s\) carries the current allocation of skeleton volume to that phase,
including packing and stress-free volume change.  If the distension split is
suppressed, \(B_s\) reduces to the nonlinear Biot coefficient of
\cite{foster2025}.  The phase Piola stresses are related by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf P_s'
	=
	\mbf P_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\left(1-B_s\right)
	\bar p_E
	\bar J_s
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-T},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pk_biot_split}
\end{equation}
%
Pushing these stresses to the current configuration and applying the
distension map from \eqref{eq:MC_solid_stress_definition} converts the pressure
correction into a Cauchy stress.  Therefore
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\left(1-B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_biot_split}
\end{empheq}
%
This relation holds for a general composition-dependent phase energy.  The
optional common-pressure specialization in
\sref{MC_additive_component_specialization} later decomposes \(B_s\) and
\(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}\) into component contributions only under its
stated component-EOS path.

\subsection{Solid phase and overall momentum after the Biot transform}
\label{sec:MC_phase_overall_momentum_biot}

The solid volume-fraction restriction gives the phase pressure in terms of the
equivalent pore pressure \(\bar p_E=-\lambda\),
%
\begin{align}
	\bar p_E
	 & =
	-\lambda
	\notag \\
	 & =
	\bar p_s-\omega_s^+,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_equivalent_pressure}
\end{align}
%
Substituting the phase stress split into the solid momentum balance gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\rho_s\mbf a_s
		={} &
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
			+
			\left(1-B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I
			+
			\bs\sigma_s^+
			\right]
		-\phi_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_s\omega_s^+\right)
		+\rho_s\mbf g+\mbf b_s
		\\
		    & +
		\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_s^\alpha
		\left(
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_s
		\right),
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_phase_momentum_biot}
\end{equation}
%
Plastic flow remains conjugate to \(\bs\sigma_s'\).  A constitutive model
calibrated with the fixed-pressure stress
\(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}\) must first use
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_split} to reconstruct \(\bs\sigma_s'\).  Maxwell stress
enters the momentum balance separately and affects plastic response indirectly
through the resulting deformation and material stress.

After the solid partial stresses are summed, the solid-phase Biot terms combine
as
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s'
	 & =
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\left(1-B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I,
	\label{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_expanded}   \\
	\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}
	 & \equiv
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime},
	\label{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_definition} \\
	\Aboxed{
		B
	 & =
		1-\sum_{s\in\mc S}\left(1-B_s\right)
	}
	.
	\label{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_coefficient}
\end{align}
%
Because the phase Piola stresses are all measured per unit skeleton reference
volume, their pressure corrections add directly.  The aggregate coefficient is
therefore the combination in
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_coefficient}; in the single-solid limit it
reduces to \(B=B_s\).

After summing the solid phase balances, the Biot-pressure and electrical parts in
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_phase_momentum_biot} can be rewritten using
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_definition},
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		 & \sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left(
			\left(1-B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I
			+\bs\sigma_s^+
			\right)
			-\phi_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
			-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_s\omega_s^+\right)
			\right]
		\\
		 & \qquad =
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			-B\bar p_E\mbf I
			+
			\sum_{s\in\mc S}
			\phi_s\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_s
			\right]
		+
		\left(
		1-\sum_{s\in\mc S}\phi_s
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:solid_biot_pressure_remainder_rollup}
\end{equation}
%
Using the volume-fraction constraint, the coefficient of
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E\) in the summed solid balance is the total fluid
volume fraction \(\phi=\sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f\).

For the summed fluid balance, apply the fluid stress restriction
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_stress_free} to
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary}.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:saturation_weighted_primitive_potential_sum}, using
\(\sum_f\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f=\mbf0\), and applying the saturation-derivative
convention stated after \eqref{eq:interfacial_internal_energy} gives the
interfacial state-gradient identity
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	S_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\gamma_f
	=
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
	+
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}.
	\label{eq:interfacial_state_gradient_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Thus, writing \(\phi_f=\phi S_f\), the summed fluid pressure and electrical
terms give
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
			\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
		\right)
		-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\bar p_f-\omega_f^+
		\right)
	\right]
	={}&
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
	\right)
	-\phi\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\notag\\
	&
	+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\bar p_E-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_pressure_gradient_rollup}
\end{align}
%
The final term follows from the common pore-volume restriction
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_restriction}.  It vanishes in the reversible
limit and carries the dynamic capillary pressure through the summed momentum
balance.

Adding the solid and fluid pressure terms and using
\(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi=1\) and
\(\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\) gives
%
\begin{align}
	& \sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\left\{
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\left(1-B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I
			+\bs\sigma_s^+
			\right]
		-\phi_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_s\omega_s^+\right)
		\right\}
		\notag\\
	& \quad
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left(
				\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
			\right)
			-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\bar p_f-\omega_f^+
			\right)
		\right]
		\notag\\
	& \qquad =
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			-B\bar p_E\mbf I
			+\mbf E\otimes\mbf d
		\right]
		\notag\\
	& \qquad
		-\phi
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
		\notag\\
	& \qquad
		-\phi
		\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
		\notag\\
	& \qquad
		+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left(
		\bar p_f-\bar p_E-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f .
	\label{eq:MC_pressure_gradient_overall_rollup}
\end{align}
%
The pairwise interaction forces cancel when all phase momentum balances are
summed.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:MC_pressure_gradient_overall_rollup} then gives the overall mixture
momentum balance
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	\sum_\xi\rho_\xi\mbf a_\xi
	={}&
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left[
		\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}
		-
		B\bar p_E\mbf I
		+
		\mbf E\otimes\mbf d
	\right]
	+\rho\mbf g
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\notag\\
	&
	+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\bar p_E-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f
	\notag\\
	&
	+\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_\xi
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot}
\end{empheq}
%
The corresponding overall natural traction is
%
\begin{equation}
	\left[
		\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}
		-
		B\bar p_E\mbf I
		+
		\mbf E\otimes\mbf d
	\right]\mbf n
	=
	\bar{\mbf t}
	\qquad
	\text{on }\Gamma_t .
	\label{eq:overall_natural_traction}
\end{equation}
%
The thermocapillary, capillary-history-gradient, dynamic-capillary, and
conversion terms in
\eqref{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot} remain bulk sources and do not
modify \eqref{eq:overall_natural_traction} unless an additional external-surface
model is introduced.

The term proportional to
\(\left.\partial\gamma/\partial\theta_{\mc F}\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}\)
is the interfacial Gibbs--Duhem thermal contribution generated by the bulk
free-energy aggregation.  Indeed,
\(-\left.\partial\gamma/\partial\theta_{\mc F}\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}\)
is the interfacial entropy per current fluid volume, so the sign agrees with
the bulk Gibbs--Duhem identity
\cite[eq.~(59)]{bothe2015continuum}.  The contribution is therefore a bulk
thermocapillary analogue, not a separately postulated sharp-interface
Marangoni traction.  The latter has the tangential form
\(\nabla_s\sigma\) and requires an interfacial normal, surface projector, and
surface momentum balance
\cite{jasnow1996thermocapillary,guo2015thermocapillary}; none of those
geometric fields belongs to the present averaged state set.  The bulk term
vanishes in the isothermal limit or when the interfacial potential has no
explicit temperature dependence.  The capillary-history-gradient term records
spatial variation of the drainage--imbibition state and vanishes when
\(\mbf h\) is spatially uniform or \(\gamma\) is independent of it.
The dynamic-capillary term vanishes when \(\mc T=\mbf0\) or when the
saturation field is spatially uniform.

\subsection{Additive solid component specialization}
\label{sec:MC_additive_component_specialization}

The general phase energy need not be additive.  An additive representation is
useful, however, when components share one solid motion but their stored-energy
contributions can be distinguished.  Examples include adsorbates and bound
water in a mineral framework, lattice substitutions or defects, and guest
occupancy in hydrate cages.  All components still use the same solid velocity,
\(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\), and \(\mbf A_s^e\); the split applies only to the stored
energy.  Each component potential may depend on the full composition, so the
model can retain nonideal interactions.  When no meaningful component-energy
split exists, the general phase energy
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_general_free_energy} remains the appropriate model.
The component decomposition below retains the phase electric enthalpy
\(\omega_s^+\) and the equivalent pressure
\(\bar p_E=\bar p_s-\omega_s^+\).  Electrical storage remains a phase-level
constitutive quantity governed by
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_pressure_specific_volume}--\eqref{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot}.

Introduce component potentials \(\psi_s^\alpha\), intrinsic densities
\(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\), and pressures \(\bar p_s^\alpha\).  The additive phase
energy is
%
\begin{equation}
	\psi_s
	=
	\sum_\alpha
	\eta_s^\alpha
	\psi_s^\alpha(
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
	\bar\rho_s^\alpha,\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S}),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_free_energy}
\end{equation}
%
Then the pressure average, stress, and distension restrictions become
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_mixture_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\bar p_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_s^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_pressure_average} \\
		\bs\sigma_s'
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\alpha},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_stress_sum}         \\
		\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\alpha}
		 & =
		\rho_s^\alpha
		\mbf A_s^{-T}
		\left(
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
		\right)
		\mbf A_s^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_component_stress}   \\
		\bs\sigma_s'
		 & =
		\rho_s
		\sum_\alpha
		\eta_s^\alpha
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\mbf A_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_distension_force}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The storage multipliers provide the same component pressures.  Since
\(\sum_\alpha\pi_s^\alpha/\phi_s=\bar p_s\), the additive model assigns
component \(\alpha\) the pressure share
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\pi_s^\alpha}{\phi_s}
	=
	\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\bar p_s^\alpha
	=
	\frac{\pi_s^\alpha}{\phi_s\phi_s^\alpha},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:additive_component_pressure_multiplier_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Summing over components recovers the phase pressure.  Thus
\(\pi_s^\alpha\) and \(\phi_s^\alpha\) determine
\(\bar p_s^\alpha\) without introducing another pressure closure in the general
additive energy representation.  The common-pressure path used for the optional
component Biot decomposition below is an additional mechanical equilibrium
specialization; through
\eqref{eq:additive_component_pressure_multiplier_identity}, it restricts the
shares to
\(\pi_s^\alpha/\phi_s=\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_s\).

Only phase plastic rates appear in the residual inequality, so the components
do not have independent plastic kinematics.  Composition can nevertheless
change the common phase rates through component contributions to their
mobilities.  One sufficient form is
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_component_resolved_plastic_flow_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\left(
		\sum_\alpha
		\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}^\alpha(\mc X_s)
		\right)
		(\mbf S_s)^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_component_true_plastic_flow_rule}       \\
		\dot{\mbf A}_s^p(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\left(
		\sum_\alpha
		\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}^\alpha(\mc X_s)
		\right)
		(\mbf A_s^e)^T
		\bs\sigma_s'
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-T},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_component_distension_plastic_flow_rule} \\
		\sum_\alpha
		\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}^\alpha(\mc X_s)
		 & \ge0,
		\qquad
		\sum_\alpha
		\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}^\alpha(\mc X_s)
		\ge0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_component_plastic_mobilities}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The component mobilities sum to the mobility of the common solid plastic rate.
Nonnegative component mobilities make each contribution dissipative.  More
general composition-dependent flow directions are admissible provided their
combined plastic power remains nonnegative.

\subsubsection{General additive component Biot decomposition}
\label{sec:MC_component_biot_derivation}

The phase electrical enthalpy can be decomposed consistently with the additive
Helmholtz energy.  Define component electrical enthalpy densities
\(\omega_s^{\alpha+}\) such that
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_electrothermodynamic_decomposition}
	\begin{align}
		\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\eta_s^\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
		\right),
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_electrothermodynamic_energy} \\
		\bar v_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\eta_s^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha,
		\qquad
		\omega_s^+
		=
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\omega_s^{\alpha+},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_electric_enthalpy_average}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The second line follows from
\(\eta_s^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha=\phi_s^\alpha\bar v_s\), which is equivalent to
the density rules in \eqref{eq:MC_solid_density_mixture_rules}.  Together with
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_pressure_average}, it gives the component and phase
equivalent pressures
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_equivalent_pressures}
	\begin{align}
		\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
		 & \equiv
		\bar p_s^\alpha-\omega_s^{\alpha+}
		=
		-
		\left.
		\p{
			\left(
			\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
			\right)
		}{\bar v_s^\alpha}
		\right\vert_{
			\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
			\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
		},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_component_equivalent_pressure} \\
		\bar p_E
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_equivalent_pressure_average}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Thus neither the component mechanical pressures nor their equivalent pressures
must be equal.  The multipliers determine the mechanical pressures through
\eqref{eq:additive_component_pressure_multiplier_identity}; the component
electrical enthalpies then determine their conjugate equivalent pressures.

At fixed composition, the component differential and its Legendre transform
are
%
\begin{align}
	\left.
	d\left(
	\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	-\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\,d\bar v_s^\alpha,
	\notag \\
	\left.
	d\left(
	\psi_s^\alpha
	+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
	+\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	\bar v_s^\alpha\,d\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_legendre_differential}
\end{align}
%
Since
\(\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha=\bar p_s^\alpha-\omega_s^{\alpha+}\), the transformed
component potential has the equivalent forms
%
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde\psi_s^\alpha
	=
	\psi_s^\alpha
	+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
	+\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha
	=
	\psi_s^\alpha+\bar p_s^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_legendre_transform}
\end{equation}
%
The second form permits the transform to be evaluated from the component
mechanical pressure.  Its natural pressure variable remains
\(\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\): in general
\(d\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
=d\bar p_s^\alpha-d\omega_s^{\alpha+}\).

For an independent component-pressure experiment, hold all component equivalent
pressures fixed during the instantaneous deformation perturbation.  The
specific-volume derivative then satisfies
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	}
	=
	\sum_\alpha
	\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s^\alpha}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_specific_volume_path_identity}
\end{equation}
%
The component nonlinear Biot contribution is therefore
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	B_s^\alpha
	\equiv
	1
	-
	\frac{a_s\eta_s^\alpha}{v_{s0}}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s^\alpha}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_biot}
\end{empheq}
%
The component Legendre transforms give the stress contributions
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\alpha}
	=
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime\alpha}
	+
	(1-B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_biot_split}
\end{equation}
%
Summing over components gives the general additive mixture rule
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_biot_mixture_rule}
	\begin{align}
		\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime\alpha},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_double_prime_sum} \\
		\bs\sigma_s'
		 & =
		\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
		+
		\sum_\alpha
		(1-B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\mbf I,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_general_biot_stress_sum} \\
		(1-B_s)\bar p_E
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		(1-B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_biot_pressure_mixture_rule}
	\end{align}
	\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
		B_s
		=
		1
		-
		\frac{
			\displaystyle\sum_\alpha
			(1-B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
		}{
			\displaystyle\sum_\alpha
			\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
		},
		\qquad
		\bar p_E\ne0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_biot_mixture_coefficient}
	\end{empheq}
\end{subequations}
%
The product relation follows by comparing the summed component stress with the
phase stress form \eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_split}.  Identification of this
coefficient with the fixed-\(\bar p_E\) derivative in
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_coefficient} requires the component equations of state
and their pressure-redistribution path to reproduce that phase derivative.
The additive decomposition alone supplies the pressure-weighted stress rule;
it does not select that redistribution path.

The product relation
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_biot_pressure_mixture_rule} remains well defined when
\(\bar p_E=0\); the quotient form then cannot identify \(B_s\) from the
pressure correction.  If all component equivalent pressures are equal to
\(\bar p_E\), the general rule reduces to
%
\begin{equation}
	B_s
	=
	1
	-\sum_\alpha
	(1-B_s^\alpha),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_common_equivalent_pressure_limit}
\end{equation}
%
Equality of all mechanical component pressures gives this reduction only when
the component electrical enthalpy densities are also equal.  The general
pressure-weighted rule therefore applies directly to components whose
multiplier-derived pressures or electrical responses differ.

\subsection{Single-component solid specialization}
\label{sec:MC_single_component_rollup}

For a single-component solid phase, set
%
\begin{equation}
	\eta_s^s=1,
	\qquad
	\dot\eta_s^s=0,
	\qquad
	\phi_s^s=1,
	\qquad
	\bar\rho_s^s=\bar\rho_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_single_component_constraints}
\end{equation}
%
The phase free energy then has no composition argument,
%
\begin{equation}
	\psi_s
	=
	\psi_s(
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,\bar\rho_s,\theta_{\mc S}),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S,
	\label{eq:MC_single_component_free_energy}
\end{equation}
%
the composition restriction disappears, and the additive rules reduce to
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_s^s=\bar p_s,
	\qquad
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime s}=\bs\sigma_s',
	\qquad
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime s}=\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime},
	\qquad
	B_s^s=B_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_single_component_reductions}
\end{equation}
%
The one-component limit removes only the independent composition rate.  The
phase still retains its stress-free thermal maps, plastic and distension
variables, and conversion sources.

The phase Biot coefficient and stress split remain valid without component
sums, as do the phase and overall momentum balances.  With one solid and one
fluid phase, the overall coefficient is simply \(B=B_s\).

\subsection{Scalar distension simplifications}
\label{sec:MC_scalar_distension_simplifications}

For isotropic pore-structure change, each distension factor is spherical.  This
specialization retains volumetric accommodation but excludes anisotropic pore
collapse and fabric evolution,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf A_s^\bullet=(a_s^\bullet)^{1/3}\mbf I,
	\qquad
	\bullet\in\{e,p,0\},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_factors}
\end{equation}
%
The factors multiply,
%
\begin{equation}
	a_s=a_s^e a_s^p a_s^0,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_product}
\end{equation}
%
and the true solid deformation relation becomes
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf F=a_s^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}_s,
	\qquad
	J=a_s\bar J_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_deformation}
\end{equation}
%
The isotropic distension factors cancel from the push-forward in
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_stress_definition}, leaving
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_solid_stress}
\end{equation}
%
For a generic multicomponent solid, the tensor distension restriction
\eqref{eq:MC_distension_force_definition} reduces to the trace condition
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right)
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{a_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	a_s^e,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_stress_restriction}
\end{equation}
%
Using the phase-level Biot transform, the same condition can be written as a
restriction on the phase double-prime trace,
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime})
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{a_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	a_s^e
	-\left(1-B_s\right)\bar p_E,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_double_prime_distension_trace}
\end{equation}
%
The same specialization reduces the plastic-distension power in
\eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} to
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\bs\sigma_s':
	\mbf A_s^e\dot{\mbf A}_s^p
	(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
	=
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right)
	\frac{\dot{a}_s^p}{a_s^p}.
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_plastic_distension_power}
\end{equation}
%
An associated scalar closure using the nonnegative plastic-distension mobility
from \eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_distension_mobility_nonnegative} is
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\dot a_s^p}{a_s^p}
	=
	\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}(\mc X_s)
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_plastic_distension_flow_rule}
\end{equation}
%
Its contribution to the plastic power is
\(\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}
[\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s')/3]^2\ge0\).
Thus scalar plastic distension describes hydrostatic compaction or dilation,
with the material mean stress as its conjugate force.

\subsection{Distension and crystallization pressure}
\label{sec:MC_distension_crystallization_pressure}

Crystallization affects momentum only through constitutive ingredients already
present in the theory: the solid free energy, the precipitation kinetics, and
the reaction affinity.  In the scalar setting, the volumetric kinematics are
%
\begin{equation}
	\ln J=\ln a_s+\ln\bar J_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_volumetric_split}
\end{equation}
%
The scalar stress restriction depends on the total volumetric strain
\(\ln J=\ln a_s+\ln\bar J_s\).  A simple transformed volumetric energy with
tangent bulk modulus \(K_s^\star\) is
%
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde W_{{\rm vol},s}
	=
	\frac{K_s^\star}{2}
	\left(\ln J\right)^2,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_component_transformed_volumetric_energy}
\end{equation}
%
Holding \(\bar J_s\) and \(\bar p_E\) fixed,
%
\begin{equation}
	a_s
	\left.
	\p{\widetilde W_{{\rm vol},s}}{a_s}
	\right\vert_{\bar J_s,\bar p_E}
	=
	K_s^\star\ln J,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_component_transformed_volumetric_trace}
\end{equation}
%
Its derivative gives the fixed-pressure mean stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime})
	=
	K_s^\star\ln J,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_component_double_prime_trace_from_energy}
\end{equation}
%
and the single-prime trace follows from the Biot stress split,
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{a_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	a_s^e
	=
	K_s^\star\ln J
	+\left(1-B_s\right)\bar p_E,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_component_cn_trace_satisfied}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the solid energy supplies a crystallization-induced stress through the
same constitutive stress used in the momentum equation.

The precipitation affinity provides the complementary chemical condition.  For
a mechanism \((m)\) that forms solid phase \(s_{(m)}\),
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_affinity_kinetics}
\end{equation}
%
The kinetic law is driven by the temperature-weighted stoichiometric sum in
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate}.  In the local-thermal-equilibrium limit,
its driving coefficient is the traditional affinity minus the explicit
phase-transfer work in
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}.  The dissipation
condition reduces to \(\mc A_{(m)}\dot r_{(m)}\ge0\) when that transfer-work
offset vanishes.

If a classical crystallization pressure
\(\bar p_{\text{xtal},s_{(m)}}\) is prescribed, it closes the affinity through
the sign convention stated at the beginning of this section.  Cauchy stress is
tension-positive, so the positive compressive mean pressure acting on the
forming solid is
\(-\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}')/3\).  Creating a unit mass of solid
against that compression costs its specific volume times this mechanical
pressure.  The displayed pressure-work form uses a mass-based progress variable
normalized by
\(\sum_\alpha\nu_{s_{(m)} (m)}^\alpha=1\).  For another positive solid mass
yield, the right-hand side is multiplied by that yield; for a molar progress
basis, \(\bar v_{s_{(m)}}\) is replaced by the corresponding molar volume.  The
net chemical-minus-mechanical driving force is therefore
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	\bar v_{s_{(m)}}
	\left[
		\bar p_{\text{xtal},s_{(m)}}
		+\frac{1}{3}
		\operatorname{tr}
		\left(
		\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}'
	\right)
	\right],
	\qquad
	\bar v_{s_{(m)}}=\frac{1}{\bar\rho_{s_{(m)}}},
	\qquad
	s_{(m)}\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_affinity_volumetric}
\end{equation}
%
For example, using the classical supersaturation relation
\(\bar p_{\text{xtal},s_{(m)}}=R\theta_{\mc F}\ln\Omega_{(m)}/V_{s_{(m)}}\),
where the fluid temperature is used because supersaturation and the
fluid--solid precipitation mechanism are evaluated in the mobile subsystem,
\(R\) is the gas constant, \(\Omega_{(m)}\) is the supersaturation ratio, and
\(V_{s_{(m)}}\) is the molar volume of the precipitating solid, gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	\bar v_{s_{(m)}}
		\left[
			\frac{R\theta_{\mc F}}{V_{s_{(m)}}}
			\ln\Omega_{(m)}
			+\frac{1}{3}
			\operatorname{tr}
			\left(
			\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}'
		\right)
		\right],
	\qquad
	s_{(m)}\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_supersaturation_affinity}
\end{equation}
%
At full reversible equilibrium,
\(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}\) and \(\mc A_{(m)}=0\), so the chemical
crystallization pressure equals the positive compressive phase mean pressure
\(-\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}')/3\) permitted by the constitutive
law.  Equivalently, the equilibrium supersaturation is
%
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}
	=
	\exp\left[
	-\frac{
		V_{s_{(m)}}
		\operatorname{tr}
		\left(
		\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}'
		\right)
	}{
		3R\theta_{\mc F}
	}
	\right],
	\qquad
	s_{(m)}\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_equilibrium_supersaturation}
\end{equation}
%
The stress-free limit gives \(\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}=1\), whereas compression
raises the equilibrium threshold.  Under local thermal equilibrium and a
vanishing phase-transfer offset, \eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate} gives
\(\dot r_{(m)}=K_{(m)}\mc A_{(m)}/\theta\); therefore
\(\Omega_{(m)}>\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}\) drives precipitation and
\(\Omega_{(m)}<\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}\) drives dissolution.  If stress and
affinity are solved simultaneously,
\(\Omega_{(m)}\) is an output rather than an independently prescribed load.

\section{Multicomponent model summary}
\label{sec:MC_model_summary_closure_count}

This section records bulk pointwise unknown and equation counts for a reduced,
fully implicit active-phase formulation under the closures stated below.  These
counts are not a complete boundary-value-problem specification.  One mass
fraction per phase and one phase volume fraction are reconstructed from
\eqref{eq:el_eta_constraint} and \eqref{eq:volume_fraction_constraint}.
The composition-normalization multipliers \(\Pi_\xi\) are not primary unknowns:
subtracting the component stationarity equations eliminates them through
\eqref{eq:pi_difference_system}.  The storage multipliers
\(\pi_\xi^\alpha\) are then evaluated from that difference system and the
phase-sum relation \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_storage}.  Phase pressures and
electrochemical potentials are likewise evaluated from the constitutive
restrictions.  The normalization
\eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_reference_normalization} fixes the transfer-work
level in the reference solid phase.  One equation
\eqref{eq:MC_admissible_conversion_component} then evolves the shared transfer
potential \(\tau\), while the \(P-1\) equations
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_reference_transfer_work_recovery} determine the remaining
phase-reference coefficients \(L_\xi^N\).  The component differences
\eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_component_difference}, equivalently the full
recovery \eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_full_recovery}, reconstruct
\(L_\xi^\alpha\), \(\alpha\ne N\), as algebraically recovered variational
coefficients rather than additional primary fields or freely prescribed
constitutive functions.

Initial and boundary data, potential gauges, pure-Neumann compatibility
conditions, and switching conditions for phase or component appearance are not
included in the pointwise counts.  A charged reaction that transfers charge
between thermal subsystems retains the scalar neutral-force kinetic row.  Its
reaction-energy transfer may be Onsager-coupled subject to
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}; the displayed rate count is
unchanged unless a chosen closure introduces additional internal variables.

Both relative-flux families, the phase heat fluxes, the nonzero pairwise
interaction forces in the general extension, and the independent fluid--solid
heat-exchange supply are retained as auxiliary fields so that their admissible
closures appear explicitly in the count.  Because all solid phases share one
skeleton velocity, the nonzero interaction pairs comprise fluid--fluid and
fluid--solid pairs; solid--solid drag vanishes identically.  The concrete
closure \eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_law} instead retains one aggregate
fluid--skeleton interaction per fluid phase.  The total solid scalar distension
factors are closed kinematically by
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_distension_evolution}, while the scalar plastic factors
\(a_s^p\) follow \eqref{eq:MC_scalar_plastic_distension_flow_rule}.  The
electroquasistatic formulation includes the electric potential and Gauss' law.
Mechanical interaction-energy supplies are constitutive outputs associated
with the pairwise interaction forces and must satisfy
\eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} and
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility}; they are therefore not
counted as an independent field family.
Let \(P_{\mc F}=|\mc F|\), \(P_{\mc S}=|\mc S|\), and
\(P=P_{\mc F}+P_{\mc S}\).  Let \(N^{\mc F}\) be the number of tracked
components in each fluid phase and \(N^{\mc S}\) the number of tracked
components in each solid phase.  If the component counts vary by phase, the
products in the tables are replaced by the corresponding phase sums.  Let \(N_{(m)}\)
be the number of independent conversion mechanisms and \(d\) the spatial
dimension.  The number of continuous capillary-history variables is \(N_h\).
The dynamic-capillary resistance \(\mc T\) is constitutive, and the saturation
rates are obtained from the independent phase volume fractions, so the optional
closure introduces no additional pointwise unknown family.
