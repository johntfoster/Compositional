\section{Constitutive models and the entropy inequality}
\label{sec:multicomponent_solid_skeletons}
\label{sec:multicomponent_solid_mixture_rules}
\label{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}

Section~\ref{sec:virtual_power_derivation} supplies the coordinate and field
balances and the Onsager rate statements.  This section completes the
porous-medium constitutive theory for stress, pressure, reaction, transport,
and plastic response.  It first defines the common solid motion, the fluid and
solid thermal subsystems, the constitutive state variables, and the stored
energies.  The entropy inequality then supplies the reversible stress and
pressure relations and restricts the dissipative laws.  The electroquasistatic
approximation has already been imposed; electromagnetic-wave dynamics are
outside the model.

Cauchy stress is tension-positive, so the momentum balance uses
\(+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma\).  Thermodynamic phase pressures, capillary
pressures, and the equivalent pore pressure are positive in compression and
therefore enter the total stress as negative spherical contributions, opposite
to the tension-positive stress.  A single prime marks the material stress at
fixed intrinsic density, a double prime the fixed-pressure effective stress, and a
superscript \(+\) the electrical stress contribution.  The Coleman--Noll
analysis below uses these conventions throughout.

Every phase may contain multiple components.  Each solid phase \(s\in\mc S\)
is a load-bearing multicomponent solid with mass fractions \(\eta_s^\alpha\),
and all solid phases share the skeleton trajectory
\begin{equation}
	\mbf x=\bs\chi(\mbf X,t),
	\qquad
	\mbf F=\nabla_{\mbf X}\bs\chi,
	\qquad
	J=\det\mbf F .
	\label{eq:common_solid_skeleton_motion}
\end{equation}
Adsorbed species, host minerals, hydrate cages, bound water, and hydrate guest
molecules move with this common skeleton motion.  Their distinct mechanical
effects enter through the composition dependence of the free energy,
stress-free swelling, thermal expansion, elastic moduli, and plastic flow.

The thermal model distinguishes an equilibrated mobile-fluid subsystem from
the solid skeleton,
%
\begin{equation}
	\theta_\xi
	=
	\begin{cases}
		\theta_{\mc F}, & \xi=f\in\mc F,  \\
		\theta_{\mc S}, & \xi=s\in\mc S .
	\end{cases}
	\label{eq:MC_two_temperature_specialization}
\end{equation}
%
All mobile phases share \(\theta_{\mc F}\), and all load-bearing solid phases
share \(\theta_{\mc S}\).  Heat exchange between the subsystems is retained,
so either temperature may be larger.  Multiphase geothermal models use the
same fluid/rock temperature grouping \cite{faraz2021boiling}, whereas
constituent mixture theory permits a separate temperature for every phase
\cite{drumheller2000,hanyga2004thermal}.  Local thermal equilibrium is the
limit \(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}\).

Constitutive rates are evaluated in the frame moving with the solid skeleton.
For a spatial state field \(\Gamma\), define the skeleton velocity and the
skeleton-following (heavy-dot) rate by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf v_{\mc S}
	=
	\left.
	\frac{\partial\bs\chi}{\partial t}
	\right\vert_{\mbf X},
	\qquad
	\phasedot\Gamma
	\equiv
	\frac{D_{\mc S}\Gamma}{Dt}
	=
	\frac{\partial\Gamma}{\partial t}
	+
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\Gamma .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_rate_convention}
\end{equation}
%
The heavy dot applies to \(\theta_{\mc F}\), \(\theta_{\mc S}\), each
\(\phi_\xi\), \(\bar\rho_\xi\), \(\eta_\xi^\alpha\), the phase thermodynamic
potentials, and the solid kinematic fields and internal variables \(\mbf A_s\)
and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s\).  The ordinary dot marks a rate following phase
\(\xi\), as in the component-insertion rates \(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha\) and
\(\dot c_\xi^\alpha\).  Because all solid phases share the skeleton trajectory,
\(D_s/Dt=D_{\mc S}/Dt\), while mobile phases retain their own phase velocities.
For any phase field \(\Gamma_\xi\), the phase-following and skeleton-following
rates are related by
%
\begin{equation}
	\dot{\Gamma}_\xi
	=
	\phasedot\Gamma_\xi
	+
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\Gamma_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_skeleton_transport_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Thus \(\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}\) is the bulk velocity of phase \(\xi\)
relative to the skeleton, while the relative component fluxes describe the
motion of components within the phase.  This solid-observer convention appears
in thermodynamic formulations of multicomponent multiphase poroelastic flow
\cite[eq.~(2)]{seguin2019multicomponent} and is consistent with the
solid-observed saturation rate used by Hassanizadeh and Gray
\cite[eq.~(35)]{hassanizadeh1993thermodynamic}.

Summing \eqref{eq:averaged_component_spatial_mass} over \(\alpha\), using the
zero-sum relative component flux \eqref{eq:averaged_relative_flux_sum}, and
rewriting in the skeleton frame gives the phase mass balance
%
\begin{equation}
	\phasedot\rho_\xi
	+
	\rho_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left[
		\rho_\xi
		\left(
		\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)
	\right]
	=
	\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_phase_mass_balance}
\end{equation}
%
For any phase-specific quantity \(\Gamma_\xi\), expand
\(\partial_t(\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi)+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot(\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi\mbf v_\xi)\)
by product rules, substitute
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_phase_mass_balance}, and then use
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_skeleton_transport_identity}.  This gives the conservative
transport identity
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
	\left(
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
	\right)
	+
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	={}&
	\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
	\left(
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
	\right)
	+
	\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left[
		\rho_\xi\Gamma_\xi
		\left(
		\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)
	\right]
	\notag \\
	={}&
	\rho_\xi\phasedot\Gamma_\xi
	+
	\Gamma_\xi
	\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	+
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\Gamma_\xi
	\notag \\
	={}&
	\rho_\xi\dot{\Gamma}_\xi
	+
	\Gamma_\xi
	\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity}
\end{align}
%
The source term \(\Gamma_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha\) is the
mass-transfer source from \eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_phase_mass_balance}, scaled by
\(\Gamma_\xi\).  The divergence term in
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity} measures transport
through a control surface moving with the skeleton.  For a solid phase,
\(\mbf v_\xi=\mbf v_{\mc S}\), so this relative-transport contribution is
zero.  For a mobile phase, \(\mbf v_\xi\neq\mbf v_{\mc S}\), so the term is
generally nonzero.

In the same observer frame, the rate of the pointwise volume-fraction
constraint is
%
\begin{equation}
	\phasedot\phi_\xi
	\equiv
	\frac{D_{\mc S}\phi_\xi}{Dt}
	=
	\frac{\partial\phi_\xi}{\partial t}
	+
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi,
	\qquad
	\sum_\xi\phasedot\phi_\xi=0 .
	\label{eq:MC_skeleton_volume_fraction_rates}
\end{equation}
%
Each volume fraction may change, but the rates must sum to zero.  The
skeleton-following rates above apply to phase-indexed fields, whereas several
fields in the formulation are shared by the mixture as a whole and take a
single value.  The mechanism rate \(r_{(m)}\) is one local process rate common
to all phases participating in mechanism \((m)\).  The electric potential
\(\varphi\) is one Eulerian field for the mixture.  The transfer multiplier
\(\tau\) is likewise one scalar field shared by all phases, and its material
rate \(D_\xi\tau/Dt\) is evaluated along the phase trajectory in the
component-insertion relation.

\subsection{Solid kinematics and state variables}
\label{sec:MC_solid_kinematics}

The Coleman--Noll procedure in \sref{MC_coleman_noll_procedure} requires the
solid kinematic fields, their rate relations, and the constitutive state
arguments.  This subsection defines each.  Extending Drumheller's
distension/true-deformation split for reacting mixtures
\cite{drumheller2000} and its porous-media form in Foster et
al.~\cite{foster2026reactingporous}, the solid kinematic split is
phase-specific but uses the common skeleton deformation,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf F=\mbf A_s\bar{\mbf F}_s,
	\qquad
	J=a_s\bar J_s,
	\qquad
	a_s=\det\mbf A_s,
	\qquad
	\bar J_s=\det\bar{\mbf F}_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_decomposition}
\end{equation}
%
The distension tensor \(\mbf A_s\) maps the true deformation of solid phase
\(s\) into the common skeleton deformation.  It represents the pore-space
allocation and packing needed to accommodate swelling and reaction-induced
volume change between phases.  Extending
Drumheller's elastic--plastic treatment of true deformation and distension
\cite{drumheller2000}, the elastic, plastic, and stress-free factors are
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_kinematic_splits}
	\begin{align}
		\mbf A_s
		 & =
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p\mbf A_s^0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_split} \\
		\bar{\mbf F}_s
		 & =
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_true_split}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Here \(\mbf A_s^e\) and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\) are elastic factors,
\(\mbf A_s^p\) and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p\) are plastic internal variables, and
\(\mbf A_s^0\) and \(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0\) are reversible stress-free maps that can
include temperature and solid-composition effects,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf A_s^0
	=
	\mbf A_s^0(\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\}),
	\qquad
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^0
	=
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^0(\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\}),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_stress_free_maps}
\end{equation}
%
The stress-free maps capture composition- and temperature-driven changes of
the stress-free state, including adsorption swelling and shrinkage,
hydrate-cage volume changes, and composition-dependent thermal expansion.
A shared \(\theta_{\mc S}\) remains compatible with phase-specific thermal
expansion: each phase retains its own functions \(\mbf A_s^0\) and
\(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0\), and therefore its own derivative with respect to
\(\theta_{\mc S}\).
A plastic internal state vector \(\mbf p_s\) per solid phase augments these
plastic internal variables; its evolution
\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_state_evolution} carries hardening, softening,
and rate dependence in the plastic closures of \sref{MC_plastic_flow_closures}.
Thermo-poroelastic models make the same physical distinction by assigning pore
volume, fluid, and grain thermal expansions separately
\cite{palciauskas1982thermally,ghabezloo2009stress,ghabezloo2011micromechanics}.
As in standard finite-strain multiplicative plasticity
\cite{simo1988framework,simo1998} and Drumheller's mixture treatment
\cite{drumheller2000}, the true-plastic flow is assumed isochoric,
%
\begin{equation}
	\det\bar{\mbf F}_s^p=1,
	\qquad
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]=0,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_isochoric_plastic_flow}
\end{equation}
%
The dependent elastic arguments are
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf A_s^e
	=
	\mbf A_s
	(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
	(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1},
	\qquad
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	=
	\bar{\mbf F}_s
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_elastic_arguments}
\end{equation}
%

The solid distension rate \(\phasedot a_s/a_s\) enters the Coleman--Noll
dissipation.  The solid-phase mass-balance relations supply this rate.
Summing the component material measure
\eqref{eq:augmented_component_material_measure} over \(\alpha\) for the solid
phase gives
%
\begin{equation}
	J\phi_s\bar\rho_s
	=
	\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}+\mc C_s,
	\qquad
	\mc C_s=\sum_\alpha\mc C_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_phase_material_mass}
\end{equation}
%
With true local conservation in the solid material,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar J_s\bar\rho_s=\bar\rho_{s0},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_solid_true_mass_conservation}
\end{equation}
%
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_phase_material_mass} becomes
%
\begin{equation}
	a_s\phi_s\bar\rho_{s0}
	=
	\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}+\mc C_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_mass_relation}
\end{equation}
%
The rate form follows by taking the natural logarithm of \(J=a_s\bar J_s\)
and applying the solid-following material derivative, using the kinematic
identity \(\phasedot J/J=\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}\) and true
mass conservation \eqref{eq:MC_solid_true_mass_conservation},
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\frac{\phasedot{a}_s}{a_s}
	=
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+\frac{\phasedot{\bar\rho}_s}{\bar\rho_s},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_evolution}
\end{empheq}
%
The entropy inequality is exploited with all other constitutive arguments
held fixed.  The nonelectrical state sets are
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc X_s
	=
	\{
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
	\bar\rho_s,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S}
	\},
	\qquad
	\mc X_f
	=
	\{
	\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F}
	\},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_state_sets}
\end{equation}
%
The interfacial state set is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc X_\gamma
	=
	\left\{
	\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}
	\right\},
	\qquad
	\mbf h\in\mathbb R^{N_h}.
	\label{eq:MC_interfacial_state_set}
\end{equation}
%
The phase energies use \(\mc X_f\) and \(\mc X_s\), while \(\mc X_\gamma\)
controls the shared pore-scale interfacial storage and therefore the stored
capillary-pressure contribution.  The capillary-history
variables are therefore internal variables of the fluid subsystem rather than
additional composition coordinates of an individual fluid phase.  The
saturation rates \(\phasedot S_f\) are process rates and are not members of
\(\mc X_\gamma\).  The resistance \(\mc T\) belongs to the dissipative closure,
not to the stored energy.  It may depend on the current constitutive and
pore-structure state, but it is held fixed while the instantaneous saturation
rates are varied by Onsager stationarity.

The intrinsic density rate needed in the Coleman--Noll analysis follows from
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_true_split}, isochoric plastic flow, and the
composition-dependent stress-free true deformation.  In the derivatives below,
the subscript \(\mc X_s\) indicates that all other members of the solid state
set are held fixed:
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{\phasedot{\bar\rho}_s}{\bar\rho_s}
	={} &
	-\operatorname{tr}
	\left[
		\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		\left(
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\phasedot\theta_{\mc S}
		+
		\sum_\beta
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\phasedot\eta_s^\beta
		\right)
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
		\right],
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_density_rate}
\end{align}
%

The Helmholtz free energies that supply the stored-energy terms are
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{alignedat}{2}
		\psi_f
		& =
		\psi_f(
		\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F}),
		\qquad&
		f\in\mc F,
		\\
		\psi_s
		& =
		\psi_s(
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\bar\rho_s,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S}),
		\qquad&
		s\in\mc S .
	\end{alignedat}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_general_free_energy}
\end{equation}
%
The same held-fixed convention applies to derivatives annotated by
\(\mc X_f\).  Because the mass fractions in each phase sum to one, only
\(N-1\) composition rates are independent.  The reversible composition
relations below are therefore written as differences from component \(N\).

\pagebreak[3]
Electrical storage is represented by the phase electric-enthalpy densities
\(\omega^{+}_{\xi}\) introduced in \sref{virtual_power_derivation}.  Because
\(\omega_\xi^+\) is measured per current phase volume, phase \(\xi\) contributes
\(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\) per current mixture volume.  The interfacial Helmholtz
energy has a different normalization: \(\gamma\) is measured per current fluid
volume and contributes \(\phi\gamma\) to the mixture.  All phases experience
the same electric field, but each phase may have a different dielectric
response.  The phase and mixture electric displacements \(\mbf d_\xi\) and
\(\mbf d\) are defined in \eqref{eq:electric_field_definitions} with the
minus sign characteristic of the electric-enthalpy representation
\cite{dorfmann2017nonlinear_electroelasticity}.  As stated there, \(\mbf E\)
is the electrical state variable and \(\mbf d_\xi\) is obtained by
differentiating \(\omega_\xi^+\).

For a fluid phase,
\(\omega_f^+=\omega_f^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F})\).
Its density, composition, and temperature dependence permits the scalar
effective permittivity and dielectric pressure to change with fluid state.
Such changes are relevant in porous rocks: the dielectric response of saturated
sandstone depends on porosity and pore-fluid salinity, while measurements on
fluid-saturated artificial shales show dependence on clay content and
pore-fluid salinity and cation composition
\cite{kirichek2018dielectric_sandstone,beloborodov2017dielectric_shales}.
The solid phase has the analogous dependence on the field, intrinsic density,
solid composition, and \(\theta_{\mc S}\).  These dependencies contribute to the
solid dielectric pressure and to the density, composition, and temperature
coefficients collected in the entropy inequality.

As shown in Appendix~\ref{sec:technical_setting}, objectivity of a potential with only
one spatial-vector electrical argument makes \(\omega_\xi^+\) even in
\(\mbf E\) and makes \(\mbf d_\xi\) parallel to \(\mbf E\).  The present
electrical response is therefore isotropic and excludes permanent polarization
at zero field, bedding-directed dielectric anisotropy, and independent
dielectric relaxation.  Bedding-direction effects would require an additional
objective structural argument and corresponding stress and torque terms.
Electrical coupling retained here includes Maxwell stress, phase equilibrium,
electrochemical transport, and electro-osmotic cross-coupling.  This choice is
consistent with laboratory water-driven hydraulic-fracture self-potential
signals in saturated granite for which the response was dominated by
electrokinetic coupling~\cite{moore2007self_potential}.  Plastic evolution
depends on the phase material stress and composition, so electrical loading
affects plastic response through the coupled Maxwell momentum solution.

The phase Maxwell stress \(\bs\sigma_\xi^{+}\), defined in
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}, contributes to the phase momentum
balance through the total reversible stress
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}\).  The density, composition, and temperature
derivatives of \(\omega^{+}_{\xi}\) enter the corresponding coefficients in the
entropy inequality.  This separation assigns each electrical power contribution
to a single governing equation.

The derivation first allows a general composition-dependent phase Helmholtz
energy \(\psi_\xi\).  The later additive specialization in
\sref{MC_additive_component_specialization} is optional and resolves the solid
energy into component contributions without changing the common solid motion.

\subsection{Coleman--Noll procedure}
\label{sec:MC_coleman_noll_procedure}

The Coleman--Noll procedure tests the constitutive assumptions against the
local entropy inequality.  The balance laws and kinematic constraints restrict
the admissible rates; after those restrictions are imposed, coefficients of the
remaining independent reversible rates must vanish and the residual
force--rate products must be nonnegative
\cite{coleman1974thermodynamics}.

The three variational or thermodynamic steps used in the manuscript have
different purposes.  Hamilton--d'Alembert stationarity supplies the phase
coordinate equations and multiplier relations, including any d'Alembert work
specified for a dissipative force.  The Onsager construction then supplies
near-equilibrium choices for saturation redistribution, reaction, and
relative-transport kinetics.  Finally, Coleman--Noll determines which
constitutive stresses, pressures, entropies, and force--rate products are
compatible with the entropy inequality, and therefore which of the Onsager
dissipative laws are admissible.  Relations obtained independently from the
stored energy variation and the entropy inequality must agree because both use
the same energies and constraints.

The Coleman--Noll exploitation uses the absolute component potential
defined by the constituent-mass derivative of the phase material and electric
enthalpy storage at fixed phase volume fraction,
%
\begin{equation}
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\equiv
	\left.
	\frac{
		\partial
		\left(
		\rho_\xi\psi_\xi+\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+
		\right)
	}{
		\partial\rho_\xi^\alpha
	}
	\right\vert_{
		\phi_\xi,\theta_{\mc G},\mbf E,
		\{\rho_\xi^\beta\}_{\beta\ne\alpha},
		\text{kinematic and internal fields}
	},
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G .
	\label{eq:MC_absolute_neutral_component_potential}
\end{equation}
%
The corresponding electrochemical potential is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi .
	\label{eq:C45_combined_def}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\varphi\) has units of energy per charge and \(z_\xi^\alpha\) has units
of charge per component mass.  These are constitutive state quantities, not
variational multipliers.  For a solid phase, the kinematic and internal fields
held fixed in
\eqref{eq:MC_absolute_neutral_component_potential} are
\(\mbf A_s\), \(\bar{\mbf F}_s\), \(\mbf A_s^p\), and
\(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p\).  For a fluid phase, the same definition reduces to the
fixed-volume Helmholtz constituent potential, augmented by the composition and
density dependence of \(\omega_f^+\).

Let \(e_\xi\) denote the specific internal energy of phase \(\xi\), and let
\(\mathfrak{s}_\xi\) denote its specific entropy.  For fluid phases,
%
\begin{equation}
	e_f=\psi_f+\theta_{\mc F}\mathfrak{s}_f,
	\qquad
	\psi_f=\psi_f(\bar\rho_f,\{\eta_f^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc F}),
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
%
For multicomponent solid phases,
%
\begin{equation}
	e_s=\psi_s+\theta_{\mc S}\mathfrak{s}_s,
	\qquad
	\psi_s=\psi_s(
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
	\bar\rho_s,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S}),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
%
The unresolved interface carries the corresponding caloric quantities per
current fluid-subsystem volume.  Let \(u_\gamma\) denote its internal-energy
density and let \(\mathfrak{s}_\gamma\) denote its entropy density.  Their
Legendre relation is
%
\begin{equation}
	u_\gamma
	=
	\gamma+\theta_{\mc F}\mathfrak{s}_\gamma .
	\label{eq:MC_interfacial_legendre_relation}
\end{equation}
%
Thus \(\phi u_\gamma\) and \(\phi\mathfrak{s}_\gamma\) are the corresponding
internal-energy and entropy densities per current mixture volume.  They belong
to the fluid thermal subsystem and follow the skeleton control volume.  The
phase quantities \(e_f\) and \(\mathfrak{s}_f\) contain only the caloric storage
assigned to phase \(f\).

The free-energy rates are
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f\phasedot\psi_f
	={} &
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\rho_f
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\bar\rho_f}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\phasedot{\bar\rho}_f
	+
	\sum_\beta
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\phasedot\eta_f^\beta
	+
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\phasedot\theta_{\mc F}
	\right),
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_free_energy_rate}
	\\
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s\phasedot\psi_s
	={} &
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\rho_s
	\left[
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		:
		\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e
		+
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\mbf A_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		:
		\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^e
		\right.
	\notag       \\
	    & \left.
		+
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\phasedot{\bar\rho}_s
		+
		\sum_\beta
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\phasedot\eta_s^\beta
		+
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\phasedot\theta_{\mc S}
		\right].
	\label{eq:MC_solid_free_energy_rate}
\end{align}
%
The fluid Helmholtz energy has no elastic-deformation argument, so its material
elastic stress is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_f'=\mbf 0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_stress_free}
\end{equation}
%
Pressure is retained separately through the intrinsic-density coefficient.
The remaining reversible stress in the fluid momentum equation is therefore
the Maxwell stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
	=
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{f},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_total_reversible_stress}
\end{equation}
%
The interfacial Helmholtz density \(\gamma\), like \(u_\gamma\), is measured per
current fluid-subsystem volume and has the state set
\eqref{eq:MC_interfacial_state_set}.  Temperature
dependence permits the stored capillary pressure to vary with fluid temperature, as in
the nonisothermal porous-media formulation of Hassanizadeh and Gray
\cite{hassanizadeh1993thermodynamic}.  The internal-variable construction makes
the stored pressure contribution depend on the drainage--imbibition path
\cite{wei2006capillary_hysteresis}.  The mixture stores
\(\phi\gamma\) per current volume.  Differentiating this quantity along the
skeleton trajectory and using the interfacial potentials \(\gamma_f\) from
\eqref{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives} gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
	\left[
		\phi\gamma
		\right]
	=
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\gamma_f\phasedot\phi_f
	+
	\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\cdot\phasedot{\mbf h}
	+
	\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\phasedot\theta_{\mc F} .
	\label{eq:MC_interfacial_helmholtz_rate}
\end{equation}
%
This term is the interfacial Helmholtz-storage rate obtained after the
fluid-subsystem energy and entropy balances are combined through
\eqref{eq:MC_interfacial_legendre_relation}.  The first and second laws
separately carry \(D_{\mc S}(\phi u_\gamma)/Dt\) and
\(D_{\mc S}(\phi\mathfrak{s}_\gamma)/Dt\).  The
history rate records the change in interfacial storage as the pore-scale fluid
arrangement changes; its dissipative restriction is derived below.  The density
\(\phi\gamma\) represents unresolved interfaces; no sharp interface or
interface velocity is introduced.  A
sharp-interface theory would instead require separate surface balances of
energy and entropy
\cite{dreyer2018bulk_surface_electrothermodynamics,bothe2022sharp_interface}.

The material-source convention follows Drumheller's constituent balance and
adopts local-equilibrium insertion: mass inserted into or removed from phase
\(\xi\) carries the recipient-phase specific internal energy \(e_\xi\) and
specific entropy \(\mathfrak{s}_\xi\).  Isolating the source-carried parts of the
corresponding conservative storage rates gives
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_closure}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\left[
			\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
			\left(
			\rho_\xi e_\xi
			\right)
			+
			\rho_\xi e_\xi
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
			+
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left[
				\rho_\xi e_\xi
				\left(
				\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
				\right)
			\right]
		\right]
		\right\vert_{\dot e_\xi=0}
		 & =
		e_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha,
		\label{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_energy} \\
		\left.
		\left[
			\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
			\left(
			\rho_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi
			\right)
			+
			\rho_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
			+
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left[
				\rho_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi
				\left(
				\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
				\right)
			\right]
		\right]
		\right\vert_{\dot{\mathfrak{s}}_\xi=0}
		 & =
		\mathfrak{s}_\xi
		\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha .
		\label{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_entropy}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
% Agreeing with source-carried internal energy \(e_\xi\) and entropy
\(\mathfrak{s}_\xi\), the d'Alembert conversion power
\eqref{eq:W_def} uses \(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\), where \(\psi_\xi\) is the
recipient-phase Helmholtz storage and \(L_\xi^\alpha\) is the specific
generalized transfer-work potential; \(-L_\xi^\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha\) is
the corresponding transfer-work rate.  A non-equilibrium source state would
require additional source energy and entropy data and a corresponding
entropy-production restriction; it is outside the adopted closure.
Equivalently, setting \(\Gamma_\xi=e_\xi\) in
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity} produces the
phase-following storage
\(\rho_\xi\dot e_\xi+e_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha\).  The present
convention therefore transports internal energy, together with pressure and
other constraint powers kept explicit on the right-hand side, with bulk phase
mass.  Let
\(\mbf L_\xi=\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf v_\xi\) be the phase velocity gradient,
\(\mbf q_\xi\) the total nonadvective material-energy flux assigned to phase
\(\xi\), \(\rho_\xi r_\xi\) the external heat supply, and
\(\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}\) the internal-energy supply from the other phases,
all per unit current mixture volume where appropriate.  Thus
\(\mbf q_\xi\) collects the gauge-invariant nonadvective material-energy
transport that remains after bulk phase convection has been separated; it
accommodates conduction, interdiffusion energy transport, and any other
nonadvective contribution.  A charged
component flux relative to its phase also receives the electrical work
\(z_\xi^\alpha\mbf j_\xi^\alpha\cdot\mbf E\).  This work is written directly
in terms of the electric field so that both the energy balance and the
nonisothermal transport force are independent of the arbitrary zero of
\(\varphi\).  The uncoupled Fourier specialization adopted below neglects
Dufour and other heat--component-transport cross effects.
The source-carried phase energy and entropy exclude the interfacial quantities
\(u_\gamma\) and \(\mathfrak{s}_\gamma\).  Conversion changes the interfacial
storage through its induced saturation, capillary-history, and temperature
rates.

One energy balance is assigned to the fluid thermal subsystem and one to the
solid thermal subsystem, following the constituent structure of Drumheller and
Bedford \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical}.  Conversion work follows the
generalized form of Drumheller \cite[eq.~(50)]{drumheller2000}.  The fluid
balance contains the averaged interfacial energy, and the solid balance contains
the solid-phase energies.  Storage rates are evaluated in the skeleton frame
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_rate_convention}, as in the solid-following balances of
Seguin and Walkington \cite[eqs.~(10)--(12)]{seguin2019multicomponent}.
Consistently with those balances, bulk phase motion transports internal energy
through the skeleton control surface and transports entropy in the entropy
inequality below.  These bulk convective fluxes are distinct from the
nonadvective energy flux \(\mbf q_\xi\) and from the component-relative
molecular-diffusion and mechanical-dispersion fluxes.  Internal energy exchanged
between phases cancels by mechanism.  The later closures impose
equal-and-opposite ordinary heat and reaction-associated transfers; the
mechanical contribution cancels against interphase momentum power only after
the subsystem balances are combined with the phase kinetic-energy balances.

Electrical storage is written in the electric-enthalpy representation
\cite{montanaro2015thermo_electroelasticity}.  Phase \(\xi\) contributes
\(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\), while the shared interfacial internal energy
\(\phi u_\gamma\) appears only in the fluid-subsystem balance.  Expanding the electric-enthalpy
rate exposes material-state, Maxwell mechanical-power, and common
electric-potential-rate contributions.  In particular,
\(\mbf d_\xi=-\partial\omega_\xi^+/\partial\mbf E\) makes
\(-\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\phasedot{\mbf E}\) the constitutive electric-field-rate
power; current-volume transport separates its geometric part into
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^+:\mbf L_\xi\) and leaves
	\(\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\phasedot\varphi\) as the shared
	field-coordinate part.  The relative material flux in the energy balance
	written below therefore carries \(\rho_\xi e_\xi\); the
electrostatic field energy is Eulerian storage whose boundary power is supplied
by the global identity below, not an additional material advective flux.

The field rates \(\phasedot{\mbf E}\) and \(\phasedot\varphi\) are evaluated in the
skeleton frame.  The electric field and potential are spatial fields with no
reference configuration; the skeleton frame is chosen because the free charge
coupled to them is carried by the component material measures, and the balances
below are written on skeleton-moving control volumes, so the skeleton velocity
supplies the convective part of the field rates.

The last contribution cannot be
assigned independently to the fluid and solid thermal subsystems because
\(\varphi\) is one shared field.  Summing it over all phases and using Gauss'
law gives the global electrostatic power identity
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\phasedot\varphi
	&=
	\mbf d\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\phasedot\varphi
	\notag \\
	&=
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\phasedot\varphi\,\mbf d
	\right)
	-
	\varrho\phasedot\varphi .
	\label{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}
\end{align}
%
The bulk term \(-\varrho\phasedot\varphi\) is the rate form of the free-charge
source work in \eqref{eq:W_def}, and the divergence gives the electrical
boundary power.  In particular, its flux is
\(\phasedot\varphi\bar q\) on \(\Gamma_{\bar q}\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity} is an entropy-free field-power
identity that separates the shared electric-potential-rate work from the two
thermal-subsystem balances.  It is applied globally before either
	thermal-subsystem balance is divided by its temperature.  Here \(\varrho\) is
	the material-carried charge density defined by
\eqref{eq:mixture_charge_density_definition}.

The two thermal-subsystem balances therefore retain the material-state rate of
each phase electric enthalpy at fixed \(\mbf E\) and its Maxwell mechanical
power, while the shared electric-potential-rate term is carried by
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  The resulting balance for
\(\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}\) is
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		 & \sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
			\left[
				\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
				\left(
				\rho_\xi e_\xi
				\right)
				+
				\rho_\xi e_\xi
				\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
				+
				\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
				\left[
					\rho_\xi e_\xi
					\left(
					\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
					\right)
				\right]
				+
				\omega_\xi^+\phasedot\phi_\xi
			+
			\phi_\xi
			\left.
			\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_\xi}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			\phasedot{\bar\rho}_\xi
			\right.
		\\
		 & \qquad
			\left.
			+
			\phi_\xi
			\sum_\alpha
			\left.
			\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			\phasedot\eta_\xi^\alpha
			+
			\phi_\xi
			\left.
			\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			\phasedot\theta_{\mc G}
			+
			\bs\sigma_\xi^+:\mbf L_\xi
			\right]
		\\
		 & \qquad
		+
		\delta_{\mc G\mc F}
		\frac{D_{\mc S}}{Dt}
		\left(
		\phi u_\gamma
		\right)
		\\
		 & =
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
		\left[
			\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}:\mbf L_\xi
			+
			\phi_\xi\bar p_\xi
			\frac{\phasedot{\bar\rho}_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}
			+
			\bar p_\xi\phasedot\phi_\xi
			+
			\lambda\phasedot\phi_\xi
			+
			\sum_\alpha
			\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
			\phasedot\eta_\xi^\alpha
			-
			\sum_\alpha L_\xi^\alpha\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha
			\right.
		\\
		 & \left.
			\qquad\qquad
			-
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\mbf q_\xi
			+
			\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
			\left(
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
				+
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)\cdot\mbf E
			+
			\rho_\xi r_\xi
			+
			\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
			\right].
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:MC_energy_balance}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\delta_{\mc G\mc F}\) is the Kronecker selector,
\(\delta_{\mc G\mc F}=1\) when \(\mc G=\mc F\) and zero otherwise, so the
interfacial storage term enters only the fluid balance.  The symbol \(\delta\)
is also used for variational displacements; the \(\mc G\mc F\) subscript always
distinguishes the selector.  Also
\(e_\xi=\psi_\xi+\theta_\xi\mathfrak{s}_\xi\).  By
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_conservative_transport_identity}, the first three terms
in each phase contribution simplify to
\(\rho_\xi\dot e_\xi+e_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha\); the source-carried
energy convention is exact.  The material-plus-field first law consists of the
two instances of \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}, the phase kinetic-energy
balances obtained from momentum balance, and the global field-power identity
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  The field-power identity carries
the shared power \(\mbf d\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\phasedot\varphi\) and its source
\(-\varrho\phasedot\varphi\) outside the thermal balances, so each subsystem
balance uses only its own temperature.
For \(\mc G=\mc F\), the final storage term on the left is the
skeleton-following interfacial internal-energy rate.  The unresolved-interface
specialization assigns it no separate interface-relative advective flux.
The electrical transport term in \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance} is the work of
the electric field on component charge transported relative to phase
\(\xi\).  It is not a potential-energy flux.  Both
\(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\) and \(\mbf q_\xi\) are unchanged under
\(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\), so each subsystem balance is gauge
invariant term by term.  Phase-advective electrical work belongs to the
Maxwell mechanical and global field-power block; the term displayed here is
only the additional work on charge moving relative to that phase and therefore
does not duplicate the phase mechanical power.
The direct observer-and-gauge test for this term is
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
		(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha)^\star
		+
		(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha)^\star
	\right)\cdot\mbf E^\star
	&=
	\left[
		\mbf Q
		\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
		\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
	\right]\cdot\mbf Q\mbf E
	\notag \\
	&=
	\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot\mbf E .
	\label{eq:MC_relative_current_work_gauge_objectivity}
\end{align}
%
The additive gauge function does not appear because
\(\mbf E^\star=-\nabla_{\mbf x^\star}\varphi^\star=\mbf Q\mbf E\).
The other occurrences of \(\varphi\) in the material-plus-field first law are
only through \(\mbf E\), \(\phasedot{\mbf E}\), and the globally closed identity
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  Thus the complete energy
equation, not only its final constitutive reduction, passes the gauge test.
When the two subsystem equations are summed, the relative material-energy
transport remains
\(\sum_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot[
\rho_\xi e_\xi(\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S})]\), as it must in a
skeleton-moving control volume.  Adding the phase kinetic-energy balances makes
the mechanical interaction contribution cancel by its later
equal-and-opposite allocation; ordinary heat and reaction-associated transfers
cancel in the same way.  In the nonporous single-phase limit the phase follows the
skeleton, so the relative flux vanishes and the limit derived below is
unchanged.  Thus the observer transformation introduces neither an artificial
source nor a loss of total energy.

The multiplier power
\(\lambda\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\phasedot\phi_\xi\) transfers reversible work between the
two thermal subsystems.  It vanishes only after the subsystem balances are
summed, because \(\sum_\xi\phasedot\phi_\xi=0\).  Retaining this power in each
subsystem balance is necessary when
\(\theta_{\mc F}\ne\theta_{\mc S}\), since the two balances are divided by
different temperatures in the entropy inequality.

The mechanical power uses the total reversible stress
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}\).  The electric-enthalpy rate contains the
corresponding Maxwell power explicitly; subtracting it from the total
mechanical power leaves the material stress power used in the constitutive
restrictions.  The electric-potential-gradient, bulk-source, and boundary-power
terms have already been separated through
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}; they are not included in either
local thermal entropy supply.

The connection with a conventional thermo-electroelastic energy balance is most
transparent in the nonporous, single-phase limit.  Let
\(\mc G=\{\xi\}\), set \(\phi_\xi=1\), suppress conversion, composition,
interfacial, and interphase-exchange terms, and let the skeleton observer follow
phase \(\xi\).  Phase mass conservation then gives
\(\phasedot\rho_\xi/\rho_\xi=-\operatorname{tr}\mbf L_\xi\).  Using
\(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\),
\(\phasedot{\mbf E}=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\phasedot\varphi-\mbf L_\xi^T\mbf E\), and
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}, the electrical terms in
\eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance} and
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity} combine into the material-volume
rate of \(\omega_\xi^+\), and the balance reduces to
%
\begin{align}
	\rho_\xi
	\frac{D_\xi}{Dt}
	\left(
	e_\xi+\frac{\omega_\xi^+}{\rho_\xi}
	\right)
	={} &
	\rho_\xi\phasedot e_\xi
	+
	\phasedot\omega_\xi^+
	+
	\omega_\xi^+\operatorname{tr}\mbf L_\xi
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\left(
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
	-
	\bar p_\xi\mbf I
		\right):\mbf L_\xi
		-
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf q_\xi
		+
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\phasedot\varphi\,\mbf d
		\right)
		-
		\varrho\phasedot\varphi
		+
		\rho_\xi r_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_single_phase_energy_limit}
\end{align}
%
Reference~\cite{montanaro2015thermo_electroelasticity} instead writes its
equation~(12) for the material internal energy \(\varepsilon\), with electrical
power \(\mbf E^{M}\cdot\rho\phasedot{\bs\pi}\), where
\(\bs\pi=\mbf P/\rho\) is the polarization per unit mass.  Combining that
balance with the electric-work term in its Helmholtz free-energy definition,
equation~(16), gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho
	\frac{D}{Dt}
	\left(
	\varepsilon-\mbf E^{M}\cdot\bs\pi
	\right)
	=
	\bs\tau:\mbf L
	-
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf q
	-
	\mbf P\cdot\phasedot{\mbf E}^{M}
	+
	\rho r .
	\label{eq:MC_montanaro_electric_enthalpy_limit}
\end{equation}
%
Equations \eqref{eq:MC_single_phase_energy_limit} and
\eqref{eq:MC_montanaro_electric_enthalpy_limit} are equivalent descriptions of
the same single-phase thermo-electroelastic energy balance under the
energy-density and stress identifications derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:single_phase_energy_audit}.
Reference~\cite{montanaro2015thermo_electroelasticity} writes the polarization
work \(-\mbf P\cdot\phasedot{\mbf E}^{M}\) on the right-hand side and treats
polarization as a state variable.  The present formulation instead uses the
total electric displacement \(\mbf d\), which Gauss' law relates directly to
free charge, and the electric enthalpy \(\omega_\xi^+\), whose dependence on
density, saturation, composition, and temperature represents the effective
dielectric response of the porous medium.  The Maxwell stress enters the
mechanical power through \(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}\), and the free-charge
source and electrical boundary flux appear explicitly on the right-hand side
of \eqref{eq:MC_single_phase_energy_limit}.  The field-power identity
\eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}, vacuum-displacement split, and
associated isotropic stress shift convert between the two accounting
conventions and relate the polarization-work term
\(-\mbf P\cdot\phasedot{\mbf E}\) used by Montanaro to the free-charge and
boundary-flux terms displayed here.  Polarization is therefore recovered
constitutively from \(\mbf P=\mbf d-\varepsilon_0\mbf E\) and is not an
additional state variable.  An independent polarization evolution law would be
required only for relaxation, hysteresis, ferroelectricity, or other
additional dielectric physics.

The solid stress power is decomposed by
\(\mbf F=\mbf A_s\bar{\mbf F}_s\),
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf L_s
	=
	\phasedot{\mbf A}_s\mbf A_s^{-1}
	+
	\mbf A_s
	\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_F_split_for_power}
\end{equation}
%
Using \eqref{eq:MC_solid_kinematic_splits}, the solid stress power is
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+}:\mbf L_s
	={} &
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
		\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^e
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+})^T
		\mbf A_s^e
		\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^p
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}

The local entropy inequality is written in the same skeleton frame as the
subsystem energy balances.  Its entropy flux contains entropy convected through
the skeleton control surface, entropy transported by the gauge-invariant
material-energy flux, and the chemical-potential-weighted contribution

enters through the gauge-invariant volume power in
\eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}, not through a potential-dependent entropy flux.
In the \(N-1\) independent component coordinates, the neutral contribution is

The admissible rates in this inequality already satisfy the four constraints
introduced in \sref{virtual_power_derivation}, the component charge balance

electrostatic power identity \eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  The

enforced by the augmented component material measure and its density and
composition kinematics; and \(C_4\) imposes zero net insertion.

\(\Pi_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot\eta_\xi^\alpha=0\), which permits all \(N\)
mass-fraction rates to be retained until the composition coefficients are
reduced to \(N-1\) independent differences by subtracting the
reference-component coefficient.

applied separately to temperature-weighted fluid and solid field powers.

\eqref{eq:MC_reduced_entropy_inequality} and the separate term containing the

phase-following thermodynamic rate is expressed in the skeleton frame.  Indeed,
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_skeleton_transport_identity} gives the exact identity

\begin{equation}

	\label{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity}
\end{equation}

Consequently, the second term on the left is not an additional constitutive
force conjugate to bulk phase velocity.  Separating it from the first term is
only an observer-dependent expansion of the single phase-following rate on the
right.  Treating that term as an independent dissipative force would count
the same thermodynamic rate twice: once through the Coleman--Noll coefficients
of the phase state variables and again through a bulk interaction law.

In \eqref{eq:MC_reduced_entropy_inequality}, the interfacial contribution is the
negative of the storage rate
\eqref{eq:MC_interfacial_helmholtz_rate}.  The remaining derivatives of
\(\omega_\xi^+\) measure how electrical storage changes with density,
composition, and temperature.  The explicit relative-velocity term is retained
in the intermediate skeleton-rate form so that the change of observer remains
visible.  Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity} does
not state that this term vanishes.  It states that the term must be recombined
with the skeleton-rate free-energy contribution before the collected inequality
is separated into reversible and residual parts.  The explicit reassembly is
given after the collected inequality; applying the reversible restrictions to
its residual block alone would use the identity incorrectly.
The Maxwell part of the stress power is removed
from the total mechanical power by
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}:\mbf L_\xi
	-
	\bs\sigma_\xi^+:\mbf L_\xi
	=
	\bs\sigma_\xi':\mbf L_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_material_stress_power_identity}
\end{equation}
The material stress \(\bs\sigma_\xi'\) is therefore conjugate to the material
Helmholtz-energy rate, while the momentum equation continues to use the total
stress \(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}\).  This is the same division obtained from an
electromagnetic energy-flux balance: the field contribution supplies Maxwell
power, and the remaining mechanical power acts on the material free energy
\cite{nika2025heterogeneous_dielectrics}.  Both the displacement relation
\(\mbf d_\xi=-\partial\omega_\xi^+/\partial\mbf E\) and the Maxwell stress
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition} are generated from the same phase
electric enthalpy \cite{dorfmann2017nonlinear_electroelasticity}.

\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_free_energy_rate}--\eqref{eq:MC_solid_free_energy_rate}, the
solid density rate \eqref{eq:MC_solid_density_rate}, and the stress-power split
\eqref{eq:MC_stress_power_split}.  Next use
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate} to separate component
insertion into conversion and relative-flux contributions.  The neutral
component-source term and entropy-flux divergence combine by the product rule,
while the direct relative-current work in \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance} supplies
the electric-field part of the transport force.  A charge-rate projection
derived with the source terms below records the equivalent potential-charge
accounting separately.

corresponding boundary fluxes in the integrated statement.

		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
		\right]
		\right]\phasedot\theta_{\mc S}
		\label{eq:MC_collected_entropy_temperature}
\end{align}
\noindent\emph{Reaction and relative-transport rates.}
\par\nopagebreak[4]
\begin{align}
	{} & -
	\sum_m
	\left[
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
			\sum_\alpha
			\nu_{f (m)}^\alpha
			\left(
			\psi_f+L_f^\alpha
			\right)
		+
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
			\sum_\alpha
			\nu_{s (m)}^\alpha
			\left(
			\psi_s+L_s^\alpha
			\right)
				\right]r_{(m)}
	\notag                  \\
	       & -
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right]
		\label{eq:MC_collected_entropy_reaction_transport}
\end{align}
\noindent\emph{Plastic, interaction-energy, heat, and intermediate
observer-conversion terms.}
\par\nopagebreak[4]
\begin{align}
	{} & +
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s^e
		\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^p
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]
		\notag                  \\
	       & +
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
		\mbf A_s^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
		\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		\right]
		\notag                  \\
	       & +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\left[
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		-
		\rho_\xi
		\left(
		\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\left(
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\psi_\xi
		+
		\mathfrak{s}_\xi
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
		\right)
	\right]
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\frac{
		\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	}{
		\theta_{\mc G}^2
		}
		.
		\label{eq:MC_collected_entropy_residual}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%

Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy} is an audit expansion in
skeleton-rate coordinates that groups the collected entropy into the mechanism
blocks below; the reversible--residual division is applied after the observer
reassembly.  In particular, the first term on the left of
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity} has been distributed
among the density, composition, temperature, and solid-kinematic coefficients
in \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_elastic_distension}--%
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_temperature}, while its observer correction
appears intact in \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_residual}.  To show the
required operation explicitly, solve
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity} for that correction
and sum over the phases:
%
\begin{align}
	&-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\psi_\xi
	+
	\mathfrak{s}_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	\right)
	\notag \\
	&\quad =
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\dot{\psi}_\xi
	+
	\mathfrak{s}_\xi\dot{\theta}_{\mc G}
	\right)
	+\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\rho_\xi
	\left(
	\phasedot\psi_\xi
	+
	\mathfrak{s}_\xi\phasedot\theta_{\mc G}
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly}
\end{align}
%
The final positive sum in
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly} cancels the negative
skeleton-rate free-energy contribution that was expanded into the preceding
blocks of \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy}.  This is an algebraic
reassembly of the complete collected inequality; both contributions remain
in the entropy inequality through their phase-following replacement in the
second line.  What remains from the pair is the
phase-following rate in the second line.  For a mobile fluid phase its
constitutive chain rule is
%
\begin{equation}
	\dot{\psi}_f
	=
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\bar\rho_f}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\dot{\bar\rho}_f
	+
	\sum_\beta
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\dot{\eta}_f^\beta
	+
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\dot{\theta}_{\mc F},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F.
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_phase_following_free_energy_chain_rule}
\end{equation}
%
For every solid phase,
\(\mbf v_s=\mbf v_{\mc S}\), so the phase-following and skeleton-following
rates coincide and \eqref{eq:MC_solid_free_energy_rate} already supplies this
chain rule.  Only after
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly} is substituted into the
whole of \eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy} are the reversible restrictions
applied.  If the coefficients in the first four blocks were set to zero before
this reassembly, the observer correction in the residual block would
incorrectly survive as an independent force--flux product.

After this ordering is enforced, coefficients of elastic deformation,
intrinsic density, composition, temperature, and volume-fraction rates give
the reversible constitutive restrictions.  Reaction rates, component-relative
fluxes, plastic rates, interphase energy exchange, and heat flux remain as
dissipative force--rate products.  The resulting residual is treated in
the subsequent source-and-closure subsection.

\subsection{Reversible restrictions}
\label{sec:MC_reversible_restrictions}

After the observer correction has been reassembled according to
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly}, the reversible
restrictions are obtained by setting to zero the coefficients of rates that
can vary independently at a fixed constitutive state.  The stress
appearing in these coefficients is the material stress
\(\bs\sigma_\xi'\).  The total stress in the momentum equation is
\(\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}=\bs\sigma_\xi'+\bs\sigma_\xi^+\).

The coefficient of
\(\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^e(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}\) gives
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	\mbf A_s
	={} &
	\rho_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\right)^T
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\left(
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\bar\rho_s
	+
	\phi_s
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\bar\rho_s}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
	\bar\rho_s
	-
	\phi_s\bar p_s
	\right)\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_stress_before_pressure}
\end{align}
%
For a fluid, \(\bs\sigma_f'=\mbf0\) because the Helmholtz energy has no elastic
deformation argument.  After the isotropic Maxwell contribution is combined
with the pressure relation, the remaining nonpressure reversible stress enters
the fluid momentum balance with dynamic pressure lag
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary} through
\(\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f\); the fluid pressure is determined separately by
the intrinsic-density coefficient.

Setting the intrinsic-density coefficients to zero defines the fluid and solid
phase pressures,
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pressure_definitions}
	\begin{align}
		\bar p_f
		 & =
		\bar\rho_f^2
		\left.
		\p{\psi_f}{\bar\rho_f}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
		+
		\bar\rho_f
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\bar\rho_f}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E},
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F,
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_pressure_definition} \\
		\bar p_s
		 & =
		\bar\rho_s^2
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		+
		\bar\rho_s
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\bar\rho_s}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_pressure_definition}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Using the solid pressure relation in the elastic-deformation coefficient removes
the spherical density term and gives the material solid stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\mbf A_s^{-T}
	\left(
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
	\right)
	\mbf A_s^T,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
The electrical part of the solid phase-map variation supplies the solid Maxwell
stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{s}
	=
	\left.
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	\right\vert_{\xi=s},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_solid_electrical_stress_contribution}
\end{equation}
%
The total solid stress is
\(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+}=\bs\sigma_s'+\bs\sigma_s^+\).  This total stress enters
momentum balance, whereas only \(\bs\sigma_s'\) is conjugate to the solid
material free-energy rate.

For the linear dielectric choice
\(\omega^{+}_{\xi}=-\frac{1}{2}\mbf E\cdot\mbf d_\xi\), the sum of the
phase-attributed electrical stresses is the usual Maxwell stress
\(\mbf E\otimes\mbf d-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mbf E\cdot\mbf d\right)\mbf I\), with
\(\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\).

The coefficient of the elastic distension rate gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\mbf A_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	(\mbf A_s^e)^T,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_distension_force_definition}
\end{equation}
%
The solid and fluid volume-fraction rates are constrained by
\(\sum_\xi\phasedot\phi_\xi=0\), so their reversible coefficients are determined up
to the common multiplier \(\lambda\).  The multiplier power was retained in
both subsystem energy balances; consequently, the same \(\lambda\) appears in
the solid and fluid coefficients even when their temperatures differ.

For the fluid phases, let
\(\phasedot\phi=D_{\mc S}\phi/Dt\) and use
\(\phasedot\phi_f=S_f\phasedot\phi+\phi\phasedot S_f\), with
\(\sum_f\phasedot S_f=0\).  The fluid volume-fraction power then separates into a
pore-volume part and a saturation-redistribution part:
%
\begin{align}
	&\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\lambda+\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
	\right)\phasedot\phi_f
	\notag\\
	&\qquad={}
	\left[
	\lambda
	+\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
	\right)
	\right]\phasedot\phi
	+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
	\right)\phasedot S_f .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_rate_decomposition}
\end{align}
%
The solid volume-fraction rates and the total fluid pore-volume rate remain
reversible.  Setting their coefficients to zero gives
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_volume_fraction_restrictions}
	\begin{align}
		0
		      & =
		\lambda
		+
		\bar p_s
		-
		\omega^{+}_{s},
		\quad &
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_volume_fraction_restriction} \\
		0
		      & =
		\lambda
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f
		\left(
		\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
		\right).
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_restriction}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The second relation and
\(\sum_fS_f\gamma_f=\gamma\) preserve the equivalent-pressure identity for the
actual phase pressures
\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}.  The final term in
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_rate_decomposition} is retained in the
residual inequality and closed by the dynamic pressure-lag Onsager relation.  If
\(\mc T=\mbf0\), that closure makes the phase coefficients equal; the common
pore-volume relation then recovers the individual reversible fluid relations.
This split does not require \(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}\).

Only \(N-1\) composition rates are independent in each fluid phase.  Subtracting
the component-\(N\) coefficient from the other coefficients gives
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	&\rho_f
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\psi_f}{\eta_f^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
	\right)
	+
	\phi_f
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\eta_f^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\eta_f^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E}
	\right)
	\notag \\
	&\qquad =
	\frac{\pi_f^\beta}{\eta_f^\beta}
	-
	\frac{\pi_f^N}{\eta_f^N},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N-1,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_composition_projection}
\end{empheq}
%
The corresponding component differences for a solid phase also contain the
composition dependence of its stress-free maps,
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	& \rho_s
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\eta_s^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\right)
	+
	\phi_s
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\eta_s^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\eta_s^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
	\right)
	\notag    \\
	& \quad
	-
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left(
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		-
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\eta_s^N}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		\right)
		(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
		\right]
	\notag    \\
	& \quad
	-
	\operatorname{tr}\left[
	\left(
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
	\right)
	\right.
	\notag    \\
	& \left.
	\quad
	\left(
	\left.
	\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	-
	\left.
	\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^N}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	\right)
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
	\right]
	\notag    \\
	& \qquad
	=
	\frac{\pi_s^\beta}{\eta_s^\beta}
	-
	\frac{\pi_s^N}{\eta_s^N},
	\qquad
	\beta=1,\ldots,N-1,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_solid_composition_projection}
\end{empheq}
%
Finally, \(\phasedot\theta_{\mc F}\) and \(\phasedot\theta_{\mc S}\) are independent, so
their coefficients determine the total entropy of each thermal subsystem.  The
phasewise caloric energies transported by phases with distinct velocities
require an allocation within those totals.  A sufficient constitutive
specialization assigns the temperature derivative of each phase energy to that
phase and retains the interfacial entropy as separate fluid-subsystem storage:
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_entropy_definitions}
	\begin{align}
		\rho_f\mathfrak{s}_f
		={} &
		-\rho_f
		\left.
		\p{\psi_f}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f}
		-\phi_f
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{f}}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_f,\mbf E},
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F,
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_entropy_definition}
		\\
		\mathfrak{s}_\gamma
		={} &
		-
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h},
		\label{eq:MC_interfacial_entropy_definition}
		\\
		\rho_s\mathfrak{s}_s
		={} &
		-\rho_s
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		-\phi_s
		\left.
		\p{\omega^{+}_{s}}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s,\mbf E}
		\notag  \\
		    & +
		\operatorname{tr}\left[
			(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
			(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
			(\bs\sigma_s')^T
			\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
			\left.
			\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\theta_{\mc S}}
			\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
			(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
		\right]
		\notag  \\
		    & +
		\operatorname{tr}\left[
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
		\mbf A_s^{-1}
		(\bs\sigma_s')^T
		\mbf A_s
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
		\right],
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S.
		\label{eq:MC_solid_entropy_definition}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Summing \eqref{eq:MC_fluid_entropy_definition} over the mobile phases and
adding \(\phi\) times \eqref{eq:MC_interfacial_entropy_definition} recovers
the fluid-subsystem temperature coefficient.  Summing
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_entropy_definition} recovers the solid-subsystem
coefficient.  The phasewise relations are the caloric allocation used by the
transport terms, while their subsystem sums are the Coleman--Noll
restrictions.

\subsection{Sources, residual dissipation, and closures}
\label{sec:MC_residual_dissipation}

The reversible restrictions of \sref{MC_reversible_restrictions} settle the
reversible stress, pressure, and energy relations.  This subsection assembles
what remains.  It first converts the component-supply terms into the
thermodynamic potentials and forces that appear in the source projection: the
within-phase transfer-work potential \(L_\xi^\alpha\), the conversion
affinity, and the relative-transport force
(\sref{MC_component_supply_forces}).  Substituting the reversible restrictions
and these forces into the collected entropy inequality then leaves the residual
dissipation inequality (\sref{MC_residual_inequality}).  The remaining
subsubsections close each dissipative mechanism admitted by that inequality:
dynamic pressure lag and capillary history
(\sref{MC_capillary_history_closure}), reaction and relative transport
(\sref{MC_reaction_transport_closures}), plastic flow
(\sref{MC_plastic_flow_closures}), interphase mechanical interaction
(\sref{MC_interphase_mechanical_interaction}), and thermal exchange and
conduction (\sref{MC_thermal_exchange_conduction}).  The mechanism index
\((m)\) covers chemical reaction, phase transformation, sorption or
desorption, and ion exchange; ``conversion'' is the generic term for any such
local process.  Three reference choices recur: the reference component \(N\),
the phase transfer-work potentials \(L_\xi^N\), and the solid transfer
reference \(s_\star\), each introduced where first needed.

\subsubsection{Component supply and thermodynamic forces}
\label{sec:MC_component_supply_forces}

The material increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) records both conversion and
component transport relative to phase \(\xi\).  Its variational coefficient
\eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} contains the specific generalized
transfer-work potential
\(L_\xi^\alpha\), while the entropy inequality contains the combination
\(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\) multiplying the component-supply rate.

The stress-free maps of a solid phase vary with composition, and
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_elastic_arguments} gives
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_composition_elastic_chain_rule}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^e}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{
			\mbf A_s,\mbf A_s^p,\theta_{\mc S}
		}
		={} &
		-\mbf A_s^e\mbf A_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\mbf A_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\mbf A_s^0)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_distension_composition_chain_rule}
		\\
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{
			\bar{\mbf F}_s,\bar{\mbf F}_s^p,\theta_{\mc S}
		}
		={} &
		-\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\bar{\mbf F}_s^p
		\left.
		\p{\bar{\mbf F}_s^0}{\eta_s^\beta}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0)^{-1}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_true_deformation_composition_chain_rule}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The component potential introduces no new primary field: an equation of state
or free-energy material evaluates it algebraically.

Using \(\rho_\xi=\sum_\alpha\rho_\xi^\alpha
=\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\), the constituent-mass chain rule together with the
density relation \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_storage} and the component
stationarity relation \eqref{eq:Pi_componentwise} gives the phase Euler
identity
%
\begin{equation}
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\psi_\xi
	+
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}.
	\label{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Appendix~\ref{app:component_potential_euler_derivation} gives the complete
constituent-mass chain rule leading to
\eqref{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity}.  For solids,
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_composition_elastic_chain_rule} and the material-stress
relations convert the implicit elastic derivatives into the two stress-power
terms in \eqref{eq:MC_solid_composition_projection}, so the same cancellation
applies.

Only the within-phase differences of the single specific generalized
transfer-work potential are fixed at this stage.  Subtracting the
reference-component form of \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} from its
component-\(\alpha\) form gives
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N
	={} &
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
	-
	\frac{\pi_\xi^N}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^N}
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N,
	\qquad
	\alpha=1,\ldots,N-1 .
	\label{eq:MC_transfer_work_component_difference}
\end{empheq}
%
The second equality subtracts the constituent derivatives
\eqref{eq:MC_absolute_neutral_component_potential}; the common density and
normalization terms cancel and leave the chemical-potential difference
supplied by the composition restrictions
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_composition_projection} and
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_composition_projection}.  Consequently,
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	-
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^N+z_\xi^N\varphi
	\right)
	=
	\mu_\xi^\alpha-\mu_\xi^N .
	\label{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_potential_difference}
\end{equation}
%
The generalized transfer potentials therefore retain the usual within-phase
electrochemical-potential differences even though their absolute values need
not equal the electrochemical potentials away from phase equilibrium.  At
nonuniform temperature the transport force uses the equivalent gauge-invariant
split into a component-potential thermal gradient and the electric field, derived
with the relative-transport force below.

Equations \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} and
\eqref{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity} also identify the common
within-phase offset explicitly:
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	-
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_phase_offset}
\end{equation}
%
The right-hand side is independent of the component index, which is the
absolute counterpart of
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_potential_difference}.
Equivalently, every specific generalized transfer-work potential is closed by
the explicit algebraic recovery
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	L_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	-
	\psi_\xi
	+
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi .
	\label{eq:MC_transfer_work_full_recovery}
\end{empheq}
%
The phase free-energy model supplies \(\psi_\xi\) and \(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\),
and the transfer-potential equation determines the component-independent offset
\(D_\xi\tau/Dt-\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi/2\).

To make the retained-\(\tau\) system square, choose one solid phase
\(s_\star\) as the transfer reference.  All solid phases follow the skeleton,
so \(D_{s_\star}/Dt=D_{\mc S}/Dt\).  The normalization
%
\begin{equation}
	\psi_{s_\star}+L_{s_\star}^N
	=
	\hat\mu_{s_\star}^N,
	\qquad
	s_\star\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_transfer_work_reference_normalization}
\end{equation}
%
fixes the common additive level of the specific transfer-work potentials.  The
reference-component form
of \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} then supplies the single evolution
equation
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{D_{\mc S}\tau}{Dt}
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
	+
	\hat\mu_{s_\star}^N
	-
	\psi_{s_\star}
	-
	\frac{\pi_{s_\star}^N}
	{\phi_{s_\star}\bar\rho_{s_\star}\eta_{s_\star}^N}
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S},
	\qquad
	s_\star\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_admissible_conversion_component}
\end{align}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_admissible_conversion_component} evolves \(\tau\) by
integration along each skeleton trajectory; for a stationary skeleton
\(D_{\mc S}\tau/Dt=0\) and \(\tau\) retains its initial value.
Appendix~\ref{sec:technical_setting} states the corresponding additive
normalization of that initial value.  The second equality follows from
\eqref{eq:MC_neutral_component_euler_identity}; the expanded first line records
the thermodynamic normalization used by the general phase equations.
Once \(\tau\) is known, the remaining \(P-1\) phase transfer-work potentials are
recovered algebraically from
%
\begin{equation}
	L_\xi^N
	=
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	+
	\frac{\pi_\xi^N}
	{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^N},
	\qquad
	\xi\ne s_\star .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_reference_transfer_work_recovery}
\end{equation}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_full_recovery}, or equivalently
\eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_component_difference}, then reconstructs every
\(L_\xi^\alpha\) with \(\alpha\ne N\).  Thus one scalar evolution
determines \(\tau\), \(P-1\) algebraic equations determine the remaining phase
transfer-work potentials, and the component-difference restrictions introduce
no additional primary fields.

Substituting the component material-rate identity
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate} into the
temperature-weighted supply power separates conversion from transport relative
to the phase,
%
\begin{align}
	 & -
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\notag     \\
	 & \quad =
	-\sum_m
	\left[
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
				\right]r_{(m)}
	\notag \\
	 & \qquad +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\frac{
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection}
\end{align}
%
The traditional electrochemical affinity of mechanism \(m\) is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	\equiv
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_affinity_projection}
\end{equation}
%
For a mechanism satisfying \eqref{eq:mechanism_charge_conservation},
the explicit electrical term cancels from the affinity:
%
\begin{align}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	={} &
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\notag \\
	={} &
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	-
	\varphi
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	z_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\notag \\
	={} &
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:electrochemical_affinity_charge_cancellation}
\end{align}
%
Charge conservation for each mechanism cancels the common electric-potential
term from its traditional affinity.  Electrical effects nevertheless remain in
the constitutive state dependence of \(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\), in the field force
on charged transport, and in electrochemical phase equilibrium.

Substituting the phase offset
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_phase_offset} into the generalized
stoichiometric sum and using the affinity definition
\eqref{eq:MC_affinity_projection} gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	+
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		-
		\frac{1}{2}
		\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}
\end{equation}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation} is an identity
that expresses the conversion driving force in two equivalent representations.
When the transfer-work term vanishes, the identity reduces to the usual
generalized-coefficient representation of \(\mc A_{(m)}\), whose value is
fixed separately by the thermodynamic-equilibrium condition.  The
transfer-work term is associated with net mass insertion into a phase; it
vanishes for a chemical reaction that conserves mass separately within every
phase and at full reversible equilibrium, and it contributes for
phase-change, dissolution, precipitation, and pore-filling mechanisms that
move net mass between phases.

For a mechanism confined to one thermal subsystem, the conversion contribution
in \eqref{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection} equals
\(\mc A_{(m)}r_{(m)}/\theta_{\mc G}\) when the mechanism conserves mass
separately within every participating phase; a phase-changing mechanism
retains the transfer-work term in
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}.  A fluid--solid
mechanism spans two different temperatures, so its contribution is written as
the temperature-weighted sum
%
\begin{equation}
	-
	r_{(m)}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_temperature_weighted_reaction_power}
\end{equation}
%
This neutral reaction contribution is gauge invariant for every mechanism;
the charge-rate identity below records the corresponding gauge accounting.
When a mechanism transfers net charge between the fluid and solid subsystems,
the reaction-energy transfer is carried as a separate contribution, a local
bulk representative-volume effect, and is coupled to the neutral reaction
force through the mechanismwise admissibility condition of
\sref{MC_reaction_transport_closures}.

Multiplying the complete component-rate balance by
\(\varphi z_\xi^\alpha/\theta_{\mc G}\), applying the product rule to the
relative charge flux, and summing gives the temperature-weighted charge-rate
identity
%
\begin{align}
	0
	={} &
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left\{
		\frac{\varphi z_\xi^\alpha}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\left[
		\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha
		-
		\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha r_{(m)}
		\right]
		\right.
	\notag \\
	& \left.
		\qquad
		+
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\varphi z_\xi^\alpha
			\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)
		\right]
		-
		\frac{z_\xi^\alpha}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right\}.
	\label{eq:MC_charge_rate_entropy_projection}
\end{align}
%
Equation \eqref{eq:MC_charge_rate_entropy_projection} is gauge invariant as a
whole.  Under \(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\), its change is
\(c_\varphi(t)/\theta_{\mc G}\) times the complete charge-weighted component
balance and therefore vanishes.  The energy representation retains the direct,
gauge-invariant relative-current work of \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}, so the
divergence and electric-field pieces of the identity enter the first law only
through that combined work.  The charge-rate identity therefore serves as an
alternative accounting check, and the reaction coefficient is the neutral
coefficient \(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\) for every mechanism.  When charge is
conserved separately within each thermal subsystem, the temperature-weighted
stoichiometric sum may equivalently be written with electrochemical
coefficients, because the added electrical sum vanishes.  For charge transfer
between subsystems, gauge invariance requires the neutral form; its physical
reaction-energy transfer is coupled through the mechanismwise admissibility
condition of \sref{MC_reaction_transport_closures}.

In the local-thermal-equilibrium limit,
\(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}=\theta\), total charge conservation permits the
common electric-potential term to be restored inside the full stoichiometric
sum, and this expression becomes
%
\begin{align}
	-
	r_{(m)}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	&=
		-\frac{r_{(m)}}{\theta}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\notag \\
	&=
		\frac{r_{(m)}}{\theta}
	\left[
		\mc A_{(m)}
		-
		\sum_\xi
		\left(
			\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
			-
			\frac{1}{2}
			\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
		\right)
		\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\right].
	\label{eq:MC_reaction_power_local_thermal_equilibrium}
\end{align}
%
For phasewise mass-conserving chemistry, or when the transfer-work offsets
vanish at reversible equilibrium, this is the usual affinity power
\(\mc A_{(m)}r_{(m)}/\theta\).
Thus chemical conversion and interphase energy exchange remain distinct
contributions to the two-temperature entropy inequality, as in Drumheller's
constituent formulation \cite[eq.~(88)]{drumheller2000}.  Their closures may be
uncoupled or Onsager-coupled, subject to the combined dissipation inequality
and the interaction-energy conservation condition of
\sref{MC_interphase_mechanical_interaction}.

The remaining part of
\eqref{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection} is the power of component transport
relative to a phase.  Select the same reference component \(N\) used in the
Onsager calculation; the separate zero-sum convention
\eqref{eq:separate_relative_flux_sum_closure} converts the full component sum
to \(N-1\) independent potential differences.  Combining the component-relative
term already present in the entropy flux \eqref{eq:MC_entropy_inequality}, the
transport part of \eqref{eq:MC_helmholtz_source_projection}, and the direct
relative-current work in \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}, the product rule cancels
the neutral flux divergence pointwise and leaves the electric-field work
unchanged:
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		 & -
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\frac{\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)
		\right]
		\\
		 & \quad +
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
		\\
		 & \quad -
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\left(
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
				+
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\\
		 & \quad =
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\frac{
					\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
				}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right)
			+
			\frac{
				z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right].
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:relative_transport_product_rule}
\end{equation}
%
The divergence terms are retained in the strong form and contribute the
corresponding boundary fluxes in the integrated statement, while the product
rule reduces the volume part to the local production density.  The local
entropy production from relative transport is
%
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right].
	\label{eq:MC_relative_transport_power}
\end{equation}
%
After the zero-sum reduction, the first part is the standard neutral
molecular-diffusion force in nonisothermal mixture thermodynamics
\cite{bothe2015continuum,bothe2023diffusion_fluxes}; the second is the electric
force per temperature.  The complete force uses \(\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi=-\mbf E\)
rather than the gauge-dependent combination
\(\varphi\nabla_{\mbf x}(1/\theta_{\mc G})\), so it is invariant under
\(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\).  The present theory adopts the same
force for mechanical dispersion as a constitutive analogy to the
molecular-diffusion force.  At uniform local temperature, the two parts combine
into \(\nabla_{\mbf x}(\mu_\xi^\alpha-\mu_\xi^N)/\theta_{\mc G}\).
The observer and gauge rules collected in
Appendix~\ref{sec:technical_setting} confirm that the force is equivariant
under the Euclidean observer transformation and invariant under
\(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\), since \(c_\varphi(t)\) carries no
spatial gradient.

\subsubsection{Residual inequality}
\label{sec:MC_residual_inequality}

Plastic flow is driven by the material stress \(\bs\sigma_s'\).  Mapping \(\bs\sigma_s'\) through
the current distension and elastic true-deformation factors gives the stress
conjugate to the true-plastic rate.  Because true-plastic flow is isochoric, only
the deviatoric part performs plastic work.  Define that driving stress by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf S_s
	\equiv
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-1}
	\mbf A_s^{-1}
	\left(
	(\bs\sigma_s')^T
	-\frac{1}{3}
	\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right)\mbf I
	\right)
	\mbf A_s
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S.
	\label{eq:true_plastic_driving_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
The trace of \(\mbf S_s\) vanishes, so it performs no work on a spherical
plastic rate and is conjugate to the isochoric rate
\(\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:true_plastic_driving_stress_definition} extends
Drumheller's deviatoric true-plastic driving stress through the phase distension
map
\cite[eqs.~(108)--(112)]{drumheller2000}.

Applying the reversible pressure, stress, distension, composition, entropy,
and common pore-volume relations from
\sref{MC_reversible_restrictions} removes their rate coefficients from
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy}.  The saturation-redistribution term from
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_rate_decomposition} remains dissipative.
The coordinate-stationarity relation
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential} replaces its
pressure-force differences by the generalized saturation-force differences;
their common scalar performs no power because \(\sum_f\phasedot S_f=0\).
The component-supply terms
are then replaced by the temperature-weighted reaction and relative-transport
powers derived in \eqref{eq:MC_temperature_weighted_reaction_power} and
\eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_power}.  Gauss' law was already used in the
global field-power identity \eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity}.  The
component charge balance constrains the admissible rates and is recorded by
\eqref{eq:MC_charge_rate_entropy_projection}; its potential-dependent terms
are accounted through that balance and the field-power identity, so charge
accumulation and the field rate enter the residual only through the admissible
rates.

The bulk-translation term is removed from the residual only through the
ordering displayed in
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly}.  Substituting that
identity into the complete inequality first cancels the skeleton-rate
free-energy contribution recovered from
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_elastic_distension}--%
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_temperature}.  The remaining member of the pair
is the phase-following rate, whose fluid chain rule is
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_phase_following_free_energy_chain_rule}.  The reversible
relations from \sref{MC_reversible_restrictions} are then substituted into this
reassembled form.  Every term in
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_entropy_residual} is carried through the reassembly and
settled by those reversible restrictions; the identity itself supplies the
bulk-translation elimination.

This change of rate coordinates preserves the physical phase forces:
pressure, capillary, Maxwell, body-force, inertia, and conversion-insertion
powers remain in the phase momentum equations.  The observer correction
enters once, through the phase-following rate, and the reassembly absorbs the
coefficient
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\psi_\xi+
\mathfrak{s}_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}\)
into that rate.  The remaining bulk-translation constitutive mechanism is
interphase mechanical interaction, whose dissipated power is converted to the
internal-energy supplies below.  What remains is the residual dissipation inequality
%
\begin{align}
		0\le{}\; &
		\sum_m
		\left[
			-
			\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
				\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
				\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
				\left(
				\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
				\right)
			\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
			\right]r_{(m)}
			\notag\\
		         & +
		\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\phasedot S_f
		\notag\\
		         & -
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\frac{
					\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
				}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right)
			+
			\frac{
				z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right]
		\notag\displaybreak[3]\\
		         & -
		\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
		\cdot\phasedot{\mbf h}
		\notag\\
		         & +
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\bs\sigma_s':
		\mbf A_s^e
		\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^p
		(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
		\notag\\
		         & +
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\operatorname{tr}\left[
			\mbf S_s
			\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p
			(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
			\right]
			\notag\\
		         & +
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
			-
			\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
		\frac{
			\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
		}{
			\theta_{\mc G}^2
		}.
	\label{eq:MC_residual_dissipation}
\end{align}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} retains the saturation pressure-lag
and capillary-history force--rate products together with the other dissipative
mechanisms.  The bulk velocity is set by the phase momentum balance together
with the interphase drag law; pressure, capillarity, electrostatics, gravity,
inertia, and conversion all drive a moving phase through those balances, and
the residual inequality carries only the dissipative mechanisms listed.
Chemical-potential differences drive component-relative diffusion and
dispersion through \eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_power} and remain distinct
from the bulk drag force.  Thermo-osmotic or other cross effects enter through
an explicit positive-semidefinite coupled closure, separate from the
observer-conversion term in
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_rate_free_energy_transport_identity}.

\subsubsection{Dynamic pressure lag and capillary-history closures}
\label{sec:MC_capillary_history_closure}

The global Onsager statement \eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity} and its collected
variation \eqref{eq:collected_onsager_variation} include the independent
saturation rates.  The same generalized force supplies the d'Alembert work in
\eqref{eq:W_def}, and coordinate stationarity identifies
its phase differences through
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}.  Substitution of the
Onsager closure \eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_onsager_relation} into the
saturation term in \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} gives
%
\begin{align}
	&\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\phasedot S_f
	\notag\\
	&\qquad={}
	\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\sum_{f\ne f_\star}\sum_{g\ne f_\star}
	\mc T_{fg}\phasedot S_f\phasedot S_g
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_dissipation}
\end{align}
%
The nonnegativity holds for every saturation-rate vector when the resistance
is symmetric and positive semidefinite:
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc T_{fg}=\mc T_{gf},
	\qquad
	\sum_{f\ne f_\star}\sum_{g\ne f_\star}
	y_f\mc T_{fg}y_g\ge0
	\quad
	\text{for all real }\{y_f\}_{f\ne f_\star}.
	\label{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_resistance_admissibility}
\end{equation}
%
For two fluid phases, take \(f_\star=w\) and write
\(\mc T_{nw}\equiv\mc T_{nn}\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:pc_def} and
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}, together with
\eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_force_rate_relation} give
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	=
	p_{c,nw}
	+\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+
	+\mc T_{nw}\phasedot S_n,
	\qquad
	\mc T_{nw}\ge0,
	\qquad
	(w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:MC_two_phase_dynamic_capillary_pressure}
\end{empheq}
%
Because \(\phasedot S_n=-\phasedot S_w\), the final term may equivalently be written
\(-\mc T_{nw}\phasedot S_w\).  Setting \(\mc T=\mbf0\) recovers the zero-rate
electrocapillary relation obtained from
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}.  Imposing electrical
neutrality as well gives
\eqref{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery}.  This closure adds
rate dependence to the dissipative pressure response while the stored
interfacial energy retains only its saturation and history dependence.
The thermodynamic distinction between the phase-pressure difference and stored
capillary pressure, followed by a linear saturation-rate closure, is developed
by Hassanizadeh and Gray and Hassanizadeh, Celia, and Dahle
\cite{hassanizadeh1993thermodynamic,hassanizadeh2002dynamic}.  Laboratory
measurements support rate-dependent pressure differences and show that the
coefficient may depend on grain size and on the drainage--imbibition path
\cite{campsroach2010dynamic,mirzaei2013hysteretic}.

The reversible coordinate variation holds \(\mbf h\) fixed, so the
capillary-history state has no Euler--Lagrange equation.  Its skeleton-following
rate is an internal dissipative rate.  When it is uncoupled from the other
residual mechanisms, a sufficient pointwise restriction is
%
\begin{equation}
	0
	\le
	-
	\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\cdot\phasedot{\mbf h} .
	\label{eq:MC_capillary_history_admissibility}
\end{equation}
%
A material model supplies \(N_h\) evolution equations for \(\mbf h\) that
satisfy \eqref{eq:MC_capillary_history_admissibility}, with
\(\phasedot{\mbf h}=D_{\mc S}\mbf h/Dt\).  Drainage--imbibition rules may use the
saturation-rate direction to select loading or unloading and store reversal and
trapping data in \(\mbf h\).  Between switching events the state is continuous,
so the capillary pressure may follow primary, secondary, and higher-order
scanning paths \cite{wei2006capillary_hysteresis}.  The same history state may
also enter a separately calibrated relative-permeability closure.
Setting \(N_h=0\), or holding \(\mbf h\) fixed, recovers the single-valued
stored capillary law and removes the history-dissipation term.  The displayed
dynamic and history laws are uncoupled.  A joint Onsager block coupling
\(\phasedot S_f\) and \(\phasedot{\mbf h}\) is admissible when its symmetric resistance
is positive semidefinite; loading--unloading switches may instead retain the
present nonsmooth history rule.  Coupled capillary, transport, and thermal
kinetics are admissible when their combined force--rate form remains
nonnegative.

\subsubsection{Reaction and relative-transport closures}
\label{sec:MC_reaction_transport_closures}

The source-carried energy in
\eqref{eq:MC_local_equilibrium_mass_source_energy} accounts for the energy of
mass inserted into each recipient phase.  Any additional reaction-associated
internal-energy supply only reallocates energy among phases and must conserve
mixture energy mechanism by mechanism:
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\sum_\xi\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)}
		 & =0,
		\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_mixture} \\
		\left.
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)}
		 & =
		-
		\left.
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)} .
		\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_subsystems}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
For a mechanism confined to one thermal subsystem, the reaction-associated
allocation may be set to zero because the source-carried terms already enter
that subsystem balance.  For a mechanism spanning the two thermal subsystems,
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_subsystems} constitutively allocates
the latent or reaction heat between the two subsystem balances.

Inequality \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} is the admissibility condition
restricting the admissible kinetic laws.  The quadratic Onsager potential in
\sref{onsager_reaction_transport} supplies the following uncoupled
near-equilibrium choice for any mechanism:
%
\begin{equation}
	r_{(m)}
	=
	-K_{(m)}
	\left[
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right],
	\qquad
	K_{(m)}>0 .
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate}
\end{equation}
%
The force in \eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate} is gauge invariant.  For a
mechanism that conserves charge separately within each thermal subsystem, it
may equivalently be evaluated with electrochemical coefficients.  A mechanism
that transfers charge between the thermal subsystems may couple its reaction
rate to the physical reaction-energy transfer, but the pair must satisfy the
mechanismwise condition
%
\begin{align}
	0\le{}&
	\left[
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
				\right]r_{(m)}
	\notag \\
	&+
	\left(
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		-
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)
	\left.
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)} .
	\label{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}
\end{align}
%
The second term follows directly from the equal-and-opposite allocation in
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_subsystems}.  A positive-semidefinite
Onsager matrix acting on these two force--rate pairs is one coupled closure;
block-diagonal choices are also admissible when each term is nonnegative.
Both forces are gauge invariant in the direct-field energy representation.

Stationarity with respect to the dispersion and diffusion fluxes gives
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
	+
	\theta_{\mc G}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
		\frac{
			\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\right)
	+
	\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	 & =\mbf 0,
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_dispersion_stationarity}
	\\
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
	+
	\theta_{\mc G}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
		\frac{
			\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\right)
	+
	\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	 & =\mbf 0,
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N.
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_diffusion_stationarity}
\end{align}
%
The diffusion row is the inverse-mobility form of the reduced
\(N-1\)-component Fick--Onsager closure
\cite{bothe2015continuum,bothe2023diffusion_fluxes}.  For charged species,
Dreyer, Guhlke, and M{\"u}ller write the chemical-potential gradient and
electric force separately; under isothermal electrostatics their force combines
into the electrochemical-potential difference used here
\cite{dreyer2018bulk_surface_electrothermodynamics}.  The nonisothermal closure
keeps those two parts separate: the absolute component potential is divided by
\(\theta_{\mc G}\) before differentiation, whereas the electric force is
\((z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N)\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\).  This ordering is required
for electric-gauge invariance.

Solving these relations in the \(N-1\) independent flux coordinates gives
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	 & =-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\beta-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
			z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:MC_dispersion_closure}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	 & =-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\beta-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
			z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:MC_diffusion_closure}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,k}^N
	 & =
	-\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha,
	\qquad
	k\in\{{\rm disp},{\rm diff}\}.
	\label{eq:MC_relative_transport_closures}
\end{align}
%
The final row reconstructs the reference-component flux and enforces the
separate zero-sum specialization
\eqref{eq:separate_relative_flux_sum_closure}.  The reduced mobilities and
their inverses act only on the \(N-1\) independent component coordinates.
Consequently the laws use component-potential differences and the electric field
and are invariant under both a common phasewise component-potential shift and
\(\varphi\mapsto\varphi+c_\varphi(t)\).  At uniform temperature they reduce to
the familiar electrochemical-potential-gradient form.
Substitution of
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate} and
\eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_closures} into the reaction and transport part
of \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} gives
%
\begin{align}
	 & \sum_m
	\left[
		-
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
			\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
			\left(
			\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
			\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]r_{(m)}
	\notag \\
	 & \quad -
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right]
	\notag           \\
	 & \qquad=
	\sum_m
	\frac{r_{(m)}^2}{K_{(m)}}
	\notag           \\
	 & \qquad\quad +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
	\notag           \\
	 & \qquad\quad +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
	\ge0.
	\label{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_transport_dissipation}
\end{align}
%
Thus the reaction and relative-transport terms are dissipative when each
\(\mbf D_{\xi,k}\) is symmetric positive definite on the \(N-1\) independent
component coordinates.  Diffusion and dispersion may use different mobilities
under the same gauge-invariant nonisothermal force; the neutral limit follows by
setting \(z_\xi^\alpha=0\).  Dispersion may depend on resolved flow
and pore geometry~\cite{plumb1988dispersion,gray2009tcat_transport}.  Reaction,
diffusion, and dispersion may also be cross-coupled, provided their combined
force--rate form remains nonnegative~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.
When reaction and reaction-energy transfer are cross-coupled, their additional
block must satisfy
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}.

\subsubsection{Plastic-flow closures}
\label{sec:MC_plastic_flow_closures}

The two plastic powers in \eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} admit the following
uncoupled associated closures, analogous to Drumheller's sufficient
construction~\cite{drumheller2000}.  A plastic internal state vector
\(\mbf p_s\) per solid phase carries hardening, softening, and rate
dependence through the flow-rule mobilities; because the state set \(\mc X_s\)
contains the composition \(\{\eta_s^\alpha\}\), these are single phase-level
laws acting on the phase mixture as a whole rather than on individual
components:
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_flow_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s)
		(\mbf S_s)^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_deformation_flow_rule}            \\
		\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^p(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s)
		(\mbf A_s^e)^T
		\bs\sigma_s'
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-T},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_distension_flow_rule}             \\
		\phasedot{\mbf p}_s
		 & =
		\mbf g_s
		(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s,\mbf S_s,\bs\sigma_s'),
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_state_evolution} \\
		\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s)
		 & \ge0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_deformation_mobility_nonnegative} \\
		\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s)
		 & \ge0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_generic_plastic_distension_mobility_nonnegative}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Because \(\operatorname{tr}\mbf S_s=0\), the first closure preserves
isochoric true-plastic flow.  The nonnegative scalar mobilities make both
plastic powers nonnegative separately.  The mobilities depend on the
equilibrium state set \(\mc X_s\) and the plastic internal state vector
\(\mbf p_s\), whose evolution
\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_state_evolution} carries hardening, softening,
and rate dependence inside the plastic closure.  The additive specialization
retains these single phase-level laws.  Coupled or non-associated
plastic deformation--distension laws may replace
\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_flow_rules}, but their combined conjugate power in
\eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} must remain nonnegative.  This combined
criterion also permits a frictional dilation specialization: under compressive
mean stress, plastic dilation gives a negative plastic-distension power that is
supported by positive true-plastic dissipation.  A shared rate constraint and
stress-dependent
dissipation can then retain normality in the generalized forces while producing
a non-associated pressure-dependent rule after reduction to true-stress space
\cite{collins1997application}.

\subsubsection{Interphase mechanical interaction}
\label{sec:MC_interphase_mechanical_interaction}

The component-relative diffusion and dispersion fluxes were closed in
\sref{MC_reaction_transport_closures}.  The interaction considered here is
different: it is the momentum exchange caused by relative motion of the bulk
phases.  Conservation of total mixture energy requires the phase interaction
supplies associated with mechanical momentum exchange to satisfy
\cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
	\left.
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm mech}
	+\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	=0 .
	\label{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\mbf b_\xi\) is the momentum supplied to phase \(\xi\), while
\(\left.\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}\right\vert_{\rm mech}\) is the associated
internal-energy supply.  The full \(e_\xi^{\rm int}\) in the subsystem energy
balance is the sum of this mechanical contribution, ordinary heat exchange, and
any reaction-associated interphase energy transfer.  The reversible solid and
pore-volume constraint powers are absent because their coefficients have
already vanished through \eqref{eq:MC_volume_fraction_restrictions}; the
saturation-redistribution power remains separately in
\eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_dissipation}.
Because the free-energy convection term has been recombined with the
phase-following reversible rate, the entropy contribution of this mechanism is
the temperature-weighted mechanical internal-energy supply.  When it is closed
separately from reaction, plasticity, component-relative transport, and heat
conduction, its sufficient thermodynamic admissibility condition is
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\left.
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm mech}
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility}
\end{equation}
%
The cited mixture theories support complementary parts of this construction,
which \eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} assembles.  Drumheller
and Bedford's two-temperature inequality couples interaction-energy transfer to
relative mechanical power, and together with their total energy balance it is
the basis for requiring \eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} and
temperature-weighted interaction heating simultaneously
\cite[eq.~(4.8)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}.  Gray's porous-medium
inequalities pair solid-relative velocities with combined interaction and
thermodynamic forces, and his slow-flow example closes the phase momentum
through the pressure-gradient equation
\cite[eqs.~(33), (37), (44), (47), and (77)]{gray1999thermodynamics}.  Seguin
and Walkington provide the closest common-temperature analogue, whose
reduction yields a pressure-driven force balance, a drag law, and Darcy flow
\cite[eqs.~(15), (32), (33), (35), (37), (40), and (41)]{seguin2019multicomponent}.
Bothe and Dreyer make a related combined-force rearrangement for constituent
diffusion in a common-temperature miscible fluid, a comparison that concerns
diffusion and Gibbs--Duhem reduction
\cite[eqs.~(88)--(93)]{bothe2015continuum}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} and the
energy-conservation identity \eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} must
hold simultaneously.  A more general Onsager model may couple mechanical
interaction heating to heat transfer or reaction; then nonnegativity of the
coupled block is the operative requirement.

In a general multiphase closure, the momentum supply may be written as the sum
of antisymmetric pairwise forces,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_\xi
	=
	\sum_{\zeta\ne\xi}
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}
	=
	-\mbf b_{\xi\zeta},
	\label{eq:MC_pairwise_interaction_force}
\end{equation}
%
where \(\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}\) is the force exerted on phase \(\xi\) by phase
\(\zeta\).  The pairwise mechanical power is therefore
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\xi<\zeta}
	\mbf b_{\zeta\xi}\cdot
	\left(
	\mbf v_\xi-\mbf v_\zeta
	\right).
	\label{eq:MC_interaction_power_pairing}
\end{equation}
%
For a concrete reservoir-oriented closure, retain fluid--skeleton exchange,
use the common solid velocity, and neglect direct fluid--fluid drag.  The
fluid and aggregate solid momentum supplies are prescribed by
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_law}
	\begin{align}
		\mbf b_f
		={}&
		-\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right),
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F,
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_force}
		\\
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\mbf b_s
		={}&
		-\sum_{f\in\mc F}\mbf b_f,
		\label{eq:MC_aggregate_solid_interaction_force}
		\\
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}
		={}&
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^T>0,
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F .
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_drag_tensor}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_force} is a purely dissipative
drag law; the reversible pressure, capillary, Maxwell, body-force, inertia,
and conversion-insertion terms remain in the phase momentum balance, and
\(\mbf b_f\) carries only the dissipative drag.  Here
\(\mbf y\cdot\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}\mbf y>0\) for every
\(\mbf y\ne\mbf 0\).  Because all solid phases have velocity
\(\mbf v_{\mc S}\), \eqref{eq:MC_aggregate_solid_interaction_force} is
sufficient to determine the total solid interaction power.  A constituent
model may partition the aggregate solid supply among the solid phases without
changing the following result.

The associated internal-energy supplies are closed by
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_drag_heating_equal_partition}
	\begin{align}
		\left.
		\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		={}&
		\frac{1}{2}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right),
		\qquad
		f\in\mc F,
		\label{eq:MC_fluid_interaction_energy_supply}
		\\
		\left.
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		={}&
		\frac{1}{2}
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right).
		\label{eq:MC_solid_interaction_energy_supply}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Thus the nonnegative drag heating is divided equally between the fluid and solid
thermal subsystems.  This equal partition is a sufficient closure, not a
required microscopic statement about where viscous heating occurs.  Any other
partition is admissible if it satisfies both
\eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} and
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility}.  Summing
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_interaction_energy_supply} and
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_interaction_energy_supply}, and using
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_force} and
\eqref{eq:MC_aggregate_solid_interaction_force}, gives
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\left.
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		+
		\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	={}&
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left[
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\cdot
		\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)
		+
		\mbf b_f\cdot
		\left(
			\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
			\right)
	\right]
	=0 .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_skeleton_interaction_energy_verification}
\end{align}
%
Hence \eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} is satisfied exactly.
Substitution of the same energy supplies into
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} leaves
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		+
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)
	\left(
		\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)\cdot
	\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
	\left(
		\mbf v_f-\mbf v_{\mc S}
	\right)
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_positive}
\end{equation}
%
This proves the joint admissibility condition for arbitrary skeleton velocity:
only the relative velocities enter.  More general positive-semidefinite block
closures may retain direct fluid--fluid drag or couple this mechanism to heat
transfer, but \eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_law}--
\eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_positive} provide one complete local closure.

\subsubsection{Thermal exchange and heat conduction}
\label{sec:MC_thermal_exchange_conduction}

Ordinary heat exchange transfers internal energy between the fluid and solid
thermal subsystems even when there is no phase-relative motion.  It is distinct
from the drag-generated heating above and from the reaction-associated energy
exchange required by a charged cross-subsystem mechanism.  The reaction-associated
exchange remains paired with \(r_{(m)}\) in the coupled closure described after
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate}; the heat-transfer coefficient below
accounts for ordinary heat exchange.

A sufficient bulk fluid--solid heat-exchange law is
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left.
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm heat}
		 & =
		h_{\mc F\mc S}
		\left(
		\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
		\right),
		\\
		\left.
		\sum_{s\in\mc S}\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm heat}
		 & =
		-h_{\mc F\mc S}
		\left(
		\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
		\right),
		\qquad
		h_{\mc F\mc S}\ge0 .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_law}
\end{equation}
%
Together, the mechanical, ordinary-heat, and reaction-associated allocations
give the explicit mixture cancellation
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		+
		\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	={}&
	\sum_\xi
	\left(
		\left.
		\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
		\right\vert_{\rm mech}
		+
		\mbf b_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	\notag \\
	&+
	\left.
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\rho_f e_f^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm heat}
	+
	\left.
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\rho_s e_s^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{\rm heat}
	\notag \\
	&+
	\sum_m
	\left.
	\sum_\xi
	\rho_\xi e_\xi^{\rm int}
	\right\vert_{{\rm rxn},(m)}
	\notag \\
	={}&0 .
	\label{eq:MC_total_internal_exchange_cancellation}
\end{align}
%
The first group vanishes by
\eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation}, the second by
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_law}, and each term in the final sum by
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_allocation_mixture}.  Adding the global field-power
identity \eqref{eq:MC_electrostatic_power_identity} then completes the
material-plus-field first-law accounting.  The ordinary heat exchange
contributes
%
\begin{equation}
	h_{\mc F\mc S}
	\left(
	\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
	\right)
	\left(
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	-
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)
	=
	h_{\mc F\mc S}
	\frac{
		\left(
		\theta_{\mc S}-\theta_{\mc F}
		\right)^2
	}{
		\theta_{\mc F}\theta_{\mc S}
	}
	\ge0
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_positive}
\end{equation}
%
to the entropy production.

The simplest uncoupled closure represents the gauge-invariant material part of
the nonadvective energy flux within phase \(\xi\in\mc G\) by Fourier's law,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf q_\xi
	=
	-\mbf K_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G},
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\mbf K_\xi=\mbf K_\xi^T\ge0 ,
	\label{eq:MC_heat_flux_law}
\end{equation}
%
where
\(\mbf y\cdot\mbf K_\xi\mbf y\ge0\) for every \(\mbf y\), and
\(\theta_{\mc F},\theta_{\mc S}>0\).  More general closures may couple
\(\mbf q_\xi\) to the component-relative thermodynamic forces, provided the
combined heat--transport Onsager block remains positive semidefinite.  The
unresolved interfacial contribution is included in the fluid-subsystem sum
\(\sum_{f\in\mc F}\mbf q_f\); no additional interfacial heat-flux field is
introduced.  The
uncoupled closure gives the entropy contribution
%
\begin{equation}
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\frac{
		\mbf q_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	}{
		\theta_{\mc G}^2
	}
	=
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\frac{
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
		\cdot
		\mbf K_\xi
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc G}
	}{
		\theta_{\mc G}^2
	}
	\ge0 .
	\label{eq:MC_heat_flux_positive}
\end{equation}
%
The reaction and component-relative transport terms are controlled by
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_transport_dissipation}; the fluid--skeleton
interaction closure reduces the mechanical-interaction condition
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} to the nonnegative form
\eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_positive}; and ordinary heat exchange and
conduction are controlled by
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_solid_heat_exchange_positive} and
\eqref{eq:MC_heat_flux_positive}.  These are separate closures even though
their entropy contributions appear in the same residual inequality.

\subsection[Example mechanisms: reactions, phase transformations,
	sorption/desorption, and ion exchange]
{\raggedright Example mechanisms: reactions, phase transformations,
	sorption/desorption, and ion exchange}
\label{sec:MC_example_mechanisms}

The mechanism index \((m)\) covers any local conversion described by the
phase/component stoichiometry \(\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\).  The principal mechanism
types differ only in whether conversion changes the component identity, the
phase label, or both.

A true chemical reaction changes component identity.  It may occur entirely
within one phase or involve components from several phases.  For example,
aqueous carbonic-acid dissociation,
\(\mathrm{H_2CO_3}\rightleftharpoons
\mathrm{H^+}+\mathrm{HCO_3^-}\), changes the aqueous component populations
without transferring a component to another phase.  On a molar progress basis,
the signed molar coefficients are \((-1,+1,+1)\).  If \(M_\alpha\) denotes
component molar mass, the mass coefficients used in the balance and affinity
are
%
\begin{align}
	\left(
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{H^+}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}
	\right)
	&=
	\left(
	-M_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}},
	M_{\mathrm{H^+}},
	M_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}
	\right),
	\notag \\
	\sum_\alpha\nu_{f (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	-M_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}
	+
	M_{\mathrm{H^+}}
	+
	M_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}
	=0,
	\notag \\
	\frac{1}{F_{\rm Far}}
	\sum_\alpha
	z_f^\alpha\nu_{f (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	0+1-1
	=0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:MC_carbonic_acid_mass_charge_checks}
\end{align}
%
where
\(M_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}=
M_{\mathrm{H^+}}+M_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}\) and \(F_{\rm Far}\) is the Faraday
constant.  Thus both mass and charge conservation are explicit before these
coefficients enter the affinity and kinetic relations.

A pure phase transformation preserves component identity and changes only its
phase label.  Vaporization and condensation are examples.  For a component
\(g\) transferred from phase \(\xi\) to phase \(\zeta\), the one-to-one
stoichiometry below uses a mass-progress basis: \(r_{(m)}\) is mass
transferred per current mixture volume per time, and the dimensionless mass
yields are
%
\begin{equation}
	\nu_{\zeta (m)}^g=1,
	\qquad
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^g=-1 .
	\label{eq:MC_one_to_one_transfer_stoichiometry}
\end{equation}
%
All other stoichiometric coefficients vanish.  The corresponding affinity is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	\mu_\xi^g-\mu_\zeta^g .
	\label{eq:MC_one_to_one_transfer_affinity}
\end{equation}
%
The transformation can therefore change phase fractions and phase
compositions even though the total mass of component \(g\) is unchanged.  For
the same charged component in both phases, charge conservation gives
\(z_\xi^g=z_\zeta^g\), so the explicit electric-potential terms cancel.
The conversion power additionally contains the difference of the two
phase-transfer offsets according to
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}; it vanishes at
reversible phase equilibrium but need not vanish during a dynamic
transformation with relative phase motion.

Sorption/desorption is treated by the same one-to-one transfer structure, with
\(\xi=f\) a mobile phase and \(\zeta=s\) a solid phase whose constituents share
the skeleton motion.  It includes surface adsorption and bulk absorption
without requiring those pore-scale processes to be represented separately.
For a neutral component \(g\), away from local thermal equilibrium an
admissible kinetic law must satisfy
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\frac{
	\psi_f+L_f^g
	}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	-
	\frac{
	\psi_s+L_s^g
	}{\theta_{\mc S}}
	\right)r_{(m)}
	\ge0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_sorption_temperature_weighted_dissipation}
\end{equation}
%
Charged sorption that transfers net charge between the thermal subsystems uses
the same gauge-invariant neutral reaction force and must satisfy
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}; its reaction and
reaction-energy rates may be Onsager-coupled.
The sorbed amount \(\eta_s^g\) may also enter \(\psi_s\), \(\mbf A_s^0\),
\(\bar{\mbf F}_s^0\), and the plastic flow law.  Thus adsorption, desorption,
hydrate formation, and hydrate dissociation can change stiffness, swelling,
compaction, and other solid properties through the evolving composition of the
load-bearing phase.

\pagebreak[3]
Ion exchange is a coupled set of transfers between mobile ions and charged
solid-site components.  For example, calcium uptake accompanied by sodium
release can be represented by
\(2\,\mathrm{NaX}_s+\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}_f
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{CaX_2}_s+2\,\mathrm{Na}^{+}_f\), where
\(\mathrm{X^-}\) denotes a monovalent solid exchange site, so
\(\mathrm{NaX}_s\) and \(\mathrm{CaX_2}_s\) are neutral solid components.  The
signed molar coefficients in the order
\((\mathrm{NaX}_s,\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}_f,
\mathrm{CaX_2}_s,\mathrm{Na}^{+}_f)\) are
\((-2,-1,+1,+2)\).  The corresponding mass coefficients and conservation
checks are
%
\begin{align}
	\left(
	\nu_{s (m)}^{\mathrm{NaX}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}},
	\nu_{s (m)}^{\mathrm{CaX_2}},
	\nu_{f (m)}^{\mathrm{Na^+}}
	\right)
	&=
	\left(
	-2M_{\mathrm{NaX}},
	-M_{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}},
	M_{\mathrm{CaX_2}},
	2M_{\mathrm{Na^+}}
	\right),
	\notag \\
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	-2M_{\mathrm{NaX}}
	-M_{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}}
	+
	M_{\mathrm{CaX_2}}
	+
	2M_{\mathrm{Na^+}}
	=0,
	\notag \\
	\frac{1}{F_{\rm Far}}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	z_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	&=
	0-2+0+2
	=0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_ion_exchange_mass_charge_checks}
\end{align}
%
Here \(M_{\mathrm{NaX}}=M_{\mathrm{Na^+}}+M_{\mathrm X}\) and
\(M_{\mathrm{CaX_2}}=M_{\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}}+2M_{\mathrm X}\).  The mechanism
therefore changes fluid composition and solid-site occupancy while satisfying
both conservation restrictions.  Its driving force is the corresponding
electrochemical affinity; equilibrium or kinetic ion-exchange models provide
the remaining closure.

\subsection{Phase reaction-modified Biot coefficients}
\label{sec:MC_phase_biot_legendre_transform}

The entropy inequality gives the single-prime solid stress in intrinsic-density
variables.  Reservoir and poromechanics equations commonly use equivalent pore
pressure as a controlled variable.  A phase-level Legendre transform exchanges
intrinsic specific volume for \(\bar p_E\) and gives the corresponding
double-prime stress \cite{callen1985thermodynamics}.  We first apply this
transformation to the general phase energy and then develop the additive
component specialization.

The required current and reference specific volumes are
%
\begin{equation}
	v_s=\frac{1}{\rho_s},
	\qquad
	v_{s0}=\frac{1}{\rho_{s0}},
	\qquad
	\bar v_s=\frac{1}{\bar\rho_s},
	\qquad
	\bar v_{s0}=\frac{1}{\bar\rho_{s0}},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_specific_volumes}
\end{equation}
%
The solid volume-fraction restriction identifies the thermodynamic variable
conjugate to intrinsic specific volume,
%
\begin{align}
	\bar p_E
	 & =
	\bar p_s-\omega_s^+
	\notag \\
	 & =
	-
	\left.
	\p{
		\left(
		\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
		\right)
	}{\bar v_s}
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pressure_specific_volume}
\end{align}
%
Here \(\omega_s^+\bar v_s\) is the electric enthalpy per unit intrinsic solid
mass.  Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_phase_pressure_specific_volume} gives
\(-\bar p_E\) as the coefficient of \({\rm d}\bar v_s\) when the remaining
state variables are fixed.  The Legendre term \(\bar p_E\bar v_s\) converts
this coefficient into the pressure differential,
%
\begin{align}
	\left.
	\mathrm d\left(
	\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	-\bar p_E\,{\rm d}\bar v_s,
	\notag \\
	\left.
	{\rm d}\left(
	\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s+\bar p_E\bar v_s
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	\bar v_s\,{\rm d}\bar p_E,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_differential_cancellation}
\end{align}
%
Thus \(\bar p_E\) is conjugate to \(\bar v_s\), and the phase pressure satisfies
\(\bar p_s=\bar p_E+\omega_s^+\).  Define the transformed phase potential and
its pressure derivative by
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_transform}
	\begin{gather}
		\widetilde\psi_s
		=
		\psi_s
		+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
		+\bar p_E\bar v_s,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_transform_definition} \\
		\bar v_s
		=
		\left.
		\p{\widetilde\psi_s}{\bar p_E}
		\right\vert_{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_transform_pressure_derivative}
	\end{gather}
\end{subequations}
%
At fixed equivalent pressure, the common skeleton Jacobian \(J\) parameterizes
the specific-volume response.  Through \(J=a_s\bar J_s\), this response includes
the concurrent allocation of volume between elastic true deformation and
elastic distension.  The Legendre identity and the kinematic split give
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_phase_specific_volume_deformation_chain_rule}
	\begin{gather}
		\left.
		\mathrm d
		\left[
		\widetilde\psi_s
		-
		\left(
		\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
		\right)
		\right]
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
		=
		\bar p_E
		\left.
		\p{\bar v_s}{J}
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
		\mathrm dJ,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_phase_fixed_pressure_volume_differential} \\
		\mathrm dJ
		=
		a_s\bar J_s
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-T}
		:
		\mathrm d\bar{\mbf F}_s^e
		+
		a_s\bar J_s
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-T}
		:
		\mathrm d\mbf A_s^e,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_common_jacobian_elastic_variation}
	\end{gather}
\end{subequations}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_phase_fixed_pressure_volume_differential} evaluates the
constitutive derivative with respect to the common volume.  The kinematic
identity \eqref{eq:MC_common_jacobian_elastic_variation} resolves the
corresponding differential into independent elastic true-deformation and elastic-distension
contributions, with the plastic and stress-free states fixed.  The coefficient
of \(\mathrm d\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\) determines the intrinsic Piola stress, and the
coefficient of \(\mathrm d\mbf A_s^e\) determines the distension force.  The
fixed-pressure derivative \(\partial\bar v_s/\partial J\) contains the
equilibrated response of \(a_s\) along this common-volume path.
%
The Legendre identity gives two intrinsic Piola stresses.  Differentiating the
transformed potential at fixed \(\bar p_E\) gives the single-prime stress and
recovers its intrinsic-volume form.  Differentiating the electrothermodynamic
phase potential \(\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s\) along the same pressure path gives
the double-prime stress,
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pk_stresses}
	\begin{align}
		\bar{\mbf P}_s'
		 & =
		\bar\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{\widetilde\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\mbf A_s^e,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_phase_single_prime_pk_stress} \\
		\bar\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{\widetilde\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\mbf A_s^e,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
		 & =
		\bar\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar v_s,\mbf A_s^e,\{\eta_s^\alpha\},
			\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_phase_legendre_pk_identity}   \\
		\bar{\mbf P}_s^{\prime\prime}
		 & =
		\bar\rho_{s0}
		\left.
		\p{
			\left(
			\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
			\right)
		}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\mbf A_s^e,\theta_{\mc S},
			\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_phase_double_prime_pk_stress}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
To obtain the relation between the Piola stresses, set
\(\mathrm d\mbf A_s^e=\mbf 0\) in
\eqref{eq:MC_common_jacobian_elastic_variation}.  Substitute the resulting
expression for \(\mathrm dJ\) into
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_fixed_pressure_volume_differential}, then equate the
coefficients of \(\mathrm d\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\) using the stress definitions
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_single_prime_pk_stress} and
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_double_prime_pk_stress}.  This gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar{\mbf P}_s'
	=
	\bar{\mbf P}_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\bar p_E
	\frac{a_s}{\bar v_{s0}}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E}
	\bar J_s
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-T},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pk_legendre_split}
\end{equation}

Define the phase nonlinear Biot coefficient by
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar B_s
	\equiv
	1
	-
	\frac{a_s}{\bar v_{s0}}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\{\eta_s^\alpha\},\mbf E},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_biot_coefficient}
\end{empheq}
%
The factor \(1-\bar B_s\) is the fraction of a common volumetric perturbation
carried by the true-solid specific volume of phase \(s\) at the current reacted
state.  Its complement \(\bar B_s\) measures the distension response.  The
factor \(a_s\) supplies the current allocation of skeleton volume to the phase,
including packing and stress-free volume change.  In the nonreacting
single-solid specialization, aggregation of the intrinsic phase coefficient
with the solid volume fraction and use of \(a_s\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\) recover the
nonlinear Biot coefficient of \cite{foster2025}.  Substitution
into \eqref{eq:MC_phase_pk_legendre_split} gives the intrinsic Piola-stress
split,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar{\mbf P}_s'
	=
	\bar{\mbf P}_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\left(1-\bar B_s\right)
	\bar p_E
	\bar J_s
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^{-T},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_pk_biot_split}
\end{equation}
%
Push-forward through the true volume ratio \(\bar J_s\) and the distension map
gives the intrinsic Cauchy stresses.  Using
\(\bar\rho_{s0}=\bar J_s\bar\rho_s\) from
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_true_mass_conservation}, these maps are
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_intrinsic_cauchy_stress_maps}
	\begin{align}
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s'
		 & =
		\frac{1}{\bar J_s}
		\mbf A_s^{-T}
		\bar{\mbf P}_s'
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
		\mbf A_s^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_intrinsic_cauchy_push_forward} \\
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime}
		 & =
		\frac{1}{\bar J_s}
		\mbf A_s^{-T}
		\bar{\mbf P}_s^{\prime\prime}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
		\mbf A_s^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_intrinsic_double_prime_cauchy_push_forward}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Multiplication by the phase volume fraction gives the partial phase Cauchy
stresses,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\phi_s\bar{\bs\sigma}_s',
	\qquad
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
	=
	\phi_s\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_partial_cauchy_volume_fraction}
\end{equation}
%
Applying \eqref{eq:MC_intrinsic_cauchy_stress_maps} and
\eqref{eq:MC_partial_cauchy_volume_fraction} to
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_pk_biot_split} converts the pressure correction into the
partial Cauchy stress used in \eqref{eq:MC_solid_stress_definition}, giving
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_biot_split}
\end{empheq}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_split} applies to the general
composition-dependent phase energy.  The additive specialization in
\sref{MC_additive_component_specialization} resolves \(\bar B_s\) and
\(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}\) into component contributions.  The component
pressure-redistribution path preserves the fixed-\(\bar p_E\) derivative, and
the resulting contributions sum to \eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_coefficient}.

\subsection{Solid phase and overall momentum from the Biot--Terzaghi
effective-stress relation}
\label{sec:MC_phase_overall_momentum_biot}

The solid volume-fraction restriction gives the phase pressure in terms of the
equivalent pore pressure \(\bar p_E=-\lambda\),
%
\begin{align}
	\bar p_E
	 & =
	-\lambda
	\notag \\
	 & =
	\bar p_s-\omega_s^+,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_phase_equivalent_pressure}
\end{align}
%
Substituting the phase stress split into the solid momentum balance gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\rho_s\mbf a_s
		={} &
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
			+
			\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I
			+
			\bs\sigma_s^+
			\right]
		-\phi_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_s\omega_s^+\right)
		+\rho_s\mbf g+\mbf b_s
		\\
		    & +
		\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_s^\alpha
		\left(
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_s
		\right),
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_phase_momentum_biot}
\end{equation}
%
Plastic flow remains conjugate to \(\bs\sigma_s'\).  A constitutive model
calibrated with the fixed-pressure stress
\(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}\) must first use
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_split} to reconstruct \(\bs\sigma_s'\).  Maxwell stress
enters the momentum balance separately and affects plastic response indirectly
through the resulting deformation and material stress.

After the solid partial stresses are summed, the solid-phase Biot terms combine
as
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s'
	 & =
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}
	+
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I,
	\label{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_expanded}   \\
	\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}
	 & \equiv
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime},
	\label{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_definition} \\
	\Aboxed{
		B
	 & =
		1-\sum_{s\in\mc S}\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)
	}
	.
	\label{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_coefficient}
\end{align}
%
Because the phase partial stresses are all measured per unit current skeleton
volume, their pressure corrections add directly.  The aggregate coefficient is
therefore the combination in
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_coefficient}; in the single-solid limit it
reduces to \(B=1-\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\).

After summing the solid phase balances, the Biot-pressure and electrical parts in
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_phase_momentum_biot} can be rewritten using
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_definition},
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		 & \sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left(
			\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I
			+\bs\sigma_s^+
			\right)
			-\phi_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
			-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_s\omega_s^+\right)
			\right]
		\\
		 & \qquad =
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			-B\bar p_E\mbf I
			+
			\sum_{s\in\mc S}
			\phi_s\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_s
			\right]
		+
		\left(
		1-\sum_{s\in\mc S}\phi_s
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:solid_biot_pressure_remainder_rollup}
\end{equation}
%
Using the volume-fraction constraint, the coefficient of
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E\) in the summed solid balance is the total fluid
volume fraction \(\phi=\sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f\).

For the summed fluid balance, apply the fluid stress restriction
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_stress_free} to
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary}.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:saturation_weighted_primitive_potential_sum}, using
\(\sum_f\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f=\mbf0\), and applying the saturation-derivative
convention stated after \eqref{eq:interfacial_internal_energy} gives the
interfacial state-gradient identity
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	S_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\gamma_f
	=
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
	+
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}.
	\label{eq:interfacial_state_gradient_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Thus, writing \(\phi_f=\phi S_f\), the summed fluid pressure and electrical
terms give
%
\begin{align}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left[
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
			\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
		\right)
		-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\bar p_f-\omega_f^+
		\right)
	\right]
	={}&
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
		\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
	\right)
	-\phi\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\notag\\
	&
	+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\bar p_E-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f .
	\label{eq:MC_fluid_pressure_gradient_rollup}
\end{align}
%
The final term follows from the common pore-volume restriction
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_restriction}.  It vanishes in the reversible
limit and carries the dynamic pressure lag through the summed momentum balance.

Adding the solid and fluid pressure terms and using
\(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi=1\) and
\(\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\) gives
%
\begin{align}
	& \sum_{s\in\mc S}
		\left\{
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\bar p_E\mbf I
			+\bs\sigma_s^+
			\right]
		-\phi_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_s\omega_s^+\right)
		\right\}
		\notag\\
	& \quad
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left(
				\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
			\right)
			-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\bar p_f-\omega_f^+
			\right)
		\right]
		\notag\\
	& \qquad =
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left[
			-B\bar p_E\mbf I
			+\mbf E\otimes\mbf d
		\right]
		\notag\\
	& \qquad
		-\phi
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
		\notag\\
	& \qquad
		-\phi
		\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
		\left.
		\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
		\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
		\notag\\
	& \qquad
		+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left(
		\bar p_f-\bar p_E-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f .
	\label{eq:MC_pressure_gradient_overall_rollup}
\end{align}
%
The pairwise interaction forces cancel when all phase momentum balances are
summed.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:MC_pressure_gradient_overall_rollup} then gives the overall mixture
momentum balance
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	\sum_\xi\rho_\xi\mbf a_\xi
	={}&
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left[
		\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}
		-
		B\bar p_E\mbf I
		+
		\mbf E\otimes\mbf d
	\right]
	+\rho\mbf g
	+\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_\xi
	\right)
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
	\notag\\
	&
	-\phi
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf h\right)^T
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\mbf h}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}}
	\notag\\
	&
	+\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\bar p_E-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}S_f
	.
	\label{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot}
\end{empheq}
%
On a boundary portion where the phase virtual motions coincide with the common
mixture virtual motion, the phase boundary powers add.  Summing
\eqref{eq:phase_natural_traction} and
\eqref{eq:fluid_natural_traction}, then using
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_split},
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_biot_rollup_coefficient},
\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}, and
\(\bs\sigma_f'=\mbf0\), gives the overall natural traction,
%
\begin{align}
	\bar{\mbf t}
	&\equiv
	\sum_\xi\mbf t_\xi \notag\\
	&=
	\left\{
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\left[
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+}
	-\phi_s
	\left(
	\bar p_E+\omega_s^+
	\right)\mbf I
	\right]
	+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\left[
	\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
	-\phi_f\bar p_f\mbf I
	\right]
	\right\}\mbf n
	\notag\\
	&=
	\left[
		\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}
		-
		B\bar p_E\mbf I
		+
		\mbf E\otimes\mbf d
		-
		\phi\gamma\mbf I
	\right]\mbf n
	\qquad
	\text{on }\Gamma_{\bar{\mbf t}} .
	\label{eq:overall_natural_traction}
\end{align}
%
The term \(-\phi\gamma\mbf I\) is the stored interfacial-energy contribution
identified by the saturation-weighted phase-pressure relation.  The
thermocapillary, capillary-history-gradient, dynamic pressure-lag, and conversion
terms in \eqref{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot} remain bulk sources.
Additional external-surface physics requires a separate surface model.

The term proportional to
\(\partial\gamma/\partial\theta_{\mc F}\)
is the interfacial Gibbs--Duhem thermal contribution generated by the bulk
free-energy aggregation.  Indeed,
\(\mathfrak{s}_\gamma=-\partial\gamma/\partial\theta_{\mc F}\)
is the interfacial entropy per current fluid-subsystem volume, so the sign agrees with
the bulk Gibbs--Duhem identity
\cite[eq.~(59)]{bothe2015continuum}.  The contribution is therefore a bulk
thermocapillary analogue of the sharp-interface Marangoni traction.  In the
sharp-interface description the Marangoni traction is the tangential surface
force arising from gradients of surface tension along the interface; it is
obtained from a surface momentum balance using the interfacial normal and
surface projector
\cite{jasnow1996thermocapillary,guo2015thermocapillary}.  The averaged
description expresses the same physical effect through the bulk interfacial
potential \(\gamma\) and the state variables \(\theta_{\mc F}\), \(\mbf h\),
and \(\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F}\), so the traction follows directly from the averaged
state set.  The bulk term vanishes in the isothermal limit or when the
interfacial potential has no explicit temperature dependence.  The
capillary-history-gradient term records spatial variation of the
drainage--imbibition state and vanishes when \(\mbf h\) is spatially uniform
or \(\gamma\) is independent of it.  The dynamic pressure-lag term vanishes
when \(\mc T=\mbf0\) or when the saturation field is spatially uniform.

\subsection{Additive solid component specialization}
\label{sec:MC_additive_component_specialization}

The general phase energy need not be additive.  An additive representation is
useful, however, when components share one solid motion but their stored-energy
contributions can be distinguished.  Examples include adsorbates and bound
water in a mineral framework, lattice substitutions or defects, and guest
occupancy in hydrate cages.  The components share the solid velocity \(\mbf v_s\)
and the phase-level elastic measures, the true deformation \(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\)
and the distension \(\mbf A_s^e\); the split applies only to the stored energy.
Each component potential may depend on the full composition, so the model can
retain nonideal interactions.  When no meaningful component-energy
split exists, the general phase energy
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_general_free_energy} remains the appropriate model.
The component decomposition below retains the phase electric enthalpy
\(\omega_s^+\) and the equivalent pressure
\(\bar p_E=\bar p_s-\omega_s^+\).  Electrical storage remains a phase-level
constitutive quantity governed by
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_pressure_specific_volume}--\eqref{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot}.

Introduce component potentials \(\psi_s^\alpha\), intrinsic densities
\(\bar\rho_s^\alpha\), and pressures \(\bar p_s^\alpha\).  The component
intrinsic densities and component solid volume fractions satisfy
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_solid_density_mixture_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\bar\rho_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_intrinsic_density_rule} \\
		\eta_s^\alpha
		 & =
		\frac{\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_solid_mass_fraction_rule}
	\end{align}
	\begin{equation}
		\phi_s^\alpha
		=
		\frac{\eta_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha},
		\qquad
		\sum_\alpha\phi_s^\alpha=1,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_solid_component_volume_fraction_rule}
	\end{equation}
\end{subequations}
%
The additive phase energy is
%
\begin{equation}
	\psi_s
	=
	\sum_\alpha
	\eta_s^\alpha
	\psi_s^\alpha(
	\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
	\bar\rho_s^\alpha,\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S}),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_free_energy}
\end{equation}
%
Then the pressure average, stress, and distension restrictions become
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_mixture_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\bar p_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_s^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_pressure_average} \\
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s'
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\alpha},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_stress_sum}         \\
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\alpha}
		 & =
		\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha
		\mbf A_s^{-T}
		\left(
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
		\right)
		\mbf A_s^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_component_stress}   \\
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s'
		 & =
		\bar\rho_s
		\sum_\alpha
		\eta_s^\alpha
		\left.
		\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\mbf A_s^e}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
		(\mbf A_s^e)^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_distension_force}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The corresponding partial phase stress is
\(\boldsymbol\sigma_s'=\phi_s\bar{\boldsymbol\sigma}_s'\), relating the
intrinsic additive stresses to the momentum-balance stress
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_stress_definition}.
%
The storage multipliers provide the same component pressures.  Since
\(\sum_\alpha\pi_s^\alpha/\phi_s=\bar p_s\), the additive model assigns
component \(\alpha\) the pressure share
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\pi_s^\alpha}{\phi_s}
	=
	\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	\bar p_s^\alpha
	=
	\frac{\pi_s^\alpha}{\phi_s\phi_s^\alpha},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:additive_component_pressure_multiplier_identity}
\end{equation}
%
Summing over components recovers the phase pressure.  Thus
\(\pi_s^\alpha\) and \(\phi_s^\alpha\) determine
\(\bar p_s^\alpha\) without introducing another pressure closure in the general
additive energy representation.

The additive split of the stored energy does not extend to the plastic
closure.  The phase plastic law remains a single composition-dependent
function of the solid phase: one mobility \(\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}\) and one
\(\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}\) act on the phase plastic rates, and the composition
\(\{\eta_s^\alpha\}\) enters through the state set \(\mc X_s\).  Thus absorbed
water, adsorbed species, or guest molecules modify the plastic response of the
phase as a whole through the composition dependence of the common mobilities;
the components share the single phase plastic deformation and its flow rule.
The additive specialization therefore retains the phase-level closure of
\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_flow_rules},
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_plastic_flow_rules}
	\begin{align}
		\phasedot{\bar{\mbf F}}_s^p(\bar{\mbf F}_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\Lambda_{\bar{\mbf F}_s}(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s)
		(\mbf S_s)^T,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_plastic_deformation_flow_rule} \\
		\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^p(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}
		 & =
		\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s)
		(\mbf A_s^e)^T
		\bs\sigma_s'
		(\mbf A_s^e)^{-T},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_plastic_distension_flow_rule}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The mobilities are nonnegative as in
\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_deformation_mobility_nonnegative}--\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_distension_mobility_nonnegative},
so each plastic power is separately dissipative, and the internal-state
evolution follows \eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_state_evolution}.

\subsubsection{General additive component Biot decomposition}
\label{sec:MC_component_biot_derivation}

The additive Helmholtz energy admits a corresponding component decomposition
of the phase electrical enthalpy.  Introduce component electrical enthalpy
densities \(\omega_s^{\alpha+}\) satisfying
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_electrothermodynamic_decomposition}
	\begin{align}
		\psi_s+\omega_s^+\bar v_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\eta_s^\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
		\right),
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_electrothermodynamic_energy} \\
		\bar v_s
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\eta_s^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha,
		\qquad
		\omega_s^+
		=
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\omega_s^{\alpha+},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_electric_enthalpy_average}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The density rules \eqref{eq:MC_solid_density_mixture_rules} give
\(\eta_s^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha=\phi_s^\alpha\bar v_s\), which establishes
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_electric_enthalpy_average}.  Combining this identity with
the mechanical-pressure average \eqref{eq:MC_additive_pressure_average} gives
the component equivalent pressures and their phase average,
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_equivalent_pressures}
	\begin{align}
		\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
		 & \equiv
		\bar p_s^\alpha-\omega_s^{\alpha+}
		=
		-
		\left.
		\p{
			\left(
			\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
			\right)
		}{\bar v_s^\alpha}
		\right\vert_{
			\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
			\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
		},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_component_equivalent_pressure} \\
		\bar p_E
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\phi_s^\alpha\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_equivalent_pressure_average}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
Each component may carry its own mechanical and equivalent pressures.  The
storage multipliers determine the mechanical-pressure shares through
\eqref{eq:additive_component_pressure_multiplier_identity}, and the component
electrical enthalpies convert those shares to the conjugate equivalent
pressures in \eqref{eq:MC_additive_component_equivalent_pressure}.

At fixed composition, the specific-volume conjugacy and its Legendre
differential are
%
\begin{align}
	\left.
	\mathrm d\left(
	\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	-\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\,\mathrm d\bar v_s^\alpha,
	\notag \\
	\left.
	\mathrm d\left(
	\psi_s^\alpha
	+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
	+\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha
	\right)
	\right\vert_{
		\bar{\mbf F}_s^e,\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	&=
	\bar v_s^\alpha\,\mathrm d\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_legendre_differential}
\end{align}
%
Since
\(\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha=\bar p_s^\alpha-\omega_s^{\alpha+}\), the transformed
component potential has the equivalent forms
%
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde\psi_s^\alpha
	=
	\psi_s^\alpha
	+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
	+\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha
	=
	\psi_s^\alpha+\bar p_s^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_legendre_transform}
\end{equation}
%
The form \(\psi_s^\alpha+\bar p_s^\alpha\bar v_s^\alpha\) evaluates the
transform directly from the component mechanical pressure.  The transformed
potential uses \(\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\) as its pressure variable, with
\(\mathrm d\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
=\mathrm d\bar p_s^\alpha-\mathrm d\omega_s^{\alpha+}\).

Consider an instantaneous deformation at fixed composition, temperature,
electric field, and component equivalent pressures.  Along this path, the
component Legendre identity gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\mathrm d
	\left[
	\widetilde\psi_s^\alpha
	-
	\left(
	\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
	\right)
	\right]
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	}
	=
	\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s^\alpha}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	}
	\mathrm dJ,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_fixed_pressure_volume_differential}
\end{equation}
%
The double-prime component stress is the deformation response of the component
electrothermodynamic potential at fixed component equivalent pressures,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime\alpha}
	\equiv
	\phi_s^\alpha\bar\rho_s^\alpha
	\mbf A_s^{-T}
	\left[
	\left.
	\p{
		\left(
		\psi_s^\alpha+\omega_s^{\alpha+}\bar v_s^\alpha
		\right)
	}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\mbf A_s^e,
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},\mbf E
	}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T
	\right]
	\mbf A_s^T,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_double_prime_stress}
\end{equation}
%
For the component Piola-stress response, set
\(\mathrm d\mbf A_s^e=\mbf 0\) in
\eqref{eq:MC_common_jacobian_elastic_variation}.  Substitution of the resulting
\(\mathrm dJ\) into
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_component_fixed_pressure_volume_differential}, followed
by comparison of the coefficients of
\(\mathrm d\bar{\mbf F}_s^e\), gives the pressure contribution to the
component Piola stress.  The push-forward
\eqref{eq:MC_intrinsic_cauchy_stress_maps} converts this contribution to
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\alpha}
	-
	\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime\alpha}
	=
	\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha
	\frac{a_s\eta_s^\alpha}{\bar v_{s0}}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s^\alpha}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	}
	\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_fixed_pressure_stress_difference}
\end{equation}
%
The additive specific-volume relation in
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_electric_enthalpy_average} gives the phase tangent along
the same fixed-composition path,
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	}
	=
	\sum_\alpha
	\eta_s^\alpha
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s^\alpha}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_specific_volume_path_identity}
\end{equation}
%
The corresponding component nonlinear Biot coefficient is
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar B_s^\alpha
	\equiv
	1
	-
	\frac{a_s\eta_s^\alpha}{\bar v_{s0}}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s^\alpha}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_biot}
\end{empheq}
%
Summing the component coefficient complements and using
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_specific_volume_path_identity} gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\alpha
	\left(1-\bar B_s^\alpha\right)
	=
	\frac{a_s}{\bar v_{s0}}
	\left.
	\p{\bar v_s}{J}
	\right\vert_{
		\{\bar p_{E,s}^\beta\},\theta_{\mc S},
		\{\eta_s^\beta\},\mbf E
	},
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_biot_tangent_sum}
\end{equation}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:MC_additive_component_biot_tangent_sum} is the phase
specific-volume tangent measured while every component equivalent pressure is
fixed.  It equals \(1-\bar B_s\) from
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_coefficient} when the component equations of state map
this experiment to a phase deformation at fixed \(\bar p_E\).  Each
\(\bar B_s^\alpha\) contributes to the intrinsic phase stress; multiplication
of the summed intrinsic stress by \(\phi_s\) gives the partial phase stress.
Using \eqref{eq:MC_additive_component_biot} in
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_component_fixed_pressure_stress_difference} gives the
component stress split,
%
\begin{equation}
	\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\alpha}
	=
	\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime\alpha}
	+
	(1-\bar B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_additive_component_biot_split}
\end{equation}
%
Summing the component stresses gives the phase double-prime stress and its
pressure correction.  Comparison with the phase stress split
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_split} then gives the pressure-weighted compatibility
condition,
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_additive_biot_mixture_rule}
	\begin{align}
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime}
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime\alpha},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_double_prime_sum} \\
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s'
		 & =
		\bar{\bs\sigma}_s^{\prime\prime}
		+
		\sum_\alpha
		(1-\bar B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha\mbf I,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S,
		\label{eq:MC_additive_general_biot_stress_sum} \\
		(1-\bar B_s)\bar p_E
		 & =
		\sum_\alpha
		(1-\bar B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_biot_pressure_mixture_rule}
	\end{align}
	\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
		\bar B_s
		=
		1
		-
		\frac{1}{\bar p_E}
		\sum_\alpha
		(1-\bar B_s^\alpha)\bar p_{E,s}^\alpha,
		\qquad
		\bar p_E\ne0,
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
		\label{eq:MC_additive_biot_mixture_coefficient}
	\end{empheq}
\end{subequations}
%
For \(\bar p_E\ne0\), the boxed expression solves the pressure-weighted
compatibility condition for \(\bar B_s\).  It gives the derivative-defined
phase coefficient \eqref{eq:MC_phase_biot_coefficient} when the component
equations of state produce the fixed-\(\bar p_E\) tangent described by
\eqref{eq:MC_additive_component_biot_tangent_sum}.  Thus the component equations
of state specify how the component pressure contributions combine to recover
the phase coefficient.

\subsection{Scalar distension simplifications}
\label{sec:MC_scalar_distension_simplifications}

For isotropic pore-structure change, each distension factor is spherical.  This
specialization retains volumetric accommodation but excludes anisotropic pore
collapse and fabric evolution,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf A_s^e=(a_s^e)^{1/3}\mbf I,
	\qquad
	\mbf A_s^p=(a_s^p)^{1/3}\mbf I,
	\qquad
	\mbf A_s^0=(a_s^0)^{1/3}\mbf I,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_factors}
\end{equation}
%
The factors multiply,
%
\begin{equation}
	a_s=a_s^e a_s^p a_s^0,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_product}
\end{equation}
%
and the true solid deformation relation becomes
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf F=a_s^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}_s,
	\qquad
	J=a_s\bar J_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_deformation}
\end{equation}
%
The isotropic distension factors cancel from the push-forward in
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_stress_definition}, leaving
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_s'
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	(\bar{\mbf F}_s^e)^T,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_solid_stress}
\end{equation}
%
For a generic multicomponent solid, the tensor distension restriction
\eqref{eq:MC_distension_force_definition} reduces to the trace condition
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right)
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{a_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	a_s^e,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_distension_stress_restriction}
\end{equation}
%
Using the phase-level Biot--Terzaghi effective-stress relation, the same
condition can be written as a
restriction on the phase double-prime trace,
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime})
	=
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{a_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	a_s^e
	-\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\bar p_E,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_double_prime_distension_trace}
\end{equation}
%
The same specialization reduces the plastic-distension power in
\eqref{eq:MC_residual_dissipation} to
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\bs\sigma_s':
	\mbf A_s^e\phasedot{\mbf A}_s^p
	(\mbf A_s^p)^{-1}(\mbf A_s^e)^{-1}
	=
	\sum_{s\in\mc S}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right)
	\frac{\phasedot{a}_s^p}{a_s^p}.
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_plastic_distension_power}
\end{equation}
%
An associated scalar closure using the nonnegative plastic-distension mobility
from \eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_distension_mobility_nonnegative} is
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\phasedot a_s^p}{a_s^p}
	=
	\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}(\mc X_s,\mbf p_s)
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\left(
	\bs\sigma_s'
	\right),
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_scalar_plastic_distension_flow_rule}
\end{equation}
%
Its contribution to the plastic power is
\(\Lambda_{\mbf A_s}
[\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s')/3]^2\ge0\).
Thus scalar plastic distension describes hydrostatic compaction or dilation,
with the material mean stress as its conjugate force.

\subsection{Distension and crystallization pressure}
\label{sec:MC_distension_crystallization_pressure}

Crystallization affects momentum only through constitutive ingredients already
present in the theory: the solid free energy, the precipitation kinetics, and
the reaction affinity.  This subsection specializes these ingredients to the
scalar setting, in which the volumetric kinematics are
%
\begin{equation}
	\ln J=\ln a_s+\ln\bar J_s,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_volumetric_split}
\end{equation}
%
The scalar stress restriction depends on this total volumetric strain, so a
simple transformed volumetric energy with skeleton bulk modulus
\(K_s^\star\) is
%
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde W_{{\rm vol},s}
	=
	\frac{K_s^\star}{2}
	\left(\ln J\right)^2,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_component_transformed_volumetric_energy}
\end{equation}
%
Holding \(\bar J_s\) and \(\bar p_E\) fixed,
%
\begin{equation}
	a_s
	\left.
	\p{\widetilde W_{{\rm vol},s}}{a_s}
	\right\vert_{\bar J_s,\bar p_E}
	=
	K_s^\star\ln J,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_component_transformed_volumetric_trace}
\end{equation}
%
Its derivative gives the fixed-pressure mean stress,
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime})
	=
	K_s^\star\ln J,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S ,
	\label{eq:MC_component_double_prime_trace_from_energy}
\end{equation}
%
and the single-prime trace follows from the Biot--Terzaghi effective-stress
relation,
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s
	\left.
	\p{\psi_s}{a_s^e}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_s}
	a_s^e
	=
	K_s^\star\ln J
	+\phi_s\left(1-\bar B_s\right)\bar p_E,
	\qquad
	s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_component_cn_trace_satisfied}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the solid energy supplies a crystallization-induced stress through the
same constitutive stress used in the momentum equation.

The precipitation affinity provides the complementary chemical condition.  For
a mechanism \((m)\) that forms solid phase \(s_{(m)}\),
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_affinity_kinetics}
\end{equation}
%
The kinetic law is driven by the temperature-weighted stoichiometric sum in
\eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate}.  In the local-thermal-equilibrium limit,
its driving coefficient is the traditional affinity minus the explicit
phase-transfer work in
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}.  The dissipation
condition reduces to \(\mc A_{(m)}r_{(m)}\ge0\) when that transfer-work
offset vanishes.

If a classical crystallization pressure
\(\bar p_{\text{xtal},s_{(m)}}\) is prescribed, it closes the affinity through
the sign convention stated at the beginning of this section: Cauchy stress is
tension-positive, so the positive compressive mean pressure acting on the
forming solid is
\(-\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}')/3\).  Forming a unit mass of solid
against that compression requires mechanical work equal to the specific volume
times this compressive mean pressure.  The displayed pressure-work term uses a
mass-based progress variable whose stoichiometric coefficients are normalized
by
\(\sum_\alpha\nu_{s_{(m)} (m)}^\alpha=1\).  For a solid mass yield different
from unity, the pressure-work term is multiplied by that yield; on a molar
progress basis, the specific volume \(\bar v_{s_{(m)}}\) is replaced by the
corresponding molar volume.  The net chemical-minus-mechanical driving force
is therefore
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	\bar v_{s_{(m)}}
	\left[
		\bar p_{\text{xtal},s_{(m)}}
		+\frac{1}{3}
		\operatorname{tr}
		\left(
		\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}'
	\right)
	\right],
	\qquad
	\bar v_{s_{(m)}}=\frac{1}{\bar\rho_{s_{(m)}}},
	\qquad
	s_{(m)}\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_affinity_volumetric}
\end{equation}
%
For example, the classical supersaturation relation
\(\bar p_{\text{xtal},s_{(m)}}=R\theta_{\mc F}\ln\Omega_{(m)}/V_{s_{(m)}}\)
expresses the crystallization pressure in terms of the gas constant \(R\), the
supersaturation ratio \(\Omega_{(m)}\), and the molar volume \(V_{s_{(m)}}\)
of the precipitating solid.  The fluid temperature \(\theta_{\mc F}\) is used
because supersaturation and the fluid--solid precipitation mechanism are
evaluated in the mobile subsystem.  Substituting this relation gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	=
	\bar v_{s_{(m)}}
		\left[
			\frac{R\theta_{\mc F}}{V_{s_{(m)}}}
			\ln\Omega_{(m)}
			+\frac{1}{3}
			\operatorname{tr}
			\left(
			\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}'
		\right)
		\right],
	\qquad
	s_{(m)}\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_supersaturation_affinity}
\end{equation}
%
At full reversible equilibrium,
\(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}\) and \(\mc A_{(m)}=0\), so the chemical
crystallization pressure equals the positive compressive phase mean pressure
\(-\operatorname{tr}(\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}')/3\) permitted by the constitutive
law.  Equivalently, the equilibrium supersaturation is
%
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}
	=
	\exp\left[
	-\frac{
		V_{s_{(m)}}
		\operatorname{tr}
		\left(
		\bs\sigma_{s_{(m)}}'
		\right)
	}{
		3R\theta_{\mc F}
	}
	\right],
	\qquad
	s_{(m)}\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:MC_crystallization_equilibrium_supersaturation}
\end{equation}
%
The stress-free limit gives \(\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}=1\), whereas compression
raises the equilibrium threshold.  Under local thermal equilibrium and a
vanishing phase-transfer offset, \eqref{eq:MC_onsager_reaction_rate} gives
\(r_{(m)}=K_{(m)}\mc A_{(m)}/\theta\); therefore
\(\Omega_{(m)}>\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}\) drives precipitation and
\(\Omega_{(m)}<\Omega_{(m)}^{\rm eq}\) drives dissolution.  If stress and
affinity are solved simultaneously,
\(\Omega_{(m)}\) is an output rather than an independently prescribed load.

\section{Multicomponent model summary}
\label{sec:MC_model_summary_closure_count}

This section records bulk pointwise unknown and equation counts for a reduced,
fully implicit active-phase formulation under the closures stated below.  These
counts are not a complete boundary-value-problem specification.  One mass
fraction per phase and one phase volume fraction are reconstructed from
\eqref{eq:el_eta_constraint} and \eqref{eq:volume_fraction_constraint}.
Subtracting the component stationarity equations eliminates the
composition-normalization multipliers \(\Pi_\xi\) through
\eqref{eq:pi_difference_system}, and the storage multipliers
\(\pi_\xi^\alpha\) then follow from that difference system and the phase-sum
relation \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_storage}.  Phase pressures and
electrochemical potentials are likewise evaluated from the constitutive
restrictions.

The normalization
\eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_reference_normalization} fixes the reference-solid
value of the specific transfer-work potential.  One equation
\eqref{eq:MC_admissible_conversion_component} evolves the shared transfer
potential \(\tau\), and the \(P-1\) equations
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_reference_transfer_work_recovery} determine the
transfer-work potentials \(L_\xi^N\) of the remaining phases.  The component
differences
\eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_component_difference}, equivalently the full
recovery \eqref{eq:MC_transfer_work_full_recovery}, reconstruct
\(L_\xi^\alpha\), \(\alpha\ne N\), as algebraically recovered variational
coefficients rather than primary fields or freely prescribed constitutive
functions.

Initial and boundary data, potential gauges, pure-Neumann compatibility
conditions, and switching conditions for phase or component appearance are not
included in the pointwise counts.  A charged reaction that transfers charge
between thermal subsystems retains the scalar neutral-force kinetic row.  Its
reaction-energy transfer may be Onsager-coupled subject to
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}; the displayed rate count is
unchanged unless a chosen closure introduces additional internal variables.
The plastic internal state vector \(\mbf p_s\) with evolution
\eqref{eq:MC_generic_plastic_state_evolution} is one such addition,
contributing additional components per solid phase to the count.

Both relative-flux families, the phase heat fluxes, the fluid--skeleton
interaction forces, and the independent fluid--solid heat-exchange supply are
retained as auxiliary fields so that their closures appear explicitly in the
count.  The closure \eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_law} retains one
fluid--skeleton interaction force per fluid phase.  The aggregate solid force
then follows from \eqref{eq:MC_aggregate_solid_interaction_force}.  The general
pairwise representation \eqref{eq:MC_pairwise_interaction_force} remains
available for constitutive extensions and is not included in the present
count.  The total solid scalar distension
factors are closed kinematically by
\eqref{eq:MC_solid_distension_evolution}, while the scalar plastic factors
\(a_s^p\) follow \eqref{eq:MC_scalar_plastic_distension_flow_rule}.  The
electroquasistatic formulation includes the electric potential and Gauss' law.
Mechanical interaction-energy supplies are constitutive outputs associated
with the interaction forces and must satisfy
\eqref{eq:MC_interaction_energy_conservation} and
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility}; they are therefore not
counted as an independent field family.
The phase entropies and internal energies and the interfacial quantities
\(\mathfrak{s}_\gamma\) and \(u_\gamma\) are algebraically evaluated from
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_legendre_relation}--%
\eqref{eq:MC_interfacial_legendre_relation} and
\eqref{eq:MC_entropy_definitions}.  They introduce no additional primary
fields or evolution equations.
Let \(P_{\mc F}=|\mc F|\), \(P_{\mc S}=|\mc S|\), and
\(P=P_{\mc F}+P_{\mc S}\).  Let \(N^{\mc F}\) be the number of tracked
components in each fluid phase and \(N^{\mc S}\) the number of tracked
components in each solid phase.  If the component counts vary by phase, the
products in the tables are replaced by the corresponding phase sums.  Let \(N_{(m)}\)
be the number of independent conversion mechanisms and \(d\) the spatial
dimension.  The number of continuous capillary-history variables is \(N_h\).
The dynamic pressure-lag resistance \(\mc T\) is constitutive, and the
saturation rates are obtained from the independent phase volume fractions, so
the optional closure introduces no additional pointwise unknown family.
