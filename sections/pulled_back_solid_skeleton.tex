\section{Solid-reference form for reservoir simulation}
\label{sec:reservoir_simulation}

This section rewrites the transport and mechanics equations in a form suitable
for solution on the trajectory of the solid skeleton.  The solid motion
$\bs\chi$ supplies the computational map from the solid reference coordinate
$\mbf X$ to the current coordinate $\mbf x$, with deformation gradient
$\mbf F$ and Jacobian $J$.

\subsection{Relative flux vectors}
\label{sec:relative_flux_vectors}

The phase momentum equation \eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} determines
Darcy-scale relative fluxes once interphase drag is written in terms of a
velocity relative to the solid skeleton.  For each fluid phase \(f\), define the
current relative mass flux by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf w_f
	=\phi_f\bar\rho_f\left(\mbf v_f-\mbf v_s\right)
	=\rho_f\left(\mbf v_f-\mbf v_s\right),
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:relative_mass_flux_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Thus
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf v_f
	=\mbf v_s+\frac{\mbf w_f}{\rho_f},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
%
A sufficient fluid--skeleton interaction closure consistent with
\eqref{eq:MC_interphase_exchange_law} is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_f
	=
	-\frac{\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}\phi_f}{\bar\rho_f}\,
	\bs\kappa_f^{-1}\mbf w_f,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:fluid_phase_interaction_law}
\end{equation}
%
Here $\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}$ is the dynamic viscosity and $\bs\kappa_f$ is the
permeability tensor for phase $f$.  Phase relative permeability may be absorbed
into $\bs\kappa_f$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:fluid_phase_interaction_law} contains
only dissipative drag; all reversible driving forces remain in the phase
momentum balance.  From \eqref{eq:relative_mass_flux_definition}, the two drag
tensors are related by
\(\mbf k_{f\mc S}^{-1}
=\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}\bs\kappa_f^{-1}\).
Thus positive definiteness of \(\bs\kappa_f\), together with the
interaction-energy allocation
\eqref{eq:MC_drag_heating_equal_partition}, makes
\eqref{eq:MC_mechanical_interaction_admissibility} nonnegative.

Substituting \eqref{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction} and
\eqref{eq:fluid_phase_interaction_law} into the fluid momentum balance
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} gives, for each fluid phase,
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{1}{\rho_f}
	\left(
		\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}\bs\kappa_f^{-1}
		+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha\mbf I
	\right)\mbf w_f
		= & \;
		\rho_f\left(\mbf g-\mbf a_f\right)
		\notag \\
	  &
		-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
		\bar p_E+\gamma_f
	\right)
		\notag \\
	  &
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
		\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
	\right)
		+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_s\right),
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S,
	\label{eq:relative_flux_linear_system}
\end{align}
%
By the component mass balance and the separate zero-sum closure for the
component-relative fluxes, the scalar
\(\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha\) is the net phase-mass source due to
conversion.  It is a source coefficient in the phase momentum balance, not a
transport mobility or an independent contribution to dissipation.  The
component-relative transport itself is closed through
\eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_closures}.

To solve the preceding linear system for \(\mbf w_f\), invert the coefficient
multiplying that flux.  Define the resulting conversion-corrected mobility
tensor by
%
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_f
	=
	\left(
		\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}\bs\kappa_f^{-1}
		+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha\mbf I
	\right)^{-1},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:modified_relative_flux_permeability}
\end{equation}
%
The resistance inside the inverse has units
\({\rm kg\,m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}\), and
\(\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_f\) therefore has units
\({\rm m^3\,s\,kg^{-1}}={\rm m^2\,Pa^{-1}\,s^{-1}}\).  Despite the retained
\(\kappa\) symbol, it is a mobility rather than a permeability.  The underlying
drag law is dissipative when the phase permeability
\(\bs\kappa_f\) is symmetric positive definite, \(\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}>0\), and
\(\phi_f>0\).  The added scalar conversion-insertion term comes from the
momentum source and is not a second drag coefficient.  If \(\kappa_{f,i}\) are
the eigenvalues of
\(\bs\kappa_f\), the inverse in
\eqref{eq:modified_relative_flux_permeability} exists exactly when
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}}{\kappa_{f,i}}
	+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	\ne 0
	\qquad\text{for every eigenvalue }\kappa_{f,i},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:modified_relative_flux_nonsingularity}
\end{equation}
%
Algebraic elimination is therefore possible for any nonsingular combined
resistance, even when it is indefinite.  The stronger positive-resistance
condition
%
\begin{equation}
	\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}\mbf z\cdot\bs\kappa_f^{-1}\mbf z
	+
	\left(\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha\right)
	\mbf z\cdot\mbf z
	>0,
	\qquad
	\mbf z\ne\mbf0,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:modified_relative_flux_positive_definiteness}
\end{equation}
%
This condition is sufficient for invertibility and preserves the sign of the resistance
operator, but it is not necessary for invertibility and does not by itself
guarantee good conditioning.  Thermodynamic dissipation remains attached to the
underlying drag and component-transport closures.  The first loss of positive
definiteness occurs in the direction of largest phase permeability.  If
\(\kappa_{f,\max}\) and \(\kappa_{f,\min}\) are the largest and smallest
eigenvalues of \(\bs\kappa_f\), respectively, the positive-resistance
condition is equivalently
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	>
	-\frac{\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}}{\kappa_{f,\max}},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:modified_relative_flux_scalar_admissibility}
\end{equation}
%
Within this positive-definite regime, the spectral condition number is
%
\begin{equation}
	\operatorname{cond}_2
	\left(
		\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}\bs\kappa_f^{-1}
		+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha\mbf I
	\right)
	=
	\frac{
		\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}/\kappa_{f,\min}
		+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	}{
		\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}/\kappa_{f,\max}
		+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:modified_relative_flux_condition_number}
\end{equation}
%
Thus quantitative conditioning requires the denominator to remain bounded away
from zero, not merely positive.
For an aqueous reaction that removes fluid mass on a time scale \(t_{\rm rxn}\),
\(\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha\simeq-\rho_f/t_{\rm rxn}\).  The singular threshold
at the first loss of positive definiteness is
%
\begin{equation}
	t_{\rm rxn}
	=
	\frac{\rho_f\kappa_{f,\max}}{\phi_f^2\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:modified_relative_flux_reaction_timescale_threshold}
\end{equation}
%
For longer time scales the combined resistance remains positive definite.  At
equality it is singular in the largest-permeability direction; at shorter time
scales it has lost positive definiteness and is invertible only away from the
remaining eigenvalue crossings identified by
\eqref{eq:modified_relative_flux_nonsingularity}.
With representative reservoir values
\(\rho_f=10^3\,{\rm kg\,m^{-3}}\), \(\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}=10^{-3}\,{\rm Pa\,s}\),
\(\phi_f=0.3\), and \(\kappa_{f,\max}=10^{-12}\,{\rm m^2}\), this time is about
\(10^{-5}\,{\rm s}\), corresponding to a removal rate of order
\(10^8\,{\rm kg\,m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}\).  For conversion rates whose characteristic
time is much longer than this value, the source correction is a small
perturbation of the Darcy drag.  The estimate supplies a lower reaction-time
bound for the positive-resistance form of the algebraic relative-flux closure.

The resulting relative mass flux is
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	\mbf w_f
	= & \;
	\rho_f\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_f
	\left[
		\rho_f\left(\mbf g-\mbf a_f\right)
		\right.
		\notag \\
	& \phantom{\;\rho_f\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_f}
		\left.
		-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\bar p_E+\gamma_f
		\right)
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
			\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
		\right)
		+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
		\left(
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_s
		\right)
		\right],
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:generalized_fluid_darcy_mass_flux}
\end{empheq}
%
This expression is the multicomponent fluid-phase analogue of a generalized
Darcy flux.  Its driving force contains body force and inertia, equivalent and
capillary pressure gradients, Maxwell stress, and conversion insertion.
The observer-conversion term is absent from this bulk force because
\eqref{eq:MC_collected_observer_correction_reassembly} explicitly recombines it
with the skeleton-rate free-energy contribution before the residual inequality
is formed; it is part of the reversible phase-following thermodynamic rate, not
a second bulk force.
Chemical-potential gradients drive component-relative transport
through \eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_closures}; that dissipative role is
separate from both the bulk phase force above and the role of \(\tau\) in the
conversion variation.  The
exact nonsingularity condition above permits algebraic flux elimination, while
the stronger positive-definiteness condition selects a positive-resistance
operator.  Neither condition makes the conversion source dissipative or
replaces the interaction-energy allocation in
\eqref{eq:MC_drag_heating_equal_partition}.

The neutral, conversion-free, negligible-inertia specialization
\(\omega_f^+=0\), \(\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f=\mbf0\),
\(\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha=0\), and \(\mbf a_f=\mbf0\) reduces
\eqref{eq:generalized_fluid_darcy_mass_flux} to
%
\begin{equation}
	\phi_f\left(\mbf v_f-\mbf v_s\right)
	=
	-\frac{\bs\kappa_f}{\mu_f^{\mathrm{visc}}}
	\left(
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_f
		-\bar\rho_f\mbf g
	\right),
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:standard_darcy_mass_flux_limit}
\end{equation}
%
This is Darcy's law for phase \(f\) relative to the deforming solid skeleton.
It follows directly from the neutral, conversion-free, negligible-inertia
specialization; neither isothermality nor a spatially uniform Helmholtz energy is
required.  Nonisothermal cross effects may modify this law only when they are
introduced explicitly through a coupled constitutive closure.

\subsection{Solid-reference relative fluxes}
\label{sec:solid_reference_relative_fluxes}

The current relative flux in \eqref{eq:generalized_fluid_darcy_mass_flux} is
converted to a flux through surfaces in the solid reference configuration by the
Piola transform.  Define the reference relative mass flux
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf W_f
	=
	J\mbf F^{-1}\mbf w_f,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:reference_relative_mass_flux}
\end{equation}
%
Define the pulled-back conversion-corrected mobility tensor
%
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde{\mbf K}_f
	=
	J\mbf F^{-1}
	\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_f
	\mbf F^{-T},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:pulled_back_modified_permeability}
\end{equation}
%
The flux assembled in the reference component balances is then
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf W_f
	= & \; \rho_f\widetilde{\mbf K}_f
	\left[
		\rho_f\mbf F^T\left(\mbf g-\mbf a_f\right)
		-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf X}
		\left(
			\bar p_E+\gamma_f
		\right)
		\right.
		\notag \\
	& \phantom{\;\rho_f\widetilde{\mbf K}_f}
		\left.
		+\mbf F^T
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left(
				\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
			\right)
		\right]
		\right.
	\notag \\
	& \phantom{\;\rho_f\widetilde{\mbf K}_f}
	\left.
	+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf X}\tau-\mbf F^T\mbf v_s\right)
	\right],
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:solid_reference_relative_flux}
\end{align}
%
Here gradients of scalar fields obey
$\nabla_{\mbf x}(\cdot)=\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}(\cdot)$, while the
divergence of a spatial flux obeys
%
\begin{equation}
	J\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf w_f
	=
	\nabla_{\mbf X}\cdot
	\mbf W_f,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:solid_reference_flux_divergence}
\end{equation}
%
Equations~\eqref{eq:reference_relative_mass_flux}--\eqref{eq:solid_reference_relative_flux}
and \eqref{eq:solid_reference_flux_divergence} give the reference flux used in
the multicomponent balances.  The expression retains phase labels, component
conversion rates, and gradients of \(\tau\).

\subsection{Component balances on the solid trajectory}
\label{sec:solid_reference_component_balances}

For a fluid phase, substituting
\eqref{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction} into
\eqref{eq:averaged_component_spatial_mass}, using
\eqref{eq:component_partial_density_representation}, and writing the storage
term on the solid trajectory gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
	\left(J\phi_f\bar\rho_f\eta_f^\alpha\right)
	\right\vert_{\mbf X}
	+\nabla_{\mbf X}\cdot
	\left[
	\eta_f^\alpha\mbf W_f
	+
	J\mbf F^{-1}
	\left(
	\mbf j_{f,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{f,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)
	\right]
	=
	J\sum_m\nu_{f (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)},
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:solid_reference_fluid_component_balance}
\end{equation}
%
For a solid phase \(s\), the phase velocity is the skeleton velocity, so there
is no bulk phase flux relative to the solid mesh.  Component diffusion and
dispersion remain admissible, giving
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left.
		\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
		\left(J\phi_s\bar\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha\right)
		\right\vert_{\mbf X}
		& {}+
		\nabla_{\mbf X}\cdot
		\left[
			J\mbf F^{-1}
			\left(
				\mbf j_{s,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
				+
				\mbf j_{s,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)
		\right]
		\\
		& {}=
		J\sum_m\nu_{s (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)},
		\qquad
		s\in\mc S .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:solid_reference_solid_component_balance}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the solid-reference storage variables are the Lagrangian component masses
\(J\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha\).  Transport through the solid mesh
contains bulk fluid motion relative to the skeleton and component diffusion or
dispersion relative to the fluid or solid phase.

\subsection{Overall momentum on the solid trajectory}
\label{sec:solid_reference_overall_momentum}

Define the general solid-reference effective first Piola--Kirchhoff stress by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf P^{\prime\prime}
	\equiv
	J\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\mbf F^{-T}.
	\label{eq:solid_reference_effective_piola_stress}
\end{equation}
%
The overall momentum balance \eqref{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot} can be
written on the solid trajectory by applying the Piola transform to the stress
divergence and multiplying the remaining spatial source terms by \(J\).  Using
\eqref{eq:solid_reference_effective_piola_stress}, this transformation gives
%
\begin{align}
	J\sum_\xi\rho_\xi\mbf a_\xi
	= & \;
	\nabla_{\mbf X}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf{P}^{\prime\prime}
		-B\bar p_E J \mbf F^{-T}
		+J\left(\mbf E\otimes\mbf d\right)\mbf F^{-T}
		\right)
	+J\rho\mbf g
	\notag \\
	&\;
	-J\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F}}
	\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}\theta_{\mc F}
	\notag \\
	&\;
	+J\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
		\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}\tau-\mbf v_\xi
	\right).
	\label{eq:solid_reference_overall_momentum}
\end{align}
%
Here
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau=\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}\tau\), so every gradient in
the conversion-insertion force is evaluated from the solid reference
configuration.  The same pull-back gives
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\theta_{\mc F}
=\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}\theta_{\mc F}\) in the thermocapillary force.

If a quasi-static solid-skeleton solve is desired, the inertial term on the left
is dropped.  If the fluid velocities are eliminated with
\eqref{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction}, then the same equation is
solved with $\mbf v_f=\mbf v_s+\mbf w_f/\rho_f$ for fluid phases and
$\mbf v_s$ for the solid phase.  This leaves the solid displacement, the
Lagrangian partial masses, and the fluid relative fluxes as the natural fields
for a reference-configuration implementation.

\subsection{Quasi-static mechanics--fluid subblock for reservoir simulation}
\label{sec:reservoir_simulation_solve_block}

A reservoir implementation can solve a useful quasi-static subblock built from
overall momentum, fluid-component storage, relative-flux reconstruction, and
capillary pressure.  This is only the mechanics--fluid part of the coupled
model.  Its finite-deformation mechanics residual follows by dropping inertia
from
\eqref{eq:solid_reference_overall_momentum},
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf{0}
	= & \;
	\nabla_{\mbf X}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf{P}^{\prime\prime}
		-B\bar p_E J \mbf F^{-T}
		+J\left(\mbf E\otimes\mbf d\right)\mbf F^{-T}
		\right)
	+J\rho\mbf g
	\notag \\
	&\;
	-J\phi
	\left.
	\p{\gamma}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\right\vert_{\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F}}
	\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}\theta_{\mc F}
	\notag \\
	&\;
	+J\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
		\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}\tau-\mbf v_\xi
	\right).
	\label{eq:reservoir_simulation_overall_momentum_summary}
\end{align}
%
The fluid component balances on the same solid trajectory are
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
	\left(J\phi_f\bar\rho_f\eta_f^\alpha\right)
	\right\vert_{\mbf X}
	+\nabla_{\mbf X}\cdot
	\left[
	\eta_f^\alpha\mbf W_f
	+
	J\mbf F^{-1}
	\left(
	\mbf j_{f,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{f,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)
	\right]
	=
	J\sum_m\nu_{f (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)} ,
	\qquad f\in\mc F ,
	\label{eq:reservoir_simulation_fluid_component_summary}
\end{equation}
%
with current velocities reconstructed from the relative mass fluxes by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf v_f
	=\mbf v_s+\frac{\mbf w_f}{\rho_f},
	\qquad
	\mbf W_f=J\mbf F^{-1}\mbf w_f,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:reservoir_simulation_velocity_flux_summary}
\end{equation}
%
The corresponding admissible reference flux is
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf W_f
	= & \; \rho_f\widetilde{\mbf K}_f
	\left[
		\rho_f\mbf F^T\left(\mbf g-\mbf a_f\right)
		-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf X}
		\left(
			\bar p_E+\gamma_f
		\right)
		\right.
		\notag \\
	& \phantom{\;\rho_f\widetilde{\mbf K}_f}
		\left.
		+\mbf F^T
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
			\left(
				\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
			\right)
		\right]
		\right.
	\notag \\
	& \phantom{\;\rho_f\widetilde{\mbf K}_f}
	\left.
	+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf X}\tau-\mbf F^T\mbf v_s\right)
	\right],
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad s\in\mc S .
	\label{eq:reservoir_simulation_reference_flux_summary}
\end{align}
%
The neutral, conversion-free, negligible-inertia specialization gives
\eqref{eq:standard_darcy_mass_flux_limit} directly.  Capillary, or
electrocapillary, pressure closes the phase pressures algebraically through the
saturation state,
%
\begin{equation}
	\phi = \sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f,
	\qquad
	S_f = \frac{\phi_f}{\phi},
	\qquad
	p_{c,nw}
	=
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	-\left(\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+\right),
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F,\quad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:reservoir_simulation_capillary_summary}
\end{equation}
%
Equivalently, a simulator may choose one reference pressure and reconstruct the
other fluid pressures from the selected capillary-pressure laws before
evaluating the fluxes.  The neutral specialization \(\omega_f^+=0\) gives
\(p_{c,nw}=\bar p_n-\bar p_w\).  Together with the Maxwell stress, the
algebraic pressure reconstruction gives the equivalent-pressure identity
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_pressure_gradient_rollup}.

In this subblock,
\eqref{eq:reservoir_simulation_overall_momentum_summary} and
\eqref{eq:reservoir_simulation_fluid_component_summary} are primary residual
equations.  Equations
\eqref{eq:reservoir_simulation_velocity_flux_summary} and
\eqref{eq:reservoir_simulation_reference_flux_summary} are constitutive
reconstructions, and
\eqref{eq:reservoir_simulation_capillary_summary} is an algebraic closure.  A
complete solve must additionally retain the solid component balances
\eqref{eq:solid_reference_solid_component_balance}, the two subsystem energy
balances \eqref{eq:MC_energy_balance}, Gauss' law \eqref{eq:gauss_law},
mechanism kinetics, solid internal-variable evolution, and the remaining
constitutive restrictions appropriate to the selected specialization.
