\section{Pulled-back equations on the solid skeleton}
\label{sec:pulled_back_solid_skeleton}

This section rewrites the transport and mechanics equations in a form suitable
for solution on the trajectory of the solid skeleton.  The solid motion
$\bs\chi_s$ supplies the computational map from the solid reference coordinate
$\mbf X_s$ to the current coordinate $\mbf x$, with deformation gradient
$\mbf F_s$ and Jacobian $J_s$.

\subsection{Relative flux vectors}
\label{sec:relative_flux_vectors}

The phase momentum equations above determine consistent Darcy-scale relative
fluxes once the interphase drag is written in terms of a velocity relative to the
solid skeleton.  For each mobile phase $l$, define the current relative mass
flux by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf w_l
	=\phi_l\bar\rho_l\left(\mbf v_l-\mbf v_s\right)
	=\rho_l\left(\mbf v_l-\mbf v_s\right).
	\label{eq:relative_mass_flux_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Thus
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf v_l
	=\mbf v_s+\frac{\mbf w_l}{\rho_l}.
	\label{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
%
The same linear-drag argument used in the reacting-mixture formulation closes
the part of the mobile-phase interaction force produced by motion through the
skeleton as
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf b_l
	=-\frac{\mu_l\phi_l}{\bar\rho_l}\,
	\bs\kappa_l^{-1}\mbf w_l ,
	\label{eq:mobile_phase_drag_law}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mu_l$ is the dynamic viscosity and $\bs\kappa_l$ is the permeability
tensor for phase $l$.  Phase relative permeability may be absorbed into
$\bs\kappa_l$.

Substituting \eqref{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction} and
\eqref{eq:mobile_phase_drag_law} into the mobile momentum balance
\eqref{eq:el_mom_a_simplified}--\eqref{eq:el_mom_g_simplified} gives, for each
mobile phase,
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{1}{\rho_l}
	\left(
	\phi_l^2\mu_l\bs\kappa_l^{-1}
	+\sum_\alpha\dot c_l^\alpha\mbf I
	\right)\mbf w_l
	= & \;
	\rho_l\left(\mbf g-\mbf a_l\right)
	-\phi_l\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_l
	\notag                          \\
	  & +\sum_\alpha\dot c_l^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_l^\alpha-\mbf v_s\right),
	\label{eq:relative_flux_linear_system}
\end{align}
%
The term $\sum_\alpha\dot c_l^\alpha\mbf I$ is the same reaction-induced
correction that appears in the three-phase reacting-mixture flux law.  It comes
from the velocity carried inside the conversion-momentum term, not from appending
a source to the mass balance after the fact.

Define the modified permeability tensor
%
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_l
	=
	\left(
	\phi_l^2\mu_l\bs\kappa_l^{-1}
	+\sum_\alpha\dot c_l^\alpha\mbf I
	\right)^{-1}.
	\label{eq:modified_relative_flux_permeability}
\end{equation}
%
Then the admissible relative mass flux is
%
\begin{align}
	\mbf w_l
	= \rho_l\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_l
	\left[
		\rho_l\left(\mbf g-\mbf a_l\right)
		-\phi_l\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_l
		+\sum_\alpha\dot c_l^\alpha
		\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_l^\alpha-\mbf v_s\right)
		\right].
	\label{eq:generalized_mobile_darcy_mass_flux}
\end{align}
%
This expression is the multicomponent mobile-phase analogue of the generalized
Darcy flux in the earlier reacting-mixture model.  The first two terms are the
usual body-force and pressure-gradient contributions.  The last term is the
thermodynamic conversion correction: mass transfer into or out of phase $l$
drives relative motion through gradients of the solved component potentials
$\mu_l^\alpha$ and through the momentum carried by the solid frame.

When conversion is absent, $\dot c_l^\alpha=0$, and when mobile-phase
inertia is negligible, \eqref{eq:generalized_mobile_darcy_mass_flux} reduces to
%
\begin{equation}
	\phi_l\left(\mbf v_l-\mbf v_s\right)
	=-\frac{\bs\kappa_l}{\mu_l}
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_l-\bar\rho_l\mbf g\right).
	\label{eq:standard_darcy_mass_flux_limit}
\end{equation}
%
which is Darcy's law for phase $l$ relative to the deforming solid skeleton.  In
the reacting case, \eqref{eq:generalized_mobile_darcy_mass_flux} should be used
instead of \eqref{eq:standard_darcy_mass_flux_limit} whenever conversion rates
are large enough for momentum production or gradients of $\mu_l^\alpha$ to
affect transport.

\subsection{Solid-reference relative fluxes}
\label{sec:solid_reference_relative_fluxes}

The current relative flux in \eqref{eq:generalized_mobile_darcy_mass_flux} is
converted to a flux through surfaces in the solid reference configuration by the
Piola transform.  Equivalently, the quantity assembled in the reference
component balances is
%
\begin{align}
	J_s\mbf F_s^{-1}\mbf w_l
	= & \;
	\rho_l J_s\mbf F_s^{-1}\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_l
	\left[
	\rho_l\left(\mbf g-\mbf a_l\right)
	+\sum_\alpha\dot c_l^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_l^\alpha-\mbf v_s\right)
	\right]
	\notag \\
	  & -\rho_l\phi_l J_s\mbf F_s^{-1}
	\widetilde{\bs\kappa}_l\mbf F_s^{-T}
	\nabla_{\mbf X_s}\bar p_l .
	\label{eq:solid_reference_relative_flux}
\end{align}
%
Here gradients of scalar fields obey
$\nabla_{\mbf x}(\cdot)=\mbf F_s^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X_s}(\cdot)$, while the
divergence of a spatial flux obeys
%
\begin{equation}
	J_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf w_l
	=
	\nabla_{\mbf X_s}\cdot
	\left(J_s\mbf F_s^{-1}\mbf w_l\right).
	\label{eq:solid_reference_flux_divergence}
\end{equation}
%
Equations~\eqref{eq:solid_reference_relative_flux} and
\eqref{eq:solid_reference_flux_divergence} are the direct multicomponent
generalization of the reference flux used in the earlier reacting-mixture model,
with phase labels, component conversion rates, and component chemical-potential
gradients retained.

\subsection{Component balances on the solid trajectory}
\label{sec:solid_reference_component_balances}

For a mobile phase, substituting
\eqref{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction} into
\eqref{eq:model_summary_mass_balance} and writing the storage term on the solid
trajectory gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
	\left(J_s\phi_l\bar\rho_l\eta_l^\alpha\right)
	\right\vert_{\mbf X_s}
	+\nabla_{\mbf X_s}\cdot
	\left(J_s\mbf F_s^{-1}\eta_l^\alpha\mbf w_l\right)
	=
	J_s\sum_m\nu_{l m}^\alpha\dot r_m .
	\label{eq:solid_reference_mobile_component_balance}
\end{equation}
%
For the solid phase, the phase velocity is the skeleton velocity, so the
relative-flux term is absent:
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
	\left(J_s\phi_s\bar\rho_s\eta_s^\alpha\right)
	\right\vert_{\mbf X_s}
	=
	J_s\sum_m\nu_{s m}^\alpha\dot r_m .
	\label{eq:solid_reference_solid_component_balance}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the solid-reference storage variables are the Lagrangian partial masses
$J_s\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha$, while transport through the solid
mesh is carried only by the mobile relative fluxes.

\subsection{Overall momentum on the solid trajectory}
\label{sec:solid_reference_overall_momentum}

The overall momentum balance \eqref{eq:overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot} can be
written on the solid trajectory by applying the Piola transform to the stress
divergence and multiplying the remaining spatial source terms by $J_s$.  The
result is
%
\begin{align}
	J_s\sum_\xi\rho_\xi\mbf a_\xi
	= & \;
	\nabla_{\mbf X_s}\cdot
	\left[
	J_s
	\left(
	\bs\sigma_s^{\prime\prime}-B_s\bar p_E\mbf I
	\right)
	\mbf F_s^{-T}
	\right]
	+J_s\rho\mbf g
	\notag \\
	  & +J_s\sum_\alpha\sum_\xi
	\dot c_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha-\mbf v_\xi
	\right).
	\label{eq:solid_reference_overall_momentum}
\end{align}
%
If a quasi-static solid-skeleton solve is desired, the inertial term on the left
is dropped.  If the mobile velocities are eliminated with
\eqref{eq:relative_flux_velocity_reconstruction}, then the same equation is
solved with $\mbf v_l=\mbf v_s+\mbf w_l/\rho_l$ for mobile phases and
$\mbf v_s$ for the solid phase.  This leaves the solid displacement, the
Lagrangian partial masses, and the mobile relative fluxes as the natural fields
for a reference-configuration implementation.
