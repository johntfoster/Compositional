\section{Technical setting, conventions, and model scope}
\label{sec:technical_setting}

This section fixes the notation and assumptions used throughout the derivation.
It supplements, but does not replace, the local definitions attached to the
governing equations and constitutive specializations.

\subsection{Indices, configurations, and measures}

The phase index is \(\xi\).  Fluid phases are indexed by \(f\in\mc F\), solid
phases by \(s\in\mc S\), and \(\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}\) denotes one of the two
thermal subsystems.  Greek superscripts \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) index
components within a phase; when a reference component is required, it is
labelled \(N\).  The parenthesized index \((m)\) labels a conversion mechanism,
including a chemical reaction, phase transformation, sorption process, or ion
exchange.  Phase and component sums are written explicitly.

All phases occupy the common current region \(\mc B\), with spatial coordinate
\(\mbf x\) and volume element \(\dv\).  Phase \(\xi\) is tracked from its own
reference region \(\mc B_{\xi0}\), with coordinate \(\mbf X_\xi\) and volume
element \(\dVxi\).  Its motion \(\bs\chi_\xi\) is assumed one-to-one and
continuously differentiable wherever that phase is active, with
\(J_\xi=\det\mbf F_\xi>0\).  All load-bearing solid phases later share the
skeleton motion \(\bs\chi(\mbf X,t)\), deformation gradient \(\mbf F\), and
Jacobian \(J>0\).  Unless a reference coordinate is shown explicitly,
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\) and \(\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\) are current-configuration
operators.

Densities without an overbar are measured per current mixture volume:
\(\rho_\xi^\alpha\) is a component partial density and
\(\rho_\xi=\sum_\alpha\rho_\xi^\alpha\) is a phase partial density.  The
intrinsic density \(\bar\rho_\xi\) is measured per current phase volume, so
\(\rho_\xi=\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\).  The active-state domain satisfies
\(\bar\rho_\xi>0\), \(0<\phi_\xi<1\),
\(\eta_\xi^\alpha\ge0\),
\(\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha=1\), and
\(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi=1\).  Constitutive relations containing
\(1/\phi_\xi\), \(1/\eta_\xi^\alpha\), or inverse mobility operators are
understood on the corresponding active subspace.  Phase appearance,
disappearance, and changes of active component set require a separate
complementarity or active-set treatment and are not supplied by the local
constitutive equations.

\subsection{Observers and rates}

The operator \(D_\zeta/Dt\) follows phase \(\zeta\), and the heavy dot
\(\phasedot{\square}_\xi\) follows phase \(\xi\).  In the constitutive and
entropy analysis, an ordinary dot denotes the derivative following the common
solid skeleton, as defined in
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_rate_convention}.  The two rates are related by
\eqref{eq:MC_phase_skeleton_transport_identity}; their difference contains the
bulk phase velocity relative to the skeleton.  The notation
\(\dot r_{(m)}\) denotes a local mechanism progress rate, not a material
derivative of a state variable.  Likewise,
\(\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha\) and
\(\phasedot c_\xi^\alpha\) are component-insertion rates attached to phase
\(\xi\).

\subsection{Mechanical and thermodynamic conventions}

Cauchy stresses are tension-positive.  In the momentum balances they appear
through \(+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma\); tractions therefore have the sign
of \(\bs\sigma\mbf n\) on a surface with outward unit normal \(\mbf n\).
Thermodynamic phase pressures, capillary pressures, and the equivalent pore
pressure are positive in compression and enter a total stress through a
negative spherical contribution.  A single prime denotes the material stress
obtained with intrinsic density held fixed, a double prime denotes the
fixed-pressure Biot stress, and a superscript \(+\) denotes the electrical
stress contribution.  The stress appearing in momentum balance is the sum of
the material and electrical contributions.

Temperatures are absolute and strictly positive.  All mobile phases share
\(\theta_{\mc F}\), all load-bearing solid phases share
\(\theta_{\mc S}\), and heat exchange between these groups is retained.  The
local-thermal-equilibrium specialization sets
\(\theta_{\mc F}=\theta_{\mc S}\).  The phase Helmholtz energies
\(\psi_\xi\), specific internal energies \(e_\xi\), and component potentials
are energies per unit phase mass.  The electric enthalpy
\(\omega_\xi^+\) is energy per current phase volume, while \(\gamma\) is
interfacial Helmholtz energy per current fluid volume.  Multiplication by the
appropriate phase or fluid volume fraction converts these quantities to energy
per current mixture volume.

The constitutive functions are assumed sufficiently differentiable for every
derivative used below.  They are objective under superposed proper-orthogonal
changes of spatial frame: scalar potentials are invariant, spatial vectors and
Cauchy stresses transform covariantly, and the solid energies depend on their
elastic arguments through frame-indifferent combinations.  Constitutive
functions also respect the material-symmetry group assigned to each phase; no
isotropy is implied unless it is imposed in a stated specialization.  The
present nonpolar continuum has no independent couple stress.  Consequently,
admissible constitutive choices must satisfy angular-momentum balance and yield
a symmetric total Cauchy stress, including any anisotropic mechanical or
electrical contributions.

\subsection{Electrical ensemble and gauge}

The electrical specialization is electroquasistatic:
\(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\), with no electromagnetic-wave dynamics.
The phase electric enthalpies take \(\mbf E\) as their electrical argument, and
\(\mbf d_\xi=-\partial\omega_\xi^+/\partial\mbf E\).  Free bulk charge
\(\varrho\) and prescribed outward surface charge \(\bar q\) are source-work
coefficients rather than additional stored energies.  Charge carried by the
material is obtained by weighting each component mass by
\(z_\xi^\alpha\); independently prescribed bulk charge is an external loading
contribution.

The boundary is partitioned into \(\Gamma_\varphi\), where \(\varphi\) is
prescribed, and \(\Gamma_q\), where
\(\mbf d\cdot\mbf n=\bar q\).  If no potential value is prescribed, the
additive gauge of \(\varphi\) must be fixed, for example by prescribing its
mean.  Observable reaction and transport laws use charge-conserving
stoichiometric projections, electrochemical-potential gradients, or coupled
gauge-invariant Onsager blocks.  The reservoir specialization takes
\(\omega_\xi^+\) to be even in \(\mbf E\), thereby excluding permanent
polarization at zero field and independent dielectric relaxation.

\subsection{Source units and stoichiometric basis}

The component source
\(\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}\) always has units of component mass per
current mixture volume per time.  The progress basis may be selected separately
for each mechanism, provided the coefficient and rate are paired consistently.
For a mass-based transfer, \(\dot r_{(m)}\) has units of mass per current
mixture volume per time and the signed \(\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\) are
dimensionless mass yields; a one-to-one phase transfer then uses \(-1\) and
\(+1\).  For a molar reaction, \(\dot r_{(m)}\) has units of amount of reaction
per current mixture volume per time and
\(\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\) is the signed molar stoichiometric coefficient
multiplied by the component molar mass.  In either basis, each internal
mechanism satisfies the mass- and charge-conservation restrictions stated in
\eqref{eq:spatial_mass_3} and
\eqref{eq:mechanism_charge_conservation}.  Relative component fluxes have units
of component mass per current area per time.

\subsection{Initial and boundary data}

The local theory does not prescribe a single boundary-value problem.  A
well-posed application supplies initial phase/component masses, active phase
fractions, temperatures, solid internal variables, and kinematic fields that
satisfy the mass-fraction, volume-fraction, mass, and charge compatibility
conditions.  The electrostatic potential has no independent evolution datum;
it is obtained at each time from Gauss' law, the current charge state, and its
boundary data.

Boundary data are imposed in complementary classes appropriate to the selected
reduction: motion or total traction for mechanics; component potential,
composition, or normal total component flux for transport; temperature or
normal heat flux for each thermal subsystem; and potential or normal electric
displacement for electrostatics.  Boundary and initial data must also respect
the zero-sum relative-flux constraints, any prescribed impermeability or
insulation conditions, and global compatibility conditions associated with
pure-Neumann mechanical, thermal, or electrical problems.
