\section{Variational principle derivation}
\label{sec:variational}
\label{sec:virtual_power_derivation}

The phase-coordinate balances are derived by Hamilton--d'Alembert stationarity,
and the dissipative process rates are closed separately by Onsager
stationarity.  The Hamilton--d'Alembert calculation follows the extended
Hamilton principles used by Bedford and Drumheller for porous and reacting immiscible mixtures
\cite{bedford1979variational,drumheller1980thermomechanical,bedford1983theories}
and determines the mixture coordinates and constraints.  Its virtual work also
carries the d'Alembert work of a constitutive dissipative force when dynamic
capillarity is active.  The Onsager calculation pairs dissipative saturation,
reaction, and relative-transport rates with their thermodynamic forces.
Exploitation of the entropy inequality then requires nonnegative dissipation
\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  This is the continuum counterpart of
using virtual work for coordinate changes and virtual power for process rates
\cite{germain1973virtualpower,gurtin2010continua}.  The saturation force is held
fixed during the coordinate variation and paired with \(\phasedot S_f\) during the
Onsager variation, so the two statements supply complementary coordinate and
constitutive equations.

The formulation applies to any specified collection of solid $\mc S$ and fluid
$\mc F$ phases.  Here fluid means a nonsolid pore
phase, such as an aqueous, oleic, or gas phase.  The symbol $s \in \mc{S}$
denotes a solid phase, $f \in\mc F$ denotes a fluid phase, and
$\xi \in \mc{S} \cup \mc{F}$ ranges over all phases.  Components within each
phase are indexed by $\alpha$.

\subsection{Hamilton--d'Alembert principle for the coordinate balances}

Let \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) be the endpoint times and let \(\delta\) denote an
admissible virtual variation that vanishes at those endpoints.  The
Hamilton--d'Alembert statement contains the kinetic and stored-energy variation,
mechanical and electrical source work, the volume and composition constraints,
and the material insertion constraint:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(T-U\right)\dt
	+
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
	\left(\delta W+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\delta C_k\right)\dt
	= 0.
	\label{eq:vp_principle}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
In the virtual-work term below, \(\mbf g\) is the body force per unit phase mass
and \(\mbf b_\xi\) is the force density exerted on phase \(\xi\) through
interaction with the other phases.  The individual interaction forces may
include dissipative drag, but they transfer momentum only within the mixture.
They therefore satisfy the global balance
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\mbf b_\xi=\mbf 0.
	\label{eq:interphase_force_global_balance}
\end{equation}
%
The energies, virtual work, and four constraints are defined together by
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
\small
\begin{align}
	T        & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}}\frac{1}{2} \rho_\xi \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \dv,
	\label{eq:T_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	U        & = \sum_{\xi}\int_{\mc{B}} \rho_{\xi}\,\psi_{\xi}(\mbf{F}_{\xi}, \bar\rho_{\xi}, \{\eta^\al_{\xi}\},\theta_\xi)\dv
	+ \int_{\mc{B}} \phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}\right) \dv
	+\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi)
	\,\dv,
	\label{eq:U_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	\delta W & = \int_{\mc B}\left[
		\sum_\xi\left(\rho_\xi\mbf g+\mbf b_\xi\right)\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
		+\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\left(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\right)
		\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		-\phi\sum_{f\in\mc F}\mc L_f^{\rm sat}
		\delta S_f
		-\varrho\,\delta\varphi
		\right]\dv
	\notag                                                                                                                                                                                                         \\
	         & \quad
	+\sum_\xi\int_{\partial\mc B}
	\mbf t_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi\,{\rm d}a
	+\int_{\partial\mc B}\bar q\,\delta\varphi\,{\rm d}a,
	\label{eq:W_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_1      & = \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \,\dv,
	\label{eq:C1_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_2      & = \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi 0}} \pi_\xi^\al \left( J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi 0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} \bar\rho_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \eta^\al_\xi \bar\rho_\xi} \right) \dV_\xi,
	\label{eq:C2_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_3      & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \left( \sum_\alpha \eta_\xi^\alpha - 1 \right) \dv,
	\label{eq:C3_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_4      & = \int_{\mc B}\tau
	\left(
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	\right)\dv .
	\label{eq:C4_def}
\end{align}
\endgroup
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here \(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\) is the mass-specific energetic supply associated
with component insertion.  Both terms have units of energy per unit mass;
\(L_\xi^\alpha\) is the specific generalized transfer-work potential, normalized
by the mass of component \(\alpha\) inserted into phase \(\xi\).  Under the
local-equilibrium insertion convention, added material carries the
recipient-phase Helmholtz storage \(\psi_\xi\), while \(L_\xi^\alpha\) supplies
the associated transfer work.  This energy partition fixes the coefficient
before stationarity is taken.  The stored-energy
variation contributes \(-\psi_\xi\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\), whereas
\(\delta W\) contributes
\(+(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha)\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\); their sum gives the
transfer-work term in the component-increment Euler--Lagrange equation.  The
thermodynamic restrictions below relate
\(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\) to the component potential.  The relation includes
the electrical contribution, a phase-common transfer-potential rate, and
inertia.  The resulting sign and conjugate pairing agrees with the
Bedford--Drumheller form
\cite[eqs.~(2.2) and (2.8)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}.

The coefficient \(\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\) is the generalized force per unit pore
volume conjugate to saturation redistribution.  It has pressure units and is
held fixed during the coordinate variation.  The saturation variation in
\eqref{eq:W_def} follows the same current-spatial convention as
\(\delta\phi_f\); the integration over \(\mc B\) makes a separate fixed-point
qualifier unnecessary.  No additional multiplier is needed for saturation
normalization.  The definitions
\(S_f=\phi_f/\phi\) and \(\phi=\sum_f\phi_f\) in
\eqref{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions} make \(\sum_fS_f=1\) an identity, and
their induced variations satisfy \(\sum_f\delta S_f=0\).  Thus only differences
of the coefficients \(\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\) perform work.  A multiplier would
only add the same scalar to every coefficient, and that common contribution
vanishes for every admissible saturation variation.

\pagebreak[4]
The electrical boundary is partitioned into
\(\Gamma_\varphi\), where \(\varphi\) is prescribed and
\(\delta\varphi=0\), and \(\Gamma_{\bar q}\), where \(\bar q\) is the prescribed
outward free surface-charge density.  The electrical terms in
\(\delta W\) define a free-charge source-work ensemble: \(\varrho\) and
\(\bar q\) prescribe source work conjugate to \(\delta\varphi\) in the volume
and on the boundary, respectively.  The component-carried
part of \(\varrho\) is the component sum in
\eqref{eq:mixture_charge_density_definition}.  As for the body-force
coefficients in \(\delta W\), the material variation holds these source
coefficients and prescribed loading data fixed.

For each phase map, let
\(\partial\mc B=\Gamma_{\mbf x_\xi}\cup
\Gamma_{\mbf t_\xi}\), with disjoint interiors,
\(\mbf x_\xi\) prescribed and \(\delta\mbf x_\xi=\mbf0\) on
\(\Gamma_{\mbf x_\xi}\), and prescribed outward traction
\(\mbf t_\xi\) on \(\Gamma_{\mbf t_\xi}\).  Accordingly, the boundary terms
in \eqref{eq:W_def} yield the natural traction and electrical conditions
derived below.

The phase electric enthalpy supplies the Maxwell contribution to the reversible
phase stress derived below.  The Hamilton--d'Alembert principle probes those
reversible coordinate changes at a specified instantaneous thermal state, so
\(\delta\theta_{\mc F}=\delta\theta_{\mc S}=0\).  The temperatures remain
constitutive arguments of the stored energy because the coordinate equations
depend on the current thermal state.  Their physical rates are supplied by the
fluid and solid energy balances and enter the entropy inequality, whose
Coleman--Noll exploitation determines the associated constitutive restrictions
\cite{coleman1974thermodynamics}.  This division between variational motion,
energy balance, and thermodynamic restriction follows the thermomechanical
mixture construction of Bedford and Drumheller
\cite[Secs.~2--4]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}.

The phase-assigned temperature is \(\theta_\xi=\theta_{\mc F}\) for
\(f\in\mc F\) and \(\theta_\xi=\theta_{\mc S}\) for \(s\in\mc S\).
Constraint \(C_1\) enforces the volume-fraction constraint, \(C_2\) enforces
the augmented component material measure, and \(C_3\) enforces mass-fraction
normalization.

Constraint \(C_4\) enforces zero net material insertion over all phases and
components.  Writing the rate of component mass addition per current mixture
volume as
\(\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha=\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\), this constraint imposes
\(\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_\xi^\alpha=0\).  Internal conversion satisfies the
constraint through stoichiometric mass conservation, while relative component
transport satisfies it phase by phase through
\eqref{eq:averaged_relative_flux_sum}.

The transfer multiplier \(\tau\) is the Bedford--Drumheller kinematic potential
conjugate to the globally conservative accumulated material insertion.  It has
units of action per unit mass, so
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\) has velocity units and
\(D_\xi\tau/Dt\) has units of energy per unit mass.  Thus \(\tau\) supplies the
conversion transfer-momentum coefficient in the reversible variation.

The coordinate variations are $\mbf x_\xi$, $\eta_\xi^\alpha$,
$\phi_\xi$, $\bar\rho_\xi$, and $\mc C_\xi^\alpha$.  The mechanism rates
and the two relative fluxes are varied later as independent process rates
through the constrained Onsager entropy-rate functional in
Section~\ref{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}.  In the optional dynamic-capillary
branch, the independent zero-sum saturation rates are Onsager rates driven by
the generalized force in
\eqref{eq:W_def}.  The entropy inequality later
supplies the constitutive admissibility condition.

\subsubsection{Extending Bedford and Drumheller transport identity}
\label{sec:extended_bd_identity}

Bedford and Drumheller~\cite[eq.~(1.28)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
give an identity for the variation of a constituent-attached spatial field
$\Upsilon_\xi$ weighted by the current phase density $\rho_\xi$.  The same
transport argument extends componentwise to the compositional setting used here.

The component material increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) is attached to the
phase-\(\xi\) reference coordinates.  It is the added component mass appearing
in \eqref{eq:augmented_component_material_measure}.  In the reversible
Hamilton--d'Alembert variation, its componentwise virtual increment is therefore
carried with the phase-attached material measure.
%
For any phase field $\Upsilon_\xi$, the Bedford--Drumheller identity extends componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho^\alpha_\xi \Upsilon_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \delta \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi0}+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\right)
	\Upsilon_\xi \,\dVxi \notag      \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left[
		\delta\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\Upsilon_\xi
		+ J_\xi\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Upsilon_\xi
	\right]\dVxi \notag              \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}}
	\left[
		\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Upsilon_\xi
		+ \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\Upsilon_\xi
		\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:bd128_mod2}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Summing both sides of \eqref{eq:bd128_mod2} over $\alpha$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi \Upsilon_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \left[\rho_\xi \delta \Upsilon_\xi + \sum_\alpha \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \Upsilon_\xi  \right]  \dv,
	\label{eq:bd128_mod}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where $\rho_\xi = \sum_\alpha \rho^\alpha_\xi$.

\subsubsection{Variation of the kinetic energy}
\label{sec:var_T_app}
% ============================================================

The variation of kinetic energy using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod} is then
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta T = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi\,\mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{v}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \dv.
	\label{eq:kinetic_energy_variation_before_time_integration}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Since $\delta\mbf{v}_\xi = \delta\dot{\mbf{x}}_\xi$ and $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_1} = \delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_2} = \mbf{0}$, integration by parts in time gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \delta T \dt
	= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left[-\rho_\xi\,\mbf{a}_\xi - \sum_\al \dot{c}_\xi^\alpha \mbf{v}_\xi \right] \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}  \dv \dt.
	\label{eq:deltaT_app}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
\subsubsection{Variation of the phase free energies}
\label{sec:var_Uxi}
% ============================================================

For every phase, solid or fluid, let $\psi_\xi$ denote the nonelectrical
specific Helmholtz free energy and let \(N\) be the number of tracked components
in that phase.  At the fixed thermal state specified above, its contribution to
the stored thermodynamic potential is written
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\xi
	=
	\sum_\xi \int_{\mc B}
	\rho_\xi
	\psi_\xi(\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi)\,\dv.
	\label{eq:U_xi_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Here \(\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}\) denotes the indexed collection of all component
mass fractions for phase \(\xi\).
The constitutive dependence on $\mbf F_\xi$ may be trivial for fluid phases,
but keeping it in the notation allows one calculation for all phases.  The local
free-energy variation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta\psi_\xi
	 & = \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}:\delta\mbf F_\xi
	+ \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
	+ \sum_\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
	\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:delta_psi_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod}, integrating the $\delta\mbf F_\xi$ term by parts,
and collecting independent variations gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\xi
	 & = \sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}\left[
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma'_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
		+ \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
	\right. \notag                  \\
	 & \qquad \left.
		+ \sum_\alpha \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+ \sum_\alpha \psi_\xi\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv
	+
	\sum_\xi\int_{\partial\mc B}
	\left(
	\bs\sigma'_\xi\mbf n
	\right)\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi\,{\rm d}a .
	\label{eq:deltaU_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The corresponding Cauchy effective stress is denoted with a single prime because
it is the fixed-intrinsic-density stress, analogous to a Terzaghi effective
stress.  The fixed-pressure Biot stress is denoted later with a double prime.
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\bs\sigma'_\xi
	\equiv \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}\mbf F_\xi^T .
	\label{eq:var_cauchy_def}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For fluid phases, \(\psi_f\) may be independent of \(\mbf F_f\), giving
\(\bs\sigma_f'=\mbf 0\).

\subsubsection{Variation of the interfacial energies}
\label{sec:var_gamma}
% ============================================================

Let $\mc I$ denote the set of oriented fluid interfaces.  Each
$(w,n)\in\mc I$ identifies a wetting phase $w$ and a non-wetting phase $n$.
The total fluid volume fraction and the fluid saturations are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi = \sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f,
	\qquad
	S_f = \frac{\phi_f}{\phi},
	\qquad
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f=1 .
	\label{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The saturations are used here only as ratios of the primitive volume-fraction
fields; the variations are still taken with respect to the primitive
$\phi_f$.  The notation $\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}$ denotes the fluid
saturation state subject to the constraint in
\eqref{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}.  Thus the energy may be written in
terms of any independent set of fluid saturations, with the remaining
saturation determined by the constraint.  The derivation below keeps this
sum constraint explicit, so all fluid phases are treated symmetrically.
The vector \(\mbf h\in\mathbb R^{N_h}\) collects continuous local
capillary-history variables carried by the solid skeleton.  These variables
record the reversal and trapping state needed to distinguish drainage,
imbibition, and scanning paths; their constitutive evolution is restricted by
the entropy inequality below.  The effective interfacial Helmholtz free-energy
density per unit current pore volume is a single constitutive potential of the
constrained saturation and capillary-history state
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma =
	\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}\right).
	\label{eq:interfacial_energy_density}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Its contribution to the stored thermodynamic potential per current mixture
volume is \(\phi\gamma\), so
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\gamma =
	\int_{\mc{B}}
	\phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}\right)
	\dv .
	\label{eq:interfacial_internal_energy}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

The reversible coordinate variation holds \(\mbf h\) and
\(\theta_{\mc F}\) fixed and therefore acts through the primitive fluid volume
fractions and their saturation ratios.

The Bedford--Drumheller spatial identity
(cf.\ \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical} equation (1.25)) gives, for each
fluid phase field $\phi_f$,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\delta\phi_\xi\right\vert_{\mbf{x}}
	= \delta\phi_\xi-\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi.
	\label{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\phi=\sum_{f\in\mc F}\delta\phi_f,
	\qquad
	\delta S_f=\frac{\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi}{\phi},
	\label{eq:saturation_variations}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
the fixed-point contribution and the transport correction from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation} are collected in one
calculation:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\gamma
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\left[
		\gamma\,\delta\phi
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}}
		\left(\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi\right)
	\right]\dv \notag                                          \\
	 & \quad
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv \notag \\
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\delta\phi_f\dv
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv .
	\label{eq:deltaU_fg}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The subscript \(g\) denotes the set of all fluid phases excluding \(f\).  The
held-fixed derivatives\footnote{Here, \(\gamma\) is defined only for
	saturations whose sum is one.  The indicated partial derivatives are evaluated
	from any twice continuously differentiable continuation to nearby saturation
	values.  At a physical saturation state, different continuations change every
	such derivative by the same scalar, so the combinations used below are
	independent of that continuation.}
are evaluated at the physical saturation state.  The physical variations
in \eqref{eq:saturation_variations} preserve \(\sum_f\delta S_f=0\).
The phase interfacial potential appearing in \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma
	+\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}}
	-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g
	\left.\p{\gamma}{S_g}\right\vert_{S_{k\neq g},\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F}},
	\label{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where the common derivative shift described in the footnote cancels from the
displayed combination.  For each oriented wetting/non-wetting pair, the
interfacial difference defines the capillary pressure
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	p_{c,nw}\equiv\gamma_n-\gamma_w,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:pc_def}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The form \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} keeps the gradient terms generated by the scalar
transport of the phase-attached volume fractions.  These terms combine with the
volume-fraction and component-storage multiplier gradients in the phase momentum
balances after the volume-fraction Euler--Lagrange equations are used.
The later thermodynamic balance evaluates the volume-fraction rates in a
control volume following the common solid skeleton, whereas the present
coordinate variations remain phase-specific.  Both
operations differentiate the same primitive volume-fraction fields, so the
conjugate interfacial coefficient remains \(\gamma_f\).

At fixed \(\mbf h\), the stored capillary contribution remains algebraic at the
current saturations and fluid temperature.  Evolution of \(\mbf h\) makes that
contribution depend on the drainage--imbibition path.  Setting \(N_h=0\)
recovers the single-valued equilibrium closure, where \(N_h\) is the number of
continuous capillary-history variables.

Dynamic capillarity is introduced through the generalized saturation force in
\eqref{eq:W_def} while leaving the stored potential
\(\gamma\) unchanged.  The generalized saturation force
\(\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\) is closed by Onsager stationarity subject to the
admissible dissipation conditions derived below.  Independent interfacial-area
evolution would require an additional stored state and kinetic equation and is
not included here.

\subsubsection{Variation of the phase electric enthalpy}
\label{sec:electric_field_variation}

The electrical contribution to the Hamilton action is assigned phase by phase
through \(U_\xi^+\).  The phase electric-enthalpy density
\(\omega_\xi^+=\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,
\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi)\) is normalized per current phase volume;
\(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\) is therefore its contribution per current mixture
volume.  Its arguments are the electric field, intrinsic phase density,
composition, and temperature.  For each phase,
%
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi)
	\,\dv
	=
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi)
	\,\dV_\xi .
	\label{eq:phase_electric_enthalpy_pullback}
\end{equation}
%
Let \(\varphi\) denote the electrostatic potential.  In the electroquasistatic
specialization, the electric field and the phase and mixture electric
displacements are
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi,
	\qquad
	\mbf d_\xi
	=
	-\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{\xi}}{\mbf E}
	\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:electric_field_definitions}
\end{equation}
%
Thus \(\mbf E\) is the electrical constitutive argument, while
\(\mbf d_\xi\) is derived from \(\omega_\xi^+\).  The electric potential
\(\varphi\) is the corresponding reversible coordinate in the variational
principle, consistent with
electroelastic potential formulations
\cite{dorfmann2017nonlinear_electroelasticity}.  The objective single-vector
electrical specialization and its material-symmetry limitation are stated in
\sref{technical_setting}.

With \(\otimes\) denoting the tensor outer product, the volume-fraction
variation is the phase-attached variation from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}, left here as
\(\delta\phi_\xi\) so its direct coefficient remains visible.  The
current-configuration variation is
%
\begin{align}
	\delta U^{+}_{\xi}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left[
		\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi
		+
		\phi_\xi
		\left.
		\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_\xi}
		\right\vert_{\mbf E,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}
		\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left[
		\phi_\xi
		\sum_\alpha
		\left.
		\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,
			\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
		\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+
		\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\varphi
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^+:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv .
	\label{eq:vp_electric_full_variation}
\end{align}
%
The mechanical coefficient in \eqref{eq:vp_electric_full_variation} defines the
reversible electrical stress assigned to the phase pullback.  Variation of the
current volume element and convection of the spatial electric field produce the
phase-attributed Maxwell stress, consistently with
electric-enthalpy virtual-work derivations
\cite{mcmeeking2007virtual_work_electrostatic}.  We denote this Maxwell stress
by \(\bs\sigma_\xi^+\),
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\phi_\xi
	\left(
	\omega_\xi^+\mbf I
	+
	\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi
	\right).
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the total reversible stress used in the phase momentum equation is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
	\equiv
	\bs\sigma_\xi'
	+
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{+}.
	\label{eq:vp_total_reversible_stress_definition}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_1$}{C1}: volume-fraction}
\label{sec:var_C1_app}
% ============================================================

The constraint $C_1$ is
imposed in the current configuration, so the natural variation is
taken at fixed spatial point $\mbf x$.  Since each $\phi_\xi$ is a
constituent-attached spatial field, \eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
expresses the variation in terms of the spatial variation $\delta\phi_\xi$ and
the motion variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_1
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\lambda \left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\delta\phi_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv.
	\label{eq:deltaC1_app}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The first term returns the volume-fraction constraint
$\sum_\xi\phi_\xi = 1$.  The remaining terms contribute $\lambda$ to each
$\delta\phi_\xi$ equation and $-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi$ to each
momentum variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_2$}{C2}: component material mass balance}

The component material mass balance constraint is

	C_2 = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha} \right) \,dV_\xi.
	\label{eq:C2_component_measure}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The multiplier \(\pi_\xi^\alpha\) enforces the component material-mass balance
for component \(\alpha\) in phase \(\xi\).  Its variation gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_2
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha \delta J_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi^2\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta\phi_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi^2 \eta^\al_\xi}\,\delta\bar\rho_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi (\eta^\al_\xi)^2}\,\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	- \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}
	\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi.
	\notag                                        \\
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi \, \dv
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}}
	\left[
		\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}\delta\phi_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		- \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv
	+
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\int_{\partial\mc B}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\mbf n\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi\,{\rm d}a .
	\label{eq:deltaC2_lines}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here the second equality uses $\dv = J_\xi {\rm d}V_\xi$ and
$\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha
	=J_\xi\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha$.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_4$}{C4}: material insertion}
\label{sec:component_rate_constraint_variations}
\label{sec:stoich_conversion_variations}
\label{sec:transport_accumulation_variations}

The Bedford--Drumheller transfer constraint contains the component material
increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) carried by phase \(\xi\).  Its rate variation must
be integrated-by-parts with the same phase material derivative before any
stationarity condition is used.  A single transfer potential \(\tau\) enforces
the total insertion-rate constraint.  Varying
\(\tau\), the material measure, and the phase map gives
%
\begin{align}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\delta C_4\,\dt
	={} &
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}
	\delta\tau
	\left[
		\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv\dt
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		-\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		+
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
		\right]\dv\dt
	+
	\left.
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\tau\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\,\dv
	\right\vert_{t_1}^{t_2}.
	\label{eq:C4_variation}
\end{align}
%
The component-material variations vanish at \(t_1\) and \(t_2\), so the final
endpoint term in \eqref{eq:C4_variation} is zero for every admissible variation.
Varying \(\tau\) in \eqref{eq:C4_variation} enforces the zero-sum insertion
rate.  Varying the material measure gives the coefficient \(D_\xi\tau/Dt\), and
varying the phase map contributes the momentum coefficient
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\).

\subsection{Euler--Lagrange equations}
\label{sec:euler_lagrange_equations}

Setting the coefficients in \eqref{eq:collected_full} to zero first gives
algebraic relations for the storage and constraint multipliers.  Substitution
of these relations into the remaining stationarity equations exposes the phase
momentum balances and the electrostatic field equation.

\subsubsection{Storage and multiplier relations}
\label{sec:el_storage_multiplier_relations}

The intrinsic-density variation \(\delta\bar\rho_\xi\) gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\al \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\al}{\phi_{\xi}}
	= \bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}
	+
	\bar\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}
	.
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_storage}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
We recognize the first term on the right-hand side as the thermodynamic phase
pressure contribution.  The second term is present when the phase electric
enthalpy depends on intrinsic density.  We define the phase pressure as
%
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\bar{p}_\xi \equiv
	\bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}
	+
	\bar\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}
	}.
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_all}
\end{equation}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} therefore identifies the total phase
pressure as the usual thermodynamic pressure plus the dielectric pressure
correction induced by the intrinsic-density dependence of the phase electric
enthalpy.

For solid phases $s \in \mc{S}$ without an explicit capillary contribution, the
phase-volume-fraction variation \(\delta\phi_\xi\) gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi_\xi \lambda
	=
	-\sum_\alpha \pi_\xi^\al
	+
	\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+ .
	\label{eq:el_lambda}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
This relation is used to simplify the phase momentum equations below and, for
fluid phases, to eliminate the volume-constraint multiplier in favor of phase
pressure and interfacial terms.  For a fluid phase the variation also carries
the primitive capillary potential, so its coefficient in
\eqref{eq:collected_full} contains the interfacial potential
\(\gamma_f\), the electric enthalpy \(\omega_f^+\), and the generalized
saturation force.  The corresponding Euler--Lagrange equation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\sum_\alpha\frac{\pi_f^\alpha}{\phi_f}-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
	-\mc L_f^{\rm sat}
	+\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g\mc L_g^{\rm sat}=0.
	\label{eq:fluid_volume_fraction_el_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_storage} and
\eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation}
	\begin{equation}
		\lambda+\bar p_f-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+
		-\mc L_f^{\rm sat}
		+\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g\mc L_g^{\rm sat}=0,
		\label{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation_a}
	\end{equation}
	or
	\begin{equation}
		\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f
		=\mc L_f^{\rm sat}
		-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g\mc L_g^{\rm sat}-\lambda .
		\label{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation_b}
	\end{equation}
\end{subequations}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Consequently the coordinate stationarity identifies the generalized-force
differences with the phase-pressure force differences,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left(\bar p_f-\omega_f^+-\gamma_f\right)
	-
	\left(\bar p_g-\omega_g^+-\gamma_g\right)
	=
	\mc L_f^{\rm sat}-\mc L_g^{\rm sat} .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
When the saturation-force differences vanish and the phases are electrically
neutral, this relation gives the standard capillary-equilibrium condition
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	=
	p_{c,nw},
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For two fluid phases with \(\mc F=\{w,n\}\) and one oriented pair \((w,n)\),
the zero-rate form of
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential} combines the intrinsic
capillary contribution \(p_{c,nw}\) with the pressure-like electrical
contribution \(\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+\).  This electrocapillary relation is
consistent with electrowetting interface balances, in which the free energy
contains surface, electrostatic, and ionic contributions and stationarity of
the interface position gives a pressure-difference balance against capillary
and Maxwell or electro-osmotic terms
\cite{papageorgiou2019film_flows_electric_fields,yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation}.
The same phase-attributed electrical enthalpy enters the phase momentum
balances, reversible stresses, and thermodynamic restrictions.  The dynamic
branch augments the pressure difference in
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential} by the lag in
\eqref{eq:MC_two_phase_dynamic_capillary_pressure}, which is
proportional to \(D_{\mc S}S_n/Dt\).
If the interfacial energy is parameterized by the wetting saturation \(S_w\),
then
\(p_{c,nw}=-{\rm d}\gamma(S_w,1-S_w,\mbf h,\theta_{\mc F})/{\rm d}S_w\) at
fixed \(\mbf h\) and \(\theta_{\mc F}\).

The same relations identify the equivalent pore pressure associated with the
volume constraint.  Define
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_E\equiv-\lambda .
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Multiplying \eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation} by the fluid
saturations and summing over fluid phases eliminates the generalized force:
because the saturations sum to unity, \(\sum_fS_f=1\), the term
\(\sum_fS_f\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\) cancels against
\((\sum_fS_f)(\sum_gS_g\mc L_g^{\rm sat})=\sum_gS_g\mc L_g^{\rm sat}\), and the
multiplier term becomes \(-\lambda\sum_fS_f=-\lambda=\bar p_E\).  The result is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\bar p_f
	=
	\bar p_E
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\omega_f^+ .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_pressure_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The primitive potentials satisfy
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_primitive_potential_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		\bar p_E
		=
		\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\left(\bar p_f-\omega_f^+\right)
		-
		\gamma
	}.
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
When \(\omega_f^+=0\), equation
\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation} reduces to the
equivalent pore pressure used in thermodynamically stable coupled multiphase
poromechanics \cite{coussy2004poromechanics,kim2013rigorous}.  The term
\(-\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f\omega_f^+\) is the present electrical extension of the
equivalent pore pressure, obtained from the phase-pressure relation above.
The common pore-volume restriction
\eqref{eq:MC_fluid_volume_fraction_restriction} restates this identity in the
actual phase pressures, and it remains valid when the dynamic lag is active
because the lag cancels from the saturation-weighted combination.

The remaining independent variations determine the composition and
component-transfer relations.
For a phase with \(N\) tracked components, the composition variations
\(\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha\) give
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	0 = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha} + \Pi_{\xi}
	- \rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	- \phi_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi},
	\qquad \alpha=1,\ldots,N,
	\label{eq:el_eta_all}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
while the multiplier variation \(\delta\Pi_\xi\) returns the phase
mass-fraction constraint
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\alpha=1}^N \eta_{\xi}^\alpha = 1.
	\label{eq:el_eta_constraint}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Thus $\Pi_{\xi}$ is the phase-level multiplier that enforces the redundant
composition coordinate.  Equation~\eqref{eq:el_eta_all} may be read
componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	+
	\phi_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}
	= \Pi_{\xi},
	\label{eq:Pi_componentwise}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The first term in \eqref{eq:Pi_componentwise} is the mixing contribution to
composition stationarity.  The second term is the composition derivative of
the phase electric enthalpy.  Coleman--Noll later combines these storage
derivatives into the component potential.  If \(\omega_\xi^+\) includes
composition-dependent dielectric or electrical-double-layer free energy, this
derivative contains the corresponding electrostatic or double-layer
contribution
\cite{newman2004electrochemical,leroy2004triple}.
%
Choosing component $N$ as the reference and comparing against
$\alpha=1,\ldots,N-1$ gives a system of $N-1$ equations,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	 & \rho_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq \alpha}}
	- \left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq N}}
	\right)
	+
	\phi_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
	- \left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq N},\theta_\xi}
	\right)
	\notag                                                 \\
	 & \qquad = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^N}{\eta_{\xi}^N},
	\qquad \alpha=1,\ldots,N-1.
	\label{eq:pi_difference_system}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

Finally, the component-material-measure variations
\(\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) give
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	= \frac{1}{2}\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	+L_\xi^\alpha
	-\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}.
	\label{eq:el_conversion_component}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} is the componentwise extension of
the Bedford--Drumheller transfer-potential equation, with microinertia omitted
\cite[eq.~(2.8)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
\cite[eq.~(48)]{drumheller2000}.  Because \(\tau\) is one scalar field shared
by all phases and components, one phase trajectory supplies its evolution
equation.  The remaining componentwise relations then fix the specific generalized transfer-work
potentials: the reference-component relation determines the reference
potential \(L_\xi^N\), and subtracting it from the other component relations
determines the within-phase differences \(L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N\).  The closure
that makes the retained-\(\tau\) system fully determined, and its thermodynamic
restrictions, are obtained later from the component-supply terms in the
Coleman--Noll procedure.

\subsubsection{Solid-phase momentum balance}
\label{sec:phase_momentum_balance}

For a solid phase \(s\in\mc S\), the phase-motion variation
\(\delta\mbf x_s\), after substitution of
\eqref{eq:el_lambda} and the equivalent-pore-pressure definition
\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_definition}, gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_s\mbf{a}_s =
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+} + \rho_s\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_s
	- \phi_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_E
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_s\omega_s^+\right)
	+\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_s^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_s\right),
	\label{eq:el_momentum_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The boundary coefficients from
\eqref{eq:deltaU_uniform},
\eqref{eq:vp_electric_full_variation},
\eqref{eq:deltaC2_lines}, and the prescribed traction work in
\eqref{eq:W_def} give the solid-phase natural traction.  Eliminating the
component-supply sum through \eqref{eq:el_lambda} and replacing
\(\lambda\) by the equivalent pore pressure
\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_definition} expresses the traction in
terms of \(\bar p_E\):
%
\begin{equation}
	\left[
		\bs\sigma_s^{\prime+}
		-
		\phi_s
		\left(
		\bar p_E+\omega_s^+
		\right)\mbf I
		\right]\mbf n
	=
	\mbf t_s
	\qquad
	\text{on }\Gamma_{\mbf t_s}.
	\label{eq:phase_natural_traction}
\end{equation}
%
The complementary prescribed-motion condition is
\(\delta\mbf x_s=\mbf0\) on \(\Gamma_{\mbf x_s}\).

The fluid-phase balance contains additional capillary forces and is reduced
separately below.

\subsubsection{Fluid-phase momentum balance}
\label{sec:fluid_phase_momentum}

For any fluid phase $f\in\mc F$, the collected variation
\eqref{eq:collected_full} gives the general intermediate form
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: general fluid-phase momentum balance; do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\rho_f\mbf a_f
	={}&
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
	+ \rho_f\mbf g + \mbf b_f
	-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	-\sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf x}\pi_f^\alpha
	\notag\\
	&+
	\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	+\left(\mc L_f^{\rm sat}-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g\mc L_g^{\rm sat}\right)\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	+\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_f^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right),
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary_general}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \(\sum_\alpha\pi_f^\alpha=\phi_f\bar p_f\) and the fluid
volume-fraction Euler--Lagrange relation
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation_a}, the multiplier,
interfacial-gradient, electric-enthalpy, and saturation-force terms collapse
as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_f\bar p_f\right)
	+\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	+\left(\mc L_f^{\rm sat}-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g\mc L_g^{\rm sat}\right)\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	=
	-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_f
	-\omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f .
	\label{eq:fluid_capillary_pressure_gradient_collapse}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The generalized saturation force enters the collected variation
\eqref{eq:collected_full} both directly, as the gradient term in
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary_general}, and through the
volume-fraction multiplier \(\lambda\) in
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation_a}.  These two
contributions cancel in the combination
\eqref{eq:fluid_capillary_pressure_gradient_collapse}, so the collapse into
the actual phase-pressure gradient carries no explicit saturation force.  The
saturation change forces instead enter the phase-pressure difference relation
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential} and, after summation,
the overall solid--fluid momentum balance.
%
Substituting the total reversible stress definition
\eqref{eq:vp_total_reversible_stress_definition}, the Maxwell stress
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}, and combining
\(-\omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\) with the isotropic Maxwell contribution
absorbs the isotropic electric-enthalpy force into the phase-pressure
gradient.  The general fluid-phase momentum balance therefore uses the actual
phase-pressure gradient,
%
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf a_f
	=
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\bs\sigma_f'
	+\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
	\right)
	-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
	\bar p_f-\omega_f^+
	\right)
	+\rho_f\mbf g+\mbf b_f
	+
	\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_f^\alpha
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f
	\right),
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary}
\end{empheq}
%
The boundary coefficients and the prescribed traction work in
\eqref{eq:W_def} give the fluid-phase natural traction.  Because the phase
pressure enters only through the volume gradient, the traction is the normal
projection of the fluid stress divergence alone,
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\bs\sigma_f'
	+\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
	\right)\mbf n
	=
	\mbf t_f
	\qquad
	\text{on }\Gamma_{\mbf t_f}.
	\label{eq:fluid_natural_traction}
\end{equation}
%
The complementary prescribed-motion condition is
\(\delta\mbf x_f=\mbf 0\) on \(\Gamma_{\mbf x_f}\).
The electrical force in the general balance is carried by the anisotropic
Maxwell term \(\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot(\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f)\), while the
isotropic electric-enthalpy contribution is absorbed into the pressure
reconstruction.  The dynamic capillary lag, closed later by Onsager
stationarity, does not appear as a body force in this individual balance; it
enters through the phase-pressure difference
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential} and, through the
saturation-weighted combination, in the summed solid--fluid momentum balance.
Related Maxwell forces appear in leaky-dielectric and electrowetting theories
\cite{melcher1969electrohydrodynamics,yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation}.

When the generalized saturation-force differences vanish, the zero-lag
reversible limit, the pressure relation
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation_b} gives
\(\bar p_f-\omega_f^+=\bar p_E+\gamma_f\), and
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary} reduces to the equivalent-pore-pressure
form
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	=
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\bs\sigma_f'
	+\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
	\right)
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}
	\left(
	\bar p_E+\gamma_f
	\right)
	+ \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
	+\sum_\alpha\dot{c}_f^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right),
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_simplified}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

Under the neutral, conversion-free specialization
\(\omega_f^+=0\) and \(\dot{c}_f^\alpha=0\),
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_dynamic_capillary} reduces to the standard
pressure-gradient form,
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	= \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f'
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f
	+ \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f,
	\qquad
	f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:neutral_conversion_free_fluid_momentum_balance}
\end{equation}
%

\subsubsection{Electrostatic field equation}
\label{sec:electrostatic_field_equation}

The electrostatic-potential variation \(\delta\varphi\) gives Gauss' law,
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d=\varrho .
	\label{eq:gauss_law}
\end{equation}
%
The corresponding natural electrical boundary condition is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf d\cdot\mbf n
	=
	\bar q
	\quad
	\text{on }\Gamma_{\bar q} .
	\label{eq:electrostatic_boundary_conditions}
\end{equation}
%
The complementary essential condition prescribes \(\varphi\) on
\(\Gamma_\varphi\), so \(\delta\varphi=0\) there.

Combined with the local charge balance from \sref{conservation_of_charge}, the
electroquasistatic field equation implies the following equivalent compatibility
forms.
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_compatibility}
	\begin{equation}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\frac{\partial\mbf d}{\partial t}
		+
		\mbf i
		\right)
		= 0.
		\label{eq:charge_gauss_eulerian_compatibility}
	\end{equation}
\end{subequations}

Equation~\eqref{eq:charge_gauss_eulerian_compatibility} uses the Eulerian
partial derivative obtained by differentiating Gauss' law at fixed
\(\mbf x\).  It is a compatibility relation, not an additional
Euler--Lagrange equation.  Its signs follow from
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d=\varrho\) and
\eqref{eq:mixture_charge_balance}; every term inside the spatial divergence has
units of current per area.
The neutral specialization additionally sets \(\omega_\xi^+=0\) and
\(z_\xi^\alpha=0\), where \(z_\xi^\alpha\) is the charge per unit component
mass.

\subsection{Onsager principle for dissipative saturation, reaction, and transport}
\label{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}

The Hamilton--d'Alembert variation determines the coordinate equations at fixed
endpoint variations and holds any constitutive d'Alembert-force coefficient
fixed.  Saturation redistribution, reactions, and relative component transport
are closed separately by an Onsager principle: at a fixed current
thermodynamic state, the process rates for the immediate response are chosen by
stationarity of an entropy-rate functional containing a dissipation potential
and the linear thermodynamic rate~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  This
statement is local in time and selects near-equilibrium rates.  The entropy-rate
form displayed here remains valid when
\(\theta_{\mc F}\ne\theta_{\mc S}\).  Its coefficients are retained in the
specific generalized transfer-work form delivered by the reversible variation; their
chemical-potential interpretation is derived later by the Coleman--Noll
procedure.  The state variables that enter the coefficients are held fixed
during this minimization.  For each active phase,
select one active reference component \(N\).  The independent process rates are
the \(|\mc F|-1\) saturation rates and the external progress rates \(r_{(m)}\),
\(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\), and
\(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\) for \(\alpha\ne N\).  The reference-component
fluxes are reconstructed separately for dispersion and diffusion by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf j_{\xi,k}^N
	=
	-\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha,
	\qquad
	k\in\{{\rm disp},{\rm diff}\}.
	\label{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
%
Thus every admissible flux variation satisfies the same zero-sum condition.
The heavy dot \(\phasedot{\square}=D_{\mc S}\square/Dt\) denotes the material
time derivative following the solid skeleton.  It is used for saturation rates
because the saturation field is defined on the current configuration of the
porous medium, whose material frame is the solid skeleton; the
solid-skeleton-following rate preserves the saturation-sum constraint
\(\sum_f S_f=1\) under differentiation and avoids the relative-velocity
advection that a phase-following rate would introduce into the intrinsic
saturation dynamics.
Choose one active reference fluid phase \(f_\star\) and write
\(\phasedot S_{f_\star}=-\sum_{f\ne f_\star}\phasedot S_f\).  The remaining saturation
rates are independent.
The held-fixed current configuration and constitutive state include
\(\mbf F_\xi\), \(\bar\rho_\xi\), \(\eta_\xi^\alpha\), \(\phi_\xi\),
\(\theta_{\mc F}\), \(\theta_{\mc S}\), \(\varphi\), \(J_\xi\), and any internal
variables entering the potentials or mobilities.

The phase free energy \(\psi_\xi\), specific generalized transfer-work potentials
\(L_\xi^\alpha\), generalized saturation forces \(\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\), and
electric potential \(\varphi\) enter as fixed state
coefficients during rate stationarity.  The
absolute conversion coefficient is
\(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\).  Relative transport depends only
on the within-phase neutral difference
\(L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N\) and the separate electric-field contribution.
Coleman--Noll will show that the neutral difference equals the corresponding
chemical-potential difference and will then introduce the electrochemical
potential.  Keeping the neutral and electric parts separate also preserves
invariance under the additive gauge of \(\varphi\) at nonuniform temperature.

The dependent accumulation rate
\(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\) is eliminated before stationarity using
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate} and
\eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}.  For \(\xi\in\mc G\), the
resulting neutral thermodynamic rate is combined with the direct work of the
electric field on the relative charge flux.  The zero-sum reconstruction gives
\(\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha
=\sum_{\alpha\ne N}(z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N)\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha\), and the
component balance and \(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\) give the
gauge-invariant identity
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\sum_\alpha
		\frac{
			\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		-
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\cdot\mbf E
		 & ={}
		\sum_m
		\left(
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right)r_{(m)}
		\\
		 & -
		\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
		\\
		 & +
		\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right).
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:component_thermodynamic_rate_reduction}
\end{equation}
%
The coefficient of each reaction rate in
\eqref{eq:component_thermodynamic_rate_reduction} is the neutral transfer
coefficient \((\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha)/\theta_{\mc G}\), which contains only
the phase free energy and the specific generalized transfer-work potential.  Within a phase,
transport is driven by the potential differences \(L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N\),
while the electric field enters separately through the direct electrical work.
For a mechanism that conserves charge separately within each thermal
subsystem, its stoichiometric sum of neutral coefficients may instead be
written with the electrochemical potentials introduced later, because the
added electric terms cancel identically under that mechanism's charge
conservation.  A mechanism that transfers net charge between the fluid and
solid subsystems must satisfy the reaction-energy admissibility condition,
evaluated mechanism by mechanism with the same neutral reaction coefficient,
and its reaction rate and reaction-energy transfer may be Onsager-coupled.
Both sides of \eqref{eq:component_thermodynamic_rate_reduction} are gauge
invariant: the electrical transport term depends on the electric field
\(\mbf E\), so adding a constant to \(\varphi\) leaves every term unchanged.

After the dependent accumulation rate has been eliminated, the Onsager
entropy-rate functional contains the saturation, reaction, and
relative-transport dissipation potentials minus their entropy production.  The
reaction and transport terms use the temperature-weighted thermodynamic rate in
\eqref{eq:component_thermodynamic_rate_reduction}:
%
\begin{align}
	\mc R
	={} &
	\Phi_{\mc{R}}
	-
	\int_{\mc B}
	\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}\mc L_f^{\rm sat}\phasedot S_f\dv
	\notag    \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_m
	\left[
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag    \\
	    & -
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\frac{L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\dv,
	\notag    \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\frac{
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\cdot
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\dv,
	\notag    \\
	    & +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\int_{\Gamma_{\bar{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha}}
	\overline{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot\mbf n\,{\rm d}a,
	\notag    \\
	\delta\mc R
	={} & 0 .
	\label{eq:onsager_stationarity}
\end{align}
%
The added surface term is the work of a prescribed external component
reservoir on \(\Gamma_{\bar{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha}\).  Here
\(\overline{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha\) is the prescribed temperature-weighted neutral
potential difference; the electrical boundary datum is provided independently
by the electric-field problem.  The reservoir term
acts on the relative component flux only; the phase-advective flux is held
fixed during the Onsager rate variation.

The near-equilibrium quadratic dissipation potential is
%
\begin{align}
	\Phi_{\mc{R}}
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\sum_{f\ne f_\star}\sum_{g\ne f_\star}
	\mc T_{fg}\phasedot S_f\phasedot S_g\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_m\frac{r_{(m)}^2}{K_{(m)}}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta\dv.
	\label{eq:onsager_quadratic_reaction_transport_potential}
\end{align}
%
Here \(K_{(m)}\) is the mobility conjugate to the temperature-weighted reaction
force, and
\(\mbf D_{\xi,k}^{\alpha\beta}\), with
\(\alpha,\beta\ne N\), are the reduced mobility tensors in the independent
relative-flux coordinates; their inverses are taken in the same
\(N-1\)-dimensional component space.  The reference flux is then reconstructed
by \eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}.  The resistance
coefficients \(\mc T_{fg}\) govern dynamic capillary redistribution; they have
units of pressure times time and are symmetric
(\(\mc T_{fg}=\mc T_{gf}\)).  Their admissibility is verified against the
entropy inequality in Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}.
The adopted quadratic
potential is block diagonal in saturation, reaction, dispersion, and diffusion rates.  For
a charged mechanism that transfers net charge between the thermal subsystems,
the neutral reaction force remains unchanged; the reaction and
interphase-energy rates may instead be included in one gauge-invariant Onsager
block satisfying the mechanismwise dissipation condition derived later.  A
block-diagonal choice is also admissible when the reaction-energy term is
closed separately.
The transport boundary data enter the stationarity through the normal
relative-flux variation.  On \(\Gamma_{m,\xi}^{\alpha}\), the normal total
component flux is prescribed, so the relative-flux variation is tangential,
\((\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha+
\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha)\cdot\mbf n=0\).  On the complementary
\(\Gamma_{\bar{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha}\), the temperature-weighted component-potential
difference is prescribed and the normal relative-flux variation is free; its
boundary contribution is retained in the collected condition below.  For each
independent component, these complementary portions prescribe either the
normal total component flux in
\eqref{eq:component_normal_flux_boundary_data} or the temperature-weighted
component-potential difference \((L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N)/\theta_{\mc G}\), with the
electric boundary datum supplied by the electrostatic boundary-value problem.
Reference-component relative fluxes are reconstructed from
\eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}, so prescribed relative-flux
data must satisfy the same zero-sum restriction.

\subsubsection{Collected Onsager variation}
\label{sec:collected_onsager_variation}

Collecting the variation of \(\mc R\) over the saturation, reaction, and
relative-transport coordinates gives the rate-dependent stationarity
condition
%
\begin{align}
	0 = \delta\mc R
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\frac{\phi}{\theta_{\mc F}}
	\sum_{f\ne f_\star}
	\left[
		\sum_{g\ne f_\star}\mc T_{fg}\phasedot S_g
		-\left(
		\mc L_f^{\rm sat}-\mc L_{f_\star}^{\rm sat}
		\right)
	\right]\delta\phasedot S_f\dv
	\notag \displaybreak[1] \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\left[
		\frac{r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}
		+
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]\delta r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag \displaybreak[1] \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\notag                  \\
	    & \qquad
	\left[
		\sum_{\beta\ne N}
		\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
		+
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
		\frac{
			L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\dv
	\notag                  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\notag                  \\
	    & \qquad
	\left[
		\sum_{\beta\ne N}
		\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
		+
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
		\frac{
			L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\dv
	\notag                  \\
	    & +
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\int_{\Gamma_{\bar{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha}}
	\left[
		\overline{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha
		-
		\frac{L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right]
	\left(
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot\mbf n\,{\rm d}a .
	\label{eq:collected_onsager_variation}
\end{align}
%
Stationarity with arbitrary normal relative-flux variation on
\(\Gamma_{\bar{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha}\) therefore recovers the prescribed
temperature-weighted component-potential difference,
\(\overline{\mc M}_\xi^\alpha=(L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N)/\theta_{\mc G}\).
The component-rate balance is the conservation law
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate}, which eliminated the
dependent accumulation rate before \eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity}; the
stationarity condition therefore contains only the independent mechanism-rate
and flux variations.  Setting each independent variation to zero and inverting
the resistances gives the near-equilibrium rate laws.  The transport power is
unchanged by a common shift of all \(L_\xi^\alpha\) in a phase or by an additive
electric gauge.  Coleman--Noll will identify the associated chemical potentials
and reaction affinity and will show how the transfer-work contribution enters
it.  Away from local thermal equilibrium, the temperature-weighted sum, with
each thermal subsystem weighted by its own temperature, drives the reaction
rate:
%
\begin{align}
	     r_{(m)}
	 & =
	-K_{(m)}
	\left[
		\left(
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\beta
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\beta
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\beta
		\right)
		\right],
	\label{eq:onsager_reaction_rate_variational}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
		\frac{
			L_\xi^\beta-L_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
		z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:onsager_dispersion_mobility_balance}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
		\frac{
			L_\xi^\beta-L_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
		z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N.
	\label{eq:onsager_diffusion_mobility_balance}
\end{align}
%
The two reference-component fluxes follow from
\eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}.
The entropy inequality in Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}
then checks that these Onsager force--rate laws are dissipative and records the
positivity assumptions that make the closures admissible.

\subsubsection{Saturation-redistribution Onsager variation}
\label{sec:saturation_redistribution_onsager_variation}

The \(|\mc F|-1\) independent saturation rates are varied at fixed current
state.  Their contribution is the
first line of the global stationarity statement
\eqref{eq:collected_onsager_variation}.  Stationarity gives the force--rate
relation
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_onsager_relation}
	\begin{align}
		&\mc L_f^{\rm sat}-\mc L_{f_\star}^{\rm sat}
		=
		\sum_{g\ne f_\star}\mc T_{fg}\phasedot S_g,
		\qquad
		f\ne f_\star.
		\label{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_force_rate_relation}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}
%
The resistance entries \(\mc T_{fg}\) have units of pressure times time.  They
may depend on saturation, capillary history, temperature, deformation, and
other current pore-structure variables.  Their state dependence is held fixed
during the Onsager rate variation.  The force on the left of
\eqref{eq:MC_dynamic_capillary_force_rate_relation} is the difference of the
coefficients used in the d'Alembert work
\eqref{eq:W_def}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential} identifies it with the
corresponding pressure-force difference.  The entropy inequality in
Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality} determines the
admissibility conditions on the resistance and verifies that the adopted
resistance renders saturation redistribution dissipative.
