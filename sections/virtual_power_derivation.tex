\section{Variational principle derivation}
\label{sec:variational}
\label{sec:virtual_power_derivation}

We derive the governing equations with a Hamilton--d'Alembert--Onsager
stationarity structure.  The Hamilton--d'Alembert part follows the extended
Hamilton principles used by Bedford and Drumheller for porous and reacting
immiscible mixtures
~\cite{bedford1979variational,drumheller1980thermomechanical,bedford1983theories}
and determines the mixture coordinates and constraints.  The Onsager part
determines the reaction and relative-transport rates by pairing process rates
with thermodynamic forces and then imposing the dissipation inequality
~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  This organization is the continuum
analogue of combining virtual work for coordinate variations with virtual power
for process rates~\cite{germain1973virtualpower,gurtin2010continua}.  It
preserves the Bedford--Drumheller constrained-mixture structure while keeping the
force--rate pairings explicit.

The formulation is written for a compositional mixture containing arbitrary sets
of solid $\mc S$ and fluid $\mc F$ phases.  Here fluid means a nonsolid pore
phase, such as an aqueous, oleic, or gas phase.  The symbol $s \in \mc{S}$
denotes a solid phase, $f \in\mc F$ denotes a fluid phase, and
$\xi \in \mc{S} \cup \mc{F}$ ranges over all phases.  Components within each
phase are indexed by $\alpha$.

\subsection{Hamilton--d'Alembert principle for reversible coordinates}

Let \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) be the endpoint times and let \(\delta\) denote an
admissible virtual variation that vanishes at those endpoints.  The
Hamilton--d'Alembert statement contains the kinetic and stored-energy variation,
ordinary virtual work, the volume and composition constraints, and the material
insertion constraint:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(T-U\right)\dt
	+
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
	\left(\delta W+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\delta C_k\right)\dt
	= 0.
	\label{eq:vp_principle}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The interphase interaction force may be dissipative at the phase level, but it
is internal to the mixture and globally conservative:
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\mbf b_\xi=\mbf 0.
	\label{eq:interphase_force_global_balance}
\end{equation}
%
The energies, virtual work, and four constraints are defined together by
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
\small
\begin{align}
	T        & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}}\frac{1}{2} \rho_\xi \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \dv,
	\label{eq:T_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	U        & = \sum_{\xi}\int_{\mc{B}} \rho_{\xi}\,\psi_{\xi}(\mbf{F}_{\xi}, \bar\rho_{\xi}, \{\eta^\al_{\xi}\},\theta)\dv
	+ \int_{\mc{B}} \phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta\right) \dv
	+\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta)
	\,\dv,
	\label{eq:U_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	\delta W & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left(\rho_\xi\mbf{g}+\mbf b_\xi\right)\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi \,\dv-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}L_\xi^\alpha\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\,\dv,
	\label{eq:W_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_1      & = \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \,\dv,
	\label{eq:C1_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_2      & = \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi 0}} \pi_\xi^\al \left( J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi 0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} \bar\rho_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \eta^\al_\xi \bar\rho_\xi} \right) \dV_\xi,
	\label{eq:C2_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_3      & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \left( \sum_\alpha \eta_\xi^\alpha - 1 \right) \dv,
	\label{eq:C3_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_4      & = \int_{\mc B}\tau
	\left(
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	\right)\dv .
	\label{eq:C4_def}
\end{align}
\endgroup
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here \(\mbf g\) is the body force per unit phase mass, \(\mbf b_\xi\) is the
interphase interaction force, \(L_\xi^\alpha\) is the component generalized
force conjugate to conversion insertion, and \(\theta\) is the absolute
temperature.  Constraint \(C_1\) enforces the volume-fraction constraint,
\(C_2\) enforces the augmented component material measure, and \(C_3\) enforces
mass-fraction normalization.

Constraint \(C_4\) enforces zero net material insertion over all phases and
components.  Writing the rate of component mass addition per current mixture
volume as
\(\dot c_\xi^\alpha=\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\), this constraint imposes
\(\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha=0\).  Internal conversion satisfies the
constraint through stoichiometric mass conservation, while relative component
transport satisfies it phase by phase through
\eqref{eq:averaged_relative_flux_sum}.  The component-resolved rate balance is
imposed later in the Onsager subsection.

The transfer multiplier \(\tau\) is the Bedford--Drumheller kinematic potential
conjugate to the globally conservative accumulated material insertion.  It has
units of action per unit mass, so
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\) has velocity units and
\(D_\xi\tau/Dt\) has units of energy per unit mass.  Thus \(\tau\) supplies the
conversion transfer-momentum coefficient in the reversible variation.

The variations in this section are mechanical and compositional variations at
fixed temperature.  Temperature is retained as a constitutive state variable and
is varied in the Coleman--Noll entropy argument below.
The fields $\mbf x_\xi$, $\eta_\xi^\alpha$, $\phi_\xi$, $\bar\rho_\xi$, and
$\mc C_\xi^\alpha$ are varied independently.  The mechanism rates and the two
relative fluxes are varied later as independent process rates through the
constrained Onsager power functional in
Section~\ref{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}.

\subsubsection{Extending Bedford and Drumheller transport identity}
\label{sec:extended_bd_identity}

Bedford and Drumheller~\cite[eq.~(1.28)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
give an identity for the variation of a constituent-attached spatial field
$\Gamma_\xi$ weighted by the current phase density $\rho_\xi$.  The same
transport argument extends componentwise to the compositional setting used here.

The component material increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) is attached to the
phase-\(\xi\) reference coordinates.  It is the added component mass appearing
in \eqref{eq:augmented_component_material_measure}.  In the reversible
Hamilton--d'Alembert variation, its componentwise virtual increment is therefore
carried with the phase-attached material measure.
%
For any phase field $\Gamma_\xi$, the Bedford--Drumheller identity extends componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho^\alpha_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \delta \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi0}+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\right)
	\Gamma_\xi \,\dVxi \notag        \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left[
		\delta\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\Gamma_\xi
		+ J_\xi\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	\right]\dVxi \notag              \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}}
	\left(
	\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	+ \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\Gamma_\xi
	\right)\dv .
	\label{eq:bd128_mod2}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Summing both sides of \eqref{eq:bd128_mod2} over $\alpha$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \left[\rho_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi + \sum_\alpha \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \Gamma_\xi  \right]  \dv
	\label{eq:bd128_mod}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where $\rho_\xi = \sum_\alpha \rho^\alpha_\xi$.

\subsubsection{Variation of the kinetic energy}
\label{sec:var_T_app}
% ============================================================

The variation of kinetic energy using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod} is then
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta T = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi\,\mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{v}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \dv.
	\label{eq:kinetic_energy_variation_before_time_integration}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Since $\delta\mbf{v}_\xi = \mathrm{d}(\delta\mbf{x}_\xi)/\mathrm{d}t$ and $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_1} = \delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_2} = \mbf{0}$, integration by parts in time gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \delta T \dt
	= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left[-\rho_\xi\,\mbf{a}_\xi - \sum_\al \dot c_\xi^\alpha \mbf{v}_\xi \right] \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}  \dv \dt
	\label{eq:deltaT_app}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
\subsubsection{Variation of the phase free energies}
\label{sec:var_Uxi}
% ============================================================

For every phase, solid or fluid, let $\psi_\xi$ denote the nonelectrical
specific Helmholtz free energy and let \(N\) be the number of tracked components
in that phase.  Its contribution to the stored thermodynamic potential is
written
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\xi
	=
	\sum_\xi \int_{\mc B}
	\rho_\xi
	\psi_\xi(\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\})\,\dv
	\label{eq:U_xi_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Here \(\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}\) denotes the indexed collection of all component
mass fractions for phase \(\xi\).
The constitutive dependence on $\mbf F_\xi$ may be trivial for fluid phases,
but keeping it in the notation allows one calculation for all phases.  The local
free-energy variation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta\psi_\xi
	 & = \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}:\delta\mbf F_\xi
	+ \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
	+ \sum_\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:delta_psi_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod}, integrating the $\delta\mbf F_\xi$ term by parts,
and collecting independent variations gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\xi
	 & = \sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}\Bigg[
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma'_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
	+ \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}\delta\bar\rho_\xi \notag \\
	 & \qquad
		+ \sum_\alpha \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha}}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+ \sum_\alpha \psi_\xi\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\Bigg]\dv + \text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:deltaU_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The corresponding Cauchy effective stress is denoted with a single prime because
it is the fixed-intrinsic-density stress, analogous to a Terzaghi effective
stress.  The fixed-pressure Biot stress is denoted later with a double prime.
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\bs\sigma'_\xi
	\equiv \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}\mbf F_\xi^T .
	\label{eq:var_cauchy_def}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For fluid phases, \(\psi_\xi\) is independent of \(\mbf F_\xi\), giving
\(\bs\sigma_\xi'=\mbf 0\).

\subsubsection{Variation of the interfacial energies}
\label{sec:var_gamma}
% ============================================================

Let $\mc I$ denote the set of oriented fluid interfaces.  Each
$(w,n)\in\mc I$ identifies a wetting phase $w$ and a non-wetting phase $n$.
The total fluid volume fraction and the fluid saturations are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi = \sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f,
	\qquad
	S_f = \frac{\phi_f}{\phi},
	\qquad
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f=1 .
	\label{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The saturations are used here only as ratios of the primitive volume-fraction
fields; the variations are still taken with respect to the primitive
$\phi_f$.  The notation $\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}$ denotes the fluid
saturation state subject to the constraint in
\eqref{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}.  Thus the energy may be written in
terms of any independent set of fluid saturations, with the remaining
saturation determined by the constraint.  The derivation below keeps this
simplex constraint explicit, so all fluid phases are treated symmetrically.  The
effective interfacial Helmholtz free-energy density, normalized per current
fluid volume, is a single constitutive potential of this constrained saturation
state
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma =
	\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right).
	\label{eq:interfacial_energy_density}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
and its contribution to the stored thermodynamic potential per current mixture
volume is \(\phi\gamma\), so
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\gamma =
	\int_{\mc{B}}
	\phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right)
	\dv .
	\label{eq:interfacial_internal_energy}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

The temperature argument shown in the action is held fixed during the present
mechanical variation and is suppressed in
\eqref{eq:interfacial_energy_density}--\eqref{eq:interfacial_internal_energy}.
The adopted interfacial potential
\(\gamma=\gamma(\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta)\) makes capillary mechanics enter
through the primitive fluid volume fractions and their saturation ratios.

The Bedford--Drumheller spatial identity
(cf.\ \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical} equation (1.25)) gives, for each
fluid phase field $\phi_f$,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\delta\phi_\xi\right\vert_{\mbf{x}}
	= \delta\phi_\xi-\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi.
	\label{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\phi=\sum_{f\in\mc F}\delta\phi_f,
	\qquad
	\delta S_f=\frac{\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi}{\phi},
	\label{eq:saturation_variations}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
the fixed-point contribution and the transport correction from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation} are collected in one
calculation:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\gamma
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\left[
		\gamma\,\delta\phi
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
		\left(\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi\right)
	\right]\dv \notag                                          \\
	 & \quad
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv \notag \\
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\delta\phi_f\dv
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv .
	\label{eq:deltaU_fg}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The subscript $g$ denotes the set of all fluid phases excluding the varied phase
$f$; in this variation, the \(S_g\ne S_f\) are held fixed.
The phase interfacial potential appearing in \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma
	+\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
	-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g
	\left.\p{\gamma}{S_g}\right\vert_{S_{h\neq g}},
	\label{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where the saturation derivatives are evaluated on the saturation simplex and the same ``held fixed'' notation convention applies.  The
oriented wetting/non-wetting pairs specify capillary-pressure differences.  The wetting/non-wetting
interfacial difference associated with an oriented pair is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	p_{c,nw}\equiv\gamma_n-\gamma_w,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:pc_def}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The form \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} keeps the gradient terms generated by the scalar
transport of the phase-attached volume fractions.  These terms combine with the
volume-fraction and component-storage multiplier gradients in the phase momentum
balances after the volume-fraction Euler--Lagrange equations are used.

\subsubsection{Electric field}
\label{sec:electric_field_variation}

The electrical terms are conservative except for any constitutive dielectric
relaxation introduced later.  Let \(\varphi\) denote the electrostatic potential
and let \(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\) be the electric field.  The
electrical contribution enters the Hamilton action through the phase-attributed
potential \(U_\xi^+\).  The phase electric-enthalpy density
\(\omega_\xi^+=\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,
\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta)\) is normalized per current phase volume;
\(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\) is therefore its contribution per current mixture
	volume.  Likewise, \(\gamma\) is normalized per current fluid volume and
	\(\phi\gamma\) is the interfacial Helmholtz contribution per current mixture
	volume.  The notation \(\psi_\xi\) denotes the specific phase Helmholtz free
	energy, \(\gamma\) the effective interfacial Helmholtz density, and
	\(\omega_\xi^+\) the phase electric-enthalpy density.  The adopted electric
	enthalpy depends on \(\mbf E\), intrinsic density, composition, and
	temperature.  The
concrete phase state sets
\(\mc X_\xi\) are used in held-fixed derivative annotations and are specified
in \sref{constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}.  Charge enters below through
the combined component-rate constraint
\eqref{eq:C45_combined_def}.  For each phase,
%
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+
	\,\dv
	=
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+
	\,\dV_\xi .
	\label{eq:phase_electric_enthalpy_pullback}
\end{equation}
%
In the electroquasistatic specialization, the corresponding electric
displacement fields are
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi,
	\qquad
	\mbf d_\xi
	=
	-\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{\xi}}{\mbf E}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:electric_field_definitions}
\end{equation}
%
This split keeps the Helmholtz energy \(\psi_\xi\) nonelectrical, takes
\(\mbf E\) as the independent electrical variable, and derives
\(\mbf d_\xi\) from \(\omega_\xi^+\), consistently with electroelastic
potential formulations~\cite{dorfmann2017nonlinear_electroelasticity}.  The
concrete phase state sets and even-in-\(\mbf E\) reservoir specialization are
specified in \sref{constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}.

At fixed temperature, the full variation contains a direct volume-fraction
term, the density and composition variations, the potential-gradient term, and
the geometric mechanical pullback.  With \(\otimes\) denoting the tensor outer
product, these contributions give the following current-space form.  The
volume-fraction variation is the phase-attached variation from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}, left here as
\(\delta\phi_\xi\) so its direct coefficient remains visible.  In derivatives
with respect to a member of \(\mc X_\xi\), the notation
\(\left.\partial(\cdot)/\partial z\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}\) means that
all other members of \(\mc X_\xi\) and the electric field are held fixed.  The
resulting variation is
%
\begin{align}
	\delta U^{+}_{\xi}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left[
		\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi
		+
		\phi_\xi
		\left.
		\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_\xi}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
		\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left[
		\phi_\xi
		\sum_\alpha
		\left.
		\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
		\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+
		\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\varphi
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^+:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv .
	\label{eq:vp_electric_full_variation}
\end{align}
%
After integrating the potential-gradient and phase-map-gradient terms by parts
and including the free-charge potential term with the sign appropriate to
prescribed free charge,
\eqref{eq:vp_electric_full_variation} gives the electrical contribution to the
action,
%
\begin{align}
	-\delta\sum_\xi U^{+}_{\xi}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d-\varrho
	\right)\delta\varphi\,\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi\,\dv
	-
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\left.
	\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_\xi}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
	\delta\bar\rho_\xi\,\dv
	-
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\left.
	\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
	\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha\,\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv
	+
	\text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_mechanical_power}
\end{align}
%
The mechanical coefficient in \eqref{eq:vp_electric_full_variation} defines the
reversible electrical stress assigned to the phase pullback.  Variation of the
current volume element and convection of the spatial electric field produce the
phase-attributed Maxwell stress, consistently with
electric-enthalpy virtual-work derivations
\cite{mcmeeking2007virtual_work_electrostatic}.  We denote this Maxwell stress
by \(\bs\sigma_\xi^+\),
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\phi_\xi
	\left(
	\omega_\xi^+\mbf I
	+
	\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi
	\right).
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the total reversible stress used in the phase momentum equation is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
	\equiv
	\bs\sigma_\xi'
	+
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{+}.
	\label{eq:vp_total_reversible_stress_definition}
\end{equation}

% ============================================================
\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_1$}{C1}: volume-fraction}
\label{sec:var_C1_app}
% ============================================================

The constraint $C_1$ is
imposed in the current configuration, so the natural variation is
taken at fixed spatial point $\mbf x$.  Since each $\phi_\xi$ is a
constituent-attached spatial field, \eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
expresses the variation in terms of the spatial variation $\delta\phi_\xi$ and
the motion variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_1
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\lambda \left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\delta\phi_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv.
	\label{eq:deltaC1_app}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The first term returns the volume-fraction constraint
$\sum_\xi\phi_\xi = 1$.  The remaining terms contribute $\lambda$ to each
$\delta\phi_\xi$ equation and $-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi$ to each
momentum variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$.

% ============================================================
\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_2$}{C2}: component material mass balance}
\label{sec:var_C2_app}
% ============================================================

The component material mass balance constraint is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	C_2 = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha} \right) \,dV_\xi.
	\label{eq:C2_component_measure}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The multiplier \(\pi_\xi^\alpha\) enforces the component material-mass balance
for component \(\alpha\) in phase \(\xi\).  Its variation gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_2
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha \delta J_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi^2\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta\phi_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi^2 \eta^\al_\xi}\,\delta\bar\rho_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi (\eta^\al_\xi)^2}\,\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	- \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}
	\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi.
	\notag                                        \\
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi \, \dv
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}}
	\left[
		\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}\delta\phi_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		- \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv
	+ \text{b.t.}
	\label{eq:deltaC2_lines}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here the second equality uses $\dv = J_\xi {\rm d}V_\xi$ and
$\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha
	=J_\xi\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha$. ``b.t.'' denotes boundary terms generated by integration by parts that are omitted here for brevity.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_3$}{C3}: mass partition within phase}
\label{sec:var_C3_app}
% ============================================================

%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_3
	 & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\Pi_\xi \left(\sum_\al \eta_\xi^\al - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \left(\delta\eta^\al_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\eta_\xi^\al\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv, \notag \\
	 & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\Pi_\xi \left(\sum_\al \eta_\xi^\al - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \delta\eta^\al_\xi \dv.
	\label{eq:deltaC3_app}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The last term in the first line vanishes by recognizing $\sum_\alpha \nabla_{\mbf{x}} \eta_\xi^\alpha = 0$ by \eqref{eq:mass_fraction_constraint}.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_4$}{C4}: material insertion}
\label{sec:component_rate_constraint_variations}
\label{sec:stoich_conversion_variations}
\label{sec:transport_accumulation_variations}

The Bedford--Drumheller transfer constraint contains the component material
increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) carried by phase \(\xi\).  Its rate variation must
be integrated by parts with the same phase material derivative before any
stationarity condition is used.  A single transfer potential \(\tau\) enforces
the total insertion-rate constraint.  Varying
\(\tau\), the material measure, and the phase map gives
%
\begin{align}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\delta C_4\,\dt
	={} &
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}
	\delta\tau
	\left[
		\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv\dt
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		-\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		+
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
		\right]\dv\dt
	+
	\text{b.t.}
	\label{eq:C4_variation}
\end{align}
%
Varying \(\tau\) in \eqref{eq:C4_variation} enforces the zero-sum insertion
rate.  Varying the material measure gives the coefficient \(D_\xi\tau/Dt\), and
varying the phase map contributes the momentum coefficient
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\).

Before collecting the stationarity conditions, recall that the rate of
component mass addition per current mixture volume is
\(\dot c_\xi^\alpha=\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\), which will be used in the
collected equations below.  Because \(\tau\) enforces the summed insertion rate,
the phase-map variation produces a momentum term proportional to
\(\dot c_\xi^\alpha\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\).

\subsubsection{Collected Hamilton--d'Alembert variation}
\label{sec:collected_var}
% ============================================================

The Hamilton--d'Alembert terms are now collected before any stationarity
equation is substituted back into another variation.  This collection contains
the reversible coordinate and constraint coefficients, including those from
\(\delta C_4\):
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
\small
\begin{align}
	0 ={} & \int_{t_1}^{t_2}
	\begin{aligned}[t]
		 & \left\{\vphantom{\sum_{\xi}}\right.
		\sum_{\xi} \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
			-\rho_{\xi}\mbf{a}_{\xi}
			+ \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_{\xi}^{\prime+} + \rho_{\xi}\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_{\xi}
			-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi
			-\sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\pi_\xi^\alpha
			+\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha
			\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_\xi\right)
		\Bigg]\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_{\xi} \,\dv            \\
		 & + \sum_{f\in\mc F}\int_{\mc B}
		\Bigg[
			\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
		\Bigg]\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\,\dv                  \\
		 & + \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
			\lambda + \sum_\alpha \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\phi_{\xi}}
			-
			\omega_\xi^+
		\Bigg] \delta\phi_{\xi} \,\dv                   \\
		 & + \sum_{f\in\mc F}\int_{\mc B}
		\Bigg[
			-\gamma_f
		\Bigg]\delta\phi_f\,\dv                         \\
		 & + \sum_{\xi}\int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
			-\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf{F}_{\xi},\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}
			-\phi_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			+ \sum_\alpha\frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}
		\Bigg] \delta\bar\rho_{\xi} \,\dv               \\
		 & + \sum_{\xi}\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
		\Bigg[
			\frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}+\Pi_{\xi}
			-\rho_{\xi}
			\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta^\alpha_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta^{\beta\neq\alpha}_\xi}
			-\phi_{\xi}
			\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta^\alpha_\xi}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
			\Bigg]
		\delta\eta^\alpha_\xi\,\dv                      \\
		 & + \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}\Bigg[
			\tfrac12\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi-\psi_\xi
			-L_\xi^\alpha
			-\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
			-\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\Bigg]\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\,\dv \\
		 & + \int_{\mc B}
		\Bigg[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d-\varrho
		\Bigg]\delta\varphi\,\dv
		\left.\vphantom{\sum_{\xi}}\right\}\dt
	\end{aligned}
	\notag                                 \\
	      & + \text{multiplier variations}
	+ \text{boundary terms}.  \label{eq:collected_full}
\end{align}
\endgroup
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The explicit \(\delta\varphi\) term in \eqref{eq:collected_full} is the
Hamilton electric-field coefficient.  The multiplier variations in
\eqref{eq:collected_full} include the variations that return
\eqref{eq:C1_def}--\eqref{eq:C3_def}; variation with respect to \(\tau\)
returns the zero-sum insertion rate imposed by \eqref{eq:C4_def}.  Since the
displayed Hamilton variations are arbitrary within their respective variation
classes, each bracketed integrand must vanish pointwise.

\subsubsection{Euler--Lagrange equations}

The Euler--Lagrange equation generated by arbitrary \(\delta\varphi\) is
Gauss' law,
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d=\varrho .
	\label{eq:gauss_law}
\end{equation}
%
Combined with the local charge balance from \sref{conservation_of_charge}, the
electroquasistatic field equation implies
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\dot{\mbf d}
	+
	\mbf i
	\right)
	=0 .
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_compatibility}
\end{equation}

The neutral specialization is defined by
\(\omega_\xi^+=0\) and \(z_\xi^\alpha=0\), where \(z_\xi^\alpha\) is the
charge per unit component mass.

The Euler--Lagrange equations generated by arbitrary $\delta\phi_\xi$ give,
for phases without an explicit capillary contribution,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi_\xi \lambda
	=
	-\sum_\alpha \pi_\xi^\al
	+
	\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+ .
	\label{eq:el_lambda}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For the fluid phases the corresponding equations contain the primitive capillary potentials discussed below.

Substituting \eqref{eq:el_lambda} into the Euler--Lagrange equations associated with arbitrary $\delta \mbf{x}_\xi$ and simplifying gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\mbf{a}_{\xi} =
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_{\xi}^{\prime+} + \rho_{\xi}\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_{\xi}
	+ \phi_{\xi} \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\lambda
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\right)
	+\sum_\alpha\dot c_\xi^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_\xi\right),
	\label{eq:el_momentum_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
with the fluid-phase capillary-force additions displayed separately below.

The Euler--Lagrange equation associated with arbitrary $\delta\bar\rho_\xi$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\al \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\al}{\phi_{\xi}}
	= \bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}
	+
	\bar\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
	.
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_storage}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
We recognize the first term on the right-hand side as the thermodynamic phase
pressure contribution.  The second term is present when the phase electric
enthalpy depends on intrinsic density.  We define the phase pressure as
%
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\bar{p}_\xi \equiv
	\bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}
	+
	\bar\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
	}
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_all}
\end{equation}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} therefore identifies the total phase
pressure as the usual thermodynamic pressure plus the dielectric pressure
correction induced by the intrinsic-density dependence of the phase electric
enthalpy.

The coefficient of $\delta\phi_f$ in \eqref{eq:collected_full} includes both the
primitive interfacial potential $\gamma_f$ and the electric enthalpy
\(\omega_f^+\), so the fluid-phase volume-fraction relation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\sum_\alpha\frac{\pi_f^\alpha}{\phi_f}-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0.
	\label{eq:fluid_volume_fraction_el_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\bar p_f-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0,
	\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
	\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^+-\lambda .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Consequently pressure differences between fluid phases contain both the
primitive interfacial-potential difference and the difference in electric
enthalpy assignments,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_f-\bar p_g
	=
	\gamma_f-\gamma_g
	+
	\omega_f^+-\omega_g^+ .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:pc_def}, the wetting/non-wetting relation is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	=
	p_{c,nw}
	+
	\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery}
\end{empheq}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For two fluid phases with $\mc F=\{w,n\}$ and one oriented pair $(w,n)$,
\eqref{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery} defines the
electrocapillary pressure jump: \(p_{c,nw}\) is the intrinsic capillary
contribution associated with the interfacial potential, and
\(\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+\) is the pressure-like electrical contribution.  This
relation is consistent with electrowetting interface balances in which the
free energy contains surface, electrostatic, and ionic contributions and
stationarity of the interface position gives a pressure-difference balance
against capillary and Maxwell or electro-osmotic terms
\cite{papageorgiou2019film_flows_electric_fields,yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation}.
The same phase-attributed electrical enthalpy enters the phase momentum
balances, reversible stresses, and thermodynamic restrictions.
If the interfacial energy is parameterized by the wetting saturation $S_w$, then
$p_{c,nw}=-\partial\gamma/\partial S_w$ in the neutral limit.

The same relations identify the equivalent pore pressure associated with the
volume constraint.  Define
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_E\equiv-\lambda .
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Multiplying $\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^++\bar p_E$ by the fluid saturations
and summing over fluid phases gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\bar p_f
	=
	\bar p_E
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\omega_f^+ .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_pressure_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The primitive potentials satisfy
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_primitive_potential_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		\bar p_E
		=
		\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\left(\bar p_f-\omega_f^+\right)
		-
		\gamma
	}.
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation} is the
electrical form of the equivalent pore pressure used in thermodynamically
stable coupled multiphase poromechanics
\cite{coussy2004poromechanics,kim2013rigorous}.

For a phase with $N$ tracked components, the Euler--Lagrange equations
associated with arbitrary $\delta\eta_{\xi}^\alpha$ give
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	0 = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha} + \Pi_{\xi}
	- \rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	- \phi_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E},
	\qquad \alpha=1,\ldots,N,
	\label{eq:el_eta_all}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
while variation with respect to $\Pi_{\xi}$ returns the phase mass fraction
constraint
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\alpha=1}^N \eta_{\xi}^\alpha = 1.
	\label{eq:el_eta_constraint}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Thus $\Pi_{\xi}$ is the phase-level multiplier that enforces the redundant
composition coordinate.  Equation~\eqref{eq:el_eta_all} may be read
componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	+
	\phi_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}
	= \Pi_{\xi},
	\label{eq:Pi_componentwise}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The first term in \eqref{eq:Pi_componentwise} is the usual chemical or mixing
contribution to composition stationarity.  The second term is the electrical
composition contribution; when \(\omega_\xi^+\) is specialized to an
electrolyte, double-layer, or ionic-solution model, this term contains the
corresponding electro-osmotic or osmotic contribution.
%
Choosing component $N$ as the reference and comparing against
$\beta=1,\ldots,N-1$ gives a system of $N-1$ equations,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	 & \rho_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\beta}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\kappa\neq \beta}}
	- \left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\kappa\neq N}}
	\right)
	+
	\phi_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\beta}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
	- \left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi,\mbf E}
	\right)
	\notag                                               \\
	 & \qquad = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\beta}{\eta_{\xi}^\beta}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^N}{\eta_{\xi}^N},
	\qquad \beta=1,\ldots,N-1.
	\label{eq:pi_difference_system}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here $\kappa$ indexes the collection of composition components held fixed in
the partial derivative.

The equations associated with the conversion variables are
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	= \frac{1}{2}\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	-\psi_\xi
	-L_\xi^\alpha
	-\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}.
	\label{eq:el_conversion_component}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Equation \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} is the coefficient of
\(\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) associated with the scalar transfer potential
\(\tau\).  Because \(\tau\) is shared by all components in a phase, one
component relation can evolve \(\tau\) along that phase motion; the remaining
relations constrain the storage multipliers \(\pi_\xi^\alpha\).

At this stage $L_\xi^\alpha$ remains an explicit constitutive work potential for
component transfer.  An admissible choice of $L_\xi^\alpha$ is identified by the
Coleman--Noll argument in
Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}.

\subsubsection{Fluid-phase momentum equations}
\label{sec:fluid_phase_momentum}

For any fluid phase $f\in\mc F$, the scalar-transport capillary contribution
gives the intermediate form
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf a_f = \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+} + \rho_f\mbf g + \mbf b_f -\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f -\sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf x}\pi_f^\alpha +\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f+\sum_\alpha\dot c_f^\alpha\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right).
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_start}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \(\sum_\alpha\pi_f^\alpha=\phi_f\bar p_f\) and
\(\lambda=\gamma_f+\omega_f^+-\bar p_f\), the multiplier,
interfacial-gradient, and electric-enthalpy terms collapse as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_f\bar p_f\right)
	+\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	=
	-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_f
	-\omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f .
	\label{eq:fluid_capillary_pressure_gradient_collapse}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The phase pressure-gradient form is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	& = \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
	+ \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f
	- \omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	+\sum_\alpha\dot c_f^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right),
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_simplified}
\end{empheq}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The cancellation in \eqref{eq:fluid_capillary_pressure_gradient_collapse} shows
that the primitive capillary potential enters the fluid momentum equation
through the pressure relation.  The electrical assignment contributes the
term \(-\omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\), which acts where the phase volume
fraction varies.  In the mixture description, changing \(\phi_f\) changes the
local electric-enthalpy contribution, charge capacity, and displacement assigned
to phase \(f\); the total charge density itself is weighted by the phase volume
fractions through \eqref{eq:mixture_charge_density_definition}.  The remaining
term in \eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} is therefore a phase-volume-fraction
force associated with rearranging the electric enthalpy assignment.  It is
paired with the Maxwell stress contained in
\(\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}\); both terms arise from the same electric-enthalpy
potential in the variational statement.  Leaky-dielectric and electrowetting
theories contain related electrical interfacial forces through
Maxwell stresses or electric surface-energy derivatives
\cite{melcher1969electrohydrodynamics,yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation};
the present term appears directly from the constrained phase-volume variation.

The neutral, conversion-free specialization
\(\omega_f^+=0\) and \(\dot c_f^\alpha=0\) gives
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} reduces to the standard pressure-gradient form,
%
\[
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	= \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
	+ \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f'
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f,
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
\]
%

\subsection{Onsager principle for dissipative reaction and transport}
\label{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}

The Hamilton--d'Alembert variation determines the reversible coordinate
equations at fixed endpoint variations.  Reactions and relative component
transport are determined separately by an Onsager principle: at a fixed current
thermodynamic state, the process rates for the immediate response are chosen by
stationarity of a power functional containing a dissipation potential and a
signed constraint power~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  The Onsager
statement is local in time and selects near-equilibrium rates at the current
thermodynamic state.  The state variables that enter the potentials are held
fixed during this minimization, while
\(\dot r_{(m)}\), \(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\),
\(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\), and \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\) are varied.  The
fixed current configuration and constitutive state are
\(\mbf F_\xi\), \(\bar\rho_\xi\), \(\eta_\xi^\alpha\), \(\phi_\xi\),
\(\varphi\), and \(J_\xi\).  The rate
\(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\) is evaluated from the reaction and
relative-flux rates through
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate}.

The component-rate balance enters the Onsager principle as a Lagrange
constraint term.  Since the product of \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\) with the
component-rate constraint must have units of power in the Onsager statement,
dimensional analysis identifies \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\) as an energy per unit mass,
that is, a chemical potential.  Its variational role here is to enforce the
rate constraint:
%
\begin{equation}
	C_{\rm rt}
	\equiv
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\left[
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:component_rate_residual_def}
\end{equation}
%
The neutral chemical contribution is
\(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\) times the bracket in
\eqref{eq:component_rate_residual_def}.  The charge-rate identity in
\eqref{eq:component_charge_rate_identity} is obtained by multiplying the same
rate bracket by the component charge per unit mass \(z_\xi^\alpha\); its
electric power contribution is \(\varphi z_\xi^\alpha\) times that bracket.  The
two contributions therefore combine into the electrochemical component
potential
%
\begin{equation}
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi .
	\label{eq:C45_combined_def}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\varphi\) has units of energy per charge and \(z_\xi^\alpha\) has units
of charge per unit component mass, so \(z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\) has the same
units as \(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\).  In the Onsager minimization \(\varphi\) is a
state field inherited from the Hamilton electric-field problem; it shifts the
chemical force to the electrochemical force.

The constrained Onsager power functional, often called a Rayleighian \(\mc R\),
for reaction and relative transport is stationary when the variation of the
dissipation potential balances the signed constraint variation:
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc R
	=
	\Phi_{\rm rt} - C_{\rm rt},
	\qquad
	\delta\mc R=0 .
	\label{eq:onsager_stationarity}
\end{equation}
%
The constraint term \(C_{\rm rt}\) is defined in
\eqref{eq:component_rate_residual_def}.  The near-equilibrium quadratic
dissipation potential used here is
%
\begin{align}
	\Phi_{\rm rt}
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_m\frac{\dot r_{(m)}^2}{K_{(m)}}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\sum_\beta
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\sum_\beta
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta\dv.
	\label{eq:onsager_quadratic_reaction_transport_potential}
\end{align}
%
Here \(K_{(m)}\) is the reaction mobility, and
\(\mbf D_{\xi,k}^{\alpha\beta}\) are the mobility tensors on the relative-flux
subspace satisfying \eqref{eq:averaged_relative_flux_sum}; the inverse is
understood on that subspace.  The adopted quadratic potential is block
diagonal in reaction, diffusion, and dispersion rates.  Varying
\(\Phi_{\rm rt}\) gives
%
\begin{align}
	\delta\Phi_{\rm rt}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}\,
	\delta\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		\sum_\beta
		\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		\sum_\beta
		\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\dv.
	\label{eq:reaction_transport_dissipation_variation}
\end{align}
%
The corresponding first variation of the constraint term is
%
\begin{align}
	\delta C_{\rm rt}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\delta\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\left[
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\left(\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\right)
	\delta\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha\cdot
	\left(
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\dv ,
	\label{eq:Prt_variation}
\end{align}
%
where the flux terms have been integrated by parts in space and boundary power
terms are suppressed.

\subsubsection{Collected Onsager variation}
\label{sec:collected_onsager_variation}

The Onsager variations can now be collected in the same way as the
Hamilton--d'Alembert variations above.  Using
\(\delta\mc R=\delta\Phi_{\rm rt}-\delta C_{\rm rt}\), the rate-dependent
stationarity condition is
%
\begin{align}
	0 = \delta\mc R
	={} &
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\delta\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\left[
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\left[
		\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}
		+
		\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]\delta\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		\sum_\beta
		\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
		+
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		\sum_\beta
		\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
		+
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\dv
	+
	\text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:collected_onsager_variation}
\end{align}
%
The multiplier variation in \eqref{eq:collected_onsager_variation} enforces the
component-rate balance.  The mechanism-rate and flux variations give
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}
	 & =
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha,
	\label{eq:el_conversion_affinity}
	\\
	\sum_\beta
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
	 & =\mbf 0,
	\label{eq:hamilton_dispersion_force_balance}
	\\
	\sum_\beta
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
	 & =\mbf 0.
	\label{eq:hamilton_diffusion_force_balance}
\end{align}
%
These equations are generalized force balances on the zero-sum
relative-component-flux subspace.  The transport power is invariant under a
common shift of all component potentials in a phase.  The reaction affinity is defined by the common thermodynamic
construction: the chemical potentials are projected against the stoichiometric
coefficients for each mechanism.  Thus
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	\equiv
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	=
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}.
	\label{eq:onsager_affinity_constitutive_relation}
\end{equation}
%
The resulting near-equilibrium rate laws are
%
\begin{align}
	\dot r_{(m)}
	 & =
	K_{(m)}\mc A_{(m)},
	\label{eq:onsager_reaction_rate_variational}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_\beta
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\beta,
	\label{eq:onsager_dispersion_mobility_balance}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_\beta
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\beta.
	\label{eq:onsager_diffusion_mobility_balance}
\end{align}
%
The entropy inequality in Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}
then checks that these Onsager force--rate laws are dissipative and records the
positivity assumptions that make the closures admissible.
