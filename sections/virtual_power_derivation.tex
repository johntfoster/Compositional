\section{Variational Derivation}
\label{sec:variational}
\label{sec:virtual_power_derivation}

We derive the governing equations by coupling two compatible stationarity
statements.  The reversible mechanics and constraints are obtained from a
Hamilton--d'Alembert variation, following the extended Hamilton principles used
by Bedford and Drumheller for porous and reacting immiscible mixtures
~\cite{bedford1979variational,drumheller1980thermomechanical,bedford1983theories}.
The dissipative reaction and relative-transport rates are then determined by an
Onsager stationarity condition, in which process rates are paired with
thermodynamic forces and subsequently restricted by a dissipation inequality
~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  This pairing of Hamilton--d'Alembert
mechanics with an Onsager variational statement is the continuum analogue of
combining virtual work for reversible coordinates with virtual power for
dissipative rates~\cite{germain1973virtualpower,gurtin2010continua}.  The
organization preserves the Bedford--Drumheller constrained-mixture structure
while keeping the force--rate pairings explicit.

The formulation is written for a compositional mixture containing arbitrary sets
of solid $\mc S$ and fluid $\mc F$ phases.  Here fluid means a nonsolid pore
phase, such as an aqueous, oleic, or gas phase.  The symbol $s \in \mc{S}$
denotes a solid phase, $f \in\mc F$ denotes a fluid phase, and
$\xi \in \mc{S} \cup \mc{F}$ ranges over all phases.  Components within each
phase are indexed by $\alpha$.

\subsection{Variational principle and its terms}

Let \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) be the endpoint times and let \(\delta\) denote an
admissible virtual variation that vanishes at those endpoints.  The
Hamilton--d'Alembert statement contains the reversible energy variation, the
ordinary virtual work, and the geometric and material-measure constraints:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(T-U\right)\dt
	+
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
	\left(\delta W+\sum_{k=1}^{3}\delta C_k\right)\dt
	= 0.
	\label{eq:vp_principle}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The component-rate constraints contain both material-measure rates and
dissipative process rates, so they are kept out of the Hamilton statement.  Their
stationarity is imposed together with the reaction--transport dissipation
potential by the accompanying Onsager statement
%
\begin{equation}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
	\delta\left(C_4+C_5-\Phi_{\rm rt}\right)\dt
	=0.
	\label{eq:onsager_stationarity}
\end{equation}
%
The variation in \eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity} is taken with respect to the
component-rate multiplier, the component material accumulations, the phase maps
through the phase Jacobians, the mechanism rates, and the two relative fluxes.
The sign convention is chosen so the force definitions
\(\mc A_{(m)}=\partial\Phi_{\rm rt}/\partial\dot r_{(m)}\) and
\(\mbf M_{\xi,k}^\alpha=\partial\Phi_{\rm rt}/
\partial\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha\) agree with the entropy section.  An admissible
quadratic form for \(\Phi_{\rm rt}\) is specified in
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_transport_dissipation_potential}.  In the neutral theory
\(C_5\) and the electrical part of \(U\) are absent.  The body force per unit
phase mass is \(\mbf g\), \(L_\xi^\alpha\) is the component conversion-work
potential, \(\mbf b_\xi\) is the interphase interaction force, and \(\theta\) is
the absolute temperature.  In the electrical term,
\(\omega_\xi^+=\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\), where
\(\mc X_\xi=\{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\alpha,\theta\}\) denotes the
non-field state variables held fixed in the dielectric derivative:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
\small
\begin{align}
	T        & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}}\frac{1}{2} \rho_\xi \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \dv,
	\label{eq:T_def}                                                                                                                                                                                                \\
	U        & = \sum_{\xi}\int_{\mc{B}} \rho_{\xi}\,\psi_{\xi}(\mbf{F}_{\xi}, \bar\rho_{\xi}, \eta^\al_{\xi},\theta)\dv
	+ \int_{\mc{B}} \phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta\right) \dv
	+\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\,\dv,
	\label{eq:U_def}                                                                                                                                                                                                \\
		\delta W & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left(\rho_\xi\mbf{g}+\mbf b_\xi\right)\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi \,\dv \notag                                                                                                  \\
		         &\quad-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}L_\xi^\alpha\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\,\dv,
		\label{eq:W_def}                                                                                                                                                                                                \\
	C_1      & = \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \,\dv,
	\label{eq:C1_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_2      & = \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi 0}} \tau^\al_\xi \left( J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi 0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} \bar\rho_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha+\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \eta^\al_\xi \bar\rho_\xi} \right) \dV_\xi,
	\label{eq:C2_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_3      & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \pi_\xi \left( \sum_\alpha \eta_\xi^\alpha - 1 \right) \dv,
	\label{eq:C3_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_4      & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha
	\left[\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha+\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:C4_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_5      & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B} z_\xi^\alpha\varphi
	\left[\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha+\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:vp_C5_charge_constraint}
\end{align}
\endgroup
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The bracketed term in \(C_5\) is identical to the bracketed term in \(C_4\).
The two constraints may therefore be added before taking variations:
%
\begin{equation}
	C_4+C_5
	=
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\left[\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha+\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\right]\dv,
	\label{eq:C45_combined_def}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\mu_\xi^\alpha=\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\) is the unified
component-rate multiplier obtained by adding the chemomechanical multiplier and
the charge-weighted electric-potential multiplier.

The coefficient $L_\xi^\alpha$ is the component generalized force conjugate to
both conversion and transport accumulation, and it enters the
Hamilton--d'Alembert virtual work \(\delta W\).  The same virtual work contains
gravity \(\mbf g\) and the interphase interaction force \(\mbf b_\xi\).
Constraint $C_1$ enforces the volume-fraction constraint, $C_2$ enforces the
augmented component material measure, and $C_3$ enforces mass-fraction
normalization.  Constraint $C_4$ enforces the complete component-rate balance,
including conversion stoichiometry and the divergence of the relative flux, and
its charge projection is \(C_5\).  Since \(C_4\) and \(C_5\) contain the same
component-rate bracket, their variations in \eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity} are
performed once using the unified multiplier \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\).  In the neutral
theory \(\mu_\xi^\alpha=\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha\).

The conversion coordinate \(r_{(m)}\) is retained as a generalized coordinate in
the Bedford--Drumheller sense, while the mechanism rate \(\dot r_{(m)}\) and the
relative fluxes are dissipative process rates.  Their stationarity follows from
the Onsager statement rather than from the Hamilton action.  Differentiating
\(\Phi_{\rm rt}\) with respect to these rates gives the affinities
\(\mc A_{(m)}\) and the flux forces \(\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\) and
\(\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\); the entropy inequality then restricts their
constitutive dependence on the rates.

The variations in this section are mechanical and compositional variations at
fixed temperature.  Temperature is retained as a constitutive state variable and
is varied in the Coleman--Noll entropy argument below.
The fields $\mbf x_\xi$, $\eta_\xi^\alpha$, $\phi_\xi$, $\bar\rho_\xi$, and
$\mc C_\xi^\alpha$ are varied independently.  The mechanism rates and the two
relative fluxes are independent process rates in the Onsager statement.  Their
force-rate pairings identify the corresponding generalized-force equations,
while the entropy inequality subsequently restricts their constitutive
dependence on those rates.

\subsection{Extending Bedford and Drumheller identity 1.28}
\label{sec:extended_bd_identity}

Bedford and Drumheller~\cite[eq.~(1.28)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
give an identity for the variation of a constituent-attached spatial field
$\Gamma_\xi$ weighted by the current phase density $\rho_\xi$.  The same
transport argument extends componentwise to the compositional setting used here.

The conversion and transport accumulations \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) and
\(\mc D_\xi^\alpha\) are attached to the phase-$\xi$ reference coordinates.
Their sum is the added component mass appearing in
\eqref{eq:augmented_component_material_measure}, so its variation must be pushed
forward with the same phase map as the corresponding component density.
%
For any phase field $\Gamma_\xi$, the Bedford--Drumheller identity extends componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho^\alpha_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \delta \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi0}+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha+\mc D_\xi^\alpha\right)
	\Gamma_\xi \,\dVxi \notag        \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left[
	\delta\left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi0}+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha+\mc D_\xi^\alpha\right)\Gamma_\xi
		+ J_\xi\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	\right]\dVxi \notag              \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left[
	\left(\delta\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha\right)\Gamma_\xi
		+ J_\xi\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	\right]\dVxi \notag              \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}}
	\left(
	\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	+ \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\Gamma_\xi
	\right)\dv .
	\label{eq:bd128_mod2}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Summing both sides of \eqref{eq:bd128_mod2} over $\alpha$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \left[\rho_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi + \sum_\alpha \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi} \Gamma_\xi  \right]  \dv
	\label{eq:bd128_mod}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where $\rho_\xi = \sum_\alpha \rho^\alpha_\xi$.

\subsection{Variation of the kinetic energy}
\label{sec:var_T_app}
% ============================================================

The variation of kinetic energy using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod} is then
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta T = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi\,\mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{v}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi} \dv.
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Since $\delta\mbf{v}_\xi = \mathrm{d}(\delta\mbf{x}_\xi)/\mathrm{d}t$ and $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_1} = \delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_2} = \mbf{0}$, integration by parts in time gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \delta T \dt
	= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left[-\rho_\xi\,\mbf{a}_\xi - \sum_\al \frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha+\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi} \mbf{v}_\xi \right] \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}  \dv \dt
	\label{eq:deltaT_app}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
\subsection{Variation of the phase free energies}
\label{sec:var_Uxi}
% ============================================================

For every phase, solid or fluid, let $\psi_\xi$ denote the
specific Helmholtz free energy and let \(N\) be the number of tracked components
in that phase.  The corresponding contribution to the total free energy is written
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	U \supset U_\xi & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc B}\rho_\xi\psi_\xi(\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^1, \eta_\xi^2, \dots, \eta_\xi^N)\,\dv \notag \\
	                & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc B}\rho_\xi\psi_\xi(\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta)\,\dv
	\label{eq:U_xi_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Here $\eta_\xi^\beta$ denotes the full list of component mass fractions
$\{\eta_\xi^1,\ldots,\eta_\xi^N\}$; the index $\beta$ is not summed in this argument list.
The constitutive dependence on $\mbf F_\xi$ may be trivial for fluid phases,
but keeping it in the notation allows one calculation for all phases.  The local
free-energy variation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta\psi_\xi
	 & = \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta,\theta}:\delta\mbf F_\xi
	+ \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta,\theta}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
	+ \sum_\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta}
	\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:delta_psi_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod}, integrating the $\delta\mbf F_\xi$ term by parts,
and collecting independent variations gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\xi
	 & = \sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}\Bigg[
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma'_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
	+ \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}\delta\bar\rho_\xi \notag \\
	 & \qquad
		+ \sum_\alpha \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha}}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+ \sum_\alpha \psi_\xi\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\Bigg]\dv + \text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:deltaU_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The corresponding Cauchy effective stress\footnote{Here we use the single primed stress notation $\bs{\sigma}^\prime$ to represent a Terzaghi effective stress, i.e. $\bar\rho_\xi$ held fixed.  Later we will introduce a double-primed stress $\bs{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}$ as a Biot effective stress, i.e. pressure $\bar{p}_\xi$ held fixed.} is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\bs\sigma'_\xi
	\equiv \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}\mbf F_\xi^T .
	\label{eq:var_cauchy_def}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For fluid phases this term vanishes when $\psi_\xi$ has no dependence on
$\mbf F_\xi$.

\subsection{Variation of the interfacial energies}
\label{sec:var_gamma}
% ============================================================

Let $\mc I$ denote the set of oriented fluid interfaces.  Each
$(w,n)\in\mc I$ identifies a wetting phase $w$ and a non-wetting phase $n$.
The total fluid volume fraction and the fluid saturations are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi = \sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f,
	\qquad
	S_f = \frac{\phi_f}{\phi},
	\qquad
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f=1 .
	\label{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The saturations are used here only as ratios of the primitive volume-fraction
fields; the variations are still taken with respect to the primitive
$\phi_f$.  The notation $\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}$ denotes the fluid
saturation state subject to the constraint in
\eqref{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}.  Thus the energy may be written in
terms of any independent set of fluid saturations, with the remaining
saturation determined by the constraint; the derivation below keeps the
constraint explicit instead of choosing a reference fluid phase.   The
effective fluid interfacial energy density is a single constitutive potential
of this constrained saturation state
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma =
	\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right).
	\label{eq:interfacial_energy_density}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
and the corresponding contribution to the internal energy is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\gamma =
	\int_{\mc{B}}
	\phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right)
	\dv .
	\label{eq:interfacial_internal_energy}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

The Bedford--Drumheller spatial identity
(cf.\ \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical} equation (1.25)) gives, for each
fluid phase field $\phi_f$,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\delta\phi_\xi\right\vert_{\mbf{x}}
	= \delta\phi_\xi-\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi.
	\label{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\phi=\sum_{f\in\mc F}\delta\phi_f,
	\qquad
	\delta S_f=\frac{\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi}{\phi},
	\label{eq:saturation_variations}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
the fixed-point contribution and the transport correction from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation} are collected in one
calculation:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\gamma
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\left[
		\gamma\,\delta\phi
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
		\left(\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi\right)
	\right]\dv \notag                                          \\
	 & \quad
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv \notag \\
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\delta\phi_f\dv
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv .
	\label{eq:deltaU_fg}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The subscript $g$ denotes the set of all fluid phases excluding the varied phase
$f$; in this variation, the \(S_g\ne S_f\) are held fixed.
The phase interfacial potential appearing in \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma
	+\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
	-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g
	\left.\p{\gamma}{S_g}\right\vert_{S_{h\neq g}},
	\label{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where the saturation derivatives are evaluated on the saturation simplex and the same ``held fixed'' notation convention applies.  The
oriented wetting/non-wetting pairs specify capillary-pressure differences.  The wetting/non-wetting
interfacial difference associated with an oriented pair is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	p_{c,nw}\equiv\gamma_n-\gamma_w,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:pc_def}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The form \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} keeps the gradient terms generated by the scalar
transport of the phase-attached volume fractions.  These terms combine with the
volume-fraction and component-storage multiplier gradients in the phase momentum
balances after the volume-fraction Euler--Lagrange equations are used.

\subsection{Electric field}
\label{sec:electric_field_variation}

The electrical terms are conservative except for any constitutive dielectric
relaxation introduced later.  They enter the Hamilton action through the
phase-attributed electric enthalpies \(U_\xi^+\), while the charge bookkeeping
has already been included in the combined component-rate constraint
\eqref{eq:C45_combined_def}.  For each phase,
%
\begin{equation*}
	U^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\int_{\mc B}\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\,\dv
	=
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\,\dV_\xi .
\end{equation*}
%
Here \(\omega_\xi^+\) is an electric enthalpy per unit current volume assigned
to phase \(\xi\), and
\(\mc X_\xi=\{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\alpha,\theta\}\) denotes the
non-field state variables.  In the electroquasistatic specialization,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi,
	\qquad
	\mbf d_\xi
	=
	-\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{\xi}}{\mbf E}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:electric_field_definitions}
\end{equation}
%
The full variation contains a direct volume-fraction term, state-variable
dielectric terms, the potential-gradient term, and the mechanical pullback.  The
geometric part of the pullback is
%
\begin{align*}
	\delta_{\mbf x_\xi}J_\xi
	&=
	J_\xi\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi,
	\qquad
	\delta_{\mbf x_\xi}\mbf E
	=
	-\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\right)^T\mbf E,
	\\
	\left.
	\delta_{\mbf x_\xi}U^{+}_{\xi}
	\right\vert_{\delta\varphi=\delta\phi_\xi=\delta\mc X_\xi=0}
	&=
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi
	\left[
	\omega_\xi^+\mbf I
	+
	\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi
	\right]:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dV_\xi
	\\
	&=
	\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\left[
	\omega_\xi^+\mbf I
	+
	\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi
	\right]:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv .
\end{align*}
%
Thus \(U_\xi^+\) contributes \(\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi\) to the
volume-fraction coefficient, contributes
\(\phi_\xi\delta_{\mc X_\xi}\omega_\xi^+\) to the coefficients of the non-field
state variables, and contributes the displayed Maxwell term to the phase-motion
coefficient.  The notation \(\delta_{\mc X_\xi}\omega_\xi^+\) denotes the
variation of \(\omega_\xi^+\) with respect to the variables in \(\mc X_\xi\) at
fixed \(\mbf E\).

After integrating the potential-gradient term by parts and including the free
charge potential term, the electrical contribution to the action is
%
\begin{align}
	-\delta\sum_\xi U^{+}_{\xi}
	={}&
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left(
	\varrho-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d
	\right)\delta\varphi\,\dv
	\notag\\
	&-
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi\,\dv
	-
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi\delta_{\mc X_\xi}\omega_\xi^+\,\dv
	\notag\\
	&-
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv
	+
	\text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_mechanical_power}
\end{align}
%
The reversible electrical, or Maxwell, stress assigned to the phase pullback is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\phi_\xi\omega^{+}_{\xi}\mbf I
	+
	\phi_\xi\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the total reversible stress used in the phase momentum equation is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
	=
	\bs\sigma_\xi'
	+
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{+}.
	\label{eq:vp_total_reversible_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Stationarity with respect to \(\varphi\) gives Gauss' law,
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d=\varrho .
	\label{eq:gauss_law}
\end{equation}
%
Combined with the local charge balance from \sref{conservation_of_charge}, the
electroquasistatic field equation implies
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\dot{\mbf d}
	+
	\mbf i
	\right)
	=0 .
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_compatibility}
\end{equation}

The factor multiplying \(\varphi\) in the unified component-rate multiplier is
\(z_\xi^\alpha\), the charge per unit component mass, so
\(z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\) has the units of a chemical potential.  The neutral
equations below are unchanged if one suppresses \(C_5\), \(\omega_\xi^+\), and
the distinction between \(\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha\) and \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\).

\subsection{Component-rate constraint variations}
\label{sec:component_rate_constraint_variations}
\label{sec:stoich_conversion_variations}
\label{sec:transport_accumulation_variations}

The combined constraint \eqref{eq:C45_combined_def} contains conversion,
transport accumulation, and relative transport in one component-rate balance.
Because \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) and \(\mc D_\xi^\alpha\) are phase-attached
accumulated material measures, their rate variations must be integrated by parts
with the same phase material derivative before any stationarity condition is
used.  Varying \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\), the two accumulated measures, the phase map,
the mechanism rates, and the two relative fluxes gives
%
\begin{align}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\delta(C_4+C_5)\,\dt
	={}&
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\delta\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\left[
	\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha+\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)
	\right]\dv\dt
	\notag\\
	&+
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
	-\frac{D_\xi\mu_\xi^\alpha}{Dt}
	\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha+\delta\mc D_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	+
	\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha+\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
	\right]\dv\dt
	\notag\\
	&-
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\left(\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\right)
	\delta\dot r_{(m)}\,\dv\dt
	\notag\\
	&-
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha\cdot
	\left(
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\dv\dt
	+
	\text{b.t.}
	\label{eq:C4_variation}
\end{align}
%
The multiplier variation in \eqref{eq:C4_variation} returns the complete
component-rate balance.  The accumulated-measure variations supply the same
material derivative \(D_\xi\mu_\xi^\alpha/Dt\) for both \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) and
\(\mc D_\xi^\alpha\), the map variation receives the combined material-rate
coefficient, and both relative-flux variations receive the same electrochemical
component-potential gradient \(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha\).  These are the
constraint-side coefficients that will be paired with the Onsager force
coefficients.

The remaining contribution in the Onsager statement
\eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity} is the variation of the reaction--transport
dissipation potential.  For this contribution the current thermodynamic state is
fixed while the independent process rates are varied, so
%
\begin{align*}
	\delta\Phi_{\rm rt}
	={}&
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left[
	\sum_m
	\mc A_{(m)}\,\delta\dot r_{(m)}
	+
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	\right.
	\\
	&\left.
	\qquad\qquad
	+
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right]\dv .
\end{align*}
%
Therefore the term \(-\delta\Phi_{\rm rt}\) in
\eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity} supplies the negative affinity and flux-force
coefficients that combine with \eqref{eq:C4_variation}.  This is the only place
where the Onsager potential enters the collected variational coefficients; the
later entropy argument restricts the constitutive dependence of
\(\mc A_{(m)}\) and \(\mbf M_{\xi,k}^\alpha\) on the corresponding rates.

Before collecting the stationarity conditions, define the total current-volume
component material-addition rate
%
\begin{align*}
	\dot m_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\dot c_\xi^\alpha
	+
	\frac{\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	=
	\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha+\dot{\mc D}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}.
\end{align*}
%
This rate combines conversion and relative-transport accumulation without
identifying the two dissipative mechanisms.

\subsection{Collected variational principle}
\label{sec:collected_var}
% ============================================================

The two stationarity statements are now evaluated separately and their
coefficients are collected in a single display.  Equivalently, one adds the
evaluated Hamilton--d'Alembert coefficients to the evaluated coefficients of
\(\delta(C_4+C_5-\Phi_{\rm rt})\); no new variational principle is introduced.
Before substituting any stationarity equation back into another variation, this
collection gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
	\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		0 ={} & \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \left\{
		\begin{aligned}
			& \sum_{\xi} \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
				-\rho_{\xi}\mbf{a}_{\xi}
				+ \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_{\xi}^{\prime+} + \rho_{\xi}\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_{\xi}

		&\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}

	charge-projected balance in variational form.  Thus the same scalar potential
	appears in two roles: its Hamilton variation gives Gauss' law, and its
	multiplier variation in \(C_5\) gives the charge-projected rate balance.  Since
	all displayed variations are arbitrary within their respective variation
	classes, each bracketed integrand must vanish pointwise.  The coordinate
	variations give Euler--Lagrange equations, while the rate variations give
	Onsager stationarity equations.

%
\begin{align}
	\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
	&=\mbf 0,
	\label{eq:hamilton_dispersion_force_balance}
	\\
	\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
	&=\mbf 0.
	\label{eq:hamilton_diffusion_force_balance}
\end{align}
%

fluxes whose component
sum is zero.  Equivalently, adding the same force to every component does not
change the virtual power.  They are generalized-force balances, not
constitutive laws.  Constitutive relations between each \(\mbf M\) and its flux
are restricted by the entropy inequality below.

\subsection{Euler--Lagrange equations}

The Euler--Lagrange equations generated by arbitrary $\delta\phi_\xi$ give,
for phases without an explicit capillary contribution,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi_\xi \lambda = - \sum_\alpha \tau^\al_\xi .
	\label{eq:el_lambda}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For the fluid phases the corresponding equations contain the primitive capillary potentials discussed below.

Substituting \eqref{eq:el_lambda} into the Euler--Lagrange equations associated with arbitrary $\delta \mbf{x}_\xi$ and simplifying gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\mbf{a}_{\xi} =
		\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_{\xi}^{\prime+} + \rho_{\xi}\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_{\xi}
		+ \phi_{\xi} \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\lambda
		+\sum_\alpha\dot m_\xi^\alpha
		\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha-\mbf v_\xi\right),
		\label{eq:el_momentum_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
with the fluid-phase capillary-force additions displayed separately below.

The Euler--Lagrange equation associated with arbitrary $\delta\bar\rho_\xi$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\al \frac{\tau_{\xi}^\al}{\phi_{\xi}}
	= \bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}
	.
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_tau}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
We recognize the right-hand side as the thermodynamic phase pressure $\bar{p}_\xi$ and define
%
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{\bar{p}_\xi \equiv \bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}}
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_all}
\end{equation}

The coefficient of $\delta\phi_f$ in \eqref{eq:collected_full} includes both the
primitive interfacial potential $\gamma_f$ and the electric enthalpy
\(\omega_f^+\), so the fluid-phase volume-fraction relation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\sum_\alpha\frac{\tau_f^\alpha}{\phi_f}-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0.
	\label{eq:fluid_volume_fraction_el_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\bar p_f-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0,
	\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
	\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^+-\lambda .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Consequently pressure differences between fluid phases contain both the
primitive interfacial-potential difference and the difference in electric
enthalpy assignments,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_f-\bar p_g
	=
	\gamma_f-\gamma_g
	+
	\omega_f^+-\omega_g^+ .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:pc_def}, the wetting/non-wetting relation is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	=
	p_{c,nw}
	+
	\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery}
	\end{empheq}
	% AGENT-LOCK-END
	%
	In the absence of ion exchange, reactions, or phase transformations,
	\(\dot m_f^\alpha=0\), and \eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} reduces to the
	usual pressure-gradient form, with the electrical enthalpy correction retained,
	%
	\begin{equation*}
		\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
		= \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
			+ \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
			- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f
			- \omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f,
		\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\end{equation*}
	When electrical enthalpy is absent, the final term also vanishes.
	%
For two fluid phases with $\mc F=\{w,n\}$ and one oriented pair $(w,n)$, this
reduces to the usual two-phase capillary pressure when electrical enthalpy is
absent or has the same phase assignment in the two fluids.  The pressure
difference in \eqref{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery} should
therefore be read as an electrocapillary pressure jump: \(p_{c,nw}\) remains the
intrinsic capillary contribution associated with the interfacial potential,
while \(\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+\) is a pressure-like electrical correction.  This
bookkeeping is consistent with electrowetting interface balances in which the
free energy contains surface, electrostatic, and ionic contributions and
stationarity of the interface position gives a pressure-difference balance
against capillary and Maxwell or electro-osmotic terms
\cite{yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation}.  If the interfacial energy is
parameterized by the wetting saturation $S_w$, then
$p_{c,nw}=-\partial\gamma/\partial S_w$ in the neutral limit.

The same relations identify the equivalent pore pressure associated with the
volume constraint.  Define
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_E\equiv-\lambda .
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Multiplying $\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^++\bar p_E$ by the fluid saturations
and summing over fluid phases gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\bar p_f
	=
	\bar p_E
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\omega_f^+ .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_pressure_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The primitive potentials satisfy
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_primitive_potential_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		\bar p_E
		=
		\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\left(\bar p_f-\omega_f^+\right)
		-
		\gamma
		}.
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For a phase with $N$ tracked components, the Euler--Lagrange equations
associated with arbitrary $\delta\eta_{\xi}^\alpha$ give
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	0 = \frac{\tau_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha} + \pi_{\xi}
	- \rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}},
	\qquad \alpha=1,\ldots,N,
	\label{eq:el_eta_all}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
while variation with respect to $\pi_{\xi}$ returns the phase mass fraction
constraint
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\alpha=1}^N \eta_{\xi}^\alpha = 1.
	\label{eq:el_eta_constraint}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Thus $\pi_{\xi}$ is the phase-level multiplier that enforces the redundant
composition coordinate.  Equation~\eqref{eq:el_eta_all} may be read
componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	- \frac{\tau_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}
	= \pi_{\xi},
	\label{eq:pi_componentwise}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
%
Choosing component $N$ as the reference and comparing against
$\beta=1,\ldots,N-1$ gives a system of $N-1$ equations,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\rho_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\beta}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\kappa\neq \beta}}
	- \left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\kappa\neq N}}
	\right)
	 & = \frac{\tau_{\xi}^\beta}{\eta_{\xi}^\beta}
	- \frac{\tau_{\xi}^N}{\eta_{\xi}^N},
	\qquad \beta=1,\ldots,N-1.
	\label{eq:pi_difference_system}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here $\kappa$ denotes the collection of composition components held fixed in the
partial derivative; it is not summed.

The equations associated with the conversion variables are
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\frac{D_\xi\mu_\xi^\alpha}{Dt}
	&= \frac{1}{2}\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	-\psi_\xi
	-L_\xi^\alpha
	-\frac{\tau_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha},
	\label{eq:el_conversion_component}
	\\
	\mc A_{(m)}
	&=-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha.
	\label{eq:el_conversion_affinity}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Equation \eqref{eq:el_conversion_affinity} identifies affinity from the
conversion kinematics; it does not prescribe a kinetic law.  A kinetic law is
introduced only after the entropy inequality restricts admissible dissipation
~\cite{bothe2015continuum,doi2011onsager}.

Variation with respect to \(\mc D_\xi^\alpha\) returns the same component-potential
equation \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} as variation with respect to
\(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\).  This equality follows because both accumulations enter
the material measure through \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha+\mc D_\xi^\alpha\), have the same
generalized work potential, and are governed by the same multiplier.  The
relative-flux equations therefore contain the same component chemical-potential
gradient \(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha\) that enters conversion.

At this stage $L_\xi^\alpha$ remains an explicit constitutive
component-transfer potential.  An admissible choice of $L_\xi^\alpha$ is identified
by the Coleman--Noll argument in
Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}.

\subsection{Fluid-phase momentum equations}
\label{sec:fluid_phase_momentum}

For any fluid phase $f\in\mc F$, the scalar-transport capillary contribution
gives the intermediate form
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\rho_f\mbf a_f
	 & = \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+} + \rho_f\mbf g + \mbf b_f
	\notag\\
	&\quad
		-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
		-\sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau_f^\alpha
		+\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
		+\sum_\alpha\dot m_f^\alpha
		\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_f^\alpha-\mbf v_f\right).
		\label{eq:el_mom_f_start}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using $\sum_\alpha\tau_f^\alpha=\phi_f\bar p_f$ and
$\lambda+\bar p_f-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0$, the multiplier,
interfacial-gradient, and electric-enthalpy terms collapse as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_f\bar p_f\right)
	+\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	=
	-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_f
	-
	\omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f .
	\label{eq:fluid_capillary_pressure_gradient_collapse}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The phase pressure-gradient form is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	= \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
		+ \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
		- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f
		- \omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
		+\sum_\alpha\dot m_f^\alpha
		\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_f^\alpha-\mbf v_f\right),
		\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_simplified}
\end{empheq}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
