\section{Variational Derivation}
\label{sec:variational}
\label{sec:virtual_power_derivation}

We derive the governing equations with a Hamilton--d'Alembert--Onsager
stationarity structure.  The Hamilton--d'Alembert part follows the extended
Hamilton principles used by Bedford and Drumheller for porous and reacting
immiscible mixtures
~\cite{bedford1979variational,drumheller1980thermomechanical,bedford1983theories}
and determines the mixture coordinates and constraints.  The Onsager part
determines the reaction and relative-transport rates by pairing process rates
with thermodynamic forces and then imposing the dissipation inequality
~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  This organization is the continuum
analogue of combining virtual work for coordinate variations with virtual power
for process rates~\cite{germain1973virtualpower,gurtin2010continua}.  It
preserves the Bedford--Drumheller constrained-mixture structure while keeping the
force--rate pairings explicit.

The formulation is written for a compositional mixture containing arbitrary sets
of solid $\mc S$ and fluid $\mc F$ phases.  Here fluid means a nonsolid pore
phase, such as an aqueous, oleic, or gas phase.  The symbol $s \in \mc{S}$
denotes a solid phase, $f \in\mc F$ denotes a fluid phase, and
$\xi \in \mc{S} \cup \mc{F}$ ranges over all phases.  Components within each
phase are indexed by $\alpha$.

\subsection{Hamilton--d'Alembert principle for reversible coordinates}

Let \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) be the endpoint times and let \(\delta\) denote an
admissible virtual variation that vanishes at those endpoints.  The
Hamilton--d'Alembert statement contains the kinetic and stored-energy variation,
ordinary virtual work, the volume and composition constraints, and the material
insertion constraint:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(T-U\right)\dt
	+
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
	\left(\delta W+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\delta C_k\right)\dt
	= 0.
	\label{eq:vp_principle}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Reaction rates and relative component fluxes enter this statement only through
the material insertion constraint; their constitutive stationarity is introduced
later through the constrained Onsager power functional in
Section~\ref{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}.  The body force per unit phase
mass is \(\mbf g\), \(L_\xi^\alpha\) is the component conversion-work potential,
\(\mbf b_\xi\) is the interphase interaction force, and \(\theta\) is the
absolute temperature.  The interaction force may be dissipative at the phase
level, but it is internal to the mixture and globally conservative:
\(\sum_\xi\mbf b_\xi=\mbf 0\).  In the electrical energy,
\(\omega_\xi^+=\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\), with
\(\mc X_\xi=\{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\alpha,\theta\}\) denoting the
non-field state variables held fixed in the dielectric derivative.
%
Let \(\dot c_\xi^\alpha\) denote the current-volume component material-increment
rate.
It is only a shorthand for \(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\), not an independent
rate to be constrained.  Internal conversion conserves total mass, while the
relative-flux part of \(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha\) redistributes component labels
within each phase and drops out of the phase-summed insertion constraint because
\(\sum_\alpha(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha+
\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha)=\mbf 0\).  The corresponding component-rate
balance is imposed in the Onsager subsection.
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
\small
\begin{align}
	T        & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}}\frac{1}{2} \rho_\xi \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \dv,
	\label{eq:T_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	U        & = \sum_{\xi}\int_{\mc{B}} \rho_{\xi}\,\psi_{\xi}(\mbf{F}_{\xi}, \bar\rho_{\xi}, \eta^\al_{\xi},\theta)\dv
	+ \int_{\mc{B}} \phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta\right) \dv
	+\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\,\dv,
	\label{eq:U_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	\delta W & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left(\rho_\xi\mbf{g}+\mbf b_\xi\right)\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi \,\dv-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}L_\xi^\alpha\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\,\dv,
	\label{eq:W_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_1      & = \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \,\dv,
	\label{eq:C1_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_2      & = \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi 0}} \pi_\xi^\al \left( J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi 0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} \bar\rho_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \eta^\al_\xi \bar\rho_\xi} \right) \dV_\xi,
	\label{eq:C2_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_3      & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \left( \sum_\alpha \eta_\xi^\alpha - 1 \right) \dv,
	\label{eq:C3_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_4      & = \int_{\mc B}\tau
	\left[
		\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:C4_def}
\end{align}
\endgroup
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The transfer multiplier \(\tau\) is the Bedford--Drumheller kinematic potential
conjugate to the globally conservative accumulated material insertion.  It has
units of action per unit mass, so
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\) has velocity units and
\(D_\xi\tau/Dt\) has units of energy per unit mass.  Thus \(\tau\) supplies the
conversion transfer-momentum coefficient in the reversible variation.

The coefficient $L_\xi^\alpha$ is the component generalized force conjugate to
conversion insertion, and it enters the Hamilton--d'Alembert virtual work
\(\delta W\).  The same virtual work contains gravity \(\mbf g\) and the
interphase interaction force \(\mbf b_\xi\).
Constraint $C_1$ enforces the volume-fraction constraint, $C_2$ enforces the
augmented component material measure, and $C_3$ enforces mass-fraction
normalization.  Constraint $C_4$ carries the Bedford--Drumheller material
insertion multiplier for the summed kinematic insertion constraint.

The variations in this section are mechanical and compositional variations at
fixed temperature.  Temperature is retained as a constitutive state variable and
is varied in the Coleman--Noll entropy argument below.
The fields $\mbf x_\xi$, $\eta_\xi^\alpha$, $\phi_\xi$, $\bar\rho_\xi$, and
$\mc C_\xi^\alpha$ are varied independently.  The mechanism rates and the two
relative fluxes are varied later as independent process rates in the Onsager
statement.

\subsubsection{Extending Bedford and Drumheller identity 1.28}
\label{sec:extended_bd_identity}

Bedford and Drumheller~\cite[eq.~(1.28)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
give an identity for the variation of a constituent-attached spatial field
$\Gamma_\xi$ weighted by the current phase density $\rho_\xi$.  The same
transport argument extends componentwise to the compositional setting used here.

The component material increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) is attached to the
phase-\(\xi\) reference coordinates.  It is the added component mass appearing
in \eqref{eq:augmented_component_material_measure}.  In the reversible
Hamilton--d'Alembert variation, its componentwise virtual increment is therefore
carried with the phase-attached material measure.
%
For any phase field $\Gamma_\xi$, the Bedford--Drumheller identity extends componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho^\alpha_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \delta \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi0}+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\right)
	\Gamma_\xi \,\dVxi \notag        \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left[
		\delta\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\Gamma_\xi
		+ J_\xi\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	\right]\dVxi \notag              \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}}
	\left(
	\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	+ \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\Gamma_\xi
	\right)\dv .
	\label{eq:bd128_mod2}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Summing both sides of \eqref{eq:bd128_mod2} over $\alpha$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \left[\rho_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi + \sum_\alpha \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \Gamma_\xi  \right]  \dv
	\label{eq:bd128_mod}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where $\rho_\xi = \sum_\alpha \rho^\alpha_\xi$.

\subsubsection{Variation of the kinetic energy}
\label{sec:var_T_app}
% ============================================================

The variation of kinetic energy using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod} is then
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta T = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi\,\mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{v}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \dv.
	\label{eq:kinetic_energy_variation_before_time_integration}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Since $\delta\mbf{v}_\xi = \mathrm{d}(\delta\mbf{x}_\xi)/\mathrm{d}t$ and $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_1} = \delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_2} = \mbf{0}$, integration by parts in time gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \delta T \dt
	= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left[-\rho_\xi\,\mbf{a}_\xi - \sum_\al \frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi} \mbf{v}_\xi \right] \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}  \dv \dt
	\label{eq:deltaT_app}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
\subsubsection{Variation of the phase free energies}
\label{sec:var_Uxi}
% ============================================================

For every phase, solid or fluid, let $\psi_\xi$ denote the
specific Helmholtz free energy and let \(N\) be the number of tracked components
in that phase.  The corresponding contribution to the total free energy is written
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	U \supset U_\xi & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc B}\rho_\xi\psi_\xi(\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^1, \eta_\xi^2, \dots, \eta_\xi^N)\,\dv \notag \\
	                & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc B}\rho_\xi\psi_\xi(\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta)\,\dv
	\label{eq:U_xi_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Here $\eta_\xi^\beta$ denotes the full list of component mass fractions
$\{\eta_\xi^1,\ldots,\eta_\xi^N\}$; the index $\beta$ is not summed in this argument list.
The constitutive dependence on $\mbf F_\xi$ may be trivial for fluid phases,
but keeping it in the notation allows one calculation for all phases.  The local
free-energy variation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta\psi_\xi
	 & = \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta,\theta}:\delta\mbf F_\xi
	+ \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta,\theta}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
	+ \sum_\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta}
	\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:delta_psi_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod}, integrating the $\delta\mbf F_\xi$ term by parts,
and collecting independent variations gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\xi
	 & = \sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}\Bigg[
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma'_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
	+ \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}\delta\bar\rho_\xi \notag \\
	 & \qquad
		+ \sum_\alpha \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha}}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+ \sum_\alpha \psi_\xi\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\Bigg]\dv + \text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:deltaU_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The corresponding Cauchy effective stress is denoted with a single prime because
it is the fixed-intrinsic-density stress, analogous to a Terzaghi effective
stress.  The fixed-pressure Biot stress is denoted later with a double prime.
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\bs\sigma'_\xi
	\equiv \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}\mbf F_\xi^T .
	\label{eq:var_cauchy_def}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For fluid phases this term vanishes when $\psi_\xi$ has no dependence on
$\mbf F_\xi$.

\subsubsection{Variation of the interfacial energies}
\label{sec:var_gamma}
% ============================================================

Let $\mc I$ denote the set of oriented fluid interfaces.  Each
$(w,n)\in\mc I$ identifies a wetting phase $w$ and a non-wetting phase $n$.
The total fluid volume fraction and the fluid saturations are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi = \sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f,
	\qquad
	S_f = \frac{\phi_f}{\phi},
	\qquad
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f=1 .
	\label{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The saturations are used here only as ratios of the primitive volume-fraction
fields; the variations are still taken with respect to the primitive
$\phi_f$.  The notation $\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}$ denotes the fluid
saturation state subject to the constraint in
\eqref{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}.  Thus the energy may be written in
terms of any independent set of fluid saturations, with the remaining
saturation determined by the constraint; the derivation below keeps the
constraint explicit instead of choosing a reference fluid phase.   The
effective fluid interfacial energy density is a single constitutive potential
of this constrained saturation state
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma =
	\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right).
	\label{eq:interfacial_energy_density}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
and the corresponding contribution to the internal energy is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\gamma =
	\int_{\mc{B}}
	\phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right)
	\dv .
	\label{eq:interfacial_internal_energy}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

The Bedford--Drumheller spatial identity
(cf.\ \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical} equation (1.25)) gives, for each
fluid phase field $\phi_f$,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\delta\phi_\xi\right\vert_{\mbf{x}}
	= \delta\phi_\xi-\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi.
	\label{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\phi=\sum_{f\in\mc F}\delta\phi_f,
	\qquad
	\delta S_f=\frac{\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi}{\phi},
	\label{eq:saturation_variations}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
the fixed-point contribution and the transport correction from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation} are collected in one
calculation:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\gamma
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\left[
		\gamma\,\delta\phi
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
		\left(\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi\right)
	\right]\dv \notag                                          \\
	 & \quad
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv \notag \\
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\delta\phi_f\dv
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv .
	\label{eq:deltaU_fg}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The subscript $g$ denotes the set of all fluid phases excluding the varied phase
$f$; in this variation, the \(S_g\ne S_f\) are held fixed.
The phase interfacial potential appearing in \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma
	+\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
	-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g
	\left.\p{\gamma}{S_g}\right\vert_{S_{h\neq g}},
	\label{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where the saturation derivatives are evaluated on the saturation simplex and the same ``held fixed'' notation convention applies.  The
oriented wetting/non-wetting pairs specify capillary-pressure differences.  The wetting/non-wetting
interfacial difference associated with an oriented pair is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	p_{c,nw}\equiv\gamma_n-\gamma_w,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:pc_def}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The form \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} keeps the gradient terms generated by the scalar
transport of the phase-attached volume fractions.  These terms combine with the
volume-fraction and component-storage multiplier gradients in the phase momentum
balances after the volume-fraction Euler--Lagrange equations are used.

\subsubsection{Electric field}
\label{sec:electric_field_variation}

The electrical terms are conservative except for any constitutive dielectric
relaxation introduced later.  They enter the Hamilton action through the
phase-attributed electric enthalpies \(U_\xi^+\), while the charge bookkeeping
has already been included in the combined component-rate constraint
\eqref{eq:C45_combined_def}.  For each phase,
%
\begin{equation}
	U^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\int_{\mc B}\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\,\dv
	=
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\,\dV_\xi .
	\label{eq:phase_electric_enthalpy_pullback}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\omega_\xi^+\) is an electric enthalpy per unit current volume assigned
to phase \(\xi\), and
\(\mc X_\xi=\{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^\alpha,\theta\}\) denotes the
non-field state variables.  In the electroquasistatic specialization,
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi,
	\qquad
	\mbf d_\xi
	=
	-\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{\xi}}{\mbf E}
	\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:electric_field_definitions}
\end{equation}
%
The full variation contains a direct volume-fraction term, state-variable
dielectric terms, the potential-gradient term, and the mechanical pullback.  The
mechanical part is most transparent when it is computed first on the
phase-reference configuration.  Holding \(\varphi\), \(\phi_\xi\), and the
non-field state variables fixed during this mechanical variation gives
%
\begin{align}
	\delta U^{+}_{\xi}
	={} &
	\delta
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\mc X_\xi)\,\dV_\xi
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	\phi_\xi
	\left[
		\omega_\xi^+\delta J_\xi
		+
		J_\xi
		\left.
		\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\mbf E}
		\right\vert_{\mc X_\xi}
		\cdot\delta\mbf E
		\right]\dV_\xi
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi
	\left[
		\omega_\xi^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
		+
		\mbf d_\xi\cdot
		\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\right)^T
		\mbf E
		\right]\dV_\xi
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi
	\left[
		\omega_\xi^+\mbf I
		+
		\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi
		\right]:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dV_\xi
	\notag \\
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\left[
		\omega_\xi^+\mbf I
		+
		\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi
		\right]:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv .
	\label{eq:vp_electric_pullback_variation}
\end{align}
%
Thus \(U_\xi^+\) contributes \(\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi\) to the
volume-fraction coefficient, contributes
\(\phi_\xi\delta\omega_\xi^+\) at fixed \(\mbf E\) to the coefficients of the
non-field state variables, and contributes the Maxwell term in
\eqref{eq:vp_electric_pullback_variation} to the phase-motion coefficient.

After integrating the potential-gradient term by parts and including the free
charge potential term, the electrical contribution to the action is
%
\begin{align}
	-\delta\sum_\xi U^{+}_{\xi}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left(
	\varrho-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d
	\right)\delta\varphi\,\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi\,\dv
	-
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi\left.\delta\omega_\xi^+\right\vert_{\mbf E}\,\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv
	+
	\text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_mechanical_power}
\end{align}
%
The reversible electrical, or Maxwell, stress assigned to the phase pullback is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\phi_\xi\omega^{+}_{\xi}\mbf I
	+
	\phi_\xi\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the total reversible stress used in the phase momentum equation is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
	\equiv
	\bs\sigma_\xi'
	+
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{+}.
	\label{eq:vp_total_reversible_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Stationarity with respect to \(\varphi\) gives Gauss' law,
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d=\varrho .
	\label{eq:gauss_law}
\end{equation}
%
Combined with the local charge balance from \sref{conservation_of_charge}, the
electroquasistatic field equation implies
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
	\dot{\mbf d}
	+
	\mbf i
	\right)
	=0 .
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_compatibility}
\end{equation}

The factor multiplying \(\varphi\) in the electrochemical component potential is
\(z_\xi^\alpha\), the charge per unit component mass, so
\(z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\) has the units of a chemical potential.  The neutral
equations below are recovered by suppressing \(\omega_\xi^+\) and setting
\(\mu_\xi^\alpha=\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha\).

% ============================================================
\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_1$}{C1}: volume-fraction}
\label{sec:var_C1_app}
% ============================================================

The constraint $C_1$ is
imposed in the current configuration, so the natural variation is
taken at fixed spatial point $\mbf x$.  Since each $\phi_\xi$ is a
constituent-attached spatial field, \eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
expresses the variation in terms of the spatial variation $\delta\phi_\xi$ and
the motion variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_1
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\lambda \left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\delta\phi_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv.
	\label{eq:deltaC1_app}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The first term returns the volume-fraction constraint
$\sum_\xi\phi_\xi = 1$.  The remaining terms contribute $\lambda$ to each
$\delta\phi_\xi$ equation and $-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi$ to each
momentum variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_2$}{C2}: component material mass balance}
\label{sec:var_C2_app}
% ============================================================

The component material mass balance constraint is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	C_2 = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha} \right) \,dV_\xi.
	\label{eq:C2_component_measure}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The multiplier \(\pi_\xi^\alpha\) enforces the component material-mass balance
for component \(\alpha\) in phase \(\xi\).  Its variation gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_2
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha \delta J_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi^2\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta\phi_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi^2 \eta^\al_\xi}\,\delta\bar\rho_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi (\eta^\al_\xi)^2}\,\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	- \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}
	\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi.
	\notag                                        \\
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi \, \dv
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}}
	\left[
		\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}\delta\phi_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		- \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv
	+ \text{b.t.}
	\label{eq:deltaC2_lines}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here the second equality uses $\dv = J_\xi {\rm d}V_\xi$ and
$\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha
	=J_\xi\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha$. ``b.t.'' denotes boundary terms generated by integration by parts that are omitted here for brevity.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_3$}{C3}: mass partition within phase}
\label{sec:var_C3_app}
% ============================================================

%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_3
	 & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\Pi_\xi \left(\sum_\al \eta_\xi^\al - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \left(\delta\eta^\al_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\eta_\xi^\al\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv, \notag \\
	 & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\Pi_\xi \left(\sum_\al \eta_\xi^\al - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \delta\eta^\al_\xi \dv.
	\label{eq:deltaC3_app}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The last term in the first line vanishes by recognizing $\sum_\alpha \nabla_{\mbf{x}} \eta_\xi^\alpha = 0$ by \eqref{eq:mass_fraction_constraint}.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_4$}{C4}: material insertion}
\label{sec:component_rate_constraint_variations}
\label{sec:stoich_conversion_variations}
\label{sec:transport_accumulation_variations}

The Bedford--Drumheller transfer constraint contains the phase-attached
component material increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\).  Its rate variation must be
integrated by parts with the same phase material derivative before any
stationarity condition is used.  The material insertion part is varied through
the single transfer potential \(\tau\) because the kinematic constraint is the
summed material insertion constraint.  Varying the transfer potential, the
conversion material measure, and the phase map gives
%
\begin{align}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\delta C_4\,\dt
	={} &
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}
	\delta\tau
	\left[
		\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv\dt
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		-\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		+
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
		\right]\dv\dt
	+
	\text{b.t.}
	\label{eq:C4_variation}
\end{align}
%
The transfer-multiplier variation in \eqref{eq:C4_variation} enforces the
summed kinematic insertion constraint.  The conversion-measure variation gives
the evolution coefficient \(D_\xi\tau/Dt\), and the phase-map variation receives
the conversion-rate momentum coefficient \(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\).

Before collecting the stationarity conditions, recall the current-volume
component material-increment rate
%
\begin{align}
	\dot c_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}.
	\label{eq:current_volume_material_increment_rate}
\end{align}
%
The transfer potential is tied to the summed insertion constraint, so the
Bedford--Drumheller momentum supply depends on
\(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\) through the phase-map variation.

\subsubsection{Collected Hamilton--d'Alembert variation}
\label{sec:collected_var}
% ============================================================

The Hamilton--d'Alembert terms are now collected before any stationarity
equation is substituted back into another variation.  This collection contains
the reversible coordinate and constraint coefficients, including those from
\(\delta C_4\):
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
\small
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		0 ={} & \int_{t_1}^{t_2}
		\begin{aligned}[t]
			 & \left\{\vphantom{\sum_{\xi}}\right.
			\sum_{\xi} \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
				-\rho_{\xi}\mbf{a}_{\xi}
				+ \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_{\xi}^{\prime+} + \rho_{\xi}\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_{\xi}
				-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi
				-\sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\pi_\xi^\alpha
				+\sum_\alpha\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
				\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_\xi\right)
			\Bigg]\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_{\xi} \,\dv                     \\
			 & + \int_{\mc B}\Big[
				\sum_{f\in\mc F}
				\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f
			\Big]\dv                                                 \\
			 & + \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
				\lambda + \sum_\alpha \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\phi_{\xi}}
				-
				\omega_\xi^+
			\Bigg] \delta\phi_{\xi} \,\dv                            \\
			 & - \int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\delta\phi_f\dv \\
			 & + \sum_{\xi}\int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
				-\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf{F}_{\xi},\eta_\xi^\beta}
				+ \sum_\alpha\frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}
			\Bigg] \delta\bar\rho_{\xi} \,\dv                        \\
			 & + \sum_{\xi}\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
			\left[
				\frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}+\Pi_{\xi}
				-\rho_{\xi}
				\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta^\alpha_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta^{\beta\neq\alpha}_\xi}
				\right]
			\delta\eta^\alpha_\xi\,\dv                               \\
			 & + \int_{\mc B}
			\left(
			\varrho-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d
			\right)\delta\varphi\,\dv
			\\
			 & + \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}\left[
				\tfrac12\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi-\psi_\xi
				-L_\xi^\alpha
				-\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
				-\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
				\right]\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\,\dv
			\left.\vphantom{\sum_{\xi}}\right\}\dt
		\end{aligned}
		\\
		      & + \text{multiplier variations}
		+ \text{boundary terms}.  \label{eq:collected_full}
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The explicit \(\delta\varphi\) term in \eqref{eq:collected_full} is the
Hamilton electric-field variation and gives Gauss' law.  The multiplier
variations in \eqref{eq:collected_full} include the variations that return
\eqref{eq:C1_def}--\eqref{eq:C3_def}.  The transfer-multiplier variation is
%
\begin{align}
	 & \int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}
	\delta\tau
	\left[
		\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv\dt
	.
	\label{eq:transfer_multiplier_variation}
\end{align}
%
It returns the summed kinematic insertion constraint.  Since the displayed
Hamilton variations are arbitrary within their respective variation classes,
each bracketed integrand must vanish pointwise.

\subsubsection{Euler--Lagrange equations}

The Euler--Lagrange equations generated by arbitrary $\delta\phi_\xi$ give,
for phases without an explicit capillary contribution,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi_\xi \lambda = - \sum_\alpha \pi_\xi^\al .
	\label{eq:el_lambda}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For the fluid phases the corresponding equations contain the primitive capillary potentials discussed below.

Substituting \eqref{eq:el_lambda} into the Euler--Lagrange equations associated with arbitrary $\delta \mbf{x}_\xi$ and simplifying gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\mbf{a}_{\xi} =
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_{\xi}^{\prime+} + \rho_{\xi}\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_{\xi}
	+ \phi_{\xi} \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\lambda
	+\sum_\alpha\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_\xi\right),
	\label{eq:el_momentum_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
with the fluid-phase capillary-force additions displayed separately below.

The Euler--Lagrange equation associated with arbitrary $\delta\bar\rho_\xi$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\al \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\al}{\phi_{\xi}}
	= \bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}
	.
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_storage}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
We recognize the right-hand side as the thermodynamic phase pressure $\bar{p}_\xi$ and define
%
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{\bar{p}_\xi \equiv \bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\eta_\xi^\beta}}
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_all}
\end{equation}

The coefficient of $\delta\phi_f$ in \eqref{eq:collected_full} includes both the
primitive interfacial potential $\gamma_f$ and the electric enthalpy
\(\omega_f^+\), so the fluid-phase volume-fraction relation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\sum_\alpha\frac{\pi_f^\alpha}{\phi_f}-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0.
	\label{eq:fluid_volume_fraction_el_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\bar p_f-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0,
	\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
	\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^+-\lambda .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Consequently pressure differences between fluid phases contain both the
primitive interfacial-potential difference and the difference in electric
enthalpy assignments,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_f-\bar p_g
	=
	\gamma_f-\gamma_g
	+
	\omega_f^+-\omega_g^+ .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:pc_def}, the wetting/non-wetting relation is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	=
	p_{c,nw}
	+
	\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery}
\end{empheq}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
In the absence of ion exchange, reactions, or phase transformations,
\(\dot{\mc C}_f^\alpha/J_f=0\), and \eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} reduces to
the usual pressure-gradient form, with the electrical enthalpy correction
retained,
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	= \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
	+ \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f
	- \omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f,
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:fluid_momentum_no_conversion_electric}
\end{equation}
When electrical enthalpy is absent, the final term also vanishes.
%
For two fluid phases with $\mc F=\{w,n\}$ and one oriented pair $(w,n)$, this
reduces to the usual two-phase capillary pressure when electrical enthalpy is
absent or has the same phase assignment in the two fluids.  The pressure
difference in \eqref{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery} should
therefore be read as an electrocapillary pressure jump: \(p_{c,nw}\) remains the
intrinsic capillary contribution associated with the interfacial potential,
while \(\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+\) is a pressure-like electrical correction.  This
bookkeeping is consistent with electrowetting interface balances in which the
free energy contains surface, electrostatic, and ionic contributions and
stationarity of the interface position gives a pressure-difference balance
against capillary and Maxwell or electro-osmotic terms
\cite{yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation}.  If the interfacial energy is
parameterized by the wetting saturation $S_w$, then
$p_{c,nw}=-\partial\gamma/\partial S_w$ in the neutral limit.

The same relations identify the equivalent pore pressure associated with the
volume constraint.  Define
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_E\equiv-\lambda .
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Multiplying $\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^++\bar p_E$ by the fluid saturations
and summing over fluid phases gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\bar p_f
	=
	\bar p_E
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\omega_f^+ .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_pressure_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The primitive potentials satisfy
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_primitive_potential_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		\bar p_E
		=
		\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\left(\bar p_f-\omega_f^+\right)
		-
		\gamma
	}.
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For a phase with $N$ tracked components, the Euler--Lagrange equations
associated with arbitrary $\delta\eta_{\xi}^\alpha$ give
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	0 = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha} + \Pi_{\xi}
	- \rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}},
	\qquad \alpha=1,\ldots,N,
	\label{eq:el_eta_all}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
while variation with respect to $\Pi_{\xi}$ returns the phase mass fraction
constraint
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\alpha=1}^N \eta_{\xi}^\alpha = 1.
	\label{eq:el_eta_constraint}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Thus $\Pi_{\xi}$ is the phase-level multiplier that enforces the redundant
composition coordinate.  Equation~\eqref{eq:el_eta_all} may be read
componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}
	= \Pi_{\xi},
	\label{eq:Pi_componentwise}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
%
Choosing component $N$ as the reference and comparing against
$\beta=1,\ldots,N-1$ gives a system of $N-1$ equations,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\rho_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\beta}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\kappa\neq \beta}}
	- \left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\kappa\neq N}}
	\right)
	 & = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\beta}{\eta_{\xi}^\beta}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^N}{\eta_{\xi}^N},
	\qquad \beta=1,\ldots,N-1.
	\label{eq:pi_difference_system}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here $\kappa$ denotes the collection of composition components held fixed in the
partial derivative; it is not summed.

The equations associated with the conversion variables are
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	= \frac{1}{2}\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	-\psi_\xi
	-L_\xi^\alpha
	-\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}.
	\label{eq:el_conversion_component}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Equation \eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} is the conversion-measure
coefficient associated with the scalar transfer potential \(\tau\).  Because the
coefficient is indexed by component while \(\tau\) is not, it should be read as
a phase-level transfer-potential evolution relation together with component
compatibility conditions on the storage multipliers \(\pi_\xi^\alpha\).

At this stage $L_\xi^\alpha$ remains an explicit constitutive
component-transfer potential.  An admissible choice of $L_\xi^\alpha$ is identified
by the Coleman--Noll argument in
Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}.

\subsubsection{Fluid-phase momentum equations}
\label{sec:fluid_phase_momentum}

For any fluid phase $f\in\mc F$, the scalar-transport capillary contribution
gives the intermediate form
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\rho_f\mbf a_f
	 & = \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+} + \rho_f\mbf g + \mbf b_f
	\notag                                                                   \\
	 & \quad
	-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	-\sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf x}\pi_f^\alpha
	+\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	+\sum_\alpha\frac{\dot{\mc C}_f^\alpha}{J_f}
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right).
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_start}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using $\sum_\alpha\pi_f^\alpha=\phi_f\bar p_f$ and
$\lambda+\bar p_f-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0$, the multiplier,
interfacial-gradient, and electric-enthalpy terms collapse as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_f\bar p_f\right)
	+\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	=
	-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_f
	-
	\omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f .
	\label{eq:fluid_capillary_pressure_gradient_collapse}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The phase pressure-gradient form is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	= \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
	+ \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+}
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f
	- \omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	+\sum_\alpha\frac{\dot{\mc C}_f^\alpha}{J_f}
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right),
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_simplified}
\end{empheq}

\subsection{Onsager principle for dissipative reaction and transport}
\label{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}

The Hamilton--d'Alembert variation determines the reversible coordinate
equations at fixed endpoint variations.  Reactions and relative component
transport are determined separately by an Onsager principle: at a fixed current
thermodynamic state, the process rates for the immediate response are chosen by
stationarity of a power functional containing a dissipation potential and a
signed constraint power~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  This is a local
near-equilibrium statement for rates rather than a Hamilton principle over a
full trajectory.  The state variables that enter the potentials are held fixed
during this minimization, while
\(\dot r_{(m)}\), \(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\), and
\(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\) are varied.

The component-rate balance enters the Onsager principle as a Lagrange
constraint term.  Since the multiplier is the component potential, this term has
units of power, but its variational role is to enforce the rate constraint:
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc C_{\rm rt}^{\mu}
	\equiv
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\left[
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:component_rate_residual_def}
\end{equation}
%
The neutral chemical contribution is
\(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\) times the bracket in
\eqref{eq:component_rate_residual_def}.  The charge-rate identity in
\eqref{eq:component_charge_rate_identity} is obtained by multiplying the same
rate bracket by the component charge per unit mass \(z_\xi^\alpha\); its
electric work contribution is \(\varphi z_\xi^\alpha\) times that bracket.  The
two contributions therefore combine into the electrochemical component
potential
%
\begin{equation}
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi .
	\label{eq:C45_combined_def}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\varphi\) has units of energy per charge and \(z_\xi^\alpha\) has units
of charge per unit component mass, so \(z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\) has the same
units as \(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\).  In the Onsager minimization \(\varphi\) is a
state field inherited from the Hamilton electric-field problem; it shifts the
chemical force to the electrochemical force.
The constrained Onsager power functional for reaction and relative transport is
therefore the sum of the quadratic dissipation potential and the signed
constraint power:
%
\begin{equation}
	\mc P_{\rm Ons}
	=
	\Phi_{\rm rt}-\mc C_{\rm rt}^{\mu},
	\qquad
	\delta\mc P_{\rm Ons}=0 .
	\label{eq:onsager_stationarity}
\end{equation}
%
The constraint term \(\mc C_{\rm rt}^{\mu}\) is defined in
\eqref{eq:component_rate_residual_def}; the scalar dissipation potential
\(\Phi_{\rm rt}\) is specified in
\eqref{eq:onsager_quadratic_reaction_transport_potential}.  The potential and
the constraint term are minimized together with respect to the admissible rate
variables and the multiplier \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\).  With the sign convention in
\eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity}, the dissipative virtual power generated by
\(\Phi_{\rm rt}\) balances the electrochemical virtual power obtained from
\(\mc C_{\rm rt}^{\mu}\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate} defines
\(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\) in terms of the reaction and relative-flux
rates.  In the Onsager step the current state and the phase Jacobian \(J_\xi\)
are held fixed, so \(\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\) is not an additional
independent rate variation.  It remains in the \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\)-weighted
constraint, so variation of \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\) enforces the full component-rate
balance.  The virtual dissipative power generated by the scalar potential is
%
\begin{equation}
	\delta\mc P_{\rm rt}^{\rm diss}
	\equiv
	\delta\Phi_{\rm rt}
	= \int_{\mc B}\left[
		\sum_m \mc A_{(m)}\,\delta\dot r_{(m)}
		+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\cdot
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\cdot
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:reaction_transport_dissipation_variation}
\end{equation}
%
The corresponding first variation of the constraint term is
%
\begin{align}
	\delta\mc C_{\rm rt}^{\mu}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\delta\mu_\xi^\alpha
	\left[
		\frac{\dot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		-\sum_m\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\dot r_{(m)}
		+\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\left(\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha\right)
	\delta\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha\cdot
	\left(
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\dv ,
	\label{eq:Prt_variation}
\end{align}
%
where the flux terms have been integrated by parts in space and boundary power
terms are suppressed.  Since the current state is fixed in the Onsager step,
the rate variables and the Lagrange multiplier \(\mu_\xi^\alpha\) are the
quantities varied.  The multiplier variation enforces the component-rate
balance.  The mechanism-rate and flux variations give
%
\begin{align}
	\mc A_{(m)}
	 & =
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\mu_\xi^\alpha\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha,
	\label{eq:el_conversion_affinity}
	\\
	\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
	 & =\mbf 0,
	\label{eq:hamilton_dispersion_force_balance}
	\\
	\mbf M_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	+\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\alpha
	 & =\mbf 0.
	\label{eq:hamilton_diffusion_force_balance}
\end{align}
%
These equations are generalized force balances.  They are enforced only for
relative component fluxes whose component sum is zero, so adding a common
component potential to all components in a phase does not change the admissible
transport power.

A near-equilibrium quadratic choice for the scalar potential that generates
the virtual power in \eqref{eq:reaction_transport_dissipation_variation} is
%
\begin{align}
	\Phi_{\rm rt}
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\sum_\beta
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	\cdot\mbf R_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\sum_\beta
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\cdot\mbf R_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_m\frac{\dot r_{(m)}^2}{K_{(m)}}\dv,
	\label{eq:onsager_quadratic_reaction_transport_potential}
\end{align}
%
with \(K_{(m)}>0\) and symmetric positive semidefinite resistance tensors on the
relative-flux subspace.  This specialization gives
\(\mc A_{(m)}=\dot r_{(m)}/K_{(m)}\) and
\(\mbf M_{\xi,k}^\alpha
=\sum_\beta\mbf R_{\xi,k}^{\alpha\beta}\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\beta\) for
\(k\in\{{\rm disp},{\rm diff}\}\).  On the relative-flux subspace, define the
mobility tensors by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf D_{\xi,k}^{\alpha\beta}
	\equiv
	\left(\mbf R_{\xi,k}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta},
	\qquad
	k\in\{{\rm disp},{\rm diff}\}.
	\label{eq:onsager_mobility_tensor_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Substitution into the stationarity conditions yields the near-equilibrium rate
laws used below:
%
\begin{align}
	\dot r_{(m)}
	 & =
	K_{(m)}\mc A_{(m)},
	\label{eq:onsager_reaction_rate_variational}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_\beta
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\beta,
	\label{eq:onsager_dispersion_resistance_balance}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_\beta
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\mu_\xi^\beta.
	\label{eq:onsager_diffusion_resistance_balance}
\end{align}
%
The entropy inequality in Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}
then checks that these force--rate pairings are dissipative and records the
corresponding admissible constitutive closures.
