\section{Variational principle derivation}
\label{sec:variational}
\label{sec:virtual_power_derivation}

The reversible coordinates and dissipative process rates play different roles.
This section derives both without treating one as the other.  The
Hamilton--d'Alembert calculation follows the extended Hamilton principles used
by Bedford and Drumheller for porous and reacting immiscible mixtures
\cite{bedford1979variational,drumheller1980thermomechanical,bedford1983theories}
and determines the mixture coordinates and constraints.  The Onsager
calculation pairs reaction and relative-transport rates with their
thermodynamic forces and enforces nonnegative dissipation
\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  This is the continuum counterpart of
using virtual work for coordinate changes and virtual power for process rates
\cite{germain1973virtualpower,gurtin2010continua}.  Keeping the two calculations
separate preserves the Bedford--Drumheller mixture structure and displays each
force--rate pair.

The formulation applies to any specified collection of solid $\mc S$ and fluid
$\mc F$ phases.  Here fluid means a nonsolid pore
phase, such as an aqueous, oleic, or gas phase.  The symbol $s \in \mc{S}$
denotes a solid phase, $f \in\mc F$ denotes a fluid phase, and
$\xi \in \mc{S} \cup \mc{F}$ ranges over all phases.  Components within each
phase are indexed by $\alpha$.

\subsection{Hamilton--d'Alembert principle for reversible coordinates}

Let \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) be the endpoint times and let \(\delta\) denote an
admissible virtual variation that vanishes at those endpoints.  The
Hamilton--d'Alembert statement contains the kinetic and stored-energy variation,
mechanical and electrical source work, the volume and composition constraints,
and the material insertion constraint:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(T-U\right)\dt
	+
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
	\left(\delta W+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\delta C_k\right)\dt
	= 0.
	\label{eq:vp_principle}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
In the virtual-work term below, \(\mbf g\) is the body force per unit phase mass
and \(\mbf b_\xi\) is the force density exerted on phase \(\xi\) through
interaction with the other phases.  The individual interaction forces may
include dissipative drag, but they transfer momentum only within the mixture.
They therefore satisfy the global balance
%
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\xi\mbf b_\xi=\mbf 0.
	\label{eq:interphase_force_global_balance}
\end{equation}
%
The energies, virtual work, and four constraints are defined together by
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begingroup
\small
\begin{align}
	T        & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}}\frac{1}{2} \rho_\xi \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \dv,
	\label{eq:T_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	U        & = \sum_{\xi}\int_{\mc{B}} \rho_{\xi}\,\psi_{\xi}(\mbf{F}_{\xi}, \bar\rho_{\xi}, \{\eta^\al_{\xi}\},\theta_\xi)\dv
	+ \int_{\mc{B}} \phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F}\right) \dv
	+\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi)
	\,\dv,
	\label{eq:U_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	\delta W & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}}
	\left(\rho_\xi\mbf{g}+\mbf b_\xi\right)
	\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi \,\dv
	\notag \\
	&\quad
	+\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\,\dv
	-\int_{\mc B}\varrho\,\delta\varphi\,\dv
	+\int_{\Gamma_q}\bar q\,\delta\varphi\,{\rm d}a,
	\label{eq:W_def}                                                                                                                                                                                               \\
	C_1      & = \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \,\dv,
	\label{eq:C1_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_2      & = \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi 0}} \pi_\xi^\al \left( J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi 0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} \bar\rho_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \eta^\al_\xi \bar\rho_\xi} \right) \dV_\xi,
	\label{eq:C2_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_3      & = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \Pi_\xi \left( \sum_\alpha \eta_\xi^\alpha - 1 \right) \dv,
	\label{eq:C3_def}                                                                                                                                                                                              \\
	C_4      & = \int_{\mc B}\tau
	\left(
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\frac{\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
	\right)\dv .
	\label{eq:C4_def}
\end{align}
\endgroup
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here \(L_\xi^\alpha\) is the component generalized transfer-work coefficient.
The full d'Alembert coefficient conjugate to conversion insertion is
\(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha\).  This coefficient is held fixed during the virtual
material increment.  Consequently its \(\psi_\xi\) contribution cancels the
insertion term generated by \(-\delta U_\xi\), leaving
\(+L_\xi^\alpha\) in the component-increment Euler equation.  This is the
conservative-action counterpart of the positive generalized-force term in the
Bedford--Drumheller conversion virtual work and transfer-potential equation
\cite[eqs.~(2.2) and (2.8)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}.
The electrical boundary is partitioned into
\(\Gamma_\varphi\), where \(\varphi\) is prescribed and
\(\delta\varphi=0\), and \(\Gamma_q\), where \(\bar q\) is the prescribed
outward free surface-charge density.  The electrical terms in
\(\delta W\) define a free-charge source-work ensemble: \(\varrho\) and
\(\bar q\) are generalized-load coefficients conjugate only to
\(\delta\varphi\), not additional stored-energy terms.  The component-carried
part of \(\varrho\) is the component sum in
\eqref{eq:mixture_charge_density_definition}; the same equation adds the
prescribed \(\varrho_{\rm ext}\), whose current and supply obey
\eqref{eq:external_charge_loading_balance}.  As for the body-force coefficients
in \(\delta W\), these source coefficients and prescribed loading data are not
varied again with respect to the material coordinates.

The volume-source expression in \eqref{eq:W_def} is supplemented by mechanical
surface work.  For each phase map, let
\(\partial\mc B=\overline{\Gamma_{x,\xi}}\cup
\overline{\Gamma_{t,\xi}}\), with disjoint interiors,
\(\mbf x_\xi\) prescribed and \(\delta\mbf x_\xi=\mbf0\) on
\(\Gamma_{x,\xi}\), and prescribed outward traction
\(\bar{\mbf t}_\xi\) on \(\Gamma_{t,\xi}\).  The added virtual work is
%
\begin{equation}
	\left.
	\delta W
	\right\vert_{\partial\mc B,{\rm mech}}
	=
	\sum_\xi
	\int_{\Gamma_{t,\xi}}
	\bar{\mbf t}_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi\,{\rm d}a .
	\label{eq:mechanical_boundary_virtual_work}
\end{equation}
%
This surface term does not alter any bulk stationarity equation.

This choice assigns the material electrical force to the Maxwell stress
generated by the phase electric enthalpy.  Charge transport and conversion
enter separately through the charge-weighted component-rate identity and the
electrochemical state coefficients in the Onsager principle; nonisothermal
transport keeps the neutral-potential and electric-field forces separate.
Thus neither the
electrical body force nor the component insertion work is counted a second
time through a material variation of \(\varrho\varphi\).  The phase-assigned
temperature is
\(\theta_\xi=\theta_{\mc F}\) for \(f\in\mc F\) and
\(\theta_\xi=\theta_{\mc S}\) for \(s\in\mc S\).  Constraint \(C_1\)
enforces the volume-fraction constraint, \(C_2\) enforces the augmented component
material measure, and \(C_3\) enforces mass-fraction normalization.

Constraint \(C_4\) enforces zero net material insertion over all phases and
components.  Writing the rate of component mass addition per current mixture
volume as
\(\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha=\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\), this constraint imposes
\(\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha=0\).  Internal conversion satisfies the
constraint through stoichiometric mass conservation, while relative component
transport satisfies it phase by phase through
\eqref{eq:averaged_relative_flux_sum}.  The component-resolved rate balance is
imposed later in the Onsager subsection.

The transfer multiplier \(\tau\) is the Bedford--Drumheller kinematic potential
conjugate to the globally conservative accumulated material insertion.  It has
units of action per unit mass, so
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\) has velocity units and
\(D_\xi\tau/Dt\) has units of energy per unit mass.  Thus \(\tau\) supplies the
conversion transfer-momentum coefficient in the reversible variation.

The Hamilton--d'Alembert variations are taken at fixed thermal state,
\(\delta\theta_{\mc F}=\delta\theta_{\mc S}=0\), while the energies retain
their constitutive dependence on the corresponding temperature.  The physical
rates \(\dot\theta_{\mc F}\) and \(\dot\theta_{\mc S}\) enter later in the
Coleman--Noll exploitation of the entropy inequality.
The fields $\mbf x_\xi$, $\eta_\xi^\alpha$, $\phi_\xi$, $\bar\rho_\xi$, and
$\mc C_\xi^\alpha$ are varied independently.  The mechanism rates and the two
relative fluxes are varied later as independent process rates through the
constrained Onsager entropy-rate functional in
Section~\ref{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}.

\subsubsection{Extending Bedford and Drumheller transport identity}
\label{sec:extended_bd_identity}

Bedford and Drumheller~\cite[eq.~(1.28)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
give an identity for the variation of a constituent-attached spatial field
$\Gamma_\xi$ weighted by the current phase density $\rho_\xi$.  The same
transport argument extends componentwise to the compositional setting used here.

The component material increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) is attached to the
phase-\(\xi\) reference coordinates.  It is the added component mass appearing
in \eqref{eq:augmented_component_material_measure}.  In the reversible
Hamilton--d'Alembert variation, its componentwise virtual increment is therefore
carried with the phase-attached material measure.
%
For any phase field $\Gamma_\xi$, the Bedford--Drumheller identity extends componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock:  do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho^\alpha_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \delta \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi0}+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\right)
	\Gamma_\xi \,\dVxi \notag        \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\left[
		\delta\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha\Gamma_\xi
		+ J_\xi\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
	\right]\dVxi \notag              \\
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}}
	\left[
		\rho^\alpha_\xi\delta\Gamma_\xi
		+ \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}\Gamma_\xi
		\right]\dv .
	\label{eq:bd128_mod2}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Summing both sides of \eqref{eq:bd128_mod2} over $\alpha$ gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \left[\rho_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi + \sum_\alpha \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \Gamma_\xi  \right]  \dv,
	\label{eq:bd128_mod}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where $\rho_\xi = \sum_\alpha \rho^\alpha_\xi$.

% ============================================================
\subsubsection{Variation of the kinetic energy}
\label{sec:var_T_app}
% ============================================================

The variation of kinetic energy using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod} is then
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta T = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi\,\mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{v}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \dv.
	\label{eq:kinetic_energy_variation_before_time_integration}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Since $\delta\mbf{v}_\xi = \mathrm{d}(\delta\mbf{x}_\xi)/\mathrm{d}t$ and $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_1} = \delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_2} = \mbf{0}$, integration by parts in time gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \delta T \dt
	= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left[-\rho_\xi\,\mbf{a}_\xi - \sum_\al \phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha \mbf{v}_\xi \right] \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}  \dv \dt.
	\label{eq:deltaT_app}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
\subsubsection{Variation of the phase free energies}
\label{sec:var_Uxi}
% ============================================================

For every phase, solid or fluid, let $\psi_\xi$ denote the nonelectrical
specific Helmholtz free energy and let \(N\) be the number of tracked components
in that phase.  Its contribution to the stored thermodynamic potential is
written
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\xi
	=
	\sum_\xi \int_{\mc B}
	\rho_\xi
	\psi_\xi(\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\})\,\dv.
	\label{eq:U_xi_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Here \(\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}\) denotes the indexed collection of all component
mass fractions for phase \(\xi\).
The constitutive dependence on $\mbf F_\xi$ may be trivial for fluid phases,
but keeping it in the notation allows one calculation for all phases.  The local
free-energy variation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta\psi_\xi
	 & = \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}:\delta\mbf F_\xi
	+ \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
	+ \sum_\alpha
	\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha .
	\label{eq:delta_psi_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod}, integrating the $\delta\mbf F_\xi$ term by parts,
and collecting independent variations gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\xi
	 & = \sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}\left[
		-\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma'_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
	+ \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\bar\rho_\xi}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		\right. \notag \\
	 & \qquad \left.
		+ \sum_\alpha \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha}}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+ \sum_\alpha \psi_\xi\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv + \text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:deltaU_uniform}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The corresponding Cauchy effective stress is denoted with a single prime because
it is the fixed-intrinsic-density stress, analogous to a Terzaghi effective
stress.  The fixed-pressure Biot stress is denoted later with a double prime.
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\bs\sigma'_\xi
	\equiv \rho_\xi\left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\mbf F_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}\mbf F_\xi^T .
	\label{eq:var_cauchy_def}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For fluid phases, \(\psi_f\) may be independent of \(\mbf F_f\), giving
\(\bs\sigma_f'=\mbf 0\).

\subsubsection{Variation of the interfacial energies}
\label{sec:var_gamma}
% ============================================================

Let $\mc I$ denote the set of oriented fluid interfaces.  Each
$(w,n)\in\mc I$ identifies a wetting phase $w$ and a non-wetting phase $n$.
The total fluid volume fraction and the fluid saturations are
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi = \sum_{f\in\mc F}\phi_f,
	\qquad
	S_f = \frac{\phi_f}{\phi},
	\qquad
	\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f=1 .
	\label{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
The saturations are used here only as ratios of the primitive volume-fraction
fields; the variations are still taken with respect to the primitive
$\phi_f$.  The notation $\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}$ denotes the fluid
saturation state subject to the constraint in
\eqref{eq:fluid_saturation_definitions}.  Thus the energy may be written in
terms of any independent set of fluid saturations, with the remaining
saturation determined by the constraint.  The derivation below keeps this
sum constraint explicit, so all fluid phases are treated symmetrically.  The
effective interfacial Helmholtz free-energy density, normalized per current
fluid volume, is a single constitutive potential of this constrained saturation
state
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma =
	\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right).
	\label{eq:interfacial_energy_density}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
Its contribution to the stored thermodynamic potential per current mixture
volume is \(\phi\gamma\), so
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U_\gamma =
	\int_{\mc{B}}
	\phi\,\gamma\left(\left\{S_f\right\}_{f\in\mc F}\right)
	\dv .
	\label{eq:interfacial_internal_energy}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

The fluid-temperature argument shown in the action is held fixed during the present
mechanical variation and is suppressed in
\eqref{eq:interfacial_energy_density}--\eqref{eq:interfacial_internal_energy}.
The adopted interfacial potential
\(\gamma=\gamma(\{S_f\}_{f\in\mc F},\theta_{\mc F})\) makes capillary mechanics enter
through the primitive fluid volume fractions and their saturation ratios.
Every saturation partial derivative written below is evaluated by choosing any
\(C^2\) extension of \(\gamma\) from states satisfying
\(\sum_fS_f=1\) to nearby independent saturation coordinates.  The indicated
partial derivative is taken in that extension and then evaluated at a physical
saturation state.  Holding the other saturation coordinates fixed is a
calculus convention for this derivative; an actual saturation change must
preserve their sum.  Two extensions that agree for every physical saturation
state have gradients there that differ by the same scalar in every component
direction.  The combination defining \(\gamma_f\), every difference
\(\gamma_f-\gamma_g\), and the weighted sum
\(\sum_fS_f\gamma_f\) are independent of the chosen extension.

The Bedford--Drumheller spatial identity
(cf.\ \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical} equation (1.25)) gives, for each
fluid phase field $\phi_f$,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\left.\delta\phi_\xi\right\vert_{\mbf{x}}
	= \delta\phi_\xi-\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi.
	\label{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\delta\phi=\sum_{f\in\mc F}\delta\phi_f,
	\qquad
	\delta S_f=\frac{\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi}{\phi},
	\label{eq:saturation_variations}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
the fixed-point contribution and the transport correction from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation} are collected in one
calculation:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta U_\gamma
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\left[
		\gamma\,\delta\phi
		+\sum_{f\in\mc F}
		\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
		\left(\delta\phi_f-S_f\delta\phi\right)
	\right]\dv \notag                                          \\
	 & \quad
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}
	\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv \notag \\
	 & = \int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\delta\phi_f\dv
	-\int_{\mc B}\sum_{f\in\mc F}\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f\cdot\delta\mbf x_f\dv .
	\label{eq:deltaU_fg}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The subscript \(g\) denotes the set of all fluid phases excluding \(f\).
The held-fixed notation refers to the extension just described.  The physical
variations generated by \eqref{eq:saturation_variations} still satisfy
\(\sum_f\delta S_f=0\).
The phase interfacial potential appearing in \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma
	+\left.\p{\gamma}{S_f}\right\vert_{S_{g\neq f}}
	-\sum_{g\in\mc F}S_g
	\left.\p{\gamma}{S_g}\right\vert_{S_{h\neq g}},
	\label{eq:primitive_gamma_derivatives}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
where the extended saturation derivatives are evaluated at a physical
saturation state.  A common shift of those derivatives cancels from the displayed
combination, which proves its extension invariance.  The
oriented wetting/non-wetting pairs specify capillary-pressure differences.  The wetting/non-wetting
interfacial difference associated with an oriented pair is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	p_{c,nw}\equiv\gamma_n-\gamma_w,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:pc_def}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The same pressure can be read directly as the change in interfacial energy when
an increment of saturation is transferred from the wetting phase to the
non-wetting phase,
%
\begin{equation}
	p_{c,nw}
	=
	\left.
	\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\epsilon}
	\gamma\left(
	\ldots,S_w-\epsilon,\ldots,S_n+\epsilon,\ldots,
	\theta_{\mc F}
	\right)
	\right\vert_{\epsilon=0},
	\qquad
	(w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:capillary_pressure_simplex_directional_derivative}
\end{equation}
%
The form \eqref{eq:deltaU_fg} keeps the gradient terms generated by the scalar
transport of the phase-attached volume fractions.  These terms combine with the
volume-fraction and component-storage multiplier gradients in the phase momentum
balances after the volume-fraction Euler--Lagrange equations are used.
The thermodynamic balance in \sref{MC_coleman_noll_procedure} evaluates the
volume-fraction rates in a control volume following the common solid skeleton,
whereas the present coordinate variations remain phase-specific.  Both
operations differentiate the same primitive volume-fraction fields, so the
conjugate interfacial coefficient remains \(\gamma_f\).

Because \(\gamma\) depends only on the instantaneous saturations, subject to
their sum constraint, and the fluid temperature, the resulting capillary
pressure is an algebraic
local-equilibrium closure.  Dynamic capillary pressure, drainage--imbibition
hysteresis, and independent interfacial-area evolution are outside the present
state set; retaining any of them requires additional state variables and
kinetic or history-dependent constitutive equations.

\subsubsection{Variation of the phase electric enthalpy}
\label{sec:electric_field_variation}

The electrical contribution to the Hamilton action is assigned phase by phase
through \(U_\xi^+\).  The phase electric-enthalpy density
\(\omega_\xi^+=\omega_\xi^+(\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,
\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi)\) is normalized per current phase volume;
\(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\) is therefore its contribution per current mixture
volume.  Its arguments are the electric field, intrinsic phase density,
composition, and temperature.  For each phase,
%
\begin{equation}
	U \supset U^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+
	\,\dv
	=
	\int_{\mc B_{\xi0}}
	J_\xi\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+
	\,\dV_\xi .
	\label{eq:phase_electric_enthalpy_pullback}
\end{equation}
%
Let \(\varphi\) denote the electrostatic potential.  In the electroquasistatic
specialization, the electric field and the phase and mixture electric
displacements are
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi,
	\qquad
	\mbf d_\xi
	=
	-\left.
	\p{\omega^{+}_{\xi}}{\mbf E}
	\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi},
	\qquad
	\mbf d=\sum_\xi\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi .
	\label{eq:electric_field_definitions}
\end{equation}
%
Thus \(\mbf E\) is the independent electrical variable, while
\(\mbf d_\xi\) is derived from \(\omega_\xi^+\), consistently with
electroelastic potential formulations
\cite{dorfmann2017nonlinear_electroelasticity}.  The objective single-vector
electrical specialization and its material-symmetry limitation are stated in
\sref{technical_setting}.

At fixed \(\theta_{\mc F}\) and \(\theta_{\mc S}\), the full variation contains a direct volume-fraction
term, the density and composition variations, the potential-gradient term, and
the geometric mechanical pullback.  With \(\otimes\) denoting the tensor outer
product, these contributions give the following current-space form.  The
volume-fraction variation is the phase-attached variation from
\eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}, left here as
\(\delta\phi_\xi\) so its direct coefficient remains visible.  The resulting
variation is
%
\begin{align}
	\delta U^{+}_{\xi}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left[
		\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi
		+
		\phi_\xi
		\left.
		\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_\xi}
		\right\vert_{\mbf E,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}
		\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left[
		\phi_\xi
		\sum_\alpha
		\left.
		\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,
			\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
		\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		+
		\phi_\xi\mbf d_\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\varphi
		\right]\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{\mc B}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^+:
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv .
	\label{eq:vp_electric_full_variation}
\end{align}
%
After integrating the potential-gradient and phase-map-gradient terms by parts
and adding the electrical source work already displayed in
\eqref{eq:W_def}, \eqref{eq:vp_electric_full_variation} gives the electrical
part of the Hamilton--d'Alembert statement,
%
\begin{align}
	\left(
	-\delta\sum_\xi U^{+}_{\xi}
	+
	\delta W
	\right)_{\rm electrical}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d-\varrho
	\right)\delta\varphi\,\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\omega_\xi^+\delta\phi_\xi\,\dv
	-
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\left.
	\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_\xi}
	\right\vert_{\mbf E,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}
	\delta\bar\rho_\xi\,\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & -
	\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc B}
	\phi_\xi
	\left.
	\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
	\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,
		\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
	\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha\,\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\sum_\xi\int_{\mc B}
	\left(
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	\right)\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi\,\dv
	\notag \\
	    & +
	\int_{\Gamma_q}
	\left(
	\bar q-\mbf d\cdot\mbf n
	\right)
	\delta\varphi\,{\rm d}a
	+
	\text{mechanical boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_mechanical_power}
\end{align}
%
The mechanical coefficient in \eqref{eq:vp_electric_full_variation} defines the
reversible electrical stress assigned to the phase pullback.  Variation of the
current volume element and convection of the spatial electric field produce the
phase-attributed Maxwell stress, consistently with
electric-enthalpy virtual-work derivations
\cite{mcmeeking2007virtual_work_electrostatic}.  We denote this Maxwell stress
by \(\bs\sigma_\xi^+\),
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs{\sigma}^{+}_{\xi}
	=
	\phi_\xi
	\left(
	\omega_\xi^+\mbf I
	+
	\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_\xi
	\right).
	\label{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}
\end{equation}
%
Thus the total reversible stress used in the phase momentum equation is
%
\begin{equation}
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
	\equiv
	\bs\sigma_\xi'
	+
	\bs\sigma_\xi^{+}.
	\label{eq:vp_total_reversible_stress_definition}
\end{equation}

Combining the material, electrical, and component-mass-constraint boundary
terms with \eqref{eq:mechanical_boundary_virtual_work} gives the phase natural
traction
%
\begin{equation}
	\left[
		\bs\sigma_\xi^{\prime+}
		-
		\left(
			\sum_\alpha\pi_\xi^\alpha
		\right)\mbf I
	\right]\mbf n
	=
	\bar{\mbf t}_\xi
	\qquad
	\text{on }\Gamma_{t,\xi}.
	\label{eq:phase_natural_traction}
\end{equation}
%
After the solid tractions are summed on the common skeleton and the fluid
momentum equations are rolled into the overall balance, the corresponding
traction is
%
\begin{equation}
	\left[
		\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}
		-
		B\bar p_E\mbf I
		+
		\mbf E\otimes\mbf d
	\right]\mbf n
	=
	\bar{\mbf t}
	\qquad
	\text{on }\Gamma_t .
	\label{eq:overall_natural_traction}
\end{equation}
%
The thermocapillary and conversion terms in
\eqref{eq:MC_overall_momentum_nonlinear_biot} remain bulk sources; they do not
modify \eqref{eq:overall_natural_traction} unless an additional external-surface
model is introduced.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_1$}{C1}: volume-fraction}
\label{sec:var_C1_app}
% ============================================================

The constraint $C_1$ is
imposed in the current configuration, so the natural variation is
taken at fixed spatial point $\mbf x$.  Since each $\phi_\xi$ is a
constituent-attached spatial field, \eqref{eq:transported_volume_fraction_variation}
expresses the variation in terms of the spatial variation $\delta\phi_\xi$ and
the motion variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$:
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_1
	 & = \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\lambda \left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \dv
	+ \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\delta\phi_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv.
	\label{eq:deltaC1_app}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The first term returns the volume-fraction constraint
$\sum_\xi\phi_\xi = 1$.  The remaining terms contribute $\lambda$ to each
$\delta\phi_\xi$ equation and $-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_\xi$ to each
momentum variation $\delta\mbf x_\xi$.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_2$}{C2}: component material mass balance}

The component material mass balance constraint is

	C_2 = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha} \right) \,dV_\xi.
	\label{eq:C2_component_measure}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The multiplier \(\pi_\xi^\alpha\) enforces the component material-mass balance
for component \(\alpha\) in phase \(\xi\).  Its variation gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	\delta C_2
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha \delta J_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi^2\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta\phi_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi^2 \eta^\al_\xi}\,\delta\bar\rho_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\pi_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi (\eta^\al_\xi)^2}\,\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	- \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}
	\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi.
	\notag                                        \\
	 & = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
	\delta\pi_\xi^\alpha
	\left(J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha + \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right)
	\,{\rm d}V_\xi
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	-\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\pi_\xi^\alpha \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi \, \dv
	\notag                                        \\
	 & \quad
	+ \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}}
	\left[
		\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi}\delta\phi_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi
		+ \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha
		- \frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}
		\frac{\delta \mc{C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv
	+ \text{b.t.}
	\label{eq:deltaC2_lines}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Here the second equality uses $\dv = J_\xi {\rm d}V_\xi$ and
$\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}\eta_{\xi0}^\alpha+\mc{C}_\xi^\alpha
	=J_\xi\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha$. ``b.t.'' denotes boundary terms generated by integration-by-parts that are omitted here for brevity.

\subsubsection{Variation of constraint \texorpdfstring{$C_4$}{C4}: material insertion}
\label{sec:component_rate_constraint_variations}
\label{sec:stoich_conversion_variations}
\label{sec:transport_accumulation_variations}

The Bedford--Drumheller transfer constraint contains the component material
increment \(\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) carried by phase \(\xi\).  Its rate variation must
be integrated-by-parts with the same phase material derivative before any
stationarity condition is used.  A single transfer potential \(\tau\) enforces
the total insertion-rate constraint.  Varying
\(\tau\), the material measure, and the phase map gives
%
\begin{align}
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\delta C_4\,\dt
	={} &
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}
	\delta\tau
	\left[
		\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
		\frac{\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\right]\dv\dt
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_{\mc B}\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha
	\left[
		-\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
		\frac{\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		+
		\frac{\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\cdot\delta\mbf x_\xi
		\right]\dv\dt
	+
	\text{b.t.}
	\label{eq:C4_variation}
\end{align}
%
Varying \(\tau\) in \eqref{eq:C4_variation} enforces the zero-sum insertion
rate.  Varying the material measure gives the coefficient \(D_\xi\tau/Dt\), and
varying the phase map contributes the momentum coefficient
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\).

Before collecting the stationarity conditions, recall that the rate of
component mass addition per current mixture volume is
\(\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha=\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\), which will be used in the
collected equations below.  Because \(\tau\) enforces the summed insertion rate,
the phase-map variation produces a momentum term proportional to
\(\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau\).
Under the source-work ensemble specified after \eqref{eq:W_def}, free charge
contributes to the coefficient of \(\delta\varphi\) but is not re-varied with
the phase maps, material increments, or independent thermodynamic fields.  The
corresponding mechanical and rate-level electrical couplings are already
carried by the Maxwell stress and the gauge-invariant chemical and electric
transport forces, respectively.

\subsection{Euler--Lagrange equations}
\label{sec:euler_lagrange_equations}

Setting the coefficients in \eqref{eq:collected_full} to zero first gives
algebraic relations for the storage and constraint multipliers.  Substitution
of these relations into the remaining stationarity equations exposes the phase
momentum balances and the electrostatic field equation.

\subsubsection{Storage and multiplier relations}
\label{sec:el_storage_multiplier_relations}

The intrinsic-density variation \(\delta\bar\rho_\xi\) gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_\al \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\al}{\phi_{\xi}}
	= \bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}
	+
	\bar\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}
	.
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_storage}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
We recognize the first term on the right-hand side as the thermodynamic phase
pressure contribution.  The second term is present when the phase electric
enthalpy depends on intrinsic density.  We define the phase pressure as
%
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\bar{p}_\xi \equiv
	\bar\rho^2_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\}}
		+
		\bar\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\bar\rho_{\xi}}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\{\eta_\xi^\alpha\},\theta_\xi}
		}.
	\label{eq:phase_pressure_all}
\end{equation}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} therefore identifies the total phase
pressure as the usual thermodynamic pressure plus the dielectric pressure
correction induced by the intrinsic-density dependence of the phase electric
enthalpy.

For phases without an explicit capillary contribution, the
phase-volume-fraction variation \(\delta\phi_\xi\) gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\phi_\xi \lambda
	=
	-\sum_\alpha \pi_\xi^\al
	+
	\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+ .
	\label{eq:el_lambda}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
This relation is used to simplify the phase momentum equations below and, for
fluid phases, to eliminate the volume-constraint multiplier in favor of phase
pressure and interfacial terms.  The fluid-phase variation also contains the
primitive capillary potential.

The next relations are consequences of the fluid-phase volume-fraction
Euler--Lagrange equation and this phase-pressure definition.
Returning to the fluid-phase volume-fraction variation, its coefficient in
\eqref{eq:collected_full} contains both the primitive interfacial potential
\(\gamma_f\) and the electric enthalpy \(\omega_f^+\).  The corresponding
Euler--Lagrange equation is
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\sum_\alpha\frac{\pi_f^\alpha}{\phi_f}-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0.
	\label{eq:fluid_volume_fraction_el_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:phase_pressure_storage} and
\eqref{eq:phase_pressure_all} gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\lambda+\bar p_f-\gamma_f-\omega_f^+=0,
	\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
	\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^+-\lambda .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Consequently pressure differences between fluid phases contain both the
primitive interfacial-potential difference and the difference in electric
enthalpy assignments,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_f-\bar p_g
	=
	\gamma_f-\gamma_g
	+
	\omega_f^+-\omega_g^+ .
	\label{eq:fluid_pressure_difference_primitive_potential}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \eqref{eq:pc_def}, the wetting/non-wetting relation is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
	\bar p_n-\bar p_w
	=
	p_{c,nw}
	+
	\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+,
	\qquad (w,n)\in\mc I .
	\label{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery}
\end{empheq}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
For two fluid phases with $\mc F=\{w,n\}$ and one oriented pair $(w,n)$,
\eqref{eq:wetting_nonwetting_capillary_pressure_recovery} defines the
electrocapillary pressure jump: \(p_{c,nw}\) is the intrinsic capillary
contribution associated with the interfacial potential, and
\(\omega_n^+-\omega_w^+\) is the pressure-like electrical contribution.  This
relation is consistent with electrowetting interface balances in which the
free energy contains surface, electrostatic, and ionic contributions and
stationarity of the interface position gives a pressure-difference balance
against capillary and Maxwell or electro-osmotic terms
\cite{papageorgiou2019film_flows_electric_fields,yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation}.
The same phase-attributed electrical enthalpy enters the phase momentum
balances, reversible stresses, and thermodynamic restrictions.
If the interfacial energy is parameterized by the wetting saturation $S_w$, then
\(p_{c,nw}=-{\rm d}\gamma(S_w,1-S_w,\theta_{\mc F})/{\rm d}S_w\) at fixed
\(\theta_{\mc F}\) in the neutral limit.

The same relations identify the equivalent pore pressure associated with the
volume constraint.  Define
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\bar p_E\equiv-\lambda .
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_definition}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Multiplying $\bar p_f=\gamma_f+\omega_f^++\bar p_E$ by the fluid saturations
and summing over fluid phases gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\bar p_f
	=
	\bar p_E
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	+
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\omega_f^+ .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_pressure_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The primitive potentials satisfy
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\gamma_f
	=
	\gamma .
	\label{eq:saturation_weighted_primitive_potential_sum}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		\bar p_E
		=
		\sum_{f\in\mc F} S_f\left(\bar p_f-\omega_f^+\right)
		-
		\gamma
	}.
	\label{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
When \(\omega_f^+=0\), equation
\eqref{eq:equivalent_pore_pressure_capillary_relation} reduces to the
equivalent pore pressure used in thermodynamically stable coupled multiphase
poromechanics \cite{coussy2004poromechanics,kim2013rigorous}.  The term
\(-\sum_{f\in\mc F}S_f\omega_f^+\) is the present electrical extension,
obtained from the phase-pressure relation above rather than attributed to
either reference.

The remaining independent variations determine the composition and
component-transfer relations.
For a phase with \(N\) tracked components, the composition variations
\(\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha\) give
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	0 = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha} + \Pi_{\xi}
	- \rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	- \phi_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi},
	\qquad \alpha=1,\ldots,N,
	\label{eq:el_eta_all}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
while the multiplier variation \(\delta\Pi_\xi\) returns the phase
mass-fraction constraint
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\sum_{\alpha=1}^N \eta_{\xi}^\alpha = 1.
	\label{eq:el_eta_constraint}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Thus $\Pi_{\xi}$ is the phase-level multiplier that enforces the redundant
composition coordinate.  Equation~\eqref{eq:el_eta_all} may be read
componentwise as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq\alpha}}
	+
	\phi_{\xi}\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}
	= \Pi_{\xi},
	\label{eq:Pi_componentwise}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The first term in \eqref{eq:Pi_componentwise} is the usual chemical or mixing
contribution to composition stationarity.  The second term is the composition
derivative of the phase electric enthalpy and therefore contributes to the
electrochemical component potential.  If \(\omega_\xi^+\) includes
composition-dependent dielectric or electrical-double-layer free energy, this
derivative contains the corresponding electrostatic or double-layer
contribution
\cite{newman2004electrochemical,leroy2004triple}.
%
Choosing component $N$ as the reference and comparing against
$\alpha=1,\ldots,N-1$ gives a system of $N-1$ equations,
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{align}
	 & \rho_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq \alpha}}
	- \left.\p{\psi_{\xi}}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mbf F_\xi,\bar\rho_{\xi},\eta_{\xi}^{\beta\neq N}}
	\right)
	+
	\phi_{\xi}\left(
	\left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq\alpha},\theta_\xi}
	- \left.\p{\omega_\xi^+}{\eta_{\xi}^N}\right\vert_{\mbf E,\bar\rho_\xi,\eta_\xi^{\beta\neq N},\theta_\xi}
	\right)
	\notag                                                 \\
	 & \qquad = \frac{\pi_{\xi}^\alpha}{\eta_{\xi}^\alpha}
	- \frac{\pi_{\xi}^N}{\eta_{\xi}^N},
	\qquad \alpha=1,\ldots,N-1.
	\label{eq:pi_difference_system}
\end{align}
% AGENT-LOCK-END

Finally, the component-material-measure variations
\(\delta\mc C_\xi^\alpha\) give
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
	= \frac{1}{2}\mbf v_\xi\cdot\mbf v_\xi
	+L_\xi^\alpha
	-\frac{\pi_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha}.
	\label{eq:el_conversion_component}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:el_conversion_component} is the componentwise extension of
the Bedford--Drumheller transfer-potential equation, with microinertia omitted
\cite[eq.~(2.8)]{drumheller1980thermomechanical}
\cite[eq.~(48)]{drumheller2000}.  Because \(\tau\) is one scalar field shared
by all phases and components, one phase trajectory supplies its evolution
equation.  The remaining phase-reference equations determine the corresponding
generalized transfer-work coefficients \(L_\xi^N\), while the within-phase
component differences determine \(L_\xi^\alpha-L_\xi^N\).  The square closure
and its thermodynamic restrictions are given in
Section~\ref{sec:MC_component_supply_forces}.

\subsubsection{Phase momentum balance}
\label{sec:phase_momentum_balance}

The phase-motion variation \(\delta\mbf x_\xi\), after substitution of
\eqref{eq:el_lambda}, gives
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: fixed through rendered equation (49); do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\xi}\mbf{a}_{\xi} =
	\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\bs\sigma_{\xi}^{\prime+} + \rho_{\xi}\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_{\xi}
	+ \phi_{\xi} \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\lambda
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_\xi\omega_\xi^+\right)
	+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_\xi^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_\xi\right),
	\label{eq:el_momentum_uniform}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The fluid-phase balance contains additional capillary forces and is reduced
separately below.

\subsubsection{Fluid-phase momentum balance}
\label{sec:fluid_phase_momentum}

For any fluid phase $f\in\mc F$, the scalar-transport capillary contribution
gives the intermediate form
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf a_f = \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f^{\prime+} + \rho_f\mbf g + \mbf b_f -\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f -\sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf x}\pi_f^\alpha +\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right).
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_start}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Using \(\sum_\alpha\pi_f^\alpha=\phi_f\bar p_f\) and
\(\lambda=\gamma_f+\omega_f^+-\bar p_f\), the multiplier,
interfacial-gradient, and electric-enthalpy terms collapse as
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{equation}
	-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	-\nabla_{\mbf x}\left(\phi_f\bar p_f\right)
	+\gamma_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f
	=
	-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\bar p_f
	-\omega_f^+\nabla_{\mbf x}\phi_f .
	\label{eq:fluid_capillary_pressure_gradient_collapse}
\end{equation}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
Substituting the total reversible stress definition
\eqref{eq:vp_total_reversible_stress_definition}, the Maxwell stress
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_stress_definition}, and the pressure relation
\eqref{eq:fluid_pressure_primitive_potential_relation} cancels the isotropic
Maxwell contribution against the explicit electric-enthalpy
volume-fraction force.  The fluid momentum balance is therefore
%
% AGENT-LOCK-BEGIN equation-lock: do not edit unless explicitly unlocked by user
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{align}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	& = \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
		\bs\sigma_f'
		+\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f
	\right)
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}
	\left(
		\bar p_E+\gamma_f
	\right)
	+ \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f
	+\sum_\alpha\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha
	\left(\nabla_{\mbf x}\tau-\mbf v_f\right),
	\qquad f\in\mc F .
	\label{eq:el_mom_f_simplified}
\end{empheq}
% AGENT-LOCK-END
%
The electrical force in \eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} is consequently carried
by the anisotropic Maxwell term
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot(\phi_f\mbf E\otimes\mbf d_f)\), while the isotropic
electric-enthalpy contribution is absorbed into the equivalent-pressure
relation.  The primitive capillary potential remains through
\(-\phi_f\nabla_{\mbf x}\gamma_f\).  This separated form is used in the
solid--fluid momentum roll-up.  Related Maxwell forces appear in
leaky-dielectric and electrowetting theories
\cite{melcher1969electrohydrodynamics,yamamoto2015contact_angle_saturation}.

Under the neutral, conversion-free specialization
\(\omega_f^+=0\) and \(\phasedot{c}_f^\alpha=0\),
\eqref{eq:el_mom_f_simplified} reduces to the standard pressure-gradient form,
%
\begin{equation}
	\rho_f\mbf{a}_f
	= \nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\bs\sigma_f'
	- \phi_f\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\bar p_f
	+ \rho_f\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_f .
	\label{eq:neutral_conversion_free_fluid_momentum_balance}
\end{equation}
%

\subsubsection{Electrostatic field equation}
\label{sec:electrostatic_field_equation}

The electrostatic-potential variation \(\delta\varphi\) gives Gauss' law,
%
\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d=\varrho .
	\label{eq:gauss_law}
\end{equation}
%
The boundary coefficient in
\eqref{eq:vp_electrical_mechanical_power} gives
\(\mbf d\cdot\mbf n=\bar q\) on \(\Gamma_q\), while \(\varphi\) is prescribed
on \(\Gamma_\varphi\).

Combined with the local charge balance from \sref{conservation_of_charge}, the
electroquasistatic field equation implies the following equivalent compatibility
forms.
%
\begin{subequations}
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_compatibility}
	\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
		\frac{\partial\mbf d}{\partial t}
		+
		\mbf i
		+
		\mbf i_{\rm ext}
	\right)
	=
	s_{\rm ext}.
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_eulerian_compatibility}
	\end{equation}
	%
	\begin{equation}
	\nabla_{\mbf X}\cdot
	\left[
		\left.
		\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
		\left(
		J\mbf F^{-1}\mbf d
		\right)
		\right\vert_{\mbf X}
		+
		J\mbf F^{-1}
		\left(
		\mbf i+\mbf i_{\rm ext}
		-\varrho\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)
	\right]
	=
	Js_{\rm ext}.
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_reference_compatibility}
	\end{equation}
	%
	\begin{align}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left[
		\dot{\mbf d}
		+
		\left(
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\mbf d
		-
		\left(
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right)\mbf d
		\right.
		&\notag \\
		\left.
		+
		\mbf i+\mbf i_{\rm ext}
		-\varrho\mbf v_{\mc S}
		\right]
		&=
	s_{\rm ext}.
	\label{eq:charge_gauss_skeleton_compatibility}
	\end{align}
\end{subequations}

Equation~\eqref{eq:charge_gauss_eulerian_compatibility} uses the Eulerian
partial derivative obtained by differentiating Gauss' law at fixed
\(\mbf x\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:charge_gauss_reference_compatibility}
differentiates its Piola transform on the skeleton reference, and
\eqref{eq:charge_gauss_skeleton_compatibility} is the corresponding
push-forward written with the ordinary-dot convention
\eqref{eq:MC_skeleton_rate_convention}.  Equivalently, the vector inside its
divergence may be written as
\(\dot{\mbf d}-(\mbf v_{\mc S}\cdot\nabla_{\mbf x})\mbf d
+\mbf i+\mbf i_{\rm ext}\); the two expressions differ by a curl and therefore
have the same divergence.  These are compatibility relations, not additional
Euler--Lagrange equations.  Their signs follow from
\(\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf d=\varrho\) and
\eqref{eq:mixture_charge_balance}; every term inside the spatial divergence has
units of current per area and \(s_{\rm ext}\) has units of current per volume.
The material-only limit sets
\(\varrho_{\rm ext}=0\), \(\mbf i_{\rm ext}=\mbf0\), and \(s_{\rm ext}=0\).
The neutral specialization additionally sets \(\omega_\xi^+=0\) and
\(z_\xi^\alpha=0\), where \(z_\xi^\alpha\) is the charge per unit component
mass.

\subsection{Onsager principle for dissipative reaction and transport}
\label{sec:onsager_reaction_transport}

The Hamilton--d'Alembert variation determines the reversible coordinate
equations at fixed endpoint variations.  Reactions and relative component
transport are determined separately by an Onsager principle: at a fixed current
thermodynamic state, the process rates for the immediate response are chosen by
stationarity of an entropy-rate functional containing a dissipation potential
and the linear thermodynamic rate~\cite{doi2011onsager,wang2021onsager}.  The Onsager
statement is local in time and selects near-equilibrium rates at the current
thermodynamic state.  The entropy-rate form displayed in this subsection
remains valid when \(\theta_{\mc F}\ne\theta_{\mc S}\): reaction and transport
are driven by the temperature-weighted potentials given in
\eqref{eq:MC_temperature_weighted_reaction_power} and
\eqref{eq:MC_relative_transport_closures}.  The state variables that enter the
potentials are held fixed during this minimization.  For each active phase,
select one active reference component \(N\).  The independent process rates are
\(\dot r_{(m)}\), \(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\), and
\(\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\) for \(\alpha\ne N\).  The reference-component
fluxes are reconstructed separately for dispersion and diffusion by
%
\begin{equation}
	\mbf j_{\xi,k}^N
	=
	-\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha,
	\qquad
	k\in\{{\rm disp},{\rm diff}\}.
	\label{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
%
Thus every admissible flux variation satisfies the same zero-sum condition.
The held-fixed current configuration and constitutive state include
\(\mbf F_\xi\), \(\bar\rho_\xi\), \(\eta_\xi^\alpha\), \(\phi_\xi\),
\(\theta_{\mc F}\), \(\theta_{\mc S}\), \(\varphi\), \(J_\xi\), and any internal
variables entering the potentials or mobilities.

The fixed electrochemical state coefficient used for relative transport is
%
\begin{equation}
	\mu_\xi^\alpha
	=
	\hat{\mu}_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi .
	\label{eq:C45_combined_def}
\end{equation}
%
Here \(\varphi\) has units of energy per charge and \(z_\xi^\alpha\) has units
of charge per unit component mass, so \(z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\) has the same
units as the neutral chemical potential \(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha\).  The electric
potential is inherited from the Hamilton electric-field problem and shifts the
chemical force to the electrochemical force.  The potentials
\(\mu_\xi^\alpha\), the phase free energies \(\psi_\xi\), and the
transfer-work coefficients \(L_\xi^\alpha\) are held fixed during rate
stationarity; they are thermodynamic state coefficients, not rate-constraint
multipliers.  The absolute coefficient of conversion is
\(\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha+z_\xi^\alpha\varphi\), while
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_transfer_potential_difference} shows that its
within-phase differences equal
\(\mu_\xi^\alpha-\mu_\xi^N\).  These within-phase electrochemical differences
provide the uniform-temperature relative-transport forces and enter the
stoichiometric sum that defines the traditional reaction affinity.
When temperature is nonuniform, its neutral part and the electric field must
be kept separate to preserve invariance under the additive gauge of
\(\varphi\).

The accumulation rate
\(\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi\) is not an additional independent
Onsager rate.  It is eliminated before stationarity by using
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate} and
\eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}.  For \(\xi\in\mc G\), the
resulting neutral thermodynamic rate is combined with the direct work of the
electric field on the relative charge flux.  The zero-sum reconstruction gives
\(\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha
=\sum_{\alpha\ne N}(z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N)\mbf j_{\xi,k}^\alpha\), and the
component balance and \(\mbf E=-\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\) give the
gauge-invariant identity
%
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\sum_\alpha
		\frac{
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\frac{\phasedot{\mc C}_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}
		\\
		&\quad -
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_\alpha z_\xi^\alpha
			\left(
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
				+
				\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
			\right)\cdot\mbf E
		\\
		&={}
		\sum_m
		\left(
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right)\dot r_{(m)}
		\\
		&-
		\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
		\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)
		\\
		&+
		\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\cdot
		\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right).
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:component_thermodynamic_rate_reduction}
\end{equation}
%
The component-rate coefficient is the neutral transfer coefficient
\((\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha)/\theta_{\mc G}\).  Its within-phase differences are
\(\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N\), while the direct electrical work supplies
the field-force term.  For a mechanism that conserves charge separately within
each thermal subsystem, its neutral stoichiometric sum may instead be written
with the electrochemical coefficients in
\eqref{eq:C45_combined_def}; the added electrical sum is identically
zero.  Charge transfer between the thermal subsystems requires the
reaction--energy admissibility condition stated below, with this same neutral
reaction coefficient; the two rates may be Onsager-coupled.  Because the
electrical transport term uses \(\mbf E\), not \(\varphi\) or a
potential-energy flux, both sides of
\eqref{eq:component_thermodynamic_rate_reduction} are gauge invariant.

After the dependent accumulation rate has been eliminated, the Onsager
entropy-rate functional is the dissipation potential minus the reaction and
relative-transport entropy production.  Equivalently, its linear part is the
temperature-weighted thermodynamic rate in
\eqref{eq:component_thermodynamic_rate_reduction}:
%
\begin{align}
	\mc R
	={}&
	\Phi_{\rm rt}
	+
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_m
	\left[
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
	\right]\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag \\
	&-
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\frac{\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot
	\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\dv,
	\notag \\
	&+
	\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\frac{
			z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi\cdot
		\left(
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
			+
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\dv,
	\notag \\
	&+
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\int_{\Gamma_{\mu,\xi}^{\alpha}}
		\overline{
		\left(
		\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
		\right)/\theta_{\mc G}
		}
	\left(
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot\mbf n\,{\rm d}a,
	\notag \\
	\delta\mc R
	={}&0 .
	\label{eq:onsager_stationarity}
\end{align}
%
The added surface term is the work of a prescribed external component
reservoir on \(\Gamma_{\mu,\xi}^{\alpha}\).  It prescribes the neutral
potential difference divided by temperature; the electrical boundary datum is
provided independently by the electric-field problem.  The reservoir term
acts on the relative component flux only; the phase-advective flux is held
fixed during the Onsager rate variation.

The near-equilibrium quadratic dissipation potential used here is
%
\begin{align}
	\Phi_{\rm rt}
	={} &
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_m\frac{\dot r_{(m)}^2}{K_{(m)}}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
	\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\cdot
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta\dv.
	\label{eq:onsager_quadratic_reaction_transport_potential}
\end{align}
%
Here \(K_{(m)}\) is the mobility conjugate to the temperature-weighted reaction
force, and
\(\mbf D_{\xi,k}^{\alpha\beta}\), with
\(\alpha,\beta\ne N\), are the reduced mobility tensors in the independent
relative-flux coordinates; their inverses are taken in the same
\(N-1\)-dimensional component space.  The reference flux is then reconstructed
by \eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}.  The adopted quadratic
potential is block diagonal in reaction, diffusion, and dispersion rates.  For
a charged mechanism that transfers net charge between the thermal subsystems,
the neutral reaction force remains unchanged; the reaction and
interphase-energy rates may instead be included in one gauge-invariant Onsager
block satisfying \eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}.  A
block-diagonal choice is also admissible when the reaction-energy term is
closed separately.
Varying
\(\Phi_{\rm rt}\) gives
%
\begin{align}
	\delta\Phi_{\rm rt}
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}\,
	\delta\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
		\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\left[
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
		\int_{\mc B}
	\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\left[
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
		\right]\cdot
	\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\dv.
	\label{eq:reaction_transport_dissipation_variation}
\end{align}
%
The corresponding first variation of the linear thermodynamic-rate part of
\(\mc R\), with the current state and potentials held fixed, is
%
\begin{align}
	\delta\left(\mc R-\Phi_{\rm rt}\right)
	={} &
		\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
		\left[
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
		\left(
		\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
		\right)
		\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]
		\delta\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
		\int_{\mc B}
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\left[
			\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\frac{
					\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
				}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right)
			+
			\frac{
				z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right]\cdot
		\left(
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
		\right)\dv
		\notag  \\
	    & + \text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:onsager_thermodynamic_rate_variation}
\end{align}
%
On \(\Gamma_{m,\xi}^{\alpha}\), prescribing the normal total component flux at
fixed phase velocity gives
\((\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha+
\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha)\cdot\mbf n=0\).  On the complementary
\(\Gamma_{\mu,\xi}^{\alpha}\), the boundary part of the variation, including
the reservoir term in \eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity}, is
%
\begin{align}
	\left.
	\delta\left(\mc R-\Phi_{\rm rt}\right)
	\right\vert_{\Gamma_\mu}
	={}&
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}
	\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
	\int_{\Gamma_{\mu,\xi}^{\alpha}}
	\left[
		\frac{\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		-
		\overline{
			\left(
			\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			\right)/\theta_{\mc G}
		}
	\right]
	\notag \\
	&\qquad\qquad
	{}\times
	\left(
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot\mbf n\,{\rm d}a .
	\label{eq:onsager_component_boundary_term}
\end{align}
%
Arbitrary normal relative-flux variations on this boundary therefore recover
the prescribed difference in temperature-weighted neutral potentials.  The
electric-potential or electric-displacement datum is supplied independently by
the electrostatic boundary-value problem.

For each independent component, complementary boundary portions therefore
prescribe either the normal total component mass flux
%
\begin{equation}
	\left(
		\rho_\xi^\alpha\mbf v_\xi
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
		+
		\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	\right)\cdot\mbf n
	=
	\bar m_{\xi}^{\alpha,{\rm out}}
	\qquad
	\text{on }\Gamma_{m,\xi}^\alpha,
	\label{eq:normal_total_component_mass_flux}
\end{equation}
%
or the temperature-weighted neutral-potential difference
\((\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N)/\theta_{\mc G}\) on the complementary
boundary, together with the independent electrical boundary datum.
The no-flux condition sets
\(\bar m_{\xi}^{\alpha,{\rm out}}=0\).  Reference-component relative fluxes are
reconstructed from
\eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}; prescribed relative-flux data
must consequently satisfy the same zero-sum restriction.

\subsubsection{Collected Onsager variation}
\label{sec:collected_onsager_variation}

The Onsager variations can now be collected in the same way as the
Hamilton--d'Alembert variations above.  The rate-dependent stationarity
condition is
%
\begin{align}
	0 = \delta\mc R
	={} &
	\int_{\mc B}\sum_m
	\left[
			\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}
			+
			\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
			\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
			\left(
			\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
			\right)
			\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha
		\right]\delta\dot r_{(m)}\dv
	\notag \displaybreak[1] \\
	    & +
		\int_{\mc B}
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\notag \\
	    & \qquad
		\left[
		\sum_{\beta\ne N}
			\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
			+
			\theta_{\mc G}
			\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\frac{
					\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
				}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right)
			+
			\left(
				z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
			\right)
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
		\right]\cdot
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha\dv
	\notag  \\
	    & +
		\int_{\mc B}
		\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
		\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_{\alpha\ne N}
		\notag \\
	    & \qquad
		\left[
		\sum_{\beta\ne N}
			\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
			\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
			+
			\theta_{\mc G}
			\nabla_{\mbf x}
			\left(
				\frac{
					\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
				}{\theta_{\mc G}}
			\right)
			+
			\left(
				z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
			\right)
			\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
			\right]\cdot
		\delta\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha\dv
		\notag \\
		    & +
		\text{boundary terms}.
	\label{eq:collected_onsager_variation}
\end{align}
%
The component-rate balance is the conservation law
\eqref{eq:reference_conversion_accumulation_rate}, which was used to eliminate
the dependent accumulation rate before
\eqref{eq:onsager_stationarity}; it is not regenerated by an additional
multiplier variation.  The mechanism-rate and independent-flux variations
give, for \(\xi\in\mc G\) and \(\alpha\ne N\),
%
\begin{align}
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}
	 & =
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha,
	\label{eq:el_conversion_affinity}
	\\
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\beta
	 & =
	-\theta_{\mc G}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
		\frac{
			\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\right)
	\notag \\
	 & \quad
	-
	\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi,
	\label{eq:hamilton_dispersion_force_balance}
	\\
	\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\left(\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha\beta}
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\beta
	 & =
	-\theta_{\mc G}
	\nabla_{\mbf x}
	\left(
		\frac{
			\hat\mu_\xi^\alpha-\hat\mu_\xi^N
		}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\right)
	\notag \\
	 & \quad
	-
	\left(
		z_\xi^\alpha-z_\xi^N
	\right)
	\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi.
	\label{eq:hamilton_diffusion_force_balance}
\end{align}
%
These equations give the force balances for the \(N-1\) independent
relative-component fluxes.  The transport power is unchanged by a common shift
of all neutral component potentials in a phase or by an additive electric
gauge.  The traditional reaction affinity is the
stoichiometric sum of the electrochemical potentials; the conversion
power also contains the transfer-work contribution identified in
\eqref{eq:MC_generalized_conversion_affinity_relation}.  The uncoupled
reaction equations displayed here use the explicitly gauge-invariant neutral
sum for every mechanism.  When a charged mechanism conserves charge separately
within each thermal subsystem, the electrochemical and neutral stoichiometric
sums in each subsystem are identical.  When it transfers
charge between subsystems, the neutral form is required and any coupling to
reaction-energy transfer must satisfy
\eqref{eq:MC_reaction_energy_coupled_admissibility}.  Away from local thermal
equilibrium, its
temperature-weighted sum, rather than the affinity divided by an
assigned reaction temperature, drives the rate:
%
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\dot r_{(m)}}{K_{(m)}}
	=
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\alpha
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\alpha
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\alpha .
	\label{eq:onsager_affinity_constitutive_relation}
\end{equation}
%
The resulting near-equilibrium rate laws are
%
\begin{align}
	\dot r_{(m)}
	 & =
	K_{(m)}
	\left[
	-\sum_{\mc G\in\{\mc F,\mc S\}}
	\frac{1}{\theta_{\mc G}}
	\sum_{\xi\in\mc G}\sum_\beta
	\left(
	\psi_\xi+L_\xi^\beta
	\right)
	\nu_{\xi (m)}^\beta
	\right],
	\label{eq:onsager_reaction_rate_variational}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm disp}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\beta-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
			z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N,
	\label{eq:onsager_dispersion_mobility_balance}
	\\
	\mbf j_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^\alpha
	 & =
	-\sum_{\beta\ne N}
	\mbf D_{\xi,{\rm diff}}^{\alpha\beta}
	\left[
		\theta_{\mc G}
		\nabla_{\mbf x}
		\left(
			\frac{
				\hat\mu_\xi^\beta-\hat\mu_\xi^N
			}{\theta_{\mc G}}
		\right)
		+
		\left(
			z_\xi^\beta-z_\xi^N
		\right)
		\nabla_{\mbf x}\varphi
	\right],
	\qquad
	\xi\in\mc G,
	\qquad
	\alpha\ne N.
	\label{eq:onsager_diffusion_mobility_balance}
\end{align}
%
The two reference-component fluxes follow from
\eqref{eq:onsager_reference_flux_reconstruction}.
The entropy inequality in Section~\ref{sec:constitutive_models_entropy_inequality}
then checks that these Onsager force--rate laws are dissipative and records the
positivity assumptions that make the closures admissible.
