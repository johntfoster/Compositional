\section{Variational Derivation}
\label{app:variational}
\label{sec:variational}

We derive the governing equations from Hamilton's extended principle, following Bedford \& Drumheller~\cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical} and Drumheller~\cite{drumheller2000}, adapted to a partially saturated four-phase system (solid, aquous, oelic, gas) and arbitrary miscible components $\alpha$ within each phase and with porosity-dependent solid free energies.

Each component has volume fraction~$\phi_\xi$, intrinsic density~$\bar\rho_\xi$, bulk density $\rho_\xi = \phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi$, velocity~$\mbf{v}_\xi$, deformation gradient~$\mbf{F}_\xi$, and Jacobian $J_\xi = \det(\mbf{F}_\xi)$.  The solid phases share a common trajectory ($\mbf{v}_A = \mbf{v}_B = \mbf{v}_s$, $\mbf{F}_A = \mbf{F}_B = \mbf{F}_s$, $J_A = J_B = J_s$).  The total porosity is $\phi = \phi_f + \phi_g$, the fluid saturation is $S_f = \phi_f/\phi$, and the mass added per unit reference volume to component~$\xi$ by reactions is~$c_\xi$, with rate per unit current volume $c^+_\xi = \dot{c}_\xi / J_\xi$.

The free energies of the solid phases are written as $\psi_A(\mbf{F}_s, \phi)$ and $\psi_B(\mbf{F}_s, \phi)$, retaining the full deformation gradient and the current porosity as independent arguments.  The multiplicative decomposition $\mbf{F}_s = \mbf{A}_s \bar{\mbf F}_s$ is \emph{not} imposed at this stage; it enters later in the Coleman--Noll procedure (Appendix~\ref{sec:coleman}) when a specific constitutive form is adopted.

\subsection{Variational principle and its terms}

The extended Hamilton's principle is
\begin{equation*}
  \int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left[\delta(T - U) + \delta W + \sum_k \delta C_k\right] dt = 0,
\end{equation*}
with kinetic energy, internal energy, virtual work, and four constraints:
\begin{align}
T &= \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}}\frac{1}{2} \rho_\xi \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \dv,
  \label{eq:T_def} \\
  U &= \sum_{\xi = \lbrace \xi_s, \xi_f \rbrace} \left[ \int_{\mc{B}_{s 0}} \rho_{\xi_{s0}}\,\psi_{\xi_{s}}(\mbf{F}_{\xi_s}, \bar\rho_{\xi_s}) \,  dV_{\xi_s}
     + \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_{\xi_f}\,\psi_{\xi_f}(\bar\rho_{\xi_f}, \eta^\al_{\xi_f})  \dv \right] \notag \\ 
    &\qquad + \int_{\mc{B}} \gamma_{oa}(S_a) \,\dv + \int_{\mc{B}} \gamma_{go}(S_g)\,\dv, 
  \label{eq:U_def} \\
  \delta W &= \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left[(\rho_\xi\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_\xi)\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi + \sum_\alpha \mc{L}^\alpha_\xi\,\frac{\delta c^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi}\right] \,\dv,
  \label{eq:W_def} \\
  C_1 &= \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \,\dv,
  \label{eq:C1_def} \\
  C_2 &= \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}} \mu^\al_\xi \left( J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi 0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} \bar\rho_{\xi 0} + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \eta^\al_\xi \bar\rho_\xi} \right) \dV_\xi, 
  \label{eq:C2_def} \\
  C_3 &= \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \pi_\xi \left( \sum_\alpha \eta_\xi^\alpha - 1 \right) \dv,
  \label{eq:C3_def} \\
  C_4 &= \int_{\mc{B}} \tau \sum_\xi \sum_\alpha \hat{c}^\al_\xi \dv.
  \label{eq:C4_def}
\end{align}
%
where $\xi_s$ is the subset of $\xi$ of single-component immicible solid phases, e.g.\ $\xi_s = \lbrace A, B \rbrace$ where $A$ is a reactant and $B$ is a solid product. $\xi_f = \lbrace a, o, g \rbrace$ are the mobile aqueous, oeliec, and gas phases.  The Helmholtz free energies are $\psi_{\xi_s}$, $\psi_{\xi_f}$.  The interaction forces satisfy $\sum_\xi \mbf{b}_\xi = \mbf{0}$, and $\mc{L}^\alpha_\xi$ is a generalized force conjugate to mass transfer (identified later via Coleman--Noll).  The Lagrange multipliers~$\lambda$, $\mu^\alpha_\xi$, $\pi_\xi$, and~$\tau$ enforce the volume fraction constraint, component mass conservation, component mass partitioning within a phase, and mixture mass balance, respectively. Temperature is not treated as a kinematic variable in the variational principle; any thermal constitutive restrictions are deferred to the mixture entropy inequality.

We treat $\mbf{x}_\xi$, $\eta^\alpha_\xi$, $\phi_\xi$, $\bar\rho_\xi$, and $c_\xi^\alpha$ as independent fields (subject to constraints) and compute the variations in what follows.

\subsection{Extending Beford and Drumheller identity 1.28}

Below we extend the useful identity (1.28) of Bedford and Drumheller \cite{drumheller1980thermomechanical} to the present notation and for components as the conserved quantity, we write
%
\begin{align}
  \delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho^\alpha_\xi \Gamma_\xi \, \dv
  &= \delta \int_{\mc{B}} \left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi 0} + c^\al_\xi \right) \Gamma_\xi \dVxi \notag \\
  &= \int_{\mc{B}} \left( \delta \left(\rho^\alpha_{\xi 0} + c^\al_\xi \right) \Gamma_\xi + J_\xi \rho^\alpha_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi \right)  \dVxi \notag \\
  &= \int_{\mc{B}} \left( \delta c^\al_\xi \Gamma_\xi + J_\xi \rho^\alpha_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi \right)  \dVxi \notag \\
  &= \int_{\mc{B}} \left( \frac{\delta c^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \Gamma_\xi + \rho^\alpha_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi \right)  \dv
  \label{eq:bd128_mod2}
\end{align}
%
summing both sides of \eqref{eq:bd128_mod2} over $\alpha$ gives
\begin{align}
  \delta \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi \psi_\xi \, \dv
  &= \int_{\mc{B}} \left(\rho_\xi \delta \Gamma_\xi + \sum_\alpha \frac{\delta c^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \Gamma_\xi  \right)  \dv
  \label{eq:bd128_mod}
\end{align}
%
where we've used $\rho_\xi = \sum_\alpha \rho^\alpha_\xi$.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Variation of the kinetic energy}
\label{sec:var_T_app}
% ============================================================

The variation of kinetic energy of a using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod} is
\begin{equation}
  \delta T = \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_\xi\,\mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{v}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta c^\al_\xi}{J_\xi} \dv.
\end{equation}
Since $\delta\mbf{v}_\xi = \mathrm{d}(\delta\mbf{x}_\xi)/\mathrm{d}t$ and $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_1} = \delta\mbf{x}_\xi\vert_{t_2} = \mbf{0}$, integration by parts in time gives
\begin{equation}
  \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \delta T \dt
  = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \left(-\rho_\xi\,\mbf{a}_\xi - \sum_\al \frac{\dot{c}^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi} \mbf{v}_\xi \right) \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi + \frac{1}{2} \mbf{v}_\xi \cdot \mbf{v}_\xi \sum_\al \frac{\delta c_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi}  \dv \dt
  \label{eq:deltaT_app}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Variation of the solid internal energy}
\label{sec:var_Us_F}
% ============================================================

The solid contribution is most naturally written over the phase reference measures ${\rm d}V_{\xi_s}$. Accordingly,
%
\begin{align}
  \delta U_{\xi_s} = \sum_{\xi_s} \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi_{s0}}} \rho_{\xi_{s0}} \left[\left.\p{\psi_{\xi_s}}{\mbf{F}_{\xi_{s0}}}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_{\xi_s}} \delta \mbf{F}_{\xi_{s}} 
   + \left.\p{\psi_{\xi_s}}{\bar\rho_{\xi_s}}\right\vert_{\mbf{F}_{\xi_s}} \delta \bar\rho_{\xi_s} 
  \right] {\rm d}V_{\xi_s}.
  \label{eq:deltaU_solid}
\end{align}
%
The pushed-forward form of \eqref{eq:deltaU_solid} is
%
\begin{align}
  \delta U_{\xi_s} = \sum_{\xi_s}\int_{\mc{B}} 
    \rho_{\xi_s}\left.\p{\psi_{\xi_s}}{\bar\rho_{\xi_s}}\right\vert_{\mbf{F}_{\xi_s}} \delta \bar\rho_{\xi_s}
    - \sum_\al \frac{c^\al_{\xi_s}}{J_{\xi_s}}\left.\p{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_{\xi_s}}\right\vert_{\mbf{F}_{\xi_s}} \delta\bar\rho_{\xi_s}
    - \nabla_{\mbf x} \cdot \bs{\sigma}^\prime_{\xi_s} \cdot \delta \mbf{x}_{\xi_{s}}
   \dv.
  \label{eq:deltaU_solid_push}
\end{align}
where we've integrated by parts gives to get the standard stress divergence, i.e.\
%
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi_{s0}}} \rho_{\xi_{s 0}}\,\left.\p{\psi_{\xi_s}}{\mbf{F}_{\xi_s}}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_{\xi_s}} \delta \mbf{F}_{\xi_s} {\rm d}V_{\xi_s} = -\int_{\mc{B}} \nabla_{\mbf{x}} \cdot \bs\sigma'_{\xi_s} \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_{\xi_s} \dv + \text{boundary terms},
  \label{eq:stress_variation}
\end{equation}
%
where the Terzaghi effective Cauchy stress\footnote{i.e.\ density held fixed} is defined as
%
\begin{align}
  \bs\sigma'_{\xi_s} \equiv  \frac{1}{J_{\xi_s}} \rho_{\xi_{s0}}\left.\p{\psi_s}{\mbf{F}_{\xi_s}}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_{\xi_s}}\, \mbf{F}_{\xi_s}^T
  \label{eq:var_cauchy_def}
\end{align}

\subsection{Variation of the interfacial energies}
\label{sec:var_gamma}
% ============================================================

With three mobile phases (aqueous, oleic, and gas), let
\[
  \phi = \phi_a + \phi_o + \phi_g,
  \qquad
  S_a = \frac{\phi_a}{\phi},
  \qquad
  S_g = \frac{\phi_g}{\phi},
\]
so that $S_o = 1-S_a-S_g = \phi_o/\phi$.  We take the interfacial energy to be
\[
  U_\gamma = \int_{\mc{B}} \gamma_{oa}(S_a)\,\dv + \int_{\mc{B}} \gamma_{go}(S_g)\,\dv,
\]
which introduces two independent capillary pressures,
\begin{equation}
  p_{c,oa} \equiv -\frac{1}{\phi}\frac{\partial\gamma_{oa}}{\partial S_a} > 0,
  \qquad
  p_{c,og} \equiv -\frac{1}{\phi}\frac{\partial\gamma_{go}}{\partial S_g} > 0.
  \label{eq:pc_def}
\end{equation}
Using
\[
  \delta S_a = \frac{\phi_o+\phi_g}{\phi^2}\,\delta\phi_a - \frac{\phi_a}{\phi^2}(\delta\phi_o+\delta\phi_g),
  \qquad
  \delta S_g = \frac{\phi_a+\phi_o}{\phi^2}\,\delta\phi_g - \frac{\phi_g}{\phi^2}(\delta\phi_a+\delta\phi_o),
\]
together with the inter-component variation identity for the spatial fields, the variation of the interfacial energy contributes
\begin{align}
  \delta U_\gamma
  &= \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
      \left(-p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_o+\phi_g}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\delta\phi_a \notag \\
  &\qquad\qquad
      + \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\delta\phi_o \notag \\
  &\qquad\qquad
      + \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} - p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_a+\phi_o}{\phi}\right)\delta\phi_g
    \Bigg] \,\dv \notag \\
  &\quad + \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
      \left(-p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_o+\phi_g}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_a \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_a \notag \\
  &\qquad\qquad
      + \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_o \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_o \notag \\
  &\qquad\qquad
      + \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} - p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_a+\phi_o}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_g \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_g
    \Bigg] \,\dv.
  \label{eq:deltaU_fg}
\end{align}

\subsection{Variation of fluid, oleic, and gas energy}
\label{sec:var_Ufog}
% ============================================================

For each mobile phase $\xi_f \in \{a,o,g\}$, let
%
\begin{equation}
  U_{\xi_f} = \int_{\mc{B}} \rho_{\xi_f}\psi_{\xi_f} \dv,
  \label{eq:U_xi_current}
\end{equation}
%
with constitutive functions
%
\[
  \psi_{\xi_f} = \psi_{\xi_f}(\bar\rho_{\xi_f}, \eta^\alpha_{\xi}).
\]
%
%
take the variation the mobile free energy
%
\begin{align}
  \delta\psi_{\xi_f}
  &= \left.\p{\psi_{\xi_f}}{\bar\rho_{\xi_f}}\right\vert_{\eta^\al_{\xi}} \delta \bar\rho_\xi + \sum_\al
  \left.\p{\psi_\xi}{\eta^\al_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_{\xi_f},\eta^{\beta\neq\al}_\xi}
  \delta\eta^\al_\xi
  \label{eq:delta_psi_mobile}
\end{align}
%
Using \eqref{eq:bd128_mod} and collecting coefficients of the independent variations
therefore gives
%
\begin{align}
  \delta U_{\xi_f}
  &= \sum_{\xi_f \in\{a,o,g\}} \int_{\mc{B}} \left[
    \rho_{\xi_f} \left.\p{\psi_{\xi_f}}{\bar\rho_{\xi_f}}\right\vert_{\eta^\alpha_\xi}
    \delta\bar\rho_{\xi_f} + \sum_\alpha  \left(
  \left.\rho_\xi\p{\psi_{\xi_f}}{\eta^\alpha_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_{\xi_f},\eta^{\beta\neq\alpha}_\xi} \delta \eta^\al_\xi + \psi_{\xi_f} \frac{\delta c^\alpha_{\xi_f}}{J_{\xi_f}} \right)
  \right] \dv.
  \label{eq:deltaU_mobile_total}
\end{align}
Thus the mobile free energies contribute to the \(\delta\bar\rho_\xi\), \(\delta\eta^\alpha_\xi\), and \(\delta c^\alpha_\xi\) coefficients.

\subsection{Variation of constraint $C_1$: volume-fraction}
\label{sec:var_C1_app}
% ============================================================

The constraint $C_1 = \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1)\,\dv$ is written in the current configuration, so the natural variation is Eulerian, i.e. taken at fixed spatial point $\mbf{x}$.  Since each $\phi_\xi$ is a constituent-attached spatial field, we use the Bedford--Drumheller identity
\begin{equation}
  \left.\delta\phi_\xi\right\vert_{\mbf{x}}
  = \delta\phi_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi,
  \label{eq:delta_phi_BD_local}
\end{equation}
to express the variation in terms of the spatial variation $\delta\phi_\xi$ and the motion variation $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi$. Therefore
\begin{align}
  \delta C_1
  &= \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\lambda \left(\sum_\xi\phi_\xi - 1\right) \dv
  + \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \lambda\left(\delta\phi_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv.
  \label{eq:deltaC1_app}
\end{align}
The first term enforces the saturation constraint $\sum_\xi\phi_\xi = 1$. The remaining terms contribute $\lambda$ to each $\delta\phi_\xi$ equation and $-\lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_\xi$ to each momentum variation $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi$.

\subsection{Variation of constraint $C_2$: component material mass balance}
\label{sec:var_C2_app}
% ============================================================

Let
\begin{equation}
  h_\xi^\alpha \equiv J_\xi - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta^\al_{\xi 0} + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha},
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  C_2 = \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha\int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}\mu_\xi^\alpha h_\xi^\alpha\,dV_\xi.
  \label{eq:C2_component_measure}
\end{equation}
%
The variation of \(h_\xi^\alpha\) is
%
\begin{equation}
  \delta h_\xi^\alpha
  = \delta J_\xi
  + \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi^2\bar\rho_\xi \eta^\al_\xi}\,\delta\phi_\xi
  + \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi^2 \eta^\al_\xi}\,\delta\bar\rho_\xi
  + \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi (\eta^\al_\xi)^2}\,\delta\eta^\al_\xi
  - \frac{1}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta c_\xi^\alpha.
  \label{eq:dh}
\end{equation}
%
Hence
%
\begin{align}
  \delta C_2
  &= \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
      \delta\mu_\xi^\alpha\, h_\xi^\alpha \, dV_\xi
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  + \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
      \mu_\xi^\alpha \delta J_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  + \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
      \mu_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}^\alpha + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi^2\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta\phi_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  + \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
      \mu_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}^\alpha + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi^2 \eta^\al_\xi}\,\delta\bar\rho_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  + \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
      \mu_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}^\alpha + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi (\eta^\al_\xi)^2}\,\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  - \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}}
      \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta c_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi.
  \label{eq:deltaC2_lines}
\end{align}
Using $\dv = J_\xi {\rm d}V_\xi$, the individual contributions push forward as follows.

\paragraph{$\delta J_\xi$ terms (from $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi$):}
\begin{align}
  \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
    \mu_\xi^\alpha \delta J_\xi \, dV_\xi
  &= \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
    \mu_\xi^\alpha\,\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi \, \dv
  \notag \\
  &= -\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
    \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_\xi^\alpha \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi \, \dv
    + \text{b.t.}
  \label{eq:C2_dJ}
\end{align}

\paragraph{$\delta\phi_\xi$ terms:}
\begin{align}
  \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
    \mu_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta_{\xi 0}^\alpha + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi^2\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta\phi_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
  &= \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
    \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi} \delta\phi_\xi \, \dv.
  \label{eq:C2_dphi_push}
\end{align}

\paragraph{$\delta\bar\rho_\xi$ terms:}
\begin{align}
  \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
    \mu_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta_{\xi 0}^\al + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi^2 \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta\bar\rho_\xi \, {\rm d}V_\xi
  &= \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
    \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi \dv.
  \label{eq:C2_drho_push}
\end{align}

\paragraph{$\delta\eta_\xi^\al$ terms:}
\begin{align}
  \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
    \mu_\xi^\alpha\,\frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0} \eta_{\xi 0}^\alpha + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi \bar\rho_\xi (\eta_\xi^\alpha)^2}\,\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha \, {\rm d}V_\xi
  &= \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
    \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\xi}\delta\bar\rho_\xi \dv.
  \label{eq:C2_drho_push}
\end{align}

\paragraph{$\delta c_\xi^\alpha$ terms:}
\begin{align}
  -\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}_{\xi0}^\alpha}
    \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\delta c_\xi^\alpha \, dV_\xi
  &= -\sum_\xi\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}}
    \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi \eta_\xi^\alpha}\,\frac{\delta c_\xi^\alpha}{J_\xi} \, \dv.
  \label{eq:C2_dc_push}
\end{align}

\subsection{Variation of constraint $C_3$: mass partition within phase}
\label{sec:var_C3_app}
% ============================================================

\begin{align}
  \delta C_3
  &= \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\pi_\xi \left(\sum_\al \eta_\xi^\al - 1\right) \dv
  + \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}} \pi_\xi \left(\delta\eta^\al_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\eta_\xi^\al\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_\xi\right) \dv, \notag \\
  &= \sum_\xi \int_{\mc{B}} \delta\pi_\xi \left(\sum_\al \eta_\xi^\al - 1\right) \dv
  + \sum_\xi \sum_\al \int_{\mc{B}} \pi_\xi \delta\eta^\al_\xi \dv.
  \label{eq:deltaC3_app}
\end{align}
%
The first line vanishes on the constraint surface $\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha = 1$ and recovers it as an Euler--Lagrange equation.  The second line contributes $\pi_\xi$ to each $\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha$ Euler--Lagrange equation.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Variation of constraint $C_4$: global mass conservation}
\label{sec:var_C4_app}
% ============================================================

Recalling
\begin{equation}
  C_4 = \int_{\mc{B}} \tau \sum_\xi \sum_\alpha \frac{\dot{c}^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi}  \,\dv,
  \label{eq:c4_var}
\end{equation}
%
we'll compute the variations term-by-term for clarity, first let's define a new spatial field as the collection
\[
  \hat{c}^\alpha_\xi(\mbf{x}) \equiv \frac{\dot{c}^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi}.
\]
%
The variation of $\hat{c}^\alpha_\xi$ then proceeds with the identity of a variation at a fixed spatial point
%
\[
  \left.\delta\hat{c}^\alpha_\xi\right\vert_{\mbf{x}} = \delta\hat{c}^\alpha_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}} \hat{c}^\alpha_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi
\]
%
Now expanding the collection and computing the local variations
\begin{align}
  \delta\hat{c}^\alpha_\xi &=  \frac{\delta \dot{c}^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi}  - \frac{\dot{c}^\alpha_\xi }{J_\xi} \nabla_\mbf{x} \cdot \delta \mbf{x}_\xi \notag \\
   &=  \frac{\delta \dot{c}^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi}  - \hat{c}^\alpha_\xi \nabla_\mbf{x} \cdot \delta \mbf{x}_\xi \notag \\
\end{align}
%

Computing the variation of the first term in \eqref{eq:c4_var} gives
\begin{align}
  \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \int_{\mc{B}} \sum_\xi \sum_\alpha \tau \left.\delta\hat{c}^\alpha_\xi \right\vert_{\mbf x} \dv \dt 
  &=  \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \int_{\mc{B}} \sum_\xi \sum_\alpha  \frac{\tau}{J_\xi} \delta \dot{c}^\alpha_\xi - \tau \hat{c}^\alpha_\xi \nabla_\mbf{x} \cdot \delta \mbf{x} - \tau \nabla_{\mbf{x}} \hat{c}^\alpha_\xi \cdot \delta\mbf{x}_\xi \dv \dt \notag \\
   &= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \int_{\mc{B}} \sum_\xi \sum_\alpha \frac{\tau }{J_\xi} \delta \dot{c}^\alpha_\xi  - \tau \nabla_\mbf{x} \cdot \left(\hat{c}^\alpha_\xi \delta \mbf{x}_\xi \right) \dv \dt \notag \\
   &= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \int_{\mc{B}} \sum_\xi \sum_\alpha  - \frac{D_\xi \tau}{Dt}\frac{\delta c^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi}  + \frac{\dot{c}^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi} \nabla_{\mbf x} \tau \cdot  \delta \mbf{x}_\xi  \dv \dt + \text{b.t.}
   \label{eq:c4_term1}
\end{align}
%
where we've integrated by parts in time (with $\delta c_\xi^\alpha|_{t_1} = \delta c_\xi^\alpha|_{t_2} = 0$) and integrated by parts in space (supressing the boundary terms (b.t.) for brevity).  

% ============================================================
\subsection{Collected variational principle}
\label{sec:collected_var}
% ============================================================

Evaluating all variations and collecting by independent variations $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi$, $\delta\phi_\xi$, $\delta\bar\rho^\alpha_\xi$, $\delta\eta^\alpha_\xi$, and $\delta c^\alpha_\xi/J_\xi$ gives
\begin{align}
  0 = &\int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\rho_s\mbf{a}_s + \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot(\bs\sigma'_s)^T + \rho_s\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_s
    - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_s
    - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_s^\alpha
    + \sum_\alpha c^{+\alpha}_s\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau
    - P^\star_\phi\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi
  \Bigg]\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_s \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\rho_a\mbf{a}_a + \rho_a\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_a
    - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_a
    - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_a^\alpha
    + \sum_\alpha c^{+\alpha}_a\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau
    + P^\star_\phi\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_a
    + \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_o+\phi_g}{\phi} - p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_a
  \Bigg]\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_a \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\rho_o\mbf{a}_o + \rho_o\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_o
    - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_o
    - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_o^\alpha
    + \sum_\alpha c^{+\alpha}_o\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau
    + P^\star_\phi\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_o
    - \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_o
  \Bigg]\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_o \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\rho_g\mbf{a}_g + \rho_g\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_g
    - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_g
    - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_g^\alpha
    + \sum_\alpha c^{+\alpha}_g\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau
    + P^\star_\phi\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_g
    + \left(-p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_a+\phi_o}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_g
  \Bigg]\cdot\delta\mbf{x}_g \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
      \lambda + \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_s^\alpha}{\phi_s}
    \Bigg] \delta\phi_s \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
      \lambda + \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_a^\alpha}{\phi_a}
      - P^\star_\phi
      + p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_o+\phi_g}{\phi} - p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}
    \Bigg] \delta\phi_a \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
      \lambda + \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_o^\alpha}{\phi_o}
      - P^\star_\phi
      - p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} - p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}
    \Bigg] \delta\phi_o \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
      \lambda + \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_g^\alpha}{\phi_g}
      - P^\star_\phi
      - p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_a+\phi_o}{\phi}
    \Bigg] \delta\phi_g \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\frac{\rho^\alpha_{s0}\eta_s^\alpha}{J_s}
    \left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho^\alpha_s}\right\vert_{\mbf{F},\eta^\alpha_s,\phi}
    + \frac{\mu_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}
  \Bigg] \delta\bar\rho^\alpha_s \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \sum_{\xi\in\{a,o,g\}}\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\sum_\beta \rho^\beta_\xi\left.\p{\psi^\beta_\xi}{\bar\rho^\alpha_\xi}\right\vert_{\eta^\kappa_\xi,\bar\rho^{\kappa\neq\alpha}_\xi}
    + \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha}
  \Bigg] \delta\bar\rho^\alpha_\xi \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\frac{\rho^\alpha_{s0}\eta_s^\alpha}{J_s}\left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\eta^\alpha_s}\right\vert_{\mbf{F},\bar\rho^\alpha_s,\phi}
    + \frac{\mu_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\alpha}
    + \frac{\pi_s}{J_s}
  \Bigg] \delta\eta^\alpha_s \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \sum_{\xi\in\{a,o,g\}}\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    -\sum_\beta \rho^\beta_\xi\left.\p{\psi^\beta_\xi}{\eta^\alpha_\xi}\right\vert_{\bar\rho^\kappa_\xi,\eta^{\kappa\neq\alpha}_\xi}
    + \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}
    + \frac{\pi_\xi}{J_\xi}
  \Bigg] \delta\eta^\alpha_\xi \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    \tfrac{1}{2}\mbf{v}_s\cdot\mbf{v}_s + \mc{L}^\alpha_s
    - \frac{D_s\tau}{Dt}
    - \frac{\mu_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\alpha\phi_s\bar\rho_s^\alpha}
  \Bigg] \frac{\delta c^\alpha_s}{J_s} \,\dv \notag \\
  &+ \sum_{\xi\in\{a,o,g\}}\sum_\alpha \int_{\mc{B}} \Bigg[
    \tfrac{1}{2}\mbf{v}_\xi\cdot\mbf{v}_\xi + \mc{L}^\alpha_\xi + \psi_\xi^\alpha
    - \frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
    - \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha}
  \Bigg] \frac{\delta c^\alpha_\xi}{J_\xi} \,\dv.
  \label{eq:collected_full}
\end{align}
Here $c_\xi^{+\alpha} = \dot{c}_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi$ is the mass production rate per unit current volume; the effective Cauchy stress $\bs\sigma'_s$ is defined in~\eqref{eq:var_cauchy_def}; and $P^\star_\phi$ is defined in~\eqref{eq:Pstar_phi}. The multiplier variations are omitted here because they simply return the constraint equations~\eqref{eq:C1_def}--\eqref{eq:C4_def}. Since all displayed variations are independent and arbitrary, each bracketed integrand must vanish pointwise, yielding the Euler--Lagrange equations.

\subsection{Euler--Lagrange equations}

Since the variations are independent and arbitrary, each coefficient in~\eqref{eq:collected_full} must vanish.

\paragraph{Constraints and multiplier meanings.}
The variations of the multipliers recover the defining constraints:
\begin{align}
  \phi_s + \phi_a + \phi_o + \phi_g &= 1,
  \label{eq:EL_sat}
  \\
  h_\xi^\alpha &= J_\xi\eta_\xi^\alpha - \frac{\phi_{\xi0}\bar\rho_{\xi0}^\alpha + c_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha} = 0,
  \label{eq:EL_mass_constraint}
  \\
  \sum_\alpha \eta_\xi^\alpha &= 1,
  \label{eq:EL_eta_constraint}
  \\
  \sum_\xi\sum_\alpha c_\xi^{+\alpha} &= 0.
  \label{eq:EL_mass_source_constraint}
\end{align}
Accordingly, $\lambda$ is the saturation-pressure multiplier enforcing unit total volume fraction; each $\mu_\xi^\alpha$ is the component mass-conservation multiplier for species $\alpha$ in phase $\xi$; each $\pi_\xi$ enforces the partition-of-unity of the component fractions $\eta_\xi^\alpha$ within phase $\xi$; and $\tau$ is the global mass-balance potential conjugate to reaction mass transfer. In the momentum balances below, $\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau$ acts as the standard Bedford--Drumheller reaction-driving force.

\paragraph{Generalized phase pressures.}
From the coefficients of $\delta\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha$ we obtain
\begin{align}
  \mu_s^\alpha
  &= \bar\rho_s^\alpha\,\frac{\rho_{s0}^\alpha\eta_s^\alpha}{J_s}
  \left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\bar\rho_s^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf{F},\eta_s^\alpha,\phi},
  \label{eq:mu_s_alpha}
  \\
  \mu_\xi^\alpha
  &= \bar\rho_\xi^\alpha\sum_\beta \rho_\xi^\beta
  \left.\p{\psi_\xi^\beta}{\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\eta_\xi^\kappa,\bar\rho_\xi^{\kappa\neq\alpha}}, \notag \\
  \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\phi_\xi} &= \sum_\beta \bar\rho_\xi^\alpha \bar\rho_\xi^\beta \left.\p{\psi_\xi^\beta}{\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\eta_\xi^\kappa,\bar\rho_\xi^{\kappa\neq\alpha}}, 
  \qquad \xi\in\{a,o,g\}.
  \label{eq:mu_mobile_alpha}
\end{align}
These relations show that the multipliers $\mu_\xi^\alpha$ play the role of generalized partial pressures or, more precisely, density-conjugate chemical-stress potentials. For the mobile phases they reduce to the usual phase-pressure identification when the free energy depends only on its own intrinsic density.

Summing \eqref{eq:mu_mobile_alpha} over $\alpha$ we recognize the right hand side as what's commonly refered to as the \emph{partial pressure} so we define
\begin{align}
  \bar{p}^{\alpha\beta}_\xi &\equiv \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu^\alpha_\xi}{\phi_\xi}  \notag \\
  &\equiv \sum_\alpha \sum_\beta \bar\rho_\xi^\alpha \bar\rho_\xi^\beta \left.\p{\psi_\xi^\beta}{\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\eta_\xi^\kappa,\bar\rho_\xi^{\kappa\neq\alpha}}, \qquad \xi\in\{a,o,g\}.
  \label{eq:partial_p}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Partition relations.}
From the coefficients of $\delta\eta_\xi^\alpha$,
\begin{align}
  \frac{\pi_s}{J_s}
  &= \frac{\rho_{s0}^\alpha\eta_s^\alpha}{J_s}
  \left.\p{\psi_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\alpha}\right\vert_{\mbf{F},\bar\rho_s^\alpha,\phi}
  - \frac{\mu_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\alpha},
  \label{eq:pi_s_alpha}
  \\
  \frac{\pi_\xi}{J_\xi}
  &= \sum_\beta \rho_\xi^\beta
  \left.\p{\psi_\xi^\beta}{\eta_\xi^\alpha}\right\vert_{\bar\rho_\xi^\kappa,\eta_\xi^{\kappa\neq\alpha}}
  - \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha},
  \qquad \xi\in\{a,o,g\}.
  \label{eq:pi_mobile_alpha}
\end{align}
Thus $\pi_\xi$ is the phasewise indifference potential that adjusts so the component volume fractions sum to one. Thermal constitutive restrictions are instead deferred to the mixture entropy inequality, where temperature may remain phase dependent without being varied kinematically here.

\paragraph{Volume-fraction equations.}
From the coefficients of $\delta\phi_\xi$,
\begin{align}
  \lambda &= -\sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_s^\alpha}{\phi_s},
  \label{eq:lambda_s}
  \\
  \lambda &= P_\phi^\star - p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_o+\phi_g}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi} - \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_a^\alpha}{\phi_a},
  \label{eq:lambda_a}
  \\
  \lambda &= P_\phi^\star + p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi} - \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_o^\alpha}{\phi_o},
  \label{eq:lambda_o}
  \\
  \lambda &= P_\phi^\star + p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} - p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_a+\phi_o}{\phi} - \sum_\alpha \frac{\mu_g^\alpha}{\phi_g}.
  \label{eq:lambda_g}
\end{align}
These equations identify $\lambda$ as the common saturation pressure that equilibrates the phasewise generalized pressures against the porosity-conjugate stress $P_\phi^\star$ and the capillary offsets.

\paragraph{Momentum balances.}
The coefficients of $\delta\mbf{x}_\xi$ yield
\begin{align}
  \rho_s\mbf{a}_s
  &= \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot(\bs\sigma'_s)^T + \rho_s\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_s
  - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_s
  - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_s^\alpha
  + \sum_\alpha c_s^{+\alpha}\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau
  - P_\phi^\star\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi,
  \label{eq:mom_s_raw}
  \\
  \rho_a\mbf{a}_a
  &= \rho_a\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_a
  - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_a
  - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_a^\alpha
  + \sum_\alpha c_a^{+\alpha}\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau \notag \\
  &\qquad + P_\phi^\star\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_a
  &\qquad + \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_o+\phi_g}{\phi} - p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_a,
  \label{eq:mom_a_raw}
  \\
  \rho_o\mbf{a}_o
  &= \rho_o\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_o
  - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_o
  - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_o^\alpha
  + \sum_\alpha c_o^{+\alpha}\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau \notag \\
  &\qquad + P_\phi^\star\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_o
  &\qquad - \left(p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_g}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_o,
  \label{eq:mom_o_raw}
  \\
  \rho_g\mbf{a}_g
  &= \rho_g\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_g
  - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_g
  - \sum_\alpha\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\mu_g^\alpha
  + \sum_\alpha c_g^{+\alpha}\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau \notag \\
  &\qquad + P_\phi^\star\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_g
  &\qquad + \left(-p_{c,oa}\frac{\phi_a}{\phi} + p_{c,og}\frac{\phi_a+\phi_o}{\phi}\right)\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_g.
  \label{eq:mom_g_raw}
\end{align}
Using the volume-fraction relations \eqref{eq:lambda_s}--\eqref{eq:lambda_g}, the porosity and capillary terms combine into generalized phase-pressure gradients. Define
\begin{equation}
  p_\xi^{\mathrm{gen}} \equiv -\sum_\alpha \mu_\xi^\alpha,
  \label{eq:pgen_def}
\end{equation}
so that each mobile-phase momentum balance takes the Darcy--Bedford--Drumheller form
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\rho_\xi\mbf{a}_\xi = \rho_\xi\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_\xi - \nabla_{\mbf{x}} p_\xi^{\mathrm{gen}} + \sum_\alpha c_\xi^{+\alpha}\left(\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau\right) + \text{capillary/porosity corrections}, \qquad \xi\in\{a,o,g\},}
  \label{eq:mom_mobile_compact}
\end{equation}
while the solid balance is the effective-stress counterpart augmented by saturation and reaction forces:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\rho_s\mbf{a}_s = \nabla_{\mbf{x}}\cdot(\bs\sigma'_s)^T + \rho_s\mbf{g} + \mbf{b}_s - \lambda\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi_s - \nabla_{\mbf{x}} p_s^{\mathrm{gen}} - P_\phi^\star\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\phi + \sum_\alpha c_s^{+\alpha}\nabla_{\mbf{x}}\tau,}
  \label{eq:mom_s_compact}
\end{equation}
with $p_s^{\mathrm{gen}}\equiv -\sum_\alpha \mu_s^\alpha$. In the uncoupled limit $P_\phi^\star=0$ and without capillarity, these reduce to the standard phase momentum balances driven by effective stress and phase-pressure gradients.

\paragraph{Reaction potential equations.}
From the coefficients of $\delta c_\xi^\alpha/J_\xi$,
\begin{align}
  \frac{D_s\tau}{Dt}
  &= \tfrac{1}{2}\mbf{v}_s\cdot\mbf{v}_s + \mc{L}_s^\alpha
  - \frac{\mu_s^\alpha}{\eta_s^\alpha\phi_s\bar\rho_s^\alpha},
  \label{eq:tau_s_alpha}
  \\
  \frac{D_\xi\tau}{Dt}
  &= \tfrac{1}{2}\mbf{v}_\xi\cdot\mbf{v}_\xi + \mc{L}_\xi^\alpha + \psi_\xi^\alpha
  - \frac{\mu_\xi^\alpha}{\eta_\xi^\alpha\phi_\xi\bar\rho_\xi^\alpha},
  \qquad \xi\in\{a,o,g\}.
  \label{eq:tau_mobile_alpha}
\end{align}
These show that $\tau$ is the mixture mass-transfer potential: along each phase trajectory its material derivative balances kinetic energy, the transfer force $\mc{L}_\xi^\alpha$, the relevant free-energy contribution, and the density-conjugate multiplier term. This is the reacting-mixture analogue of the Bedford--Drumheller reaction potential.

\paragraph{Overall interpretation.}
Taken together, the multiplier structure is physically transparent:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\lambda$ is a saturation pressure enforcing packing of the four phases.
  \item $\mu_\xi^\alpha$ are generalized partial pressures / chemical-stress potentials enforcing component mass conservation within each phase.
  \item $\pi_\xi$ is the phasewise composition pressure enforcing $\sum_\alpha\eta_\xi^\alpha=1$.
  \item $\tau$ is a reaction or mass-transfer potential whose gradient drives momentum transfer through creation and destruction of mass.
\end{itemize}
When the constitutive choices are specialized so that each mobile free energy depends only on its own intrinsic density and composition, the above equations collapse to the familiar mixture-theory pattern: effective stress for the solid skeleton, phase-pressure gradients for the mobile phases, capillary offsets between mobile phases, and a reaction potential that enters both mass transfer and momentum exchange.

\subsection{Connection to the Coleman--Noll procedure}
\label{sec:coleman_connection}

The variational derivation and the Coleman--Noll procedure are complementary.  Hamilton's principle takes the free energies (e.g.\ $\psi_\xi(\mbf{F}_s, \phi)$) as functions of \emph{independent fields} (subject to constraints) and derives the momentum balances, pressure identifications, and body forces as Euler--Lagrange equations.  The Coleman--Noll procedure (Appendix~\ref{sec:coleman}) starts from the entropy inequality with the \emph{same} energy and derives constitutive restrictions.  The multiplicative decomposition $\mbf{F}_s = \mbf{A}_s \bar{\mbf{F}}_s$ is a constitutive choice imposed within the Coleman--Noll procedure, giving $\psi_\xi(\mbf{F}_s, \phi) = \psi^{\text{iso}}_\xi(\bar{\mbf{F}}_s) + \psi^{\text{dis}}_\xi(J_s, \phi)$, which is a specialization of the general $\psi_\xi(\mbf{F}_s, \phi)$ used in the variational principle.
